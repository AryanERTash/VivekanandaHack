["\n", "CCXX\n", "To Mrs. Ole Bull\n", "[THE MATH, P.O. BELUR, DIST. HOWRAH,\n", "March (?) 1902]\n", "DEAR MOTHER,[6]*\n", "I am glad Chinnu has arrived. Any hour you like will suit [me] for your\ncoming tomorrow. But it is ferocious heat here from 11 a.m. to 5 p.m.\n", "I would, therefore, suggest that you start after breakfast and remain the\nday here and have some Bengalee fish lunch, and go back in the evening.\n", "I insist on your taking a cab here and back. A cab to come and go costs\nquite  as  much  or  less  than  a  boat,  and  there is no change [of\ntransportation]. If the cabby does not understand Belur, tell him to go to a\nplace two miles south of Bally. He must know Bally, and then let him ask his\nway to the Math.\n", "One such drenching and capsizing experience as Mr. Okakura [Kakuzo] had the\nother day will unsettle your nerves for days; and we expect such rough\nweather every evening this month. The land route is nearer, easier, and\ncheaper from where you are. I have also instructed your servant, the bearer\nof the letter.\n", "Ever your Son,\n", "VIVEKANANDA.\n"]
