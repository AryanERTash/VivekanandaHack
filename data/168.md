["\n", "(Detroit Tribune, February 21, 1894)\n", "\nThe First Unitarian Church was crowded last night to hear Vive Kananda. The\naudience was composed of people who came from Jefferson Avenue and the upper\npart of Woodward Avenue. Most of it was ladies who seemed deeply interested\nin  the address and applauded several remarks of the Brahman with much\nenthusiasm.\n", "\nThe love that was dwelt upon by the speaker was not the love that goes with\npassion, but a pure and holy love that one in India feels for his God. As\nVive Kananda stated at the commencement of his address the subject was \"The\nLove the Indian Feels for His God.\" But he did not preach to his text. The\nmajor portion of his address was an attack on the Christian religion. The\nreligion of the Indian and the love of his God was the minor portion. The\npoints in his address were illustrated with several applicable anecdotes of\nfamous people in the history. The subjects of the anecdotes were renowned\nMogul emperors of his native land and not of the native Hindu kings.\n", "\nThe professors of religion were divided into two classes by the lecturer,\nthe followers of knowledge and the followers of devotion. The end in the\nlife of the followers of knowledge was experience. The end in the life of\nthe devotee was love.\n", "\nLove, he said, was a sacrifice. It never takes, but it always gives. The\nHindu never asks anything of his God, never prayed for salvation and a happy\nhereafter,  but  instead  lets  his whole soul go out to his God in an\nentrancing love. That beautiful state of existence could only be gained when\na person felt an overwhelming want of God. Then God came in all of His\nfullness.\n", "\nThere were three different ways of looking at God. One was to look upon Him\nas a mighty personage and fall down and worship His might. Another was to\nworship Him as a father. In India the father always punished the children\nand an element of fear was mixed with the regard and love for a father.\nStill another way to think of God was as a mother. In India a mother was\nalways truly loved and reverenced. That was the Indian's way of looking at\ntheir God.\n", "\nKananda said that a true lover of God would be so wrapt up in his love that\nhe would have no time to stop and tell members of another sect that they\nwere following the wrong road to secure the God, and strive to bring him to\nhis way of thinking.\n", "\n", "*          *          *\n", "(Detroit Journal)\n", "\nIf Vive Kananda, the Brahmin monk, who is delivering a lecture course in\nthis city could be induced to remain for a week longer, the largest hall in\nDetroit would not hold the crowds which would be anxious to hear him. He has\nbecome a veritable fad, as last evening every seat in the Unitarian church\nwas  occupied,  and many were compelled to stand throughout the entire\nlecture.\n", "\nThe speaker's subject was, \"The Love of God\". His definition of love was\n\"something  absolutely unselfish; that which has no thought beyond the\nglorification and adoration of the object upon which our affections are\nbestowed.\" Love, he said, is a quality which bows down And worships and asks\nnothing  in return. Love of God, he thought, was different. God is not\naccepted, he said, because we really need him, except for selfish purposes.\nHis lecture was replete with story and anecdote, all going to show the\nselfish motive underlying the motive of love for God. The Songs of Solomon\nwere cited by the lecturer as the most beautiful portion of the Christian\nBible and yet he had heard with deep regret that there was a possibility of\ntheir being removed. \"In fact,\" he declared, as a sort clinching argument at\nthe close, \"the love of God appears to be based upon a theory of 'What can I\nget  out of it?' Christians are so selfish in their love that they are\ncontinually asking God to give them something, including all manner of\nselfish things. Modern religion is, therefore, nothing but a mere hobby and\nfashion and people flock to church like a lot of sheep.\"\n", "\n"]
