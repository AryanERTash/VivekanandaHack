["\n", "THE IMPORTANCE OF PSYCHOLOGY\n", "\nThe idea of psychology in the West is very much degraded. Psychology is the\nscience of sciences; but in the West it is placed upon the same plane as all\nother sciences; that is, it is judged by the same criterion\u2014utility.\n", "\nHow much practical benefit will it do to humanity? How much will it add to\nour rapidly growing happiness? How much will it detract from our rapidly\nincreasing pain? Such is the criterion by which everything is judged in the\nWest.\n", "\nPeople  seem to forget that about ninety per cent of all our knowledge\ncannot, in the very nature of things, be applied in a practical way to add\nto  our  material happiness or to lessen our misery. Only the smallest\nfraction of our scientific knowledge can have any such practical application\nto our daily lives. This is so because only an infinitely small percentage\nof our conscious mind is on the sensuous plane. We have just a little bit of\nsensuous consciousness and imagine that to be our entire mind and life; but,\nas a matter of fact, it is but a drop in the mighty ocean of subconscious\nmind. If all there is of us were a bundle of sense-perceptions, all the\nknowledge  we could gain could be utilised in the gratification of our\nsense-pleasures. But fortunately such is not the case. As we get further and\nfurther away from the animal state, our sense-pleasures become less and\nless; and our enjoyment, in a rapidly increasing consciousness of scientific\nand psychological knowledge, becomes more and more intense; and \"knowledge\nfor the sake of knowledge\", regardless of the amount of sense-pleasures it\nmay conduce to, becomes the supreme pleasure of the mind.\n", "\nBut even taking the Western idea of utility as a criterion by which to\njudge, psychology, by such a standard even, is the science of sciences. Why?\nWe are all slaves to our senses, slaves to our own minds, conscious and\nsubconscious. The reason why a criminal is a criminal is not because he\ndesires to be one, but because he has not his mind under control and is\ntherefore a slave to his own conscious and subconscious mind, and to the\nmind of everybody else. He must follow the dominant trend of his own mind;\nhe cannot help it; he is forced onward in spite of himself, in spite of his\nown better promptings, his own better nature; he is forced to obey the\ndominant mandate of his own mind. Poor man, he cannot help himself. We see\nthis in our own lives constantly. We are constantly doing things against the\nbetter side of our nature, and afterwards we upbraid ourselves for so doing\nand wonder what we could have been thinking of, how we could do such a\nthing! Yet again and again we do it, and again and again we suffer for it\nand upbraid ourselves. At the time, perhaps, we think we desire to do it,\nbut we only desire it because we are forced to desire it. We are forced\nonward, we are helpless! We are all slaves to our own and to everybody\nelse's mind; whether we are good or bad, that makes no difference. We are\nled here and there because we cannot help ourselves. We say we think, we do,\netc. It is not so. We think because we have to think. We act because we have\nto. We are slaves to ourselves and to others. Deep down in our subconscious\nmind are stored up all the thoughts and acts of the past, not only of this\nlife, but of all other lives we have lived. This great boundless ocean of\nsubjective mind is full of all the thoughts and actions of the past. Each\none of these is striving to be recognised, pushing outward for expression,\nsurging, wave after wave, out upon the objective mind, the conscious mind.\nThese thoughts, the stored-up energy, we take for natural desires, talents,\netc.  It  is because we do not realise their true origin. We obey them\nblindly, unquestioningly; and slavery, the most helpless kind of slavery, is\nthe result; and we call ourselves free. Free! We who cannot for a moment\ngovern our own minds, nay, cannot hold our minds on a subject, focus it on a\npoint  to  the  exclusion of everything else for a moment! Yet we call\nourselves free. Think of it! We cannot do as we know we ought to do even for\na very short space of time. Some sense-desire will crop up, and immediately\nwe obey it. Our conscience smites us for such weakness, but again and again\nwe do it, we are always doing it. We cannot live up to a high standard of\nlife, try as we will. The ghosts of past thoughts, past lives hold us down.\nAll the misery of the world is caused by this slavery to the senses. Our\ninability  to  rise  above  the sense-life\u2014the striving for physical\npleasures, is the cause of all the horrors and miseries in the world.\n", "\nIt is the science of psychology that teaches us to hold in check the wild\ngyrations of the mind, place it under the control of the will, and thus free\nourselves from its tyrannous mandates. Psychology is therefore the science\nof  sciences,  without  which all sciences and all other knowledge are\nworthless.\n", "\nThe mind uncontrolled and unguided will drag us down, down, for ever\u2014rend\nus, kill us; and the mind controlled and guided will save us, free us. So it\nmust be controlled, and psychology teaches us how to do it.\n", "\nTo study and analyse any material science, sufficient data are obtained.\nThese facts are studied and analysed and a knowledge of the science is the\nresult. But in the study and analysis of the mind, there are no data, no\nfacts acquired from without, such as are equally at the command of all. The\nmind is analysed by itself. The greatest science, therefore, is the science\nof the mind, the science of psychology.\n", "\nIn the West, the powers of the mind, especially unusual powers, are looked\nupon  as  bordering  on  witchcraft and mysticism. The study of higher\npsychology has been retarded by its being identified with mere alleged\npsychic phenomena, as is done by some mystery-mongering order of Hindu\nfakirs.\n", "\nPhysicists obtain pretty much the same results the world over. They do not\ndiffer in their general facts, nor in the results which naturally follow\nfrom such facts. This is because the data of physical science are obtainable\nby  all  and  are  universally recognised, and the results are logical\nconclusions based upon these universally recognised facts. In the realm of\nthe mind, it is different. Here there are no data, no facts observable by\nthe physical senses, and no universally recognised materials therefore, from\nwhich to build a system of psychology after their being equally experimented\nupon by all who study the mind.\n", "\nDeep, deep within, is the soul, the essential man, the \u00c2tman. Turn the mind\ninward and become united to that; and from that standpoint of stability, the\ngyrations of the mind can be watched and facts observed, which are to be\nfound in all persons. Such facts, such data, are to be found by those who go\ndeep enough, and only by such. Among that large class of self-styled mystics\nthe world over, there is a great difference of opinion as to the mind, its\nnature, powers, etc. This is because such people do not go deep enough. They\nhave  noticed some little activity of their own and others' minds and,\nwithout  knowing anything about the real character of such superficial\nmanifestations, have published them as facts universal in their application;\nand every religious and mystical crank has facts, data, etc., which, he\nclaims, are reliable criteria for investigation, but which are in fact\nnothing more or less than his own imaginings\n", "\nIf you intend to study the mind, you must have systematic training; you must\npractice  to  bring  the  mind  under  your control, to attain to that\nconsciousness from which you will be able to study the mind and remain\nunmoved by any of its wild gyrations. Otherwise the facts observed will not\nbe reliable; they will not apply to all people and therefore will not be\ntruly facts or data at all.\n", "\nAmong that class who have gone deeply into the study of the mind, the facts\nobserved  have been the same, no matter in what part of the world such\npersons may be or what religious belief they may have. The results obtained\nby all who go deep enough into the mind are the same.\n", "\nThe mind operates by perception and impulsion. For instance, the rays of the\nlight enter by eyes, are carried by the nerves to the brain, and still I do\nnot see the light. The brain then conveys the impulse to the mind, but yet I\ndo not see the light; the mind then reacts, and the light flashes across the\nmind. The mind's reaction is impulsion, and as a result the eye perceives\nthe object.\n", "\nTo control the mind you must go deep down into the subconscious mind,\nclassify and arrange in order all the different impressions, thoughts, etc.,\nstored up there, and control them. This is the first step. By the control of\nthe subconscious mind you get control over the conscious.\n", "\n"]
