["\n", "XVII\n", "NEW YORK,\n", "26th April, 1894.\n", "DEAR SISTER (Miss Isabelle McKindley.),\n", "Your letter reached me yesterday. You were perfectly right \u2014 I enjoyed the\nfun of the lunatic Interior, (Chicago Interior, a Presbyterian newspaper\nwhich opposed Swamiji. \u2014 Ed.) but the mail you sent yesterday from India was\nreally,  as Mother Church says in her letter, a good news after a long\ninterval. There is a beautiful letter from Dewanji. The old man \u2014 Lord bless\nhim \u2014 offers as usual to help me. Then there was a little pamphlet published\nin Calcutta about me \u2014 revealing that once at least in my life the prophet\nhas been honoured in his own country. There are extracts from American and\nIndian papers and magazines about me. The extracts printed from Calcutta\npapers were especially gratifying, although the strain is so fulsome that I\nrefuse  to  send  the  pamphlet over to you. They call me illustrious,\nwonderful, and all sorts of nonsense, but they forward me the gratitude of\nthe whole nation. Now I do not care what they even of my own people say\nabout me \u2014 except for one thing. I have an old mother. She has suffered much\nall her life and in the midst of all she could bear to give me up for the\nservice  of  God and man; but to have given up the most beloved of her\nchildren  \u2014 her hope \u2014 to live a beastly immoral life in a far distant\ncountry, as Mazoomdar was telling in Calcutta, would have simply killed her.\nBut the Lord is great, none can injure His children.\n", "The cat is out of the bag \u2014 without my seeking at all. And who do you think\nis the editor of one of our leading papers which praise me so much and thank\nGod that I came to America to represent Hinduism? Mazoomdar's cousin!! \u2014\nPoor Mazoomdar \u2014 he has injured his cause by telling lies through jealousy.\nLord knows I never attempted any defence.\n", "I read the article of Mr. Gandhi in the Forum before this.\n", "If you have got the Review of Reviews of last month \u2014 read to mother the\ntestimony about the Hindus in connection with the opium question in India by\none  of the highest officials of the English in India. He compares the\nEnglish with the Hindus and lauds the Hindu to the skies. Sir Lepel Griffin\nwas one of the bitterest enemies of our race. What made this change of\nfront?\n", "I had a very good time in Boston at Mrs. Breed's \u2014 and saw Prof. Wright. I\nam going to Boston again. The tailor is making my new gown. I am going to\nspeak at Cambridge University [Harvard] and would be the guest of Prof.\nWright there. They write grand welcomes to me in the Boston papers.\n", "I am tired of all this nonsense. Towards the latter part of May I will come\nback to Chicago, and after a few day's stay would come back to the East\nagain.\n", "I spoke last night at the Waldorf hotel. Mrs. Smith sold tickets at $2 each.\nI had a full hall which by the way was a small one. I have not seen anything\nof the money yet. Hope to see in the course of the day.\n", "I made a hundred dollars at Lynn which I do not send because I have to make\nmy new gown and other nonsense.\n", "Do not expect to make any money at Boston. Still I must touch the brain of\nAmerica and stir it up if I can.\n", "Your loving brother,\n", "VIVEKANANDA.\n"]
