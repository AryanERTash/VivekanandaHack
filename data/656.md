["\n", "(RECORDED BY MISS S. E. WALDO, A DISCIPLE)\n", "SUNDAY MORNING, July 7, 1895.\n", "Infinite manifestation dividing itself in portion still remains infinite,\nand each portion is infinite.[6]*\n", "Brahman is the same in two forms \u2014 changeable and unchangeable, expressed\nand unexpressed. Know that the Knower and the known are one. The Trinity \u2014\nthe Knower, the known, and knowing \u2014 is manifesting as this universe. That\nGod the Yogi sees in meditation, he sees through the power of his own Self.\n", "What we call nature, fate, is simply God's will.\n", "So long as enjoyment is sought, bondage remains. Only imperfection can\nenjoy, because enjoyment is the fulfilling of desire. The human soul enjoys\nnature. The underlying reality of nature, soul, and God is Brahman; but It\n(Brahman)  is  unseen, until we bring It out. It may be brought out by\nPramantha or friction, just as we can produce fire by friction. The body is\nthe lower piece of wood, Om is the pointed piece and Dhy\u00e2na (meditation) is\nthe  friction. When this is used, that light which is the knowledge of\nBrahman will burst forth in the soul. Seek it through Tapas. Holding the\nbody upright, sacrifice the organs of sense in the mind. The sense-centres\nare within, and their organs without; drive them into the mind and through\nDh\u00e2r\u00e2na (concentration) fix the mind in Dhyana. Brahman is omnipresent in\nthe universe as is butter in milk, but friction makes It manifest in one\nplace. As churning brings out the butter in the milk, so Dhyana brings the\nrealisation of Brahman in the soul.\n", "All  Hindu  philosophy  declares  that  there  is  a  sixth sense, the\nsuperconscious, and through it comes inspiration.\n", "*    *    *\n", "The universe is motion, and friction will eventually bring everything to an\nend; then comes a rest; and after that all begins again. . . .\n", "So long as the \"skin sky\" surrounds man, that is, so long as he identifies\nhimself with his body, he cannot see God.\n", "SUNDAY AFTERNOON\n", "There are six schools of philosophy in India that are regarded as orthodox,\nbecause they believe in the Vedas.\n", "Vyasa's philosophy is par excellence that of the Upanishads. He wrote in\nSutra form, that is, in brief algebraical symbols without nominative or\nverb. This caused so much ambiguity that out of the Sutras came dualism,\nmono-dualism,  and  monism  or  \"roaring  Vedanta\";  and all the great\ncommentators in these different schools were at times \"conscious liars\" in\norder to make the texts suit their philosophy.\n", "The Upanishads contain very little history of the doings of any man, but\nnearly all other scriptures are largely personal histories. The Vedas deal\nalmost entirely with philosophy. Religion without philosophy runs into\nsuperstition; philosophy without religion becomes dry atheism.\n", "Vishishta-advaita is qualified Advaita (monism). Its expounder was R\u00e2m\u00e2nuja.\nHe says, \"Out of the ocean of milk of the Vedas, Vyasa has churned this\nbutter of philosophy, the better to help mankind.\" He says again, \"All\nvirtues and all qualities belong to Brahman, Lord of the universe. He is the\ngreatest Purusha. Madhva is a through-going dualist or Dvaitist. He claims\nthat even women might study the Vedas. He quotes chiefly from the Pur\u00e2nas.\nHe says that Brahman means Vishnu, not Shiva at all, because there is no\nsalvation except through Vishnu.\n"]
