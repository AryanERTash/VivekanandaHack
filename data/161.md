["\n", "THE HINDOO RELIGION\n", "(Minneapolis Star, November 25, 1893)\n", "\n\"Brahminism\" in all its subtle attraction, because of its embodiment of\nancient and truthful principles, was the subject which held an audience in\nclosest attention last evening at the First Unitarian Church [Minneapolis],\nwhile Swami Vive Kananda expounded the Hindoo faith. It was an audience\nwhich included thoughtful women and men, for the lecturer had been invited\nby the \"Peripatetics,\" and among the friends who shared the privilege with\nthem  were  ministers of varied denominations, as well as students and\nscholars. Vive Kananda is a Brahmin priest, and he occupied the platform in\nhis native garb, with caftan on head, orange colored coat confined at the\nwaist with a red sash, and red nether garments.\n", "\nHe  presented his faith in all sincerity, speaking slowly and clearly,\nconvincing his hearers by quietness of speech rather than by rapid action.\nHis words were carefully weighed, and each carried its meaning direct. He\noffered  the simplest truths of the Hindoo religion, and while he said\nnothing harsh about Christianity, he touched upon it in such a manner as to\nplace the faith of Brahma before all. The all-pervading thought and leading\nprinciple of the Hindoo religion is the inherent divinity of the soul; the\nsoul is perfect, and religion is the manifestation of divinity already\nexisting in man. The present is merely a line of demarkation between the\npast and future, and of the two tendencies in man, if the good preponderates\nhe will move to a higher sphere, if the evil has power, he degenerates.\nThese two are continually at work within him; what elevates him is virtue,\nthat which degenerates is evil.\n", "\nKananda will speak at the First Unitarian Church tomorrow morning.\n", "\n", "*          *          *\n", "\n", "(Des Moines News, November 28, 1893)\n", "\nSwami Vivekananda, the talented scholar from the far-off India, spoke at the\nCentral church last night [November 27]. He was a representative of his\ncountry and creed at the recent parliament of religions assembled in Chicago\nduring the world's fair. Rev. H. O. Breeden introduced the speaker to the\naudience. He arose and after bowing to his audience, commenced his lecture,\nthe subject of which was \"Hindoo Religion\". His lecture was not confined to\nany line of thought but consisted more of some of his own philosophical\nviews relative to his religion and others. He holds that one must embrace\nall the religions to become the perfect Christian. What is not found in one\nreligion is supplied by another. They are all right and necessary for the\ntrue Christian. When you send a missionary to our country he becomes a\nHindoo Christian and I a Christian Hindoo. I have often been asked in this\ncountry if I am going to try to convert the people here. I take this for an\ninsult. I do not believe in this idea of conversion.[1] To-day we have a\nsinful man; tomorrow according to your idea he is converted and by and by\nattains unto holiness. Whence comes this change? How do you explain it? The\nman has not a new soul for the soul must die. You say he is changed by God.\nGod is perfect, all powerful and is purity itself. Then after this man is\nconverted he is that same God minus the purity he gave that man to become\nholy. There is in our country two words which have an altogether different\nmeaning than they do in this country. They are \"religion\" and \"sect\". We\nhold  that religion embraces all religions. We tolerate everything but\nintoleration. Then there is that word \"sect\". Here it embraces those sweet\npeople who wrap themselves up in their mantle of charity and say, \"We are\nright; you are wrong.\" It reminds me of the story of the two frogs. A frog\nwas born in a well and lived its whole life in that well. One day a frog\nfrom the sea fell in that well and they commenced to talk about the sea. The\nfrog whose home was in the well asked the visitor how large the sea was, but\nwas unable to get an intelligent answer. Then the at home frog jumped from\none corner of the well to another and asked his visitor if the sea was that\nlarge. He said yes. The frog jumped again and said, \"Is the sea that large?\"\nand receiving an affirmative reply, he said to himself, \"This frog must be a\nliar; I will put him out of my well.\" That is the way with these sects. They\nseek to eject and trample those who do not believe as they do.\n", "\n"]
