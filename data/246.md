["\n", "XI\n", "\n", "U. S. A.,\n11th July, 1894.\n", "\n Dear Alasinga, \n", "\nYou must never write to me anywhere else but 541 Dearborn Ave., Chicago.\nYour last letter has travelled the whole country to come to me, and this was\nonly because I am so well known. Some of the resolutions are to be sent to\nDr. Barrows with a letter thanking him for his kindness to me and asking him\nto publish the letter in some American newspapers \u2014 as that would be the\nbest refutation of the false charges of the missionaries that I do not\nrepresent anybody. Learn business, my boy. We will do great things yet! Last\nyear I only sowed the seeds; this year I mean to reap. In the meanwhile,\nkeep up as much enthusiasm as possible in India. Let Kidi go his own way. He\nwill come out all right in time. I have taken his responsibility. He has a\nperfect right to his own opinion. Make him write for the paper; that will\nkeep him in good temper! My blessings on him.\n", "\nStart the journal and I will send you articles from time to time. You must\nsend a paper and a letter to Professor J. H. Wright of Harvard University,\nBoston, thanking him as having been the first man who stood as my friend and\nasking  him  to  publish  it in the papers, thus giving the lie to the\nmissionaries.\n", "\nIn the Detroit lecture I got $900, i.e. Rs. 2,700. In other lectures, I\nearned in one, $2,500, i.e. Rs. 7,500 in one hour, but got only 200 dollars!\nI was cheated by a roguish Lecture Bureau. I have given them up. I spent a\ngood deal here; only about $3,000 remains.\n", "\nI shall have to print much matter next year. I am going regularly to work. .\n. . The sheer power of the will will do everything. . . . You must organise\na society which should regularly meet, and write to me about it as often as\nyou can. In fact, get up as much enthusiasm as you can. Only, beware of\nfalsehood. Go to work, my boys, the fire will come to you! The faculty of\norganisation is entirely absent in our nature, but this has to be infused.\nThe great secret is \u2014 absence of jealousy. Be always ready to concede to the\nopinions of your brethren, and try always to conciliate. That is the whole\nsecret. Fight on bravely! Life is short! Give it up to a great cause. Why do\nyou not write anything about Narasimha? He is almost starving. I gave him\nsomething. Then he went over to somewhere, I do not know where, and does not\nwrite. Akshaya is a good boy. I like him very much. No use quarrelling with\nthe  Theosophists.  Do  not go and tell them all I write to you. . . .\nTheosophists are our pioneers, do you know? Now Judge is a Hindu and Col. a\nBuddhist, and Judge is the ablest man here. Now tell the Hindu Theosophists\nto support Judge. Even if you can write Judge a letter, thanking him as a\nco-religionist and for his labours in presenting Hinduism before Americans;\nthat will do his heart much good. We must not join any sect, but we must\nsympathise and work with each. . . . Work, work \u2014 conquer all by your love!\n. . .\n", "\nTry to expand. Remember the only sign of life is motion and growth. You must\nsend the passed resolution to Dr. J. H. Barrows. . ., Dr. Paul Carus. . .,\nSenator Palmer. . ., Mrs. J. J. Bagley. . ., it must come officially. . . .\nI write this because I do not think you know the ways of foreign nations. .\n. . Keep on steadily. So far we have done wonderful things. Onward, brave\nsouls, we will gain! Organise and found societies and go to work, that is\nthe only way.\n", "\nAt this time of the year there is not much lecturing to be done here; so I\nwill devote myself to my pen and write. I shall be hard at work all the\ntime, and then, when the cold weather comes and people return to their\nhomes,  I  shall  begin  lecturing again and at the same time organise\nsocieties.\n", "\nMy love and blessings to you all. I never forget anybody, though I do not\nwrite often. Then again, I am now, continuously travelling, and letters have\nto be redirected from one place to another.\n", "\nWork hard. Be holy and pure and the fire will come.\n", "\n", "Yours affectionately,\nVivekananda.\n"]
