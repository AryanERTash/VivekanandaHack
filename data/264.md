The Parliament of Religions was organised\nwith the intention of proving the superiority of the Christian religion over\nother forms of faith, but the philosophic religion of Hinduism was able to\nmaintain its position notwithstanding. I have been running\nall the time between Boston and New York, two great centres of this country,\nof which Boston may be called the brain and New York, the purse. No foolish talk now, but actual work; the Hindus\nmust back their talk with real work; if they cannot they do not deserve\nanything; that is all. America is not going to give you money for your fads.\nAnd why should they? As for me, I want to teach the truth; I do not care\nwhether here or elsewhere.\n", "\nIn future do not pay any heed to what people say either for or against you\nor  me.  Work  on, be lions; and the Lord will bless you. I shall work\nincessantly until I die, and even after death I shall work for the good of\nthe world. Truth is infinitely more weighty than untruth; so is goodness. If\nyou possess these, they will make their way by sheer gravity.\n", "\nI have no connection with the Theosophists. And Judge will help me \u2014 pooh! .\n. . Thousands of the best men do care for me; you know this, and have faith\nin the Lord. I am slowly exercising an influence in this land greater than\nall the newspaper blazoning of me can do. The orthodox feel it, but they\ncannot help it. It is the force of character, of purity, and of truth \u2014 of\npersonality. So long as I have these things, you can feel easy; no one will\nbe able to injure a hair of my head. If they try, they will fail, saith the\nLord. . . . Enough of books and theories. It is the life that is the highest\nand the only way to stir the hearts of people; it carries the personal\nmagnetism. . . . The Lord is giving me a deeper and deeper insight every\nday. Work, work, work. . . . Truce to foolish talk; talk of the Lord. Life\nis too short to be spent in talking about frauds and cranks\n", "\nYou must always remember that every nation must save itself; so must every\nman; do not look to others for help. Through hard work here, I shall be able\nnow and then to send you a little money for your work; but that is all. If\nyou have to look forward to that, better stop work. Know also that this is a\ngrand field for my ideas, and that I do not care whether they are Hindus or\nMohammedans or Christians, but those that love the Lord will always command\nmy service.\n", "\n. . . I like to work on calmly and silently, and the Lord is always with me.\nFollow me, if you will, by being intensely sincere, perfectly unselfish,\nand, above all, by being perfectly pure. My blessings go with you. In this\nshort life there is no time for the exchange of compliments. We can compare\nnotes and compliment each other to our hearts' content after the battle is\nfinished.
