["\n", "CXXIII\n", "\n", "DARJEELING,\n", "April 28, 1897.\n", "DEAR MARY,\n", "A few days ago I received your beautiful letter. Yesterday came the card\nannouncing Harriet's marriage. Lord bless the happy pair!\n", "The  whole  country  here rose like one man to receive me. Hundreds of\nthousands of persons, shouting and cheering at every place, Rajas drawing my\ncarriage, arches all over the streets of the capitals with blazing mottoes\netc.,!!! The whole thing will soon come out in the form of a book, and you\nwill have a copy soon. But unfortunately I was already exhausted by hard\nwork in England; and this tremendous exertion in the heat of Southern India\nprostrated me completely. I had of course to give up the idea of visiting\nother parts of India and fly up to the nearest hill station, Darjeeling. Now\nI feel much better, and a month more in Almora would complete the cure. By\nthe bye, I have just lost a chance of coming over to Europe. Raja Ajit Singh\nand several other Rajas start next Saturday for England. Of course, they\nwanted hard to get me to go over with them. But unfortunately the doctors\nwould not hear of my undertaking any physical or mental labour just now. So\nwith the greatest chagrin I had to give it up, reserving it for a near\nfuture.\n", "Dr. Barrows has reached America by this time, I hope. Poor man! He came here\nto preach the most bigoted Christianity, with the usual result that nobody\nlistened to him. Of course they received him very kindly; but it was my\nletter that did it. I could not put brains into him! Moreover, he seems to\nbe a queer sort of man. I hear that he was mad at the national rejoicings\nover my coming home. You ought to have sent a brainier man anyway, for the\nParliament of Religions has been made a farce of in the Hindu mind by Dr.\nBarrows. On metaphysical lines no nation on earth can hold a candle to the\nHindus; and curiously all the fellows that come over here from Christian\nland have that one antiquated foolishness of an argument that because the\nChristians are powerful and rich and the Hindus are not, so Christianity\nmust be better than Hinduism. To which the Hindus very aptly retort that,\nthat is the very reason why Hinduism is a religion and Christianity is not;\nbecause, in this beastly world it is blackguardism and that alone which\nprospers, virtue always suffers. It seems, however advanced the Western\nnations are in scientific culture, they are mere babies in metaphysical and\nspiritual education. Material science can only give worldly prosperity,\nwhilst spiritual science is for eternal life. If there be no eternal life,\nstill the enjoyment of spiritual thoughts as ideals is keener and makes a\nman happier, whilst the foolery of materialism leads to competition and\nundue ambition and ultimate death, individual and national.\n", "This Darjeeling is a beautiful spot with a view of the glorious Kanchenjanga\n(28,146 ft.) now and then when the clouds permit it, and from a near hilltop\none can catch a glimpse of Gauri Shankar (29,000 ft?) now and then. Then,\nthe people here too are so picturesque, the Tibetans and Nepalese and, above\nall,  the  beautiful Lepcha women. Do you know one Colston Turnbull of\nChicago? He was here a few weeks before I reached India. He seems to have\nhad a great liking for me, with the result that Hindu people all liked him\nvery much. What about Joe, Mrs. Adams, Sister Josephine, and all the rest of\nour friends? Where are our beloved Mills? Grinding slow but sure? I wanted\nto send some nuptial presents to Harriet, but with your \"terrible\" duties I\nmust reserve it for some near future. Maybe I shall meet them in Europe very\nsoon. I would have been very glad, of course, if you could announce your\nengagement, and I would fulfil my promise by filling up half a dozen papers\nin one letter....\n", "My hair is turning grey in bundles, and my face is getting wrinkled up all\nover; that losing of flesh has given me twenty years of age more. And now I\nam losing flesh rapidly, because I am made to live upon meat and meat alone\n\u2014 no bread, no rice, no potatoes, not even a lump of sugar in my coffee!! I\nam living with a Brahmin family who all dress in knickerbockers, women\nexcepted of course! I am also in knickers. I would have given you a surprise\nif you had seen me bounding from rock to rock like a chamois, or galloping\nmight and main up and down mountain roads.\n", "I am very well here, for life in the plains has become a torture. I cannot\nput the tip of my nose out into the streets, but there is a curious crowd!!\nFame is not all milk and honey!! I am going to train a big beard; now it is\nturning grey. It gives a venerable appearance and saves one from American\nscandal-mongers! O thou white hair, how much thou canst conceal, all glory\nunto thee, Hallelujah!\n", "The mail time is nearly up, so I finish. Good dreams, good health, all\nblessings attend you.\n", "With love to father and mother and you all,\n", "Yours,\n", "\nVIVEKANANDA.\n"]
