["\n", "CHAPTER VII\n", "LIFE AT SRINAGAR\n", "PLACE: Srinagar.\n", "TIME: June 22 to July 15, 1898.\n", "In the mornings we still had long talks as before \u2014 some-times it would be\nthe different religious periods through which Kashmir had passed, or the\nmorality  of Buddhism, or the history of Shiva-worship, or perhaps the\nposition of Srinagar under Kanishka.\n", "Once he was talking with one of us about Buddhism, and he suddenly said,\n\"The fact is, Buddhism tried to do, in the time of Ashoka, what the world\nnever  was  ready  for till now!\" He referred to the federalization of\nreligions. It was a wonderful picture, this, of the religious imperialism of\nAshoka, broken down time and again by successive waves of Christianity and\nMohammedanism, each claiming exclusive rights over the conscience of mankind\nand finally to seem to have a possibility, within measurable distance of\ntime, today!\n", "Another time the talk was of Genghis, or Chenghis, Khan, the conqueror from\nCentral Asia. \"You hear people talk of him as a vulgar aggressor\", he cried\npassionately, \"but that is not true! They are never greedy or vulgar, these\ngreat souls! He was inspired with the thought of unity, and he wanted to\nunify his world. Yes, Napoleon was cast in the same mould. And another,\nAlexander. Only those three, or perhaps one soul manifesting itself in three\ndifferent conquests!\" And then he passed on to speak of that one soul whom\nhe believed to have come again and again in religion, charged with the\ndivine impulse to bring about the unity of man in God.\n", "At this time the transfer of the Prabuddha Bharata from Madras to the newly\nestablished Ashrama at Mayavati was much in all our thoughts. The Swami had\nalways had a special love for this paper, as the beautiful name he had given\nit indicated. He had always been eager too for the establishment of organs\nof his own. The value of the journal in the education of modern India was\nperfectly evident to him, and he felt that his master's message and mode of\nthought required to be spread by this means as well as by preaching and by\nwork. Day after day, therefore, he would dream about the future of his\npapers, as about the work in its various centres. Day after day he would\ntalk of the forthcoming first number under the new editorship of Swami\nSwarupananda. And one afternoon he brought to us, as we sat together, a\npaper on which he said he had \"tried to write a letter, but it would come\nthis way!\" . . . [Vide [6]\"To the Awakened India\", Complete Works, IV]\n", "JUNE 26.\n", "The Master was longing to leave us all and go away into some place of quiet,\nalone.  But  we, not knowing this, insisted on accompanying him to the\nColoured Springs, called \"Kshir Bhav\u00e2ni\", or \"Milk of the Mother\". It was\nsaid to be the first time that Christian or Mohammedan had ever landed\nthere, and we can never be thankful enough for the glimpse we had of it\nsince afterwards it was to become the most sacred of all names to us. . . .\n", "JUNE 29.\n", "Another  day  we  went  off  quietly  by  ourselves  and  visited  the\nTakt-i-Suleiman, a little temple very massively built on the summit of a\nsmall  mountain  two  or three thousand feet high. It was peaceful and\nbeautiful, and the famous Floating Gardens could be seen below us for miles\naround.  The Takt-i-Suleiman was one of the great illustrations of the\nSwami's argument when he would take up the subject of the Hindu love of\nnature  as  shown in the choice of sites for temples and architectural\nmonuments. As he had declared, in London, that the saints lived on the\nhill-tops in order to enjoy the scenery, so now he pointed out \u2014 citing one\nexample after another \u2014 that our Indian people always consecrated places of\npeculiar beauty and importance by making there their altars of worship. And\nthere was no denying that the little Takt, crowning the hill that dominated\nthe whole valley, was a case in point.\n", "Many lovely fragments of those days come into mind, as:\n", "Therefore, Tulasi, take thou care to live with\nall, for who can tell where, or in what garb,\nthe Lord Himself may next come to thee?\nOne God is hidden in all these, the Torturer\nof all, the Awakener of all, the Reservoir\nof all being, the One who is bereft of all\nqualities.\nThere the sun does not shine, nor the moon,\nnor the stars.\n", "There was the story of how R\u00e2vana was advised to take the form of R\u00e2ma in\norder to cheat Sit\u00e2. He answered, \"Have I not thought of it? But in order to\ntake a man's form you must meditate on him; and Rama is the Lord Himself; so\nwhen I meditate on him, even the position of Brahm\u00e2 becomes a mere straw.\nHow, then, could I think of a woman?\"\n", "\"And so\", commented the Swami, \"even in the commonest or most criminal life,\nthere are these glimpses\". It was ever thus. He was constantly interpreting\nhuman life as the expression of God, never insisting on the heinousness or\nwickedness of the act or a character.\n", "\"In that which is dark night to the rest of the world, there the man of\nself-control is awake. That which is life to the rest of the world is sleep\nto him.\"\n", "Speaking of Thomas \u00e0 Kempis one day, and of how he himself used to wander as\na Sanny\u00e2sin with the Git\u00e2 and the Imitation as his whole library, one word,\nhe said, came back to him, inseparably associated with the name of the\nWestern monk:\n", "Silence! ye teachers of the world, and silence!\nye prophets! Speak Thou alone, O Lord, unto my soul!\n", "Again:\n", "The soft Shirisha flower can bear the weight of\nhumming bees, but not of birds \u2014\nSo Um\u00e2, don't you go and make Tapasy\u00e2!\nCome, Uma, come! delight and idol of my soul!\nBe seated, Mother, on the lotus of my heart,\nAnd let me take a long, long look at you.\nFrom my birth up, I am gazing,\nMother, at your face \u2014\nKnow you suffering what trouble,\nand pain?\nBe seated, therefore, Blessed One,\non the lotus of my heart,\nAnd dwell there for evermore.\n", "Every  now  and  then there would be long talks about the Gita \u2014 \"that\nwonderful poem, without one note in it of weakness or unmanliness.\" He said\none day that it was absurd to complain that knowledge was not given to women\nor to Shudras. For the whole gist of the Upanishads was contained in the\nGita. Without it, indeed, they could hardly be understood; and women and all\ncastes could read the Mah\u00e2bh\u00e2rata.\n", "JULY 4.\n", "With great fun and secrecy the Swami and his one non-American disciple\nprepared to celebrate the Fourth of July. A regret had been expressed in his\nhearing that we had no American flag with which to welcome the other members\nof  the party to breakfast on their national festival; and late on the\nafternoon of the third, he brought a Pundit Durzey [Brahmin tailor] in great\nexcitement, explaining that this man would be glad to imitate it if he were\ntold how. The stars and stripes were very crudely represented, I fear, on\nthe piece of cotton that was nailed with branches of evergreens to the head\nof the dining\u2014room\u2014boat when the Americans stepped on board for early tea on\nIndependence Day! But the Swami had postponed a journey in order to be\npresent at the little festival, and he himself contributed a poem to the\naddresses that were now read aloud by way of greeting. . . . [Vide [7]\"To\nthe Fourth of July\", Complete Works, V]\n", "JULY 5.\n", "That evening someone pained him by counting the cherry-stones left on her\nplate, to see when she would be married. He somehow took the play in earnest\nand  came  the following morning surcharged with passion for the ideal\nrenunciation.\n", "JULY 6.\n", "\"These shadows of home and marriage cross even my mind now and then!\" he\ncried, with that tender desire to make himself one with the sinner that he\nso often showed. But it was across oceans of scorn for those who would\nglorify  the householder that he sought on this occasion to preach the\nreligious life. \"Is it so easy\", he exclaimed, \"to be Janaka? To sit on a\nthrone absolutely unattached? Caring nothing for wealth or fame, for wife or\nchild? One after another in the West has told me that he had reached this.\nBut I could only say, 'Such great men are not born in India'!\"\n", "And then he turned to the other side.\n", "\"Never forget\", he said to one of his hearers, \"to say to yourself, and to\nteach to your children: as is the difference between a firefly and the\nblazing  sun,  between the infinite ocean and a little pond, between a\nmustard-seed and the mountain of Meru, such is the difference between the\nhouseholder and the Sannyasin!\"\n", "\"Everything is fraught with fear: Renunciation alone is fearless.\"\n", "\"Blessed be even the fraudulent S\u00e2dhus, and those who have failed to carry\nout their vows, inasmuch as they also have witnessed to the ideal and so are\nin some degree the cause of the success of others!\"\n", "\"Let us never, never, forget our ideal!\"\n", "At such moments he would identify himself entirely with the thought he\nsought to demonstrate, and in the same sense in which a law of nature might\nbe deemed cruel or arrogant, his exposition might have those qualities.\nSitting and listening, we felt ourselves brought face to face with the\ninvisible and absolute.\n", "All  this  was  on our return to Srinagar from the real Fourth of July\ncelebration, which had been a visit to Dahl Lake. . . .\n", "At nine o'clock on the evening of the following Sunday, July the 10th, the\nfirst two [Dhira Mata and Jaya] came back unexpectedly, and presently, from\nmany different sources, we gathered the news that the Master had gone to\nAmarnath by the Sonamarg route and would return another way. He had started\nout penniless, but that could give no concern to his friends, in a Hindu\nnative state. . . .\n", "JULY 15.\n", "What were we setting out for? We were just moving to go down the river on\n", "Friday,  and  it  was close on five in the afternoon when the servants\nrecognized some of their friends in the distance, and word was brought that\nthe Swami's boat was coming towards us.\n", "An hour later he was with us, saying how pleasant it was to be back. The\nsummer had been unusually hot and certain glaciers had given way, rendering\nthe Sonamarg route to Amarnath impracticable. This fact had caused his\nreturn.\n", "But from this moment dated the first of three great increments of joy and\nrealization that we saw in him during our months in Kashmir. It was almost\nas if we could verify for ourselves the truth of that saying of his Guru:\n\"There is indeed a certain ignorance. It has been placed there by my Holy\nMother that her work may be done. But it is only like a film of tissue\npaper. It might be rent at any moment\".\n"]
