["\n", "CHAPTER VII\n", "THE MANTRA: OM: WORD AND WISDOM\n", "\nBut we are now considering not these Mah\u00e2-purushas, the great Incarnations,\nbut only the Siddha-Gurus (teachers who have attained the goal); they, as a\nrule, have to convey the gems of spiritual wisdom to the disciple by means\nof words (Mantras) to be meditated upon. What are these Mantras? The whole\nof this universe has, according to Indian philosophy, both name and form\n(N\u00e2ma-Rupa) as its conditions of manifestation. In the human microcosm,\nthere cannot be a single wave in the mind-stuff (Chittavritti) unconditioned\nby name and form. If it be true that nature is built throughout on the same\nplan, this kind of conditioning by name and form must also be the plan of\nthe building of the whole of the cosmos.\n", "\u2014 \"As one lump of clay being known, all things of clay are known\", so the\nknowledge of the microcosm must lead to the knowledge of the macrocosm. Now\nform is the outer crust, of which the name or the idea is the inner essence\nor kernel. The body is the form, and the mind or the Antahkarana is the\nname, and sound-symbols are universally associated with N\u00e2ma (name) in all\nbeings having the power of speech. In the individual man the thought-waves\nrising  in  the  limited  Mahat  or Chitta (mind-stuff), must manifest\nthemselves, first as words, and then as the more concrete forms.\n", "\nIn  the  universe,  Brahm\u00e2  or Hiranyagarbha or the cosmic Mahat first\nmanifested himself as name, and then as form, i.e. as this universe. All\nthis  expressed sensible universe is the form, behind which stands the\neternal inexpressible Sphota, the manifester as Logos or Word. This eternal\nSphota, the essential eternal material of all ideas or names is the power\nthrough which the Lord creates the universe, nay, the Lord first becomes\nconditioned as the Sphota, and then evolves Himself out as the yet more\nconcrete sensible universe.\n", "And as by no possible means of analysis can we separate the word from the idea this\nOm and the eternal Sphota are inseparable; and therefore, it is out of this\nholiest of all holy words, the mother of all names and forms, the eternal\nOm, that the whole universe may be supposed to have been created. But it may\nbe said that, although thought and word are inseparable, yet as there may be\nvarious word-symbols for the same thought, it is not necessary that this\nparticular word Om should be the word representative of the thought, out of\nwhich the universe has become manifested. To this objection we reply that\nthis Om is the only possible symbol which covers the whole ground, and there\nis none other like it. The Sphota is the material of all words, yet it is\nnot any definite word in its fully formed state. That is to say, if all the\npeculiarities which distinguish one word from another be removed, then what\nremains will be the Sphota; therefore this Sphota is called the N\u00e2da-Brahma,\nthe Sound-Brahman.\n", "\nNow, as every word-symbol, intended to express the inexpressible Sphota,\nwill so particularise it that it will no longer be the Sphota, that symbol\nwhich particularises it the least and at the same time most approximately\nexpresses its nature, will be the truest symbol thereof; and this is the Om,\nand  the  Om only;\n", "Again, all articulate sounds are produced in the space\nwithin the mouth beginning with the root of the tongue and ending in the\nlips \u2014 the throat sound is A, and M is the last lip sound, and the U exactly\nrepresents the rolling forward of the impulse which begins at the root of\nthe tongue till it ends in the lips. If properly pronounced, this Om will\nrepresent the whole phenomenon of sound-production, and no other word can do\nthis; and this, therefore, is the fittest symbol of the Sphota, which is the\nreal meaning of the Om. And as the symbol can never be separated from the\nthing signified, the Om and the Sphota are one. And as the Sphota, being the\nfiner side of the manifested universe, is nearer to God and is indeed that\nfirst manifestation of divine wisdom this Om is truly symbolic of God.\nAgain, just as the \"One only\" Brahman, the Akhanda-Sachchid\u00e2nanda, the\nundivided Existence-Knowledge-Bliss, can be conceived by imperfect human\nsouls  only from particular standpoints and associated with particular\nqualities, so this universe, His body, has also to be thought of along the\nline of the thinker's mind.\n", "\nThis direction of the worshipper's mind is guided by its prevailing elements\nor  Tattvas.  The  result is that the same God will be seen in various\nmanifestations as the possessor of various predominant qualities, and the\nsame universe will appear as full of manifold forms. Even as in the case of\nthe least differentiated and the most universal symbol Om, thought and\nsound-symbol are seen to be inseparably associated with each other, so also\nthis law of their inseparable association applies to the many differentiated\nviews of God and the universe: each of them therefore must have a particular\nword-symbol to express it. These word-symbols, evolved out of the deepest\nspiritual perception of sages, symbolise and express, as nearly as possible\nthe particular view of God and the universe they stand for. And as the Om\nrepresents the Akhanda, the undifferentiated Brahman, the others represent\nthe Khanda or the differentiated views of the same Being; and they are all\nhelpful to divine meditation and the acquisition of true knowledge.\n"]
