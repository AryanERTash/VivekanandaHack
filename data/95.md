["\n", "ALL RELIGIONS ARE GOOD\n", "(Washington Post, October 29, 1894)\n", "\nMr. Kananda spoke yesterday at the People's Church on the invitation of Dr.\nKent, pastor of the church. His talk in the morning was a regular sermon,\ndealing entirely with the spiritual side of religion, and presenting the, to\northodox  sects, rather original proposition that there is good in the\nfoundation  of every religion, that all religions, like languages, are\ndescended from a common stock, and that each is good in its corporal and\nspiritual aspects so long as it is kept free from dogma and fossilism. The\naddress in the afternoon was more in the form of a lecture on the Aryan\nrace, and traced the descent of the various allied nationalities by their\nlanguage, religion and customs from the common Sanskrit stock.\n", "\nAfter  the  meeting,  to a Post reporter Mr. Kananda said: \"I claim no\naffiliation with any religious sect, but occupy the position of an observer,\nand so far as I may, of a teacher to mankind. All religion to me is good.\nAbout the higher mysteries of life and existence I can do no more than\nspeculate, as others do. Reincarnation seems to me to be the nearest to a\nlogical explanation for many things with which we are confronted in the\nrealm of religion. But I do not advance it as a doctrine. It is no more than\na  theory  at best, and is not susceptible of proof except by personal\nexperience,  and  that proof is good only for the man who has it. Your\nexperience  is  nothing to me, nor mine to you. I am not a believer in\nmiracles \u2014 they are repugnant to me in matters of religion. You might bring\nthe world tumbling down about my ears, but that would be no proof to me that\nthere was a God, or that you worked by his agency, if there was one.\n", "\n", " He Believes It Blindly \n", "\n\"I must, however, believe in a past and a hereafter as necessary to the\nexistence of the present. And if we go on from here, we must go in other\nforms, and so comes any belief in reincarnation. But I can prove nothing,\nand any one is welcome to deprive me of the theory of reincarnation provided\nthey will show me something better to replace it. Only up to the present I\nhave found nothing that offers so satisfactory an explanation to me.\"\n", "\nMr.  Kananda is a native of Calcutta, and a graduate of the government\nuniversity there. He speaks English like a native, having received his\nuniversity training in that tongue. He has had good opportunity to observe\nthe contact between the native and the English, and it would disappoint a\nforeign missionary worker to hear him speak in very unconcerned style of the\nattempts to convert the natives. In this connection he was asked what effect\nthe Western teaching was having on the thought of the Orient.\n", "\n\"Of  course,\" he said, \"no thought of any sort can come into a country\nwithout having its effect, but the effect of Christian teaching on Oriental\nthought is, if it exists, so small as to be imperceptible. The Western\ndoctrines have made about as much impression there as have the Eastern\ndoctrines here, perhaps not so much. That is, among the higher thinkers of\nthe  country.  The  effect  of the missionary work among the masses is\nimperceptible. When converts are made they of course drop at once out of the\nnative sects, but the mass of the population is so great that the converts\nof the missionaries have very little effect that can be seen.\"\n", "\n", "The Yogis Are Jugglers\n", "\nWhen asked whether he knew anything of the alleged miraculous performances\nof the yogis and adepts Mr. Kananda replied that he was not interested in\nmiracles, and that while there were of course a great many clever jugglers\nin the country, their performances were tricks. Mr. Kananda said that he had\nseen the mango trick but once, and then by a fakir on a small scale. He held\nthe same view about the alleged attainments of the lamas. \"There is a great\nlack of trained, scientific, and unprejudiced observers in all accounts of\nthese phenomena,\" said he, \"so that it is hard to select the false from the\ntrue.\"\n"]
