["\n", "THE GITA\n", "(New Discoveries, Vol. 6, pp. 175-76.)\n", "[Mr. Frank Rhodehamel\u2019s notes of a Bhagavad-Git\u00e2 class delivered Thursday,\nMay 24, 1900, in San Francisco, California]\n", "The Git\u00e2 is the gist of the Vedas. It is not our Bible; the Upanishads are\nour Bible. It [the Gita] is the gist of the Upanishads and harmonizes the\nmany contradictory parts of the Upanishads.\n", "The Vedas are divided into two portions \u2014 the work portion and the knowledge\nportion. The work portion contains ceremonials, rules as to eating, living,\ndoing charitable work, etc. The knowledge came afterwards and was enunciated\nby kings.\n", "The work portion was exclusively in the hands of the priests and pertained\nentirely to the sense life. It taught to do good works that one might go to\nheaven and enjoy eternal happiness. Anything, in fact, that one might want\ncould be provided for him by the work or ceremonials. It provided for all\nclasses of  people good and bad. Nothing could be obtained through the\nceremonials except by the intercession of the priests. So if one wanted\nanything, even if it was to have an enemy killed, all he had to do was to\npay the priest; and the priest through these ceremonials would procure the\ndesired results. It was therefore in the interests of the priests that the\nceremonial portion of the Vedas should be preserved. By it they had their\nliving. They consequently did all in their power to preserve that portion\nintact. Many of these ceremonials were very complicated, and it took years\nto perform some of them.\n", "The knowledge portion came afterwards and was promulgated exclusively by\nkings. It was called the Knowledge of Kings. The great kings had no use for\nthe work portion with all its frauds and superstitions and did all in their\npower to destroy it. This knowledge consisted of a knowledge of God, the\nsoul, the universe, etc. These kings had no use for the ceremonials of the\npriests, their magical works, etc. They pronounced it all humbug; and when\nthe priests came to them for gifts, they questioned the priests about God,\nthe soul, etc., and as the priests could not answer such questions they were\nsent away. The priests went back to their fathers to enquire about the\nthings the kings asked them, but could learn nothing from them, so they came\nback again to the kings and became their disciples. Very little of the\nceremonials are followed today. They have been mostly done away with, and\nonly a few of the more simple ones are followed today.\n", "Then in the Upanishads there is the doctrine of Karma. Karma is the law of\ncausation  applied to conduct. According to this doctrine we must work\nforever, and the only way to get rid of pain is to do good works and thus to\nenjoy the good effects; and after living a life of good works, die and go to\nheaven and live forever in happiness. Even in heaven we could not be free\nfrom Karma, only it would be good Karma, not bad.\n", "The philosophical portion denounces all work however good, and all pleasure,\nas loving and kissing wife, husband or children, as useless. According to\nthis doctrine all good works and pleasures are nothing but foolishness and\nin their very nature impermanent. \"All this must come to an end sometime, so\nend  it  now;  it  is  vain.\" So says the philosophical portion of the\nUpanishads. It claims all the pain in the world is caused by ignorance,\ntherefore the cure is knowledge.\n", "This  idea  of one being held down fast by past Karma, or work, is all\nnonsense. No matter how dense one may be, or how bad, one ray of light will\ndissipate it all. A bale of cotton, however large, will be utterly destroyed\nby a spark. If a room has been dark for untold ages, a lamp will end it all.\nSo with each soul, however benighted he may be, he is not absolutely bound\ndown by his past Karma to work for ages to come. \"One ray of Divine Light\nwill free him, reveal to him his true nature.\"\n", "Well, the Gita harmonizes all these conflicting doctrines. As to Krishna,\nwhether or not he ever lived, I do not know. \"A great many stories are told\nof him, but I do not believe them.\"\n", "\"I doubt very much that he ever lived and think it would be a good thing if\nhe never did. There would have been one less god in the world.\"\n"]
