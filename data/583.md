["\n", "CXXII\n", "\n", "ALAMBAZAR MATH, CALCUTTA,\n", "25th Feb., 1897.\n", "DEAR MRS. BULL,\n", "Saradananda sends \u00a320 to be placed in the famine relief in India. But as\nthere is famine in his own home, I thought it best to relieve that first, as\nthe old proverb says. So it has been employed accordingly.\n", "I  have  not  a  moment to die as they stay, what with processions and\ntomtomings and various other methods of reception all over the country; I am\nalmost dead. As soon as the Birthday is over I will fly off to the hills. I\nreceived an address from the Cambridge Conference as well as one from the\nBrooklyn Ethical Association. One from the Vedanta Association of New York,\nas mentioned in Dr. Janes's letter, has not yet arrived.\n", "Also there is a letter from Dr. Janes suggesting work along the line of your\nconference,  here  in India. It is almost impossible for me to pay any\nattention to these things. I am so, so tired. I do not know whether I would\nlive even six months more or not, unless I have some rest.\n", "Now I have to start two centres, one in Madras, the other in Calcutta. The\nMadras people are deeper and more sincere, and, I am sure, will be able to\ncollect  funds  from  Madras  itself.  The  Calcutta people are mostly\nenthusiastic (I mean the aristocracy) through patriotism, and their sympathy\nwould never materialise. On the other hand, the country is full of persons,\njealous and pitiless, who would leave no stones unturned to pull my work to\npieces.\n", "But as you know well, the more the opposition, the more the demon in me is\nroused. My duty would not be complete if I die without starting the two\nplaces, one for the Sannyasins, the other for the women.\n", "I have already \u00a3500 from England about, \u00a3500 from Mr. Sturdy, and if your\nmoney be added to it, I am sure I will be able to start the two. I think,\ntherefore, you ought to send the money as soon as possible. The safest way\nis to put the money in a bank in America in your and my name jointly, so\nthat either of us may draw it. In case I die before the money is employed,\nyou will be able to draw it all and put it to the use I wanted. So that, in\ncase of my death, none of my people would be able to meddle with it. The\nEnglish money has been put in the bank in the same position in the joint\nnames of Mr. Sturdy and myself.\n", "With love to Saradananda and eternal love and gratitude to yourself,\n", "Yours etc.,\n", "\nVIVEKANANDA.\n"]
