My health, though not\nimproved as much as I [had] wished, is not bad. The liver has been benefited\n\u2014 [that] is a great gain. The rains will commence very soon in the hills. So\nit is useless for me to take all the trouble of that terrible route.\n", "I am so happy to learn the mountains are doing you good. Eat a lot, sleep as\nmuch as you can, and get plump. Stuff yourself till you get plump or you\nburst.\n", "So the place did not suit Mr. Okakura [Kakuzo] \u2014 why? There must have been\nsomething to annoy him very much that he left the place so abruptly. Did he\nnot like the scenery? Was it not sublime enough for him? Or the Japanese do\nnot like sublimity at all? They only like beauty.\n", "One of the boys writes that the little boy is getting disobedient etc. Mrs.\nSevier  wants me to take him down. So I do. I have asked Sadananda and\nanother monk (whom I want for work here) to go to Almora and wait for the\nmonsoon, and when it breaks, to come down.\n", "If you feel you are becoming the least burdensome to Mrs. Sevier, write me\nimmediately. It would be a sin to put further pressure on her \u2014 she does so\nmuch for me. However, she likes you very much and writes that you look\nbe-au-ti-ful in saris.\n", "I have just now two kids and three lambs added to the family. There was one\nmore kid, but he got himself drowned in the yellow fish tank. How is Margot?\nIs she still there, or gone away with Mr. Okakura? How is she pulling on\nwith the boys?\n", "What do you do the whole day? How do you pass the day? Write me all details,\nand frequently; but do not expect long letters from me often.\n", "Give my love to Mrs. Sevier, to Margot and the rest, and you may take a few\nspoonfuls, if you like,\n", "with only this,\n", "VIVEKANANDA.\n", "PS \u2014 Have an eye on the little chap. The boys are already jealous of him.\nThey did spoil another boy that way before.\n", "V.\n"]
