["\n", "XXXII\n", "To Miss Mary Hale\n", "\n", "C/O MRS. E. TOTTEN,\n", "1703, 1ST STREET,\n", "WASHINGTON,\n", "[November 1(?), 1894]\n", "DEAR SISTER,\n", "I have received two letters which you were very kind to take the trouble to\nwrite. I am going to talk here today, tomorrow at Baltimore, then again\nMonday  at Baltimore, and Tuesday at Washington again. So I will be in\nPhiladelphia a few days after that. I shall write to you the day I start\nfrom Washington. I shall be in Philadelphia a few days only to see Prof.\nWright, and then I go to New York and run for a little while between New\nYork and Boston, and then go to Chicago via Detroit; and then \"whist\" . . .,\nas Senator Palmer says, to England.\n", "The word \"Dharma\" means religion. I am very sorry they treated Petro very\nbadly in Calcutta. I have been very well treated here and am doing very\nwell. Nothing extraordinary in the meantime except I got vexed at getting\nloads of newspapers from India; so after sending a cart-load to Mother\nChurch and another to Mrs. Guernsey, I had to write them to stop sending\ntheir newspapers. I have had \"boom\" enough in India. Alasinga writes that\nevery village all over the country now has heard of me. Well, the old peace\nis gone for ever and no rest anywhere from heretofore. These newspapers of\nIndia will be my death, I am sure. They will now talk what I ate on such and\nsuch a date and how I sneezed. Lord bless them, it was all my foolery. I\nreally came here to raise a little money secretly and go over but was caught\nin the trap and now no more of a reserved life.\n", "Wishing you all enjoyments,\n", "I remain, yours affectionately,\n", "\nVIVEKANANDA.\n"]
