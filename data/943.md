["\n", "XCVI\n", "To Sister Nivedita\n", "ALMORA,\n", "4th July, 1897.\n", "MY DEAR MISS NOBLE,\n", "I am being played upon curiously by both good and evil influences from\nLondon these times here. . . . On the other hand, your letters are full of\nlife and sunshine, and bring strength and hope to my spirits, and they sadly\nwant these now. God knows.\n", "Although I am still in the Himalayas, and shall be here for at least a month\nmore, I started the work in Calcutta before I came, and they write progress\nevery week.\n", "Just now I am very busy with the famine, and except for training a number of\nyoung men for future work, have not been able to put more energy into the\nteaching work. The \"feeding work\" is absorbing all my energy and means.\nAlthough  we can work only on a very small scale as yet, the effect is\nmarvellous. For the first time since the days of Buddha, Brahmin boys are\nfound nursing by the bed-side of cholera-stricken pariahs.\n", "In India, lectures and teaching cannot do any good. What we want is Dynamic\nReligion. And that, \"God willing\", as the Mohammedans say, I am determined\nto show. . . . I entirely agree with the prospectus of your Society, and you\nmay take for granted my agreement with everything you will do in the future.\nI have entire faith in your ability and sympathy. I already owe you an\nimmense debt, and you are laying me every day under infinite obligations. My\nonly consolation is that it is for the good of others. Else I do not deserve\nin the least the wonderful kindness shown to me by the Wimbledon friends.\nYou good, steady, genuine English people, may the Lord always bless you. I\nappreciate you every day more and more from a distance. Kindly convey my\nlove everlasting to __  and all the rest of our friends there.\n", "With all love, yours ever in the Truth,\n", "\nVIVEKANANDA.\n"]
