["\n", "(RECORDED BY MISS S. E. WALDO, A DISCIPLE)\n", "MONDAY, July 1, 1895. (Shri Ramakrishna Deva)\n", "Shri Ramakrishna was the son of a very orthodox Brahmin, who would refuse\neven a gift from any but a special caste of Brahmins; neither might he work,\nnor even be a priest in a temple, nor sell books, nor serve anyone. He could\nonly have \"what fell from the skies\" (alms), and even then it must not come\nthrough a \"fallen\" Brahmin. Temples have no hold on the Hindu religion; if\nthey were all destroyed, religion would not be affected a grain. A man must\nonly build a house for \"God and guests\", to build for himself would be\nselfish; therefore he erects temples as dwelling places for God.\n", "Owing to the extreme poverty of his family, Shri Ramakrishna was obliged to\nbecome in his boyhood a priest in a temple dedicated to the Divine Mother,\nalso called Prakriti, or K\u00e2li, represented by a female figure standing with\nfeet  on  a male figure, indicating that until Maya lifts, we can know\nnothing. Brahman is neuter, unknown and unknowable, but to be objectified He\ncovers Himself with a veil of Maya, becomes the Mother of the Universe, and\nso brings forth the creation. The prostrate figure (Shiva or God) has become\nShava (dead or lifeless) by being covered by Maya. The Jn\u00e2ni says, \"I will\nuncover God by force\" (Advaitism); but the dualist says, \"I will uncover God\nby praying to Mother, begging Her to open the door to which She alone has\nthe key.\"\n", "The  daily  service of the Mother Kali gradually awakened such intense\ndevotion in the heart of the young priest that he could no longer carry on\nthe regular temple worship. So he abandoned his duties and retired to a\nsmall woodland in the temple compound, where he gave himself up entirely to\nmeditation. These woods were on the bank of the river Ganga; and one day the\nswift current bore to his very feet just the necessary materials to build\nhim a little enclosure. In this enclosure he stayed and wept and prayed,\ntaking no thought for the care of his body or for aught except his Divine\nMother. A relative fed him once a day and watched over him. Later came a\nSannyasini or lady ascetic, to help him find his \"Mother\". Whatever teachers\nhe needed came to him unsought; from every sect some holy saint would come\nand offer to teach him and to each he listened eagerly. But he worshipped\nonly Mother; all to him was Mother.\n", "Shri Ramakrishna never spoke a harsh word against anyone. So beautifully\ntolerant was he that every sect thought that he belonged to them. He loved\neveryone. To him all religions were true. He found a place for each one. He\nwas free, but free in love, not in \"thunder\". The mild type creates, the\nthundering type spreads. Paul was the thundering type to spread the light.\n(And it has been said by many that Swami Vivekananda himself was a kind of\nSt. Paul to Shri Ramakrishna.)\n", "The age of St. Paul, however, is gone; we are to be the new lights for this\nday. A self-adjusting organisation is the great need of our time. When we\ncan get one, that will be the last religion of the world. The wheel must\nturn, and we should help it, not hinder. The waves of religious thought rise\nand  fall,  and on the topmost one stands the \"prophet of the period\".\nRamakrishna  came  to  teach  the religion of today, constructive, not\ndestructive. He had to go afresh to Nature to ask for facts, and he got\nscientific religion which never says \"believe\", but \"see\"; \"I see, and you\ntoo can see.\" Use the same means and you will reach the same vision. God\nwill  come  to  everyone,  harmony  is  within  the reach of all. Shri\nRamakrishna's teachings are \"the gist of Hinduism\"; they were not peculiar\nto him. Nor did he claim that they were; he cared naught for name or fame.\n", "He began to preach when he was about forty; but he never went out to do it.\nHe waited for those who wanted his teachings to come to him. In accordance\nwith Hindu custom, he was married by his parents in early youth to a little\ngirl of five, who remained at home with her family in a distant village,\nunconscious  of the great struggle through which her young husband was\npassing.  When she reached maturity, he was already deeply absorbed in\nreligious devotion. She travelled on foot from her home to the temple at\nDakshineswar where he was then living; and as soon as she saw him, she\nrecognised what he was, for she herself was a great soul, pure and holy, who\nonly desired to help his work, never to drag him down to the level of the\nGrihastha (householder).\n", "Shri Ramakrishna is worshipped in India as one of the great Incarnations,\nand his birthday is celebrated there as a religious festival. . . .\n", "A curious round stone is the emblem of Vishnu, the omnipresent. Each morning\na priest comes in, offers sacrifice to the idol, waves incense before it,\nthen puts it to bed and apologises to God for worshipping Him in that way,\nbecause he can only conceive of Him through an image or by means of some\nmaterial object. He bathes the idol, clothes it, and puts his divine self\ninto the idol \"to make it alive\".\n", "*    *    *\n", "There is a sect which says, \"It is weakness to worship only the good and\nbeautiful, we ought also to love and worship the hideous and the evil.\" This\nsect prevails all over Tibet, and they have no marriage. In India proper\nthey cannot exist openly, but organise secret societies. No decent men will\nbelong to them except sub rosa. Thrice communism was tried in Tibet, and\nthrice it failed. They use Tapas and with immense success as far as power is\nconcerned.\n", "Tapas means literally \"to burn\". It is a kind of penance to \"heat\" the\nhigher nature. It is sometimes in the form of a sunrise to sunset vow, such\nas repeating Om all day incessantly. These actions will produce a certain\npower that you can convert into any form you wish, spiritual or material.\nThis idea of Tapas penetrates the whole of Hindu religion. The Hindus even\nsay that God made Tapas to create the world. It is a mental instrument with\nwhich to do everything. \"Everything in the three worlds can be caught by\nTapas.\" . . .\n", "People who report about sects with which they are not in sympathy are both\nconscious and unconscious liars. A believer in one sect can rarely see truth\nin others.\n", "*    *    *\n", "A great Bhakta (Hanuman) once said when asked what day of the month it was,\n\"God is my eternal date, no other date I care for.\"\n"]
