Hale\n", "NEW YORK\n", "18 November 1894\n", "DEAR MOTHER\n", "I  have been very late this time in writing you as Sister Mary[6]* has\nalready written to you, no doubt, about me.\n", "The clothes have all reached safe, only I will send over some of the summer\nand other clothes as it will be impossible to carry the burden all along\nwith me.\n", "The  certainty  about  going to Europe this December has gone; so I am\nuncertain when I go.\n", "Sister Mary has improved a great deal from what I saw her last. She lives\nwith a number of fox-hunting squires and is quite happy. I hope she will\nmarry one of those fellows with long pockets. I am going again to see her\ntomorrow at Mrs. Spalding's \u2014 I was there last afternoon. I will be in N.Y.\nthis  month; then I go to Boston and perhaps will be there all through\nDecember. When I was sick in Boston last spring, I went over to Chicago, and\nnot to Detroit as Mrs. Bagley expected. So this time I am going to Detroit\nfirst and then to Chicago, if possible. Else I altogether give up the plan\nof going to the West soon.\n", "There is more chance of working my plans out in the East than in the West,\nas it now appears.\n", "I have got news of the phonograph \u2014 it has reached safe, and the R\u00e2j\u00e2[7]*\nwrote to me a very nice letter on that. I have a lot of addresses and other\nnonsense  from India. I have written home to them not to send any more\nnewspapers.  My love to the babies at home and I am going to visit the\nbaby[8]* abroad.\n", "Mrs. Guernsey has been at death's door. She is now recovering slowly. I have\nnot seen her yet. She is not strong enough to see anybody. Hope she will\nsoon be strong.\n", "My love to Father Pope and everyone.\n", "Your ever affectionate son,\n", "VIVEKANANDA\n"]
