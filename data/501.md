["XL\n", "(From a letter written to H. H. the Maharaja of Khetri)\n", "1894.\n", ". . . \"It is not the building that makes the home, but it is the wife that\nmakes it,\"[1] says a Sanskrit poet, and\nhow true it is! The roof that affords you shelter from heat and cold and\nrain  is  not to be judged by the pillars that support it\u2014the finest\nCorinthian columns though they be\u2014but by the real spirit-pillar who is the\ncentre, the real support of the home\u2014the woman. Judged by that standard,\nthe American home will not suffer in comparison with any home in the world.\n", "I have heard many stories about the American home: of liberty running into\nlicence, of unwomanly women smashing under their feet all the peace and\nhappiness of home-life in their mad liberty-dance, and much nonsense of that\ntype. And now after a year's experience of American homes, of American\nwomen,  how utterly false and erroneous that sort of judgment appears!\nAmerican women! A hundred lives would not be sufficient to pay my deep debt\nof gratitude to you! I have not words enough to express my gratitude to you.\n\"The Oriental hyperbole\" alone expresses the depth of Oriental gratitude\u2014\"If the Indian Ocean were an inkstand, the highest mountain of the Himalaya\nthe pen, the earth the scroll and time itself the writer\"[2] still it will not express my gratitude to you!\n", "Last year I came to this country in summer, a wandering preacher of a far\ndistant country, without name, fame, wealth, or learning to recommend me\u2014friendless, helpless, almost in a state of destitution and American women\nbefriended me, gave me shelter and food, took me to their homes and treated\nme as their own son, their own brother. They stood my friends even when\ntheir own priests were trying to persuade them to give up the \"dangerous\nheathen\"\u2014even when day after day their best friends had told them not to\nstand by this \"unknown foreigner, may be, of dangerous character\". But they\nare better judges of character and soul\u2014for it is the pure mirror that\ncatches the reflection.\n", "And how many beautiful homes I have seen, how many mothers whose purity of\ncharacter, whose unselfish love for their children are beyond expression,\nhow many daughters and pure maidens, \"pure as the icicle on Diana's temple\",\nand withal with much culture, education, and spirituality in the highest\nsense! Is America then full of only wingless angels in the shape of women?\nThere is good and bad everywhere, true\u2014but a nation is not to be judged by\nits  weaklings called the wicked, as they are only the weeds which lag\nbehind, but by the good, the noble, and the pure who indicate the national\nlife-current to be flowing clear and vigorous.\n", "Do you judge of an apple tree and the taste of its fruits by the unripe,\nundeveloped, worm-eaten ones that strew the ground, large even though their\nnumber be sometimes? If there is one ripe developed fruit, that one would\nindicate the powers, the possibility and the purpose of the apple tree and\nnot hundreds that could not grow.\n", "And then the modern American women\u2014I admire their broad and liberal minds.\nI have seen many liberal and broad-minded men too in this country, some even\nin the narrowest churches, but here is the difference\u2014there is danger with\nthe men to become broad at the cost of religion, at the cost of spirituality\u2014women broaden out in sympathy to everything that is good everywhere,\nwithout dosing a bit of their own religion. They intuitively know that it is\na question of positivity and not negativity, a question of addition and not\nsubtraction. They are every day becoming aware of the fact that it is the\naffirmative and positive side of everything that shall be stored up, and\nthat  this  very act of accumulating the affirmative and positive, and\ntherefore soul-building forces of nature, is what destroys the negative and\ndestructive elements in the world.\n", "What a wonderful achievement was that World's Fair at Chicago! And that\nwonderful Parliament of Religions where voices from every corner of the\nearth expressed their religious ideas! I was also allowed to present my own\nideas through the kindness of Dr. Barrows and Mr. Bonney. Mr. Bonney is such\na wonderful man! Think of that mind that planned and carried out with great\nsuccess that gigantic undertaking, and he, no clergyman, a lawyer, presiding\nover the dignitaries of all the churches\u2014the sweet, learned, patient Mr.\nBonney with all his soul speaking through his bright eyes. ...\n", "Yours etc.,\nVivekananda.\n"]
