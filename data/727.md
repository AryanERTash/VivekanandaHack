["\n", "MEMOIRS OF EUROPEAN TRAVEL\n", "II\n", "We have an adage among us that one that has a disc-like pattern on the soles\nof his feet becomes a vagabond. I fear, I have my soles inscribed all over\nwith them. And there is not much room for probability, either. I have tried\nmy best to discover them by scrutinising the soles, but all to no purpose\n\u2014 the feet have been dreadfully cracked through the severity of cold, and no\ndiscs or anything of the kind could be traced. However, when there is the\ntradition, I take it for granted that my soles are full of those signs. But\nthe results are quite patent \u2014 it was my cherished desire to remain in Paris\nfor some time and study the French language and civilisation; I left my old\nfriends and acquaintances and put up with a new friend, a Frenchman of\nordinary means, who knew no English, and my French \u2014 well, it was something\nquite extraordinary! Well, who can stem the course\nof the inevitable! \u2014 And this letter I am writing to you from the last\nremaining capital of Mohammedan supremacy \u2014 from Constantinople!\n", "I have three travelling companions \u2014 two of them French and the third an\nAmerican. The American is Miss MacLeod whom you know very well; the French\nmale companion is Monsieur Jules Bois, a famous philosopher and litterateur\nof  France;  and  the French lady friend is the world-renowned singer,\nMademoiselle Calv\u00e9. \"Mister\" is \"Monsieur\" in the French language, and\n\"Miss\"  is  \"Mademoiselle\" \u2014 with a Z-sound. Mademoiselle Calv\u00e9 is the\nforemost  singer  \u2014  opera  singer  \u2014  of the present day. The foremost actress in the West, Madame Sarah\nBernhardt,  and the foremost singer, Calv\u00e9, are both of them of French\nextraction, and both totally ignorant of English, but they visit England and\nAmerica occasionally and earn millions of dollars by acting and singing.\nFrench is the language of the civilised world, the mark of gentility in the\nWest, and everybody knows it; consequently these two ladies have neither the\nleisure nor the inclination to learn English. Madame Bernhardt is an aged\nlady; but when she steps on the stage after dressing, her imitation of the\nage and sex of the role she plays is perfect! A girl or a boy \u2014 whatever\npart you want her to play, she is an exact representation of that. And that\nwonderful voice! People here say her voice has the ring of silver strings!\nMadame Bernhardt has a special regard for India; she tells me again and\nagain that our country is \"tr\u00e9s ancien, tres civilis\u00e9\" \u2014 very ancient and\nvery civilised. One year she performed a drama touching on India, in which\nshe  set up a whole Indian street-scene on the stage \u2014 men, women, and\nchildren, Sadhus and Nagas, and everything \u2014 an exact picture of India!\nAfter the performance she told me that for about a month she had visited\nevery museum and made herself acquainted with the men and women and their\ndress, the streets and bathing ghats and everything relating to India.\nMadame Bernhardt has a very strong desire to visit India. \u2014 \"C'est mon rave!\n\u2014 It is the dream of my life\", she says. Again, the Prince of Wales (His\nlate Majesty King Edward VII, the then Prince of Wales.) has promised to\ntake her over to a tiger and elephant hunting excursion. But then she said\nshe must spend some two lakhs of rupees if she went to India! She is of\ncourse in no want of money. \"La divine Sarah\" \u2014 the divine Sarah \u2014 is her\nname; how can she want money, she who never travels but by a special train!\nThat pomp and luxury many a prince of Europe cannot afford to indulge in!\nOne can only secure a seat for her performance by paying double the fees,\nand that a month in advance! Well, she is not going to suffer want of money!\nBut Sarah Bernhardt is given to spending lavishly. Her travel to India is\ntherefore put off for the present.\n", "Mademoiselle Calve will not sing this winter, she will take a rest and is\ngoing to temperate climates like Egypt etc. I am going as her guest. Calve\nhas not devoted herself to music alone, she is sufficiently learned and has\na great love for philosophical and religious literature. She was born amidst\nvery poor circumstances; gradually, through her own genius and undergoing\ngreat labour and much hardship, she has now amassed a large fortune and has\nbecome the object of adoration of kings and potentates!\n", "There are famous lady singers, such as Madame Melba, Madame Emma Ames, and\nothers; and very distinguished singers, such as Jean de Reszke, Plancon, and\nthe rest \u2014 all of whom earn two or three lakhs of rupees a year! Extraordinary beauty, youth, genius,\nand a celestial voice \u2014 all these have conspired to raise Calv\u00e9 to the\nforefront of all singers. At best some poor novels and\ndramas! Then again, learning is confined at present to a foreign tongue or\nto Sanskrit and is only for the chosen few. He is a\ngood poet, and is an advocate of the Indian Vedantic ideas that have crept\ninto the great French poets, such as Victor Hugo and Lamartine and others,\nand the great German poets, such as Goethe, Schiller, and the rest. Now he is accompanying us for travel.\n", "We have two other companions on the journey as far as Constantinople \u2014 P\u00e8re\nHyacinthe and his wife. P\u00e8re, i.e. Father Hyacinthe was a monk of a strict\nascetic section of the Roman Catholic Church. His scholarship, extraordinary\neloquence, and great austerities won for him a high reputation in France and\nin the whole Catholic Order. At forty years of\nage  P\u00e8re Hyacinthe fell in love with an American woman and eventually\nmarried her. This created a great sensation, and of course the Catholic\nOrder immediately gave him up. I, however, call him by his former\nname. It is an old, old tale, and the matter was the talk of the whole\ncontinent. The Protestants received him with honour, but the Catholics began\nto hate him. The Pope, in consideration of his attainments, was unwilling to\npart with him and asked him to remain a Greek Catholic priest, and not\nabandon the Roman Church. (The priests of the Greek Catholic section are\nallowed to marry but once, but do not get any high position). Mrs. Loyson,\nhowever, forcibly dragged him out of the Pope's fold. In course of time they\nhad  children  and grandchildren; now the very aged Loyson is going to\nJerusalem to try to establish cordial relations among the Christians and\nMussulmans.  His  wife had perhaps seen many visions that Loyson might\npossibly turn out to be a second Martin Luther and overthrow the Pope's\nthrone \u2014 into the Mediterranean. But nothing of the kind took place; and the\nonly result was, as the French say, that he was placed between two stools.\nBut Madame Loyson still cherishes her curious day-dreams! Old Loyson is very\naffable in speech, modest, and of a distinctly devotional turn of mind.\nWhenever he meets me, he holds pretty long talks about various religions and\ncreeds. When I discuss with the old man such topics as renunciation\nand monasticism etc., all those long-cherished sentiments wake up in his\naged breast, and his wife most probably smarts all the while. Besides, all\nFrench people, of both sexes, lay the whole blame on the wife; they say,\n\"That woman has spoilt one of our great ascetic monks!\" Madame Loyson is\nreally  in a sorry predicament \u2014 specially as they live in Paris, in a\nCatholic country. Once she expressed her dislike of an\nactress, saying, \"It is very bad of you to live with Mr. So-and-so without\nmarrying him\". The King lives in the Pope's ancient palace which he has seized, and\nthe ancient Italian kingdom of the Pope is now confined within the precincts\nof the Vatican. As a result of the struggle\nagainst Austria \u2014 against the age-long thraldom of Austria, the ally of the\nPope  \u2014  up rose modern Italy. Consequently Austria is against Italy \u2014\nagainst, because she lost her. Unfortunately, however, young Italy, under\nEngland's misdirection, set herself to create a powerful army and navy. But\nwhere was the money? So, involved in debt, Italy is on the way to ruin; and\nto her misfortune, she brought on herself a fresh trouble by proceeding to\nextend her empire in Africa. Defeated by the Abyssinian monarch, she has\nsunk down, bereft of glory and prestige. Prussia in the meantime defeated\nAustria in a great war and thrust her off to a great distance. It was the glamour of a connection\nwith this line that led to the fall of Napoleon the Great. Quaintly enough,\nhe took it into his head to marry a daughter of some noble royal family and\nfound a great dynasty through a succession of descendents.
