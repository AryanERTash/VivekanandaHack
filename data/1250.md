HOWRAH, BENGAL,\n", "14th October 1901.\n", "MY DEAR CHRISTINA,\n", "Just now came a letter from Mrs. Bull, but none from you, as I expected one\nthis mail.\n", "Mrs. Bull writes, \"I wrote Christina recently to ask her if she were to be\nfree in case the opportunity opened for her to go to the East. I send you\nher reply\".\n", "I  went  through several times your letter to Mrs. Bull. It surely was\nhorrible; and you have been all this time hiding the real state of affairs\nfrom me and posing great cheerfulness!!\n", "You will be a precious fool to lose the opportunity if such comes and is\noffered by Mrs. Bull. You will only have to take a year's leave. The rest\nwill all be arranged by Mrs. Bull, including, I am sure, all your anxiety\nfor those you will have to leave behind in Detroit.\n", "You have been good, too good to be human, and you are so, still. But it is\nno use making oneself unnecessarily miserable. \"Mother's will\", surely, if\nthe chance comes; and it has got to come, I know.\n", "I would not write you about my health; for after all this hide and seek,\neven though it was for my good, I think you have not much of a right to know\nthe truth about my health.\n", "But  to some things you have eternal rights, and amongst others, to my\neternal love and blessings,\n", "VIVEKANANDA.\n"]
