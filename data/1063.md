["\n", "XIX\n", "To Mrs. G. W. Hale\n", "HOTEL BELLEVUE, EUROPEAN PLAN\n", "BEACON STREET, BOSTON\n", "11 May, 1894\n", "DEAR MOTHER,\n", "I have been since the 7th, lecturing here every afternoon or evening. At\nMrs. Fairchild's I met the niece of Mrs. Howe. She was here today to invite\nme to dinner with her today. I have not seen Mr. Volkinen as yet. Of course,\nthe pay for lecture is here the poorest, and everybody has an axe to grind.\nI got a long letter full of the prattles of the babies.[6]* Your city, i.e.\nNew York, pays far better than Boston, so I am trying to go back there. But\nhere one can get work almost every day.\n", "I think I want some rest. I feel as if I am very much tired, and these\nconstant journeyings to and fro have shaken my nerves a little, but hope to\nrecoup soon. Last few days I have been suffering from cold and slight fever\nand lecturing for all that; hope to get rid of it in a day or two.\n", "I have got a very nice gown at $30. The colour is not exactly that of the\nold one, but cardinal, with more of yellow \u2014 could not get the exact old\ncolour even in New York.\n", "I  have  not much to write, for it is the repetition of the old story:\ntalking, talking, talking. I long to fly to Chicago and shut up my mouth and\ngive a long rest to mouth and lungs and mind. If I am not called for in New\nYork, I am coming soon to Chicago.\n", "Yours obediently,\n", "VIVEKANANDA.\n"]
