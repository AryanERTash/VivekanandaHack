["\n", "CXLI\n", "To Sister Christine\n", "MARSEILLES,\n", "23rd July 1899.\n", "MY DEAR CHRISTINA,\n", "Your very, very welcome wire just came. By next Sunday[6]* we arrive in\nLondon,  Albert  Dock.[7]*  We  are  a  party of four: myself, another\nSannyasin,[8]*  a Calcutta boy[9]* going to study in America, and Miss\n[Margaret] Noble. Miss Noble is a young lady from Wimbledon, near London,\nwho has been working in India on the education of girls.\n", "Our stay in England will not be long, I am afraid, as this is neither the\nseason nor am I in fit condition to work much. Anyhow, we will be in London\na few weeks \u2014 at least myself \u2014 then go to the U.S. We will talk over all\nthis and infinite things besides when we meet. I do not think even English\nsummer days are long enough for all the chatter I will assail you with.\n", "We go to Wimbledon for a day or two, and then I come back to London and find\nlodgings for myself and make plans.\n", "Come to the Dock if that is possible and discreet. Yes, it is discreet, as\nthere  is  a lady in the party and others will come to meet her. Only,\nChristina, don't if you feel the least tired or unwell. I hope you are\nenjoying London immensely.\n", "The Orientals do not like any effusion of feeling. They are trained to hide\nall expression.\n", "Is Mrs. Funkey [Mary Caroline Funke] with you? If so, give her my best love.\n", "I am much, much better just now. I am really quite another man this time. I\nwas nearly dead in Calcutta when I started, but this voyage has improved me\nimmensely.\n", "Hoping soon to see you,\n", "Ever yours in the Lord,\n", "VIVEKANANDA.\n"]
