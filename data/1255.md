["\n", "CCXI\n", "To Sister Christine\n", "THE MATH, P.O. BELUR, HOWRAH\n", "12th December 1901\n", "DEAR CHRISTINE,\n", "Well, then, you wanted to know all about my state of health, and you insist.\nYou shall have it.\n", "You know, the last three years I have been getting albuminuria now and then.\nIt is not constant, nor is it yet of any organic character. The kidneys are\nstructurally all right. Only they throw out albumen now and then.\n", "This is worse than throwing out sugar in diabetes. Albumen poisons the\nblood, attacks the heart and does all sorts of mischief. Catching cold\nalways increases it. This time it has caused a small blood vessel in the\nright eye to burst, so that I scarcely see with that eye.\n", "Then the circulation has become very rapid. The doctors have put me to bed;\nand I am forbidden to eat meat, to walk or even stand up, to read and write.\n", "Already there is some benefit in this lying-down process, as I sleep a lot\nand have a good appetite and am digesting my meals. Curious, is it not, that\ninactivity should bring on sleep and appetite? There is no cause to be\nanxious at all.\n", "Mrs. [Charlotte] Sevier arrived in Calcutta three days ago; and by the last\nadvice from Nivedita, Mrs. Bull and she will start on the 13th December, if\nthey can secure berths, or on the 30th December at least. I pray Mrs. Bull\nhas already invited you and that you have got your year's leave and are\ncoming over, and that you will get this letter in India redirected. If\nMother does not fulfil this prayer, sure She will take me across the water\nsoon, and [line excised] . . . . The doctor says if I keep to my bed for\nthree months, I will get completely cured.\n", "Now, don't worry. If good days are not coming, we will make them, that is\nall. Hang it! I must have good days now and, that too, very soon. You know,\nI always keep my word. Mother must do it, or I throw Her overboard. I am not\nso submissive as you are.\n", "Our old-school physicians pour in tons of iron and other metals \u2014 including\ngold, silver, pearls, etc. \u2014 down our throats. I should be a man of iron by\nthis time; perhaps yours will be the last touch to make my body one of\nsteel.\n", "This is our best season for eating turtles, but they are all black. The\ngreen [ones] can only be found in America. Alas! I am prevented from the\ntaste of meat.\n", "Now, noble heart, take courage. Don't mope: you have buffeted [too] many a\nstorm in life, old war horse, to be like a silly boarding-school girl.\nThings must go all right. I am not going to die or to be ill just now; I am\ndetermined to be healthy. You know my grit.\n", "Miss [Josephine] MacLeod sent you your letter. What was it about? Was there\nanything queer? I am glad she had it. She writes beautifully about you. She\nhas already started, and we will have a jolly good company this winter here\nin Calcutta.\n", "Mrs.  [Ole] Bull, Miss MacLeod, Mrs. Sevier and Nivedita and I will be\noverjoyed if somebody else will be thrown into the bargain. I can't get any\nmore value, eh? I must stop. Am going to look after my geese and ducks just\nfor five minutes, breaking the doctor's command to lie down all the time.\nOne of the geese is a silly, fearful bird, always despondent and anxious.\nShe likes to be all alone by herself and is miserable \u2014 very much like\nanother goose I know in another place.\n", "Here my story ends\nAnd spinach top bends.\nWhy is spinach withering?\nBecause the goat is browsing.\nWhy is the goat browsing?\nBecause no grass is growing.\nWhy no grass is growing?\nThe gardener is not watering.\nWhy there is no watering?\nThe Master is not commanding.\nWhy is he not commanding?\nAn ant has bitten the Master!\n", "This is a nursery rhyme told after a story, and it is true of us all. It is\nonly an ant bite, after all \u2014 the trouble here; is it not?\n", "Ever yours,\n", "VIVEKANANDA.\n"]
