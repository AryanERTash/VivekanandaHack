["\n", "CXXXIII\n", "\n", "THE MATH,\n", "(The letter was actually written from Ambala.)\n", "19th August, 1897.\n", "DEAR MRS. BULL,\n", ". . . My health is indifferent, and although I have some rest, I do not\nthink I shall be able to regain my usual vigour till winter next. I had a\nletter from Joe saying that you are both coming to India. I, of course, will\nbe very glad to see you in India, only you ought to know from the first that\nIndia is the dirtiest and unhealthiest hole in the world, with scarcely any\nEuropean comforts except in the big capitals.\n", "I learn from England that Mr. Sturdy is sending Abhedananda to New York. It\nseems that the English work is impossible without me. Only a magazine will\nbe started and worked by Mr. Sturdy. I had arranged to come to England this\nseason, but I was foolishly prevented by the doctors. In India the work is\ngoing on.\n", "I do not think any European or American will be of any service here just\nnow, and it will be hard for any Westerner to bear the climate. Annie Besant\nwith her exceptional powers works only among the Theosophists, and thus she\nsubmits to all the indignities of isolation which a Mlechchha is made to\nundergo here. Even Goodwin smarts now and then and has to be called to\norder. Goodwin is doing good work, as he is a man and can mix with the\npeople. Women have no place in men's society here, and she can do good only\namong her own sex in India. The English friends that came over to India have\nnot been of any help as yet, and do not know whether they will be of any in\nthe future. With all these, if anybody wants to try, she is welcome.\n", "If Saradananda wants to come, he may, and I am sure he will be of very good\nservice to me just now in organising the work, now that my health is broken.\nThere is a young English woman, Miss Margaret Noble, very eager to come to\nIndia to learn the state of things, so that she may do some work when she is\nback home. I have written her to accompany you in case you come via London.\nThe great difficulty is that you can never understand the situation here\nfrom a distance. The two types are so entirely different in all things that\nit is not possible to form any idea from America or England.\n", "You ought to think that you are starting for the interior of Africa, and if\nyou meet anything better, that will be unexpected.\n", "Ever yours etc.,\n", "\nVIVEKANANDA.\n"]
