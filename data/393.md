["This discussion followed the lecture on the Vedanta Philosophy delivered by the Swami at the Graduate Philosophical Society of Harvard University, U. S. A., on March 25, 1896. Vol. I.\n", "\n", "I\n", "A DISCUSSION\n", "\nQ.\u2014I  should  like to know something about the present activity of\nphilosophic thought in India. To what extent are these questions discussed?\n", "A.\u2014As I have said, the majority of the Indian people are practically\ndualists, and the minority are monists. The main subject of discussion is\nM\u00e2y\u00e2 and Jiva. When I came to this country, I found that the labourers were\ninformed of the present condition of politics; but when I asked them, \"What\nis religion, and what are the doctrines of this and that particular sect?\"\nthey said, \"We do not know; we go to church.\" In India if I go to a peasant\nand ask him, \"Who governs you?\" he says, \"I do not know; I pay my taxes.\"\nBut if I ask him what is his religion, he says, \"I am a dualist\", and is\nready to give you the details about Maya and Jiva. He cannot read or write,\nbut he has learned all this from the monks and is very fond of discussing\nit. After the day's work, the peasants sit under a tree and discuss these\nquestions.\n", "\nQ.\u2014What does orthodoxy mean with the Hindus?\n", "A. \u2014In modern times it simply means obeying certain caste laws as to\neating, drinking, and marriage. After that the Hindu can believe in any\nsystem he likes. There was never an organised church in India; so there was\nnever a body of men to formulate doctrines of orthodoxy. In a general way,\nwe say that those who believe in the Vedas are orthodox; but in reality we\nfind that many of the dualistic sects believe more in the Pur\u00e2nas than in\nthe Vedas alone.\n", "\nQ.\u2014What influence had your Hindu philosophy on the Stoic philosophy of the\nGreeks?\n", "A. \u2014It is very probable that it had some influence on it through the\nAlexandrians. There is some suspicion of Pythagoras' being influenced by the\nS\u00e2nkhya  thought. Anyway, we think the Sankhya philosophy is the first\nattempt to harmonise the philosophy of the Vedas through reason. We find\nKapila mentioned even in the Vedas: \"\nknowledge) the first-born sage Kapila.\"\n", "\nQ.\u2014What is the antagonism of this thought with Western science?\n", "A.\u2014No  antagonism at all. We are in harmony with it. Our theory of\nevolution and of \u00c2k\u00e2sha and Pr\u00e2na is exactly what your modern philosophies\nhave.  Your  belief in evolution is among our Yogis and in the Sankhya\nphilosophy. For instance, Patanjali speaks of one species being changed into\nanother by the infilling of nature\u2014\"\"\u091c\u093e\u0924\u094d\u092f\u0928\u094d\u0924\u0930\u092a\u0930\u093f\u0923\u093e\u092e\u0903 \u092a\u094d\u0930\u0915\u0943\u0924\u094d\u092f\u093e\u092a\u0942\u0930\u093e\u0924\u094d\"; only he differs from \nyou in the explanation. In the animal, the man was held in abeyance; but as soon as good\ncircumstances came, he was manifested as man. And again, as soon as fitting\ncircumstances came, the God in man manifested itself. So we have very little\nto quarrel with in the new theories. For instance, the theory of the Sankhya\nas to perception is very little different from modern physiology.\n", "\nQ.\u2014But your method is different?\n", "A.\u2014Yes. We claim that concentrating the powers of the mind is the only way\nto knowledge. In external science, concentration of mind is\u2014putting it on\nsomething external; and in internal science, it is\u2014drawing towards one's\nSelf. We call this concentration of mind Yoga.\n", "\nQ.\u2014In the state of concentration does the truth of these principles become\nevident?\n", "A.\u2014The Yogis claim a good deal. In truth they do not exist. But as\nlong as we are bound, we have to see these visions. Within these visions\nthings come in a certain regular order. Beyond them there is no law and\norder, but freedom.\n", "\nQ.\u2014Is the Advaita antagonistic to dualism?\n", "A. \u2014The Upanishads not being in a systematised form, it was easy for\nphilosophers  to  take  up texts when they liked to form a system. The\nUpanishads had always to be taken, else there would be no basis. Yet we find\nall the different schools of thought in the Upanishads. Our solution is that\nthe Advaita is not antagonistic to the Dvaita (dualism). We say the latter\nis only one of three steps. Religion always takes three steps. The first is\ndualism. Then man gets to a higher state, partial non-dualism. And at last\nhe finds he is one with the universe. Therefore the three do not contradict\nbut fulfil.\n", "\nQ.\u2014Why does Maya or ignorance exist?\n", "A.\u2014\"Why\" cannot be asked beyond the limit of causation. It can only be\nasked within Maya. We say we will answer the question when it is logically\nformulated. Before that we have no right to answer.\n", "\nQ.\u2014Does the Personal God belong to Maya?\n", "A.\u2014Yes; but the Personal God is the same Absolute seen through Maya. That\nAbsolute under the control of nature is what is called the human soul; and\nthat which is controlling nature is Ishvara, or the Personal God. If a man\nstarts from here to see the sun, he will see at first a little sun; but as\nhe proceeds he will see it bigger and bigger, until he reaches the real one.\nAt each stage of his progress he was seeing apparently a different sun; yet\nwe are sure it was the same sun he was seeing. So all these things are but\nvisions of the Absolute, and as such they are true. Not one is a false\nvision, but we can only say they were lower stages.\n", "\nQ.\u2014What  is the special process by which one will come to know the\nAbsolute?\n", "A.\u2014We say there are two processes. One is the positive, and the other, the\nnegative. The positive is that through which the whole universe is going\u2014\nthat of love. If this circle of love is increased indefinitely, we reach the\none universal love. The other is the \"Neti\", \"Neti\"\u2014\"not this\", \"not this\"\n\u2014stopping every wave in the mind which tries to draw it out; and at last\nthe mind dies, as it were, and the Real discloses Itself. We call that\nSam\u00e2dhi, or superconsciousness.\n", "\nQ.\u2014That would be, then, merging the subject in the object!\n", "A.\u2014Merging the object in the subject, not merging the subject in the\nobject.  Really  this world dies, and I remain. I am the only one that\nremains.\n", "\nQ.\u2014Some of our philosophers in Germany have thought that the whole\ndoctrine of Bhakti (Love for the Divine) in India was very likely the result\nof occidental influence.\n", "A.\u2014I do not take any stock in that\u2014the assumption was ephemeral. The\nBhakti of India is not like the Western Bhakti. The central idea of ours is\nthat  there is no thought of fear. It is always, love God. There is no\nworship through fear, but always through love, from beginning to end. In the\nsecond place, the assumption is quite unnecessary. Bhakti is spoken of in\nthe oldest of the Upanishads, which is much older than the Christian Bible.\nThe germs of Bhakti are even in the Samhit\u00e2 (the Vedic hymns). The word\nBhakti is not a Western word. It was suggested by the word Shraddh\u00e2.\n", "\nQ.\u2014What is the Indian idea of the Christian faith?\n", "A.\u2014That it is very good. The Vedanta will take in every one. We have a\npeculiar idea in India. Suppose I had a child. I should not teach him any\nreligion; I should teach him breathings\u2014the practice of concentrating the\nmind, and just one line of prayer\u2014not prayer in your sense, but simply\nsomething like this, \"I meditate on Him who is the Creator of this universe:\nmay He enlighten my mind I \" That way he would be educated, and then go\nabout hearing different philosophers and teachers. He would select one who,\nhe thought, would suit him best; and this man would become his Guru or\nteacher, and he would become a Shishya or disciple. He would say to that\nman, \"This form of philosophy which you preach is the best; so teach me.\"\nOur fundamental idea is that your doctrine cannot be mine, or mine yours.\nEach one must have his own way. My daughter may have one method, and my son\nanother, and I again another. So each one has an Ishta or chosen way, and we\nkeep it to ourselves. It is between me and my teacher, because we do not\nwant to create a fight. It will not help any one to tell it to others,\nbecause each one will have to find his own way. So only general philosophy\nand  general methods can be taught universally. For instance, giving a\nludicrous example, it may help me to stand on one leg. It would be ludicrous\nto you if I said every one must do that, but it may suit me. It is quite\npossible for me to be a dualist and for my wife to be a monist, and so on.\nOne of my son may worship Christ or Buddha or Mohammed, so long as he obeys\nthe caste laws. That is his own Ishta.\n", "\nQ.\u2014Do all Hindus believe in caste?\n", "A.\u2014They are forced to. They may not believe, but they have to obey.\n", "\nQ.\u2014Are  these exercises in breathing and concentration universally\npracticed?\n", "A.\u2014Yes; only some practice only a little, just to satisfy the requirements\nof their religion. The temples in India are not like the churches here. They\nmay all vanish tomorrow, and will not be missed. A temple is built by a man\nwho wants to go to heaven, or to get a son, or something of that sort. So he\nbuilds a large temple and employs a few priests to hold services there. I\nneed not go there at all, because all my worship is in the home. In every\nhouse is a special room set apart, which is called the chapel. The first\nduty of the child, after his initiation, is to take a bath, and then to\nworship; and his worship consists of this breathing and meditating and\nrepeating of a certain name. And another thing is to hold the body straight.\nWe believe that the mind has every power over the body to keep it healthy.\nAfter one has done this, then another comes and takes his seat, and each one\ndoes it in silence. Sometimes there are three or four in the same room, but\neach one may have a different method. This worship is repeated at least\ntwice a day.\n", "\nQ.\u2014This state of oneness that you speak of, is it an ideal or something\nactually attained?\n", "A.\u2014We say it is within actuality; we say we realise that state. The Vedas teach three things: this Self\nis first to be heard, then to be reasoned, and then to be meditated upon.\nWhen a man first hears it, he must reason on it, so that he does not believe\nit  ignorantly, but knowingly; and after reasoning what it is, he must\nmeditate upon it, and then realise it. We say religion is a superconscious state.\n", "\nQ.\u2014If you ever reach that state of superconsciousness, can you ever tell\nabout it?\n", "A.\u2014No; but we know it by its fruits. That shows the difference between these two states.\n", "\nQ. \u2014I should like to ask, in continuation of Professor\u2014's question,\nwhether you know of any people who have made any study of the principles of\nself-hypnotism,  which they undoubtedly practiced to a great extent in\nancient India, and what has been recently stated and practiced in that\nthing.
