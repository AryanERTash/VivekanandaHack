["\n", "CLXXVII\n", "To Miss Mary Hale\n", "\n", "23rd April, 1900.\n", "MY DEAR MARY,\n", "I ought to have started today but circumstances so happened that I cannot\nforgo  the temptation to be in a camp under the huge red-wood trees of\nCalifornia before I leave. Therefore I postpone it for three or four days.\nAgain after the incessant work I require a breath of God's free air before I\nstart on this bone-breaking journey of four days.\n", "Margot insists in her letter that I must keep my promise to come to see Aunt\nMary in fifteen days. It will be kept \u2014 only in twenty days instead of\nfifteen. By that I avoid the nasty snowstorm Chicago had lately and get a\nlittle strength too.\n", "Margot is a great partisan of Aunt Mary it seems, and other people besides\nme have nieces and cousins and aunts.\n", "I start tomorrow to the woods. Woof! get my lungs full of ozone before\ngetting into Chicago. In the meanwhile keep my mail for me when it comes to\nChicago and don't send it off here like a good girl as you are.\n", "I have finished work. Only a few days' rest, my friends insist \u2014 three or\nfour \u2014 before facing the railway.\n", "I have got a free pass for three months from here to New York; no expense\nexcept the sleeping car; so, you see, free, free!\n", "Yours affectionately,\n", "\nVIVEKANANDA.\n"]
