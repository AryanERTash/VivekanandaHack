## Original Dataset Text
["\n", "AN EVENING WITH OUR HINDU COUSINS\n", "(Northampton Daily Herald, April 16, 1894)\n", "\nFor Swami Vive Kananda proved conclusively that all our neighbors across the\nwater, even the remotest, are our close cousins differing only a trifle in\ncolor,  language,  customs and religion, the silver-tongued Hindu monk\nprefacing  his  address in city hall Saturday evening [April 14] by an\nhistoric sketch of the origin of his own and all other leading nations of\nthe earth which demonstrated the truth that race-kinship is more of a simple\nfact than many know or always care to admit.\n", "\nThe informal address that followed regarding some of the customs of the\nHindu people was more of the nature of a pleasant parlor talk, expressed\nwith the easy freedom of the conversational adept, and to those of his\nhearers possessing a natural and cultivated interest in the subject both the\nman and his thought were intensely interesting for more reasons than can be\ngiven here. But to others the speaker was disappointing in not covering a\nlarger scope in his word-pictures, the address, although extremely lengthy\nfor the American lecture-platform, referring to very few of the \"customs and\nmanners\" of the peculiar people considered, and of whose personal, civil,\nhome, social and religious life much more would have been gladly heard from\nthis one of the finest representatives of this oldest of races, which the\naverage student of human nature should find preeminently interesting but\nreally knows the least about.\n", "\nThe allusions to the life of the Hindu began with a picture of the birth of\nthe Hindu boy, his introduction to educational training, his marriage,\nslight reference to the home life but not what was expected, the speaker\ndiverging frequently to make comparative comments on the customs and ideas\nof his own and English-speaking races, socially, morally and religiously,\nthe inference in all cases being clearly in favor of his own, although most\ncourteously, kindly and gracefully expressed. Some of his auditors who are\ntolerably well posted as to social and family conditions among the Hindoos\nof all classes would have liked to have asked the speaker a challenging\nquestion or two on a good many of the points he touched upon. For instance,\nwhen he so eloquently and beautifully portrayed the Hindu idea of womanhood\nas the divine motherhood ideal, to be forever reverenced, even worshipped\nwith a devotion of loyalty such as the most woman-respecting unselfish and\ntruest of American sons, husbands and fathers cannot even conceive of, one\nwould have liked to know what the reply would have been to the query as to\nhow far this beautiful theory is exemplified in practice in the majority of\nHindu homes, which hold wives, mothers, daughters and sisters.\n", "\nThe rebuke to the greed for gain, the national vice of luxury-seeking,\nself-seeking the \"dollar-caste\" sentiment which taints the dominant white\nEuropean and American races to their mortal danger, morally and civilly, was\nonly too just and superbly well-put, the slow, soft, quiets unimpassioned\nmusical voice embodying its thought with all the power and fire of the most\nvehement physical utterance, and went straight to the mark like the \"Thou\nart the man\" of the prophet. But when this learned Hindu nobleman by birth,\nnature and culture attempts to prove \u2014 as he repeatedly did in his frequent\nand apparently half-unconscious digressions from the special point under\nconsideration \u2014 that the distinctively self-centred, self-cultivating,\npreeminently self-soulsaving, negative and passive, not to say selfishly\nindolent religion of his race has proven itself superior in its usefulness\nto  the  world  to  the  vitally  aggressive,  self-forgetful, do-good\nunto-others-first-last-and-always,  go-ye-into-all-the-world  and work\nreligion which we call Christianity, in whose name nine tenths of all the\nreally practical moral, spiritual and philanthropic work of the world has\nbeen and is being done, whatever sad and gross mistakes have been made by\nits unwise zealots, he attempts a large contract.\n", "\nBut  to  see  and  hear  Swami Vive Kananda is an opportunity which no\nintelligent fair-minded American ought to miss if one cares to see a shining\nlight of the very finest product of the mental, moral and spiritual culture\nof a race which reckons its age by thousands where we count ours by hundreds\nand is richly worth the study of every mind.\n", "\nSunday afternoon [April 15] the distinguished Hindu spoke to the students of\nSmith  college  at  the  vesper service, the Fatherhood of God and the\nBrotherhood of man being, virtually, his theme, and that the address made a\ndeep impression is evinced by the report of every auditor, the broadest\nliberality of true religious sentiment and precept characterizing the whole\ntrend of thought.\n"]
