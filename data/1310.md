["\n", "CHAPTER X\n", "THE SHRINE OF AMARNATH\n", "PLACE: Kashmir.\n", "TIME: July 29 to August 8, 1898.\n", "JULY 29.\n", "From this time we saw very little of the Swami. He was full of enthusiasm\nabout the pilgrimage and lived mostly on one meal a day, seeking no company\nmuch, save that of S\u00e2dhus. Sometimes he would come to a camping-ground,\nbeads in hand. Tonight two of the party went roaming about Bawan, which was\nlike a village fair, all modified by a religious tendency centering in the\nsacred springs. Afterwards with Dhira Mata it was possible to go and listen\nat the tent door to the crowd of Hindi-speaking Sadhus who were plying the\nSwami with questions.\n", "On Thursday we reached Pahalgam and camped down at the lower end of the\nvalley. We found that the Swami had to encounter high opposition over the\nquestion of our admission at all. He was supported by the Naked Swamis, one\nof whom said, \"It is true you have this strength, Swamiji, but you ought not\nto manifest it!\" He yielded at the word. That afternoon, however, he took\nhis daughter round the camp to be blessed, which really meant to distribute\nalms \u2014 and whether because he was looked upon as rich or because he was\nrecognized as strong, the next day our tents were moved up to a lovely knoll\nat the head of the camp. . . .\n", "JULY 30.\n", ". . . . . .\n", "How beautiful was the route to the next halt, Chandanwari! There we camped\non the edge of a ravine. It rained all afternoon, and I was visited by the\nSwami only for a five-minutes' chat. But I received endless touching little\nkindnesses from the servants and other pilgrims. . . .\n", ". . . Close to Chandanwari the Swami insisted on my doing my first glacier\non foot and took care to point out every detail of interest. A tremendous\nclimb of some thousands of feet was the next experience. Then a long walk\nalong a narrow path that twisted round mountain after mountain, and finally\nanother steep climb. At the top of the first mountain, the ground was simply\ncarpeted  with edelweiss. Then the road passed five hundred feet above\nSheshnag with its sulky water, and at last we camped in a cold damp place\namongst the snow-peaks, 18,000 feet high. The firs were far below, and all\nafternoon  and  evening  the  coolies had to forage for juniper in all\ndirections.  The  Tahsildar's, Swami's and my own tents were all close\ntogether, and in the evening a large fire was lighted in front. But it did\nnot burn well, and many feet below lay the glacier. I did not see the Swami\nafter we camped.\n", "Panchatarani \u2014 the place of the five streams \u2014 was not nearly such a long\nmarch.  Moreover, it was lower than Sheshnag, and the cold was dry and\nexhilarating. In front of the camp was a dry riverbed, all gravel, and\nthrough  this ran five streams, in all of which it was the duty of the\npilgrim to bathe, walking from one to the other in wet garments. Contriving\nto elude observation completely, Swamiji nevertheless fulfilled the law to\nthe last letter in this respect. . . .\n", "At these heights we often found ourselves in great circles of snow-peaks,\nthose mute giants that have suggested to the Hindu mind the idea of the\nash-encovered God.\n", "AUGUST 2.\n", "On Tuesday, August the 2nd, the great day of Amarnath, the first batch of\npilgrims must have left the camp at two! We left by the light of the full\nmoon. The sun rose as we went down the narrow valley. It was not too safe at\nthis part of the journey. But when we left our Dandies and began to climb,\nthe real danger began. . . . Then, having at last reached the bottom of\nthe farther slope, we had to toil along the glacier mile after mile to the\ncave. . . .\n", "The Swami, exhausted, had by this time fallen behind. . . . He came at last\nand with a word sent me on; he was going to bathe. Half an hour later he\nentered the cave. With a smile he knelt first at one end of the semi-circle,\nthen at the other. The place was vast, large enough to hold a cathedral; and\nthe great ice-Shiva, in a niche of deepest shadow, seemed as if throned on\nits own base. A few minutes passed, and then he turned to leave the cave.\n", "To him, the heavens had opened. He had touched the feet of Shiva. He had had\nto hold himself tight, he said afterwards, lest he \"should swoon away\". But\nso great was his physical exhaustion that a doctor said afterwards that his\nheart  ought  to  have  stopped beating, but had undergone a permanent\nenlargement instead. How strangely near fulfilment had been those words of\nhis Master, \"When he realizes who and what he is, he will give up this\nbody!\"\n", "\"I have enjoyed it so much!\" he said half an hour afterwards, as he sat on a\nrock above the stream-side, eating lunch with the kind Naked Swami and me.\n\"I thought the ice Linga was Shiva Himself. And there were no thievish\nBrahmins, no trade, nothing wrong. It was all worship. I never enjoyed any\nreligious place so much!\"\n", "Afterwards he would often tell of the overwhelming vision that had seemed to\ndraw him almost into its vertex. He would talk of the poetry of the white\nice-pillar; and it was he who suggested that the first discovery of the\nplace had been by a party of shepherds, who had wandered far in search of\ntheir flocks one summer day and had entered the cave to find themselves\nbefore the unmelting ice, in the presence of the Lord Himself. He always\nsaid too that the grace of Amarnath had been granted to him there, not to\ndie till he himself should give consent. And to me he said, \"You do not now\nunderstand. But you have made the pilgrimage, and it will go on working.\nCauses must bring their effects. You will understand better afterwards. The\neffects will come\".\n", "How beautiful was the road by which we returned next morning to Pahalgam! We\nstruck tents that night immediately on our return to them and camped later\nfor the night in a snowy pass a whole stage further on. We paid a coolie a\nfew annas here to push on with a letter; but when we actually arrived next\nafternoon we found that this had been quite unnecessary, for all morning\nlong relays of pilgrims had been passing the tents and dropping in, in the\nmost  friendly manner, to give the others news of us and our impending\narrival. In the morning we were up and on the way long before dawn. As the\nsun rose before us, while the moon went down behind, we passed above the\nLake of Death, into which about forty pilgrims had been buried one year by\nan avalanche which their hymns had started. After this we came to the tiny\ngoat-path down the face of a steep cliff by which we were able to shorten\nthe return journey so much. This was little better than a scramble, and\neveryone had perforce to do it on foot. At the bottom the villagers had\nsomething like breakfast ready. Fires were burning, Chapatties baking, and\ntea was ready to be served out. From this time on parties of pilgrims would\nleave  the  main  body at each parting of the ways, and the feeling of\nsolidarity that had grown up amongst us all throughout the journey became\ngradually less and less.\n", "That evening on the knoll above Pahalgam, where a great fire of pine-logs\nwas lighted and Dhurries spread, we all sat and talked. Our friend the Naked\nSwami joined us and we had plenty of fun and nonsense, but presently, when\nall  had gone save our own little party, we sat on with the great moon\noverhead and the towering snows and rushing rivers and the mountain-pines.\nAnd the Swami talked of Shiva and the cave and the great verge of vision.\n", "AUGUST 8.\n", "We  started for Islamabad next day, and on Monday morning as we sat at\nbreakfast, we were towed safely into Srinagar.\n"]
