## Original Dataset Text
["\n", "LXXXVIII\n", "\n", "228 WEST 39TH STREET, NEW YORK,\n", "8th Dec., 1895.\n", "DEAR MRS. BULL,\n", "Many thanks for your kind note of welcome. I arrived last Friday after ten\ndays of a very tedious voyage. It was awfully rough and for the first time\nin my life I was very badly seasick. . . . I have left some strong friends\nin England who will work in my absence expecting my arrival next summer. My\nplans are not settled yet about the work here. Only I have an idea to run to\nDetroit and Chicago meanwhile, and then come back to New York. The public\nlecture plan I intend to give up entirely, as I find the best thing for me\nto do is to step entirely out of the money question \u2014 either in public\nlectures or private classes. In the long run it does harm and sets a bad\nexample.\n", "In  England  I worked on this principle and refused even the voluntary\ncollections they made. Mr. Sturdy, being a rich man, bore the major part of\nthe expenses of lecturing in big halls \u2014 the rest I bore. It worked well.\nAgain, to use rather a vulgar illustration, even in religion there is no use\noverstocking the market. The supply must follow the demand, and the demand\nalone. If people want me, they will get up lectures. I need not bother\nmyself about these things. If you think after consultation with Mrs. Adams\nand Miss Locke that it would be practicable for me to come to Chicago for a\ncourse of lectures, write to me. Of course the money question should be left\nentirely out.\n", "My idea is for autonomic, independent groups in different places. Let them\nwork on their own account and do the best they can. As for myself, I do not\nwant to entangle myself in any organisation. Hoping you are enjoying good\nhealth both physically and mentally,\n", "I am yours, in the Lord,\n", "\nVIVEKANANDA.\n"]
