["\n", "CXLIII\n", "To Miss Mary Hale\n", "RIDGELY MANOR,\n", "September 1899.\n", "MY DEAR MARY,\n", "Yes, I have arrived. I had a letter from Isabelle from Greenacre. I hope to\nsee her soon and Harriet. Harriet Woolley has been uniformly silent. Never\nmind, I will bide my time, and as soon as Mr. Woolley becomes a millionaire,\ndemand my money. You did not write any particulars about Mother Church and\nFather Pope, only the news of something about me in some newspapers. I have\nlong ceased to take any interest in papers; only they keep me before the\npublic and get a sale of my books \"anyway\" as you say. Do you know what I am\ntrying to do now? Writing a book on India and her people \u2014 a short chatty\nsimple something. Again I am going to learn French. If I fail to do it this\nyear, I cannot \"do\" the Paris Exposition next year properly. Well, I expect\nto learn much French here where even the servants talk it.\n", "You never saw Mrs. Leggett, did you? She is simply grand. I am going to\nParis next year as their guest, as I did the first time.\n", "I have now got a monastery on the Ganga for the teaching of philosophy and\ncomparative religion and a centre of work.\n", "What have you been doing all this time? Reading? Writing? You did not do\nanything. You could have written lots by this time. Even if you had taught\nme French, I would be quite a Froggy now, and you did not, only made me talk\nnonsense. You never went to Greenacre. I hope it is getting strength every\nyear.\n", "Say, you 24 feet and 600 lbs. of Christian Science, you could not pull me up\nwith your treatments. I am losing much faith in your healing powers. Where\nis Sam? \"Bewaring\" all this time as he could; bless his heart, such a noble\nboy!\n", "I was growing grey fast, but somehow it got checked. I am sorry, only a few\ngrey hairs now; a research will unearth many though. I like it and am going\nto cultivate a long white goaty. Mother Church and Father Pope were having a\nfine time on the continent. I saw a bit on my way home. And you have been\nCinderella-ing in Chicago \u2014 good for you. Persuade the old folks to go to\nParis next year and take you along. There must be wonderful sights to see;\nthe  French are making a last great struggle, they say, before closing\nbusiness.\n", "Well, you did not write me long, long. You do not deserve this letter, but\n\u2014 I am so good you know, especially as death is drawing near \u2014 I do not want\nto quarrel with anyone. I am dying to see Isabelle and Harriet. I hope they\nhave got a great supply of healing power at Greenacre Inn and will help me\nout of my present fall. In my days the Inn was well stored with spiritual\nfood, and less of material stuff. Do you know anything of osteopathy? Here\nis one in New York working wonders really.\n", "I am going to have my bones searched by him in a week. Where is Miss Howe?\nShe is such a noble soul, such a friend. By the by, Mary, it is curious your\nfamily, Mother Church and her clergy, both monastic and secular, have made\nmore impression on me than any family I know of. Lord bless you ever and\never.\n", "I am taking rest now, and the Leggetts are so kind. I feel perfectly at\nhome. I intend to go to New York to see the Dewy procession. I have not seen\nmy friends there.\n", "Write  me all about yourselves. I so long to hear. You know Joe Joe of\ncourse. I marred their visit to India with my constant break-downs, and they\nwere  so  good, so forgiving. For years Mrs. Bull and she have been my\nguardian angels. Mrs. Bull is expected here next week.\n", "She would have been here before this, but her daughter (Olea) had a spell of\nillness. She suffered much, but is now out of danger. Mrs. Bull has taken\none of Leggett's cottages here, and if the cold weather does not set in\nfaster than usual, we are going to have a delightful month here even now.\nThe place is so beautiful \u2014 well wooded and perfect lawns.\n", "\nI tried to play golf the other day; I do not think it difficult at all \u2014\nonly it requires good practice. You never went to Philadelphia to visit your\ngolfing friends? What are your plans? What do you intend to do the rest of\nyour life? Have you thought out any work? Write me a long letter, will you?\nI saw a lady in the streets of Naples as I was passing, going along with\nthree others, must be Americans, so like you that I was almost going to\nspeak to her; when I came near I saw my mistake. Good-bye for the present.\nWrite sharp. . . .\n", "Ever your affectionate brother,\n", "\nVIVEKANANDA.\n"]
