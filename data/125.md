["\n", "FIRST PUBLIC LECTURE IN THE EAST\n(Delivered in Colombo)\n", "\nAfter his memorable work in the West, Swami Vivekananda landed at Colombo on\nthe afternoon of January 15, 1897, and was given a right royal reception by\nthe  Hindu  community there. The following address of welcome was then\npresented to him:\n", "\n", "SRIMAT VIVEKANANDA SWAMI\n", "\n Revered Sir, \n", "In pursuance of a resolution passed at a public meeting of the Hindus of the\ncity of Colombo, we beg to offer you a hearty welcome to this Island. We\ndeem it a privilege to be the first to welcome you on your return home from\nyour great mission in the West.\n", "\nWe have watched with joy and thankfulness the success with which the mission\nhas, under God's blessing, been crowned. You have proclaimed to the nations\nof Europe and America the Hindu ideal of a universal religion, harmonising\nall creeds, providing spiritual food for each soul according to its needs,\nand lovingly drawing it unto God. You have preached the Truth and the Way,\ntaught from remote ages by a succession of Masters whose blessed feet have\nwalked and sanctified the soil of India, and whose gracious presence and\ninspiration have made her, through all her vicissitudes, the Light of the\nWorld.\n", "\nTo the inspiration of such a Master, Shri Ramakrishna Paramahamsa Deva, and\nto your self-sacrificing zeal, Western nations owe the priceless boon of\nbeing placed in living contact with the spiritual genius of India, while to\nmany  of  our  own  countrymen,  delivered from the glamour of Western\ncivilisation, the value of Our glorious heritage has been brought home.\n", "\nBy your noble work and example you have laid humanity under an obligation\ndifficult to repay, and you have shed fresh lustre upon our Motherland. We\npray that the grace of God may continue to prosper you and your work, and\n", "\n", "We remain, Revered Sir,\nYours faithfully,\nfor and on behalf of the Hindus of Colombo,\n P. Coomara Swamy ,\nMember of the Legislative Council of Ceylon,\nChairman of the Meeting.\n A. Kulaveerasingham , Secretary.\n", "Colombo, January, 1897.\n", "\nThe  Swami gave a brief reply, expressing his appreciation of the kind\nwelcome he had received. He took advantage of the opportunity to point out\nthat the demonstration had not been made in honour of a great politician, or\na great soldier, or a millionaire, but of a begging Sanny\u00e2sin, showing the\ntendency of the Hindu mind towards religion. He urged the necessity of\nkeeping religion as the backbone of the national life if the nation were to\nlive, and disclaimed any personal character for the welcome he had received,\nbut insisted upon its being the recognition of a principle.\n", "\nOn the evening of the 16th the Swami gave the following public lecture in\nthe Floral Hall:\n", "\nWhat little work has been done by me has not been from any inherent power\nthat resides in me, but from the cheers, the goodwill, the blessings that\nhave followed my path in the West from this our very beloved, most sacred,\ndear  Motherland.  Some good has been done, no doubt, in the West, but\nspecially to myself; for what before was the result of an emotional nature,\nperhaps, has gained the certainty of conviction and attained the power and\nstrength of demonstration. Formerly I thought as every Hindu thinks, and as\nthe Hon. President has just pointed out to you, that this is the Punya\nBhumi, the land of Karma. Today I stand here and say, with the conviction of\ntruth, that it is so. If there is any land on this earth that can lay claim\nto be the blessed Punya Bhumi, to be the land to which all souls on this\nearth must come to account for Karma, the land to which every soul that is\nwending its way Godward must come to attain its last home, the land where\nhumanity has attained its highest towards gentleness, towards generosity,\ntowards purity, towards calmness, above all, the land of introspection and\nof spirituality \u2014 it is India. Hence have started the founders of religions\nfrom the most ancient times, deluging the earth again and again with the\npure and perennial waters of spiritual truth. Hence have proceeded the tidal\nwaves of philosophy that have covered the earth, East or West, North or\nSouth, and hence again must start the wave which is going to spiritualise\nthe material civilisation of the world. Here is the life-giving water with\nwhich must be quenched the burning fire of materialism which is burning the\ncore of the hearts of millions in other lands. Believe me, my friends, this\nis going to be.\n", "\nSo much I have seen, and so far those of you who are students of the history\nof races are already aware of this fact. The debt which the world owes to\nour Motherland is immense. Taking country with country, there is not one\nrace on this earth to which the world owes so much as to the patient Hindu,\nthe mild Hindu. \"The mild Hindu\" sometimes is used as an expression of\nreproach; but if ever a reproach concealed a wonderful truth, it is in the\nterm,  \"the mild Hindu\", who has always been the blessed child of God.\nCivilisations have arisen in other parts of the world. In ancient times and\nin modern times, great ideas have emanated from strong and great races. In\nancient and in modern times, wonderful ideas have been carried forward from\none race to another. In ancient and in modern times, seeds of great truth\nand power have been cast abroad by the advancing tides of national life; but\nmark you, my friends, it has been always with the blast of war trumpets and\nwith the march of embattled cohorts. Each idea had to be soaked in a deluge\nof  blood.  Each idea had to wade through the blood of millions of our\nfellow-beings.  Each word of power had to be followed by the groans of\nmillions, by the wails of orphans, by the tears of widows. This, in the\nmain,  other nations have taught; but India has for thousands of years\npeacefully existed. Here activity prevailed when even Greece did not exist,\nwhen Rome was not thought of, when the very fathers of the modern Europeans\nlived in the forests and painted themselves blue. Even earlier, when history\nhas no record, and tradition dares not peer into the gloom of that intense\npast, even from then until now, ideas after ideas have marched out from her,\nbut every word has been spoken with a blessing behind it and peace before\nit. We, of all nations of the world, have never been a conquering race, and\nthat blessing is on our head, and therefore we live.\n", "\nThere was a time when at the sound of the march of big Greek battalions the\nearth trembled. Vanished from off the face of the earth, with not every a\ntale left behind to tell, gone is that ancient land of the Greeks. There was\na time when the Roman Eagle floated over everything worth having in this\nworld; everywhere Rome's power was felt and pressed on the head of humanity;\nthe earth trembled at the name of Rome. But the Capitoline Hill is a mass of\nruins, the spider weaves its web where the Caesars ruled. There have been\nother nations equally glorious that have come and gone, living a few hours\nof exultant and exuberant dominance and of a wicked national life, and then\nvanishing like ripples on the face of the waters. Thus have these nations\nmade their mark on the face of humanity. But we live, and if Manu came back\ntoday he would not be bewildered, and would not find himself in a foreign\nland.  The  same  laws are here, laws adjusted and thought out through\nthousands and thousands of years; customs, the outcome of the acumen of ages\nand the experience of centuries, that seem to be eternal; and as the days go\nby, as blow after blow of misfortune has been delivered upon them, such\nblows seem to have served one purpose only, that of making them stronger and\nmore constant. And to find the centre of all this, the heart from which the\nblood flows, the mainspring of the national life, believe me when I say\nafter my experience of the world, that it is here.\n", "\nTo  the  other  nations  of  the world, religion is one among the many\noccupations of life. There is politics, there are the enjoyments of social\nlife, there is all that wealth can buy or power can bring, there is all that\nthe senses can enjoy; and among all these various occupations of life and\nall this searching after something which can give yet a little more whetting\nto the cloyed senses \u2014 among all these, there is perhaps a little bit of\nreligion. But here, in India, religion is the one and the only occupation of\nlife. How many of you know that there has been a Sino-Japanese War? Very few\nof  you,  if  any.  That  there are tremendous political movements and\nsocialistic movements trying to transform Western society, how many of you\nknow? Very few indeed, if any. But that there was a Parliament of Religions\nin America, and that there was a Hindu Sanny\u00e2sin sent over there, I am\nastonished to find that even the cooly knows of it. That shows the way the\nwind blows, where the national life is. I used to read books written by\nglobe-trotting travellers, especially foreigners, who deplored the ignorance\nof the Eastern masses, but I found out that it was partly true and at the\nsame time partly untrue. If you ask a ploughman in England, or America, or\nFrance, or Germany to what party he belongs, he can tell you whether he\nbelongs to the Radicals or the Conservatives, and for whom he is going to\nvote. In America he will say whether he is Republican or Democrat, and he\neven knows something about the silver question. But if you ask him about his\nreligion, he will tell you that he goes to church and belongs to a certain\ndenomination. That is all he knows, and he thinks it is sufficient.\n", "\nNow, when we come to India, if you ask one of our ploughmen, \"Do you know\nanything  about  politics?\" He will reply, \"What is that?\" He does not\nunderstand the socialistic movements, the relation between capital and\nlabour, and all that; he has never heard of such things in his life, he\nworks hard and earns his bread. But you ask, \"What is your religion?\" he\nreplies, \"Look here, my friend, I have marked it on my forehead.\" He can\ngive you a good hint or two on questions of religion. That has been my\nexperience. That is our nation's life.\n", "\nIndividuals have each their own peculiarities, and each man has his own\nmethod of growth, his own life marked out for him by the infinite past life,\nby all his past Karma as we Hindus say. Into this world he comes with all\nthe past on him, the infinite past ushers the present, and the way in which\nwe use the present is going to make the future. Thus everyone born into this\nworld has a bent, a direction towards which he must go, through which he\nmust live, and what is true of the individual is equally true of the race.\nEach race, similarly, has a peculiar bent, each race has a peculiar raison d'\u00eatre,\neach race has a peculiar mission to fulfil in the life of the world.\nEach race has to make its own result, to fulfil its own mission. Political\ngreatness or military power is never the mission of our race; it never was,\nand, mark my words, it never will be. But there has been the other mission\ngiven to us, which is to conserve, to preserve, to accumulate, as it were,\ninto a dynamo, all the spiritual energy of the race, and that concentrated\nenergy is to pour forth in a deluge on the world whenever circumstances are\npropitious.  Let the Persian or the Greek, the Roman, the Arab, or the\nEnglishman march his battalions, conquer the world, and link the different\nnations together, and the philosophy and spirituality of India is ever ready\nto flow along the new-made channels into the veins of the nations of the\nworld. The Hindu's calm brain must pour out its own quota to give to the sum\ntotal of human progress. India's gift to the world is the light spiritual.\n", "\nThus, in the past, we read in history that whenever there arose a greet\nconquering nation uniting the different races of the world, binding India\nwith the other races, taking her out, as it were, from her loneliness and\nfrom her aloofness from the rest of the world into which she again and again\ncast herself, that whenever such a state has been brought about, the result\nhas been the flooding of the world with Indian spiritual ideas. At the\nbeginning of this century, Schopenhauer, the great German philosopher,\nstudying from a not very clear translation of the Vedas made from an old\ntranslation into Persian and thence by a young Frenchman into Latin, says,\n\"In the whole world there is no study so beneficial and so elevating as that\nof the Upanishads. It has been the solace of my life, it will be the solace\nof my death.\" This great German sage foretold that \"The world is about to\nsee a revolution in thought more extensive and more powerful than that which\nwas  witnessed  by the Renaissance of Greek Literature\", and today his\npredictions are coming to pass. Those who keep their eyes open, those who\nunderstand the workings in the minds of different nations of the West, those\nwho are thinkers and study the different nations, will find the immense\nchange that has been produced in the tone, the procedure, in the methods,\nand in the literature of the world by this slow, never-ceasing permeation of\nIndian thought.\n", "\nBut there is another peculiarity, as I have already hinted to you. We never\npreached our thoughts with fire and sword. If there is one word in the\nEnglish language to represent the gift of India to the world, if there is\none word in the English language to express the effect which the literature\nof India produces upon mankind, it is this one word, \"fascination\". It is\nthe opposite of anything that takes you suddenly; it throws on you, as it\nwere, a charm imperceptibly. To many, Indian thought, Indian manners; Indian\ncustoms, Indian philosophy, Indian literature are repulsive at the first\nsight; but let them persevere, let them read, let them become familiar with\nthe great principles underlying these ideas, and it is ninety-nine to one\nthat the charm will come over them, and fascination will be the result. Slow\nand silent, as the gentle dew that falls in the morning, unseen and unheard\nyet producing a most tremendous result, has been the work of the calm,\npatient, all-suffering spiritual race upon the world of thought.\n", "\nOnce more history is going to repeat itself. For today, under the blasting\nlight of modern science, when old and apparently strong and invulnerable\nbeliefs have been shattered to their very foundations, when special claims\nlaid to the allegiance of mankind by different sects have been all blown\ninto atoms and have vanished into air, when the sledge-hammer blows of\nmodern antiquarian researches are pulverising like masses of porcelain all\nsorts of antiquated orthodoxies, when religion in the West is only in the\nhands  of  the ignorant and the knowing ones look down with scorn upon\nanything belonging to religion, here comes to the fore the philosophy of\nIndia, which displays the highest religious aspirations of the Indian mind,\nwhere the grandest philosophical facts have been the practical spirituality\nof the people. This naturally is coming to the rescue, the idea of the\noneness of all, the Infinite, the idea of the Impersonal, the wonderful idea\nof the eternal soul of man, of the unbroken continuity in the march of\nbeings, and the infinity of the universe. The old sects looked upon the\nworld as a little mud-puddle and thought that time began but the other day.\nIt was there in our old books, and only there that the grand idea of the\ninfinite range of time, space, and causation, and above all, the infinite\nglory of the spirit of man governed all the search for religion. When the\nmodern tremendous theories of evolution and conservation of energy and so\nforth are dealing death blows to all sorts of crude theologies, what can\nhold any more the allegiance of cultured humanity but the most wonderful,\nconvincing, broadening, and ennobling ideas that can be found only in that\nmost marvellous product of the soul of man, the wonderful voice of God, the\nVedanta?\n", "\nAt the same time, I must remark that what I mean by our religion working\nupon  the  nations outside of India comprises only the principles, the\nbackground, the foundation upon which that religion is built. The detailed\nworkings, the minute points which have been worked out through centuries of\nsocial necessity, little ratiocinations about manners and customs and social\nwell-being, do not rightly find a place in the category of religion. We know\nthat in our books a clear distinction is made between two sets of truths.\nThe one set is that which abides for ever, being built upon the nature of\nman, the nature of the soul, the soul's relation to God, the nature of God,\nperfection, and so on; there are also the principles of cosmology, of the\ninfinitude  of  creation, or more correctly speaking \u2014 projection, the\nwonderful law of cyclical procession, and so on \u2014 these are the eternal\nprinciples  founded  upon  the universal laws in nature. The other set\ncomprises the minor laws which guided the working of our everyday life They\nbelong more properly to the Pur\u00e2nas, to the Smritis, and not to the Shrutis.\nThese have nothing to do with the other principles. Even in our own nation\nthese minor laws have been changing all the time. Customs of one age, of one\nYuga, have not been the customs of another, and as Yuga comes after Yuga,\nthey will still have to change. Great Rishis will appear and lead us to\ncustoms and manners that are suited to new environments.\n", "\nThe great principles underlying all this wonderful, infinite, ennobling,\nexpansive view of man and God and the world have been produced in India. In\nIndia alone man has not stood up to fight for a little tribal God, saying\n\"My God is true and yours is not true; let us have a good fight over it.\" It\nwas only here that such ideas did not occur as fighting for little gods.\nThese great underlying principles, being based upon the eternal nature of\nman, are as potent today for working for the good of the human race as they\nwere thousands of years ago, and they will remain so, so tong as this earth\nremains, so long as the law of Karma remains, so long as we are born as\nindividuals and have to work out our own destiny by our individual power.\n", "\nAnd above all, what India has to give to the world is this. If we watch the\ngrowth and development of religions in different races, we shall always find\nthis that each tribe at the beginning has a god of its own. If the tribes\nare  allied to each other, these gods will have a generic name, as for\nexample, all the Babylonian gods had. When the Babylonians were divided into\nmany races, they had the generic name of Baal, just as the Jewish races had\ndifferent gods with the common name of Moloch; and at the same time you will\nfind that one of these tribes becomes superior to the rest, and lays claim\nto its own king as the king over all. Therefrom it naturally follows that it\nalso  wants  to  preserve  its  own  god  as the god of all the races.\nBaal-Merodach, said the Babylonians, was the greatest god; all the others\nwere inferior. Moloch-Yahveh was the superior over all other Molochs. And\nthese  questions had to be decided by the fortunes of battle. The same\nstruggle was here also. In India the same competing gods had been struggling\nwith each other for supremacy, but the great good fortune of this country\nand  of  the world was that there came out in the midst of the din and\nconfusion a voice which declared \u090f\u0915\u0902 \u0938\u0926\u094d\u0935\u093f\u092a\u094d\u0930\u093e \u092c\u0939\u0941\u0927\u093e \u0935\u0926\u0928\u094d\u0924\u093f  \u2014 \"That which exists is\nOne; sages call It by various names.\" It is not that Shiva is superior to\nVishnu, not that Vishnu is everything and Shiva is nothing, but it is the\nsame one whom you call either Shiva, or Vishnu, or by a hundred other names.\nThe names are different, but it is the same one. The whole history of India\nyou may read in these few words. The whole history has been a repetition in\nmassive language, with tremendous power, of that one central doctrine. It\nwas repeated in the land till it had entered into the blood of the nation,\ntill it began to tingle with every drop of blood that flowed in its veins,\ntill it became one with the life, part and parcel of the material of which\nit was composed; and thus the land was transmuted into the most wonderful\nland of toleration, giving the right to welcome the various religions as\nwell as all sects into the old mother-country.\n", "\nAnd herein is the explanation of the most remarkable phenomenon that is only\nwitnessed here \u2014 all the various sects, apparently hopelessly contradictory,\nyet living in such harmony. You may be a dualist, and I may be a monist. You\nmay believe that you are the eternal servant of God, and I may declare that\nI am one with God Himself; yet both of us are good Hindus. How is that\npossible? Read then \u090f\u0915\u0902 \u0938\u0926\u094d\u0935\u093f\u092a\u094d\u0930\u093e \u092c\u0939\u0941\u0927\u093e \u0935\u0926\u0928\u094d\u0924\u093f  \u2014\n\"That  which exists is One; sages call It by various names.\" Above all\nothers, my countrymen, this is the one grand truth that we have to teach to\nthe world. Even the most educated people of other countries turn up their\nnoses at an angle of forty-five degrees and call our religion idolatry. I\nhave seen that; and they never stopped to think what a mass of superstition\nthere was in their own heads. It is still so everywhere, this tremendous\nsectarianism, the low narrowness of the mind. The thing which a man has is\nthe only thing worth having; the only life worth living is his own little\nlife of dollar-worship and mammon-worship; the only little possession worth\nhaving is his own property, and nothing else. If he can manufacture a little\nclay nonsense or invent a machine, that is to be admired beyond the greatest\npossessions. That is the case over the whole world in spite of education and\nlearning. But education has yet to be in the world, and civilisation \u2014\ncivilisation has begun nowhere yet. Ninety-nine decimal nine per cent of the\nhuman race are more or less savages even now. We may read of these things in\nbooks, and we hear of toleration in religion and all that, but very little\nof it is there yet in the world; take my experience for that. Ninety-nine\nper cent do not even think of it. There is tremendous religious persecution\nyet in every country in which I have been, and the same old objections are\nraised against learning anything new. The little toleration that is in the\nworld, the little sympathy that is yet in the world for religious thought,\nis practically here in the land of the Aryan, and nowhere else. It is here\nthat Indians build temples for Mohammedans and Christians; nowhere else. If\nyou go to other countries and ask Mohammedans or people of other religions\nto build a temple for you, see how they will help. They will instead try to\nbreak  down your temple and you too if they can. The one great lesson,\ntherefore, that the world wants most, that the world has yet to learn from\nIndia, is the idea not only of toleration, but of sympathy. Well has it been\nsaid in the Mahimnah-stotra: \"As the different rivers, taking their start\nfrom different mountains, running straight or crooked, at last come unto the\nocean, so, O Shiva, the different paths which men take through different\ntendencies, various though they appear, crooked or straight, all lead unto\nThese.\" Though they may take various roads, all are on the ways. Some may\nrun a little crooked, others may run straight, but at last they will all\ncome unto the Lord, the One. Then and then alone, is your Bhakti of Shiva\ncomplete when you not only see Him in the Linga, but you see Him everywhere.\nHe is the sage, he is the lover of Hari who sees Hari in everything and in\neveryone. If you are a real lover of Shiva, you must see Him in everything\nand in everyone. You must see that every worship is given unto Him whatever\nmay be the name or the form; that all knees bending towards the Caaba, or\nkneeling in a Christian church, or in a Buddhist temple are kneeling to Him\nwhether they know it or not, whether they are conscious of it or not; that\nin whatever name or form they are offered, all these flowers are laid at His\nfeet; for He is the one Lord of all, the one Soul of all souls. He knows\ninfinitely better what this world wants than you or I. It is impossible that\nall difference can cease; it must exist; without variation life must cease.\nIt is this clash, the differentiation of thought that makes for light, for\nmotion, for everything. Differentiation, infinitely contradictory, must\nremain, but it is not necessary that we should hate each other therefore; it\nis not necessary therefore that we should fight each other.\n", "\nTherefore we have again to learn the one central truth that was preached\nonly here in our Motherland, and that has to be preached once more from\nIndia. Why? Because not only is it in our books, but it runs through every\nphase of our national literature and is in the national life. Here and here\nalone is it practiced every day, and any man whose eyes are open can see\nthat it is practiced here and here alone. Thus we have to teach religion.\nThere are other and higher lessons that India can teach, but they are only\nfor  the  learned.  The  lessons of mildness, gentleness, forbearance,\ntoleration, sympathy, and brotherhood, everyone may learn, whether man,\nwoman, or child, learned or unlearned, without respect of race, caste, or\ncreed. \"They call Thee by various names; Thou art One.\"\n"]
