["\n", "CXXX\n", "(Translated from Bengali)\n", "To Swami Brahmananda\n", "SRINAGAR,\n", "17th July, 1898.\n", "MY DEAR RAKHAL,\n", "I got all the news from your letter. . . . My opinion regarding what you\nhave written about Sarada is only that it is difficult to make a magazine in\nBengali paying; but if all of you together canvass subscribers from door to\ndoor, it may be possible. In this matter do as you all decide. Poor Sarada\nhas already been disappointed once. What harm is there if we lose a thousand\nrupees by supporting such an unselfish and very hardworking person? What\nabout the printing of Raja-Yoga? As a last resort, you may give it to Upen\non certain terms of sharing the profit in the sales. . . . About money\nmatters, the advice given previously is final. Henceforward do what you\nconsider best regarding expenditure and other things. I see very well that\nmy policy is wrong, and yours is correct, regarding helping others; that is\nto say, if you help with money too much at a time, people instead of feeling\ngrateful remark on the contrary that hey have got a simpleton to bank upon.\nI  always  lost  sight of the demoralising influence of charity on the\nreceiver. Secondly, we have no right to deviate even slightly from the\npurposes for which we collect the donations. Mrs. Bull will get her rosary\nall right if you send it care of Chief Justice Rishibar Mukhopadhyaya,\nKashmir. Mr. Mitra and the Chief Justice are taking every care of them. We\ncould not get a plot of ground in Kashmir yet, but there is a chance that we\nshall do so soon. If you can spend a winter here, you are sure to recoup\nyour health. If the house is a good one and if you have enough fuel and warm\nclothing, then life in a land of snow is nothing but enjoyable. Also for\nstomach troubles a cold climate is an unfailing remedy. Bring Yogen with\nyou; for the earth here is not stony, it is clay like that of Bengal.\n", "If the paper is brought out in Almora, the work will progress much; for poor\nSevier will have something to do, and the local people also will get some\nwork. Skilful management lies in giving every man work after his own heart.\nBy all the means in our power the Nivedita Girls' School in Calcutta should\nbe put on a firm footing. To bring Master Mahashay to Kashmir is still a far\ncry, for it will be long before a college is established here. But he has\nwritten that it is possible to start a college in Calcutta, with him as the\nprincipal, at an initial expense of a thousand rupees. I hear that you all\nalso favour this proposal. In this matter do what you all consider best. My\nhealth is all right. I have to get up seldom at night, even though I take\ntwice a day rice and potatoes, sugar, or whatever I get. Medicine is useless\n\u2014 it has no action on the system of a Knower of Brahman! Everything will be\ndigested \u2014 don't be afraid.\n", "The ladies are doing well, and they send you their greetings. Two letters\nfrom Shivananda have come. I have also received a letter from his Australian\ndisciple. I hear that the outbreak of plague in Calcutta has completely\nsubsided.\n", "Yours affectionately,\n", "\nVIVEKANANDA.\n"]
