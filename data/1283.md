The priests had the people on their side because\nthey had all the utility which appealed to the popular mind. The kings had\nall the spirituality and none of the economic element; but as they were\npowerful and the rulers of the nation, the struggle was a hard and bitter\none. The kings gradually gained a little ground, but their ideas were too\nelevated for the masses, so the ceremonial, or work portion, always had the\nmass of the people.\n", "Always remember this, that whenever a religious system gains ground with the\npeople at large, it has a strong economic side to it. It is the economic\nside of a religion that finds lodgement with the people at large, and never\nits spiritual, or philosophic, side. If you should preach the grandest\nphilosophy  in the streets for a year, you would not have a handful of\nfollowers. According to\nthe orthodox Hindus, the Vedas are not the written words at all, but they\nconsist of the words themselves orally spoken with the exact enunciation and\nintonation. This vast mass of religion has been written and consists of\nthousands  upon  thousands  of  volumes.  Anyone who knows the precise\npronunciation and intonation knows the Vedas, and no one else. In ancient\ntimes certain royal families were the custodians of certain parts of the\nVedas. The head of the family could repeat every word of every volume he\nhad,  without  missing  a  word  or an intonation. These men had giant\nintellects, wonderful memories.\n", "The  strictly orthodox believers in the Vedas, the Karmakanda, did not\nbelieve in God, the soul or anything of the sort, but that we as we are were\nthe only beings in the universe, material or spiritual. When they were asked\nwhat the many allusions to God in the Vedas mean, they say that they mean\nnothing at all; that the words properly articulated have a magical power, a\npower to create certain results. Aside from that they have no meaning.\n", "Whenever you suppress a thought, you simply press it down out of sight in a\ncoil, like a spring, only to spring out again at a moment's notice with all\nthe pent up force as the result of the suppression, and do in a few moments\nwhat it would have done in a much longer period.\n", "Every ounce of pleasure brings its pound of pain. It is the same energy that\nat one time manifests itself as pleasure and at another time as pain. As\nsoon as one set of sensations stops, another begins.
