["\n", "LXVIII\n", "\n", "\nDear Alasinga,\n", "\nI have returned about three weeks from Switzerland but could not write you\nfurther before. I have sent you by last mail a paper on Paul Deussen of\nKiel. Sturdy's plan about the magazine is still hanging fire. As you see, I\nhalve  left  the St. George's Road place. We have a lecture hall at 39\nVictoria Street. C/o E. T. Sturdy will always reach me for a year to come.\nThe rooms at Grey Coat Gardens are only lodgings for self and the other\nSwami taken for three mouths only. The work in London is growing apace, the\nclasses are becoming bigger as they go on. I have no doubt this will go on\nincreasing at this rate and the English people are steady and loyal. Of\ncourse, as soon as I leave, most of this fabric will tumble down. Something\nwill happen. Some strong man will arise to take it up. The Lord knows what\nis good. In America there is room for twenty preachers on the Vedanta and\nYoga. Where to get these preachers and where also the money to bring them?\nHalf the United States can be conquered in ten years, given a number of\nstrong and genuine men. Where are they? We are all boobies over there!\nSelfish cowards, with our nonsense of lip-patriotism, orthodoxy, and boasted\nreligious feeling! The Madrasis have more of go and steadiness, but every\nfool is married. Marriage! Marriage! Marriage! . . . Then the way our boys\nare married nowadays! . . . It is very good to aspire to be a nonattached\nhouseholder;  but  what  we  want in Madras is not that just now \u2014 but\nnon-marriage. . . .\n", "\nMy child, what I want is muscles of iron and nerves of steel, inside which\ndwells a mind of the same material as that of which the thunderbolt is made.\nStrength, manhood, Kshatra-Virya + Brahma-Teja. Our beautiful hopeful boys\n\u2014 they have everything, only if they are not slaughtered by the millions at\nthe altar of this brutality they call marriage. O Lord, hear my wails!\nMadras will then awake when at least one hundred of its very heart's blood,\nin the form of its educated young men, will stand aside from the world, gird\ntheir loins, and be ready to fight the battle of truth, marching on from\ncountry to country. One blow struck outside of India is equal to a hundred\nthousand struck within. Well, all will come if the Lord wills it.\n", "\nMiss M\u00fcller was the person who offered that money I promised. I have told\nher about your new proposal. She is thinking about it. In the meanwhile I\nthink it is better to give her some work. She has consented to be the agent\nfor the Brahmavadin and Awakened India. Will you write to her about it? Her\naddress is Airlie Lodge, Ridgeway Gardens, Wimbledon, England. I was living\nwith her over there for the last few weeks. But the London work cannot go on\nwithout my living in London. As such I have changed quarters. I am sorry it\nhas  chagrined  Miss  M\u00fcller a bit. Cannot help. Her full name is Miss\nHenrietta M\u00fcller. Max M\u00fcller is getting very friendly. I am soon going to\ndeliver two lectures at Oxford.\n", "\nI  am  busy writing something big on the Vedanta philosophy. I am busy\ncollecting passages from the various Vedas bearing on the Vedanta in its\nthreefold aspect. You can help me by getting someone to collect passages\nbearing on, first the Advaitic idea, then the Vishisht\u00e2dvaitic, and the\nDvaitic from the Samhit\u00e2s, the Br\u00e2hmanas, the Upanishads, and the Pur\u00e2nas.\nThey should be classified and very legibly written with the name and chapter\nof the book, in each case. It would be a pity to leave the West without\nleaving something of the philosophy in book form.\n", "\nThere was a book published in Mysore in Tamil characters, comprising all the\none hundred and eight Upanishads; I saw it in Professor Deussen's library.\nIs there a reprint of the same in Devan\u00e2gari? If so, send me a copy. If not,\nsend me the Tamil edition, and also write on a sheet the Tamil letters and\ncompounds, and all juxtaposed with its Nagari equivalents, so that I may\nlearn the Tamil letters.\n", "\nMr. Satyanathan, whom I met in London the other day, said that there has\nbeen a friendly review of my R\u00e2ja-Yoga book in the Madras Mail, the chief\nAnglo-Indian paper in Madras. The leading physiologist in America, I hear,\nhas been charmed with my speculations. At the same time, there have been\nsome in England, who ridiculed my ideas. Good! My speculations of course are\nawfully bold; a good deal of them will ever remain meaningless; but there\nare  hints  in it which the physiologists had better taken up earlier.\nNevertheless, I am quite satisfied with the result. \"Let them talk badly of\nme if they please, but let them talk\", is my motto.\n", "\nIn England, of course, they are gentlemen and never talk the rot I had in\nAmerica. Then again the English missionaries you see over there are nearly\nall of them from the dissenters. They are not from the gentleman class in\nEngland. The gentlemen here, who are religious; all belong to the English\nChurch. The dissenters have very little voice in England and no education. I\nnever hear of those people here against whom you time to time warn me. They\nare unknown here and dare not talk nonsense. I hope Ram K. Naidu is already\nin Madras, and you are enjoying good health.\n", "\nPersevere on, my brave lads. We have only just begun. Never despond! Never\nsay enough! . . . As soon as a man comes over to the West and sees different\nnations, his eyes open. This way I get strong workers \u2014 not by talking, but\nby practically showing what we have in India and what we have not. I wish at\nleast that a million Hindus had travelled all over the world!\n", "\n", "Yours ever with love,\nVivekananda.\n", "\n"]
