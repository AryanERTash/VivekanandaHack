["\n", "LXXII\n", "\n", "\nDear Alasinga,\n\n", "\nI am leaving England on the 16th of December for Italy, and shall catch the\nNorth German Lloyd S. S. Prinz Regent Luitpold at Naples. The steamer is due\nat Colombo on the 14th of January next.\n", "\nI intend to see a little of Ceylon, and shall then go to Madras. I am being\naccompanied  by  three English friends \u2014 Capt. and Mrs. Sevier and Mr.\nGoodwin. Mr. Sever and his wife are going to start a place near Almora in\nthe Himalayas which I intend to make my Himalayan Centre, as well as a place\nfor Western disciples to live as Brahmach\u00e2rins and Sanny\u00e2sins. Goodwin is an\nunmarried young man who is going to travel and live with me, he is like a\nSannyasin.\n", "\nI am very desirous to reach Calcutta before the birthday festival of Shri\nRamakrishna. . . . My present plan of work is to start two centres, one in\nCalcutta and the other in Madras, in which to train up young preachers. I\nhave funds enough to start the one in Calcutta, which being the scene of\nShri Ramakrishna's life-work, demands my first attention. As for the Madras\none, I expect to get funds in India.\n", "\nWe will begin work with these three centres; and later on, we will get to\nBombay and Allahabad. And from these points, if the Lord is pleased, we will\ninvade not only India, but send over bands of preachers to every country in\nthe world. That should be our first duty. Work on with a heart. 39 Victoria\nwill be the London headquarters for some time to come, as the work will be\ncarried on there. Sturdy had a big box of Brahmav\u00e2din I did not know before.\nHe is now canvassing subscribers for it.\n", "\nNow we have got one Indian magazine in English fixed. We can start some in\nthe vernaculars also. Miss M. Noble of Wimbledon is a great worker. She will\nalso canvass for both the Madras papers. She will write you. These things\nwill grow slowly but surely. Papers of this kind are supported by a small\ncircle of followers. Now they cannot be expected to do too many things at a\ntime \u2014 they have to buy the books, find the money for the work in England,\nsubscribers for the paper here, and then subscribe to Indian papers. It is\ntoo much. It is more like trading than teaching. Therefore you must wait,\nand yet I am sure there will be a few subscribers here. Again, there must be\nwork for the people here to do when I am gone, else the whole thing will go\nto pieces. Therefore there must be a paper here, so also in America by and\nby. The Indian papers are to be supported by the Indians. To make a paper\nequally acceptable to all nationalities means a staff of writers from all\nnations; and that means at least a hundred thousand rupees a year.\n", "\nYou must not forget that my interests are international and not Indian\nalone. I am in good health; so is Abhedananda.\n", "\n", "With all love and blessings,\nVivekananda.\n", "\n"]
