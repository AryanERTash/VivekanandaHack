["\n", "LXIX\n", "\n", "\nDear Alasinga,\n", "\n. . . I am not yet sure what month I shall reach India. I will write later\nabout it. The new Swami[1] delivered his maiden speech\nyesterday at a friendly society's meeting. It was good and I liked it; he\nhas the making of a good speaker in him, I am sure.\n", "\n. . . You have not yet brought out the \u2014 . . Again, books must be cheap for\nIndia to have a large sale; the types must be bigger to satisfy the public.\n. . . You can very well get out a cheap edition of  \u2014 if you like. I have\nnot  reserved  any  copyright  on it purposely. You have missed a good\nopportunity by not getting out the \u2014 book earlier, but we Hindus are so slow\nthat when we have done a work, the opportunity has already passed away, and\nthus we are the losers. Your \u2014 book came out after a year's talk! Did you\nthink the Western people would wait for it till Doomsday? You have lost\nthree-fourths of the sale by this delay. . . . That Haramohan is a fool,\nslower  than  you,  and his printing is diabolical. There is no use in\npublishing books that way; it is cheating the public, and should not be\ndone. I shall most probably return to India accompanied by Mr. and Mrs.\nSevier, Miss M\u00fcller, and Mr. Goodwin. Mr. and Mrs. Sevier are probably going\nto settle in Almora at least for some time, and Goodwin is going to become a\nSanny\u00e2sin. He of course will travel with me. It is he to whom we owe all our\nbooks. He took shorthand notes of my lectures, which enabled the books to be\npublished.  . . . All these lectures were delivered on the spur of the\nmoment, without the least preparation, and as such, they should be carefully\nrevised and edited. . . .Goodwin will have to live with me. . . . He is a\nstrict vegetarian.\n", "Yours with love,\nVivekananda.\n", "\nPS. I have sent a little note to the Indian Mirror today about Dr. Barrows\nand how he should be welcomed. You also write some good words of welcome for\nhim in the Brahmavadin. All here send love.\n", "\n"]
