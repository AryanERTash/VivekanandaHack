Of\nthese the lower (knowledge) consists of the Rig-Veda, the Yajur-Veda, the\nS\u00e2ma-Veda,  the Atharva-Veda, the Shiksh\u00e2 (or the science dealing with\npronunciation and accent), the Kalpa (or the sacrificial liturgy), grammar,\nthe  Nirukta (or the science dealing with etymology and the meaning of\nwords), prosody, and astronomy; and the higher (knowledge) is that by which\nthat Unchangeable is known.\"\n", "\nThe  higher knowledge is thus clearly shown to be the knowledge of the\nBrahman; and the Devi-Bh\u00e2gavata gives us the following definition of the\nhigher love (Para-Bhakti): \"As oil poured from one vessel to another falls\nin an unbroken line, so, when the mind in an unbroken stream thinks of the\nLord, we have what is called Para-Bhakti or supreme love.\" This kind of\nundisturbed and ever-steady direction of the mind and the heart to the Lord\nwith an inseparable attachment is indeed the highest manifestation of man's\nlove  to  God.  All  other forms of Bhakti are only preparatory to the\nattainment of this highest form thereof, viz the Para-Bhakti which is also\nknown as the love that comes after attachment (R\u00e2g\u00e2nug\u00e2). When this supreme\nlove once comes into the heart of man, his mind will continuously think of\nGod and remember nothing else. He will give no room in himself to thoughts\nother than those of God, and his soul will be unconquerably pure and will\nalone break all the bonds of mind and matter and become serenely free. He\nalone can worship the Lord in his own heart; to him forms, symbols, books,\nand doctrines are all unnecessary and are incapable of proving serviceable\nin any way. It is not easy to love the Lord thus. Ordinarily human love is\nseen to flourish only in places where it is returned; where love is not\nreturned for love, cold indifference is the natural result. There are,\nhowever, rare instances in which we may notice love exhibiting itself even\nwhere there is no return of love. We may compare this kind of love, far\npurposes of illustration, to the love of the moth for the fire; the insect\nloves the fire, falls into it, and dies. It is indeed in the nature of this\ninsect to love so. To love because it is the nature of love to love is\nundeniably the highest and the most unselfish manifestation of love that may\nbe  seen  in  the world. Such love, working itself out on the plane of\nspirituality, necessarily leads to the attainment of Para-Bhakti.\n"]
