["\n", "CXLVII\n", "To Mrs. G. W. Hale\n", "[RIDGELY MANOR], NEW YORK, N.Y.\n", "23 October 1899\n", "MY DEAR MOTHER,\n", "I was taking a few days' complete rest and so am late in replying to your\nvery  kind  note. Accept my congratulations on the anniversary of your\nmarriage. I pray many, many such returns may come to you.\n", "I am sure my previous letter was coloured by the state of my body, as indeed\nis  the whole of existence to us. Yet, Mother, there is more pain than\npleasure in life. If not, why do I remember you and your children almost\nevery  day of my life, and not many others? Happiness is liked so much\nbecause  it  is  so rare, is it not? Fifty percent of our life is mere\nlethargy, ennui; of the rest, forty percent is pain, only ten happiness \u2014\nand this for the exceptionally fortunate. We are oft-times mixing up this\nstate of ennui with pleasure. It is rather a negative state, whilst both\npleasure and pain are nearer positive, though not positive.\n", "Pleasure and pain are both feeling, not willing. They are only processes\nwhich convey to the mind excitements or motives of action. The real positive\naction is the willing, or impulse to work, of the mind \u2014 begun when the\nsensation has been taken in (pleasure and pain); thus the real is neither\npleasure nor pain. It has no connection with either. Quite different from\neither. The barking of the dog awakens his master to guard against a thief\nor receive his dearest friend. It does not follow, therefore, that the dog\nand his master are of the same nature or have any degree of kinship. The\nfeelings of pleasure or pain similarly awaken the soul to activity, without\nany kinship at all.\n", "The soul is beyond pain, beyond pleasure, sufficient in its own nature. And\nno hell can punish it, nor any heaven can bless it. So far philosophy.\n", "I am coming soon to Chicago, and hope to say \"Lord bless you\" to you and\nyour children. All love as usual to my Christian relatives, scientific or\nquacks.\n", "VIVEKANANDA\n"]
