You are\nperfectly correct in saying that we will have to learn many things from\nJapan. The help that Japan will give us will be with great sympathy and\nrespect, whereas that from the West unsympathetic and destructive. Certainly\nit is very desirable to establish a connection between India and Japan.\n", "\nAs for me, I was thrown hors de combat in Assam. The climate of the Math is\njust reviving me a bit. At Shillong \u2014 the hill sanatorium of Assam \u2014 I had\nfever, asthma, increase of albumen, and my body swelled to almost twice ills\nnormal size. These symptoms subsided, however, as soon as I reached the\nMath. It is dreadfully hot this year; but a bit of rain has commenced, and I\nhope we will soon have the monsoon in full force. I have no plans just now,\nexcept that the Bombay Presidency wants me so badly that I think of going\nthere soon. We are thinking of starting touring through Bombay in a week or\nso.\n", "\nThe 300 dollars you speak of sent by Lady Betty have not reached me yet, nor\nhave I any intimation of its arrival from General Patterson.\n", "\nHe, poor man, was rather miserable, after his wife and children sailed for\nEurope and asked me to come and see him, but unfortunately I was so ill, and\nam so afraid of going into the City that I must wait till the rains have set\nin.\n", "\nNow, Joe dear, if I am to go to Japan, this time it is necessary that I take\nSaradananda with me to carry on the work. Also I must have the promised\nletter to Li Huang Chang from Mr. Maxim; but Mother knows the rest. I am\nstill undecided.\n", "\nSo you went to Alanquinan to see the foreteller? Did he convince you of his\npowers? What did he say? Write particular s\u2019il vous plait.\n", "\nJules Bois went as far as Lahore, being prevented from entering Nepal. I\nlearn from the papers that he could not bear the heat and fell ill; then he\ntook ship et bon voyage. He did not write me a single line since we met in\nthe Math. You also are determined to drag Mrs. Bull down to Japan from\nNorway all the way \u2014 \nbien, Mademoiselle, vous \u00eates use puissante magicienne, sans doute.[2]  Well,\nJoe, keep health and spirits up; the Alanquinan man's words come out true\nmost of them; and glorie et honneur await you \u2014 and Mukti. The natural\nambition of woman is through marriage to climb up, leaning upon a man; but\nthose days are gone. You shall be great without the help of any man, just as\nyou are, plain, dear Joe \u2014 our Joe, everlasting Joe. . . .\n", "\nWe have seen enough of this life to care for any of its bubbles have we not\nJoe?  For  months I have been practicing to drive away all sentiments;\ntherefore I stop here, and good-bye just now. It is ordained by Mother we\nwork together; it has been already for the good of many; it shall be for the\ngood  of many more; so let it be. It is useless planning, useless high\nflights; Mother will find Her own way; . . . rest assured.\n", "\n", "Ever yours with love and heart's blessings,\nVivekananda.\n", "\nPS.
