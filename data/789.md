["\n", "XLIV\n", "C/O F. H. LEGGETT,\n", "21 WEST THIRTY-FOURTH STREET\n", "NEW YORK\n", "Nov., 1899.\n", "MY DEAR STURDY,\n", "This is not to defend my conduct. Words cannot wipe off the evils I have\ndone, nor any censor stop from working the good deeds, if any.\n", "For the last few months I have been hearing so much of the luxuries I was\ngiven to enjoy by the people of the West \u2014 luxuries which the hypocrite\nmyself has been enjoying, although preaching renunciation all the while:\nluxuries, the enjoyment of which has been the great stumbling-block in my\nway, in England at least. I nearly hypnotised myself into the belief that\nthere has at least been a little oasis in the dreary desert of my life, a\nlittle spot of light in one whole life of misery and gloom; one moment of\nrelaxation in a life of hard work and harder curses \u2014 even that oasis, that\nspot, that moment was only one of sense-enjoyment!!\n", "I was glad, I blessed a hundred times a day those that had helped me to get\nit, when, lo, your last letter comes like a thunderclap, and the dream is\nvanished. I begin to disbelieve your criticisms \u2014 have little faith left in\nall this talk of luxuries and enjoyments and other visions memory calls up.\nThese I state. Hope you will send it round to friends, if you think fit, and\ncorrect me where I am wrong.\n", "I remember your place at Reading, where I was fed with boiled cabbage and\npotatoes and boiled rice and boiled lentils, three times a day, with your\nwife's curses for sauce all the time. I do not remember your giving me any\ncigar  to  smoke \u2014 shilling or penny ones. Nor do I remember myself as\ncomplaining of either the food or your wife's incessant curses, though I\nlived as a thief, shaking through fear all the time, and working every day\nfor you.\n", "The next memory is of the house on St. George's Road \u2014 you and Miss Muller\nat the head. My poor brother was ill there and Miss M\u00fcller drove him away.\nThere too I don't remember to have had any luxuries as to food or drink or\nbed or even the room given to me.\n", "The next was Miss M\u00fcller's place. Though she has been very kind to me, I was\nliving on nuts and fruits. The next memory is that of the black hole of\nLondon where I had to work almost day and night and cook the meals oft-times\nfor five or six, and most nights with a bite of bread and butter.\n", "I remember Mrs. Sturdy giving me a dinner and a night's lodging in her\nplace, and then the next day criticising the black savage \u2014 so dirty and\nsmoking all over the house.\n", "With the exception of Capt. and Mrs. Sevier, I do not remember even one\npiece of rag as big as a handkerchief I got from England. On the other hand,\nthe incessant demand on my body and mind in England is the cause of my\nbreakdown in health. This was all you English people gave me, whilst working\nme to death; and now I am cursed for the luxuries I lived in!! Whosoever of\nyou have given me a coat? Whosoever a cigar? Whosoever a bit of fish or\nflesh? Whosoever of you dare say I asked food or drink or smoke or dress or\nmoney from you? Ask, Sturdy, ask for God's sake, ask your friends, and first\nask your own \"God within who never sleeps.\"\n", "You have given me money for my work. Every penny of it is there. Before your\neyes I sent my brother away, perhaps to his death; and I would not give him\na farthing of the money which was not my private property.\n", "On the other hand, I remember in England Capt. and Mrs. Sevier, who have\nclad me when I was cold, nursed me better than my own mother would have,\nborne with me in my weakness, my trials; and they have nothing but blessings\nfor me. And that Mrs. Sevier, because she did not care for honours, has the\nworship of thousands today; and when she is dead millions will remember her\nas one of the great benefactresses of the poor Indians. And they never\ncursed me for my luxuries, though they are ready to give me luxuries, if I\nneed or wish.\n", "I need not tell you of Mrs. Bull, Miss MacLeod, Mr. and Mrs. Leggett. You\nknow their love and kindness for me; and Mrs. Bull and Miss MacLeod have\nbeen to our country, moved and lived with us as no foreigner ever did,\nroughing it all, and they do not ever curse me and my luxuries either; they\nwill be only too glad to have me eat well and smoke dollar cigars if I wish.\nAnd there Leggetts and Bulls were the people whose bread whose money bought\nmy smokes and several times paid my rent, whilst I was killing myself for\nyour people, when you were taking my pound of flesh for the dirty hole and\nstarvation and reserving all this accusation of luxury.\n", "\"The clouds of autumn make great noise but send no rain;\nThe clouds of the rainy season without a word flood the earth.\"\n", "See Sturdy, those that have helped or are still helping have no criticism,\nno curses: it is only those who do nothing, who only come to grind their own\naxes, that curse, that criticise. That such worthless, heartless, selfish,\nrubbish criticise, is the greatest blessing that can come to me. I want\nnothing so much in life as to be miles off from these extremely selfish\naxe-grinders.\n", "Talking of luxuries! Take these critics up one after the other \u2014 It is all\nflesh, all flesh and no spirit anywhere. Thank God, they come out sooner or\nlater in their true colours. And you advise me to regulate my conduct, my\nwork, according to the desires of such heartless, selfish persons, and are\nat your wit's end because I do not!\n", "As to my Gurubhais (brother-disciples), they do nothing but what I insist on\ntheir doing. I don't, by all that is good and true I don't,\nwant them starved and worked and cursed for all their pains.\n", "A word more. I shall be very glad if you can point out to me where I have\npreached torturing the flesh. As for the Sh\u00e2stras (scriptures), I shall be\nonly too glad if a Sh\u00e2stri (Pundit) dares oppose us with the rules of life\nlaid down for Sannyasins and Paramahamsas.\n", "Well, Sturdy, my heart aches. That India still lives, Sturdy, India of\nundying love, of everlasting faithfulness, the unchangeable, not only in\nmanners and customs, but also in love, in faith, in friendship. And I, the\nleast of that India's children, love you, Sturdy, with Indian love, and\nwould any day give up a thousand bodies to help you out of this delusion.\n", "Ever yours,\n", "VIVEKANANDA.\n"]
