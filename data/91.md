["\n", "THE RELIGIONS OF INDIA\n", "(Boston Herald, May 17, 1894)\n", "\nThe  Brahmin  monk, Swami Vivekananda, lectured yesterday afternoon in\nAssociation Hall on \"The Religions of India\", in aid of the Ward 16 Day\nNursery. There was a large attendance.\n", "\nThe speaker first gave an account of the Mahommedans, who formed, he said,\none-fifth of the population. They believed in both Old and New Testaments,\nbut  Jesus  Christ they regarded only as a prophet. They had no church\norganization, though there was reading of the Koran.\n", "\nThe Parsees, another race, called their sacred book the Zend-Avesta, and\nbelieved in two warring deities, Armuzd the good and Ahriman the evil. They\nbelieved that finally the good would triumph over the evil. Their moral code\nwas summed up in the words: \"Good thought, good words, good deeds.\"\n", "\nThe Hindus proper looked up to the Vedas as their religious scripture. They\nheld each individual to the customs of caste, but gave him full liberty to\nthink for himself in religious matters. A part of their method was to seek\nout some holy man or prophet in order to take advantage of the spiritual\ncurrent that flowed through him.\n", "\nThe Hindus had three different schools of religion \u2014 the dualistic, the\nqualified monistic and the monistic \u2014 and these three were regarded as\nstages through which each individual naturally passed in the course of his\nreligious development.\n", "\nAll three believed in God, but the dualistic school believed that God and\nman were separate entities, while the monistic declared that there was only\none existence in the universe, this unitary existence teeing neither God nor\nsoul, but something beyond.\n", "\nThe  lecturer quoted from the Vedas to show the character of the Hindu\nreligion, and declared that, to find God, one must search one's own heart.\n", "\nReligion did not consist of pamphlets or books; it consisted of looking into\nthe  human heart, and finding there the truths of God and immortality.\n\"Whomsoever I like,\" said the Vedas, \"him I create a prophet,\" and to be a\nprophet was all there was of religion.\n", "\nThe speaker brought his lecture to a close by giving an account of the\nJains, who show remarkable kindness to dumb animals, and whose moral law is\nsummed up in the words: \"Not to injure others is the highest good.\"\n"]
