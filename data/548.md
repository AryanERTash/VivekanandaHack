["\n", "LXXXVII\n", "\n", "R.M.S. \"Britannic\",\nThursday morning, Dec. 5, 1895.\n", "DEAR ALBERTA,\n", "Received your nice letter last evening. Very kind of you to remember me. I\nam going soon to see the \"Heavenly Pair\". Mr. Leggett is a saint as I have\ntold you already, and your mother is a born empress, every inch of her, with\na saint's heart inside.\n", "I am so glad you are enjoying the Alps so much They must be wonderful. It is\nalways in such places that the human soul aspires for freedom. Even if the\nnation is spiritually poor, it aspires for physical freedom. I met a young\nSwiss in London. He used to come to my classes. I was very successful in\nLondon,  and though I did not care for the noisy city, I was very much\npleased with the people. In your country, Alberta, the Vedantic thought was\nintroduced in the beginning by ignorant \"cranks\", and one has to work his\nway through the difficulties created by such introductions. You may have\nnoticed that only a few men or women of the upper classes ever joined my\nclasses in America. Again in America the upper classes being the rich, their\nwhole time is spent in enjoying their wealth and imitating (aping?) the\nEuropeans.  On  the other hand in England the Vedantic ideas have been\nintroduced by the most learned men in the country, and there are a large\nnumber among the upper classes in England who are very thoughtful. So you\nwill be astonished to hear that I found my grounds all prepared, and I am\nconvinced that my work will have more hold on England than America. Add to\nthis  the  tremendous tenacity of the English character, and judge for\nyourself.  By this you will find that I have changed a good deal of my\nopinion about England, and I am glad to confess it. I am perfectly sure that\nwe will do still better in Germany. I am coming back to England next summer.\nIn the meanwhile my work is in very able hands. Joe Joe has been the same\nkind good pure friend to me here as in America, and my debt to your family\nis simply immense. My love and blessings to Hollister and you. The steamer\nis standing at anchor on account of fog. The purser has very kindly given me\na whole cabin by myself. Every Hindu is a Raja, they think, and are very\npolite \u2014 and the charm will break, of course, when they find that the Raja\nis penniless!!\n", "Yours with love and blessings,\n", "\nVIVEKANANDA.\n"]
