["\n", "CLXIX\n", "To Miss Josephine MacLeod\n", "1719 TURK STREET,\n", "SAN FRANCISCO, CALIF.\n", "April, 1900.\n", "MY DEAR JOE,\n", "Just a line before you start for France. Are you going via England? I had a\nbeautiful letter from Mrs. Sevier in which I find that Miss M\u00fcller sent\nsimply  a  paper  without  any other words to Kali who was with her in\nDarjeeling.\n", "Congreave is the name of her nephew, and he is in the Transvaal war; that is\nthe reason she underlined that, to show her nephew fighting the Boers in\nTransvaal. That was all. I cannot understand it any more now than then, of\ncourse.\n", "I am physically worse than at Los Angeles, mentally much better, stronger,\nand peaceful. Hope it will continue to be so.\nI have not got a reply to my letter to you; I expect it soon.\n", "One Indian letter of mine was directed by mistake to Mrs. Wheeler; it came\nall right to me in the end. I had nice notes from Saradananda; they are\ndoing beautifully over there. The boys are working up; well, scolding has\nboth sides, you see; it makes them up and doing. We Indians have been so\ndependent for so long that it requires, I am sorry, a good lot of tongue to\nmake  them  active. One of the laziest fellows had taken charge of the\nanniversary this year and pulled it through. They have planned and are\nsuccessfully working famine works by themselves without my help. . . . All\nthis comes from the terrific scolding I have been giving, sure!\n", "They are standing on their own feet. I am so glad. See Joe, the Mother is\nworking.\n", "I sent Miss Thursby's letter to Mrs. Hearst. She sent me an invitation to\nher musical. I could not go. I had a bad cold. So that was all. Another lady\nfor whom I had a letter from Miss Thursby, an Oakland lady, did not reply. I\ndon't know whether I shall make enough in Frisco to pay my fare to Chicago!\nOakland work has been successful. I hope to get about $100 from Oakland,\nthat is all. After all, I am content. It is better that I tried. . . . Even\nthe magnetic healer had not anything for me. Well, things will go on anyhow\nfor  me; I do not care how. . . . I am very peaceful. I learn from Los\nAngeles, Mrs. Leggett has been bad again. I wired to New York to learn what\ntruth was in it. I will get a reply soon, I expect.\n", "Say, how will you arrange about my mail when the Leggetts are over on the\nother side? Will you so arrange that they reach me right?\n", "I have nothing more to say; all love and gratitude is yours; already you\nknow that. You have already done more than I ever deserved. I don't know\nwhether I go to Paris or not, but I must go to England sure in May. I must\nnot go home without trying England a few weeks more. With all love,\n", "Ever yours in the Lord,\n", "VIVEKANANDA.\n", "PS. Mrs. Hansborough and Mrs. Appenul have taken a flat for a month at 1719\nTurk Street. I am with them, and shall be a few weeks.\n", "V.\n"]
