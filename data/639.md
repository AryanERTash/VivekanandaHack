["\n", "X\n", "(Translated from Bengali)\n", "\n", "(From the Diary of a Disciple)\n", "(The disciple is Sharatchandra Chakravarty, who published his records in a\nBengali book, Swami-Shishya-Samv\u00e2da, in two parts. The present series of\n\"Conversations and Dialogues\" is a revised translation from this book. Five\ndialogues of this series have already appeared in the Complete Works,Volume 5)\n", "\n[Place: The Alambazar Math. Year: 1897.]\n", "\nAfter Swamiji's first return to Calcutta from the West, he always used to\nplace before the zealous young men who visited him the lofty ideals of\nrenunciation, and anyone expressing his desire of accepting Sannyasa would\nreceive from him great encouragement and kindness. So, inspired by his\nenthusiasm some young men of great good fortune gave up their worldly life\nin those days and became initiated by him into Sannyasa. The disciple was\npresent at the Alambazar Math the day the first four of this batch were\ngiven Sannyasa by Swamiji.\n", "Often has the disciple heard it from the Sannyasins of the Math that Swamiji\nwas repeatedly requested by his brother-monks not to admit one particular\ncandidate into Sannyasa, whereupon Swamiji replied: \"Ah, if even we shrink\nfrom  working  out  the  salvation of the sinful, the heavy-laden, the\nhumiliated, and the afflicted in soul, who else are to take care of them in\nthis  world? No, don't you please stand against me in this matter.\" So\nSwamiji's strong opinion triumphed, and always the refuge of the helpless,\nhe resolved out of his great love to give him Sannyasa.\n", "The  disciple had been staying at the Math for the last two days, when\nSwamiji  called him and said: \"Well, you belong to the priestly class;\ntomorrow you get them to perform their Shr\u00e2ddha, and the next day I shall\ngive  them  Sannyasa. So get yourself ready by consulting the books of\nceremonials today.\" The disciple bowed this mandate of Swamiji, and the\nceremony was duly gone through.\n", "But the disciple became very much depressed at the thought of the great\nsternness of Sannyasa. Swamiji detecting his mental agitation asked him,\n\"Well, I see, you feel some dread in your mind at all this experience, is it\nnot so?\" And when the disciple confessed it to be so Swamiji said: \"From\nthis day these four are dead to the world, and new bodies, new thoughts, new\ngarments will be theirs from tomorrow \u2014 and shining in the glory of Brahman\nthey will live like flaming fire! ' [x_sharat_chakravarty_01.jpg] \u2014 Not by\nwork, nor by progeny, nor by wealth, but by renunciation alone some (rare\nones) attained Immortality' (Kaivalya Upanishad).\"\n", "After the ceremony, the four Brahmacharins bowed at the feet of Swamiji. He\nblessed them and said, \"You have the enthusiasm to embrace the loftiest vow\nof human life; blessed indeed is your birth, blessed your family, blessed\nthe mothers who held you in their womb! ' [x_sharat_chakravarty_02.jpg] \u2014\nThe whole family-line becomes hallowed, the mother achieves her highest!'\"\n", "That day after supper, Swamiji talked of the ideal of Sannyasa alone. To the\nzealous candidates for Sannyasa, he said: The real aim of Sannyasa is \"\n[x_sharat_chakravarty_03.jpg] \u2014 For one's highest freedom and for the good\nof  the world\". Without having Sannyasa none can really be a knower of\nBrahman \u2014 this is what the Vedas and the Vedanta proclaim. Don't listen to\nthe words of those who say, \"We shall both live the worldly life and be\nknowers  of  Brahman.\"  That  is  the  flattering  self-consolation of\ncryptopleasure-seekers.  He  who  has the slightest desire for worldly\npleasures, even a shred of some such craving, will feel frightened at the\nthought  of  the path you are going to tread; so, to give himself some\nconsolation he goes about preaching that impossible creed of harmonising\nBhoga and Ty\u00e2ga. That is all the raving of lunatics, the frothing of the\ndemented \u2014 idle theories contrary to the scriptures, contrary to the Vedas.\nNo freedom without renunciation. Highest love for God can never be achieved\nwithout    renunciation.    Renunciation    is    the    word    \u2014   \"\n\u0928\u093e\u0928\u094d\u092f: \u092a\u0928\u094d\u0925\u093e \u0935\u093f\u0926\u094d\u092f\u0924\u0947 \u0905\u092f\u0928\u093e\u092f  \u2014 There's no other way than this.\" Even the\nGita says, \" [x_sharat_chakravarty_05.jpg] \u2014 The sages know Sannyasa to be\nthe giving up of all work that has desire for its end.\"\n", "Nobody attains freedom without shaking off the coils of worldly worries. The\nvery fact that somebody lives the worldly life proves that he is tied down\nto it as the bond-slave of some craving or other. Why otherwise will he\ncling to that life at all? He is the slave either of lust or of gold, of\nposition or of fame, of learning or of scholarship. It is only after freeing\noneself from all this thraldom that one can get on along the way of freedom.\nLet people argue as loud as they please, I have got this conviction that\nunless all these bonds are given up, unless the monastic life is embraced,\nnone is going to be saved, no attainment of Brahmajn\u00e2na is possible.\n", "Disciple: Do you mean, sir, that merely taking up Sannyasa will lead one to\nthe goal?\n", "Swamiji: Whether the goal is attained or not is not the point before us now.\nBut until you get out of this wheel of Sams\u00e2ra, until the slavery of desire\nis shaken off, you can't attain either Bhakti or Mukti. To the knower of\nBrahman, supernatural powers or prosperity are mere trivialities.\n", "Disciple:  Sir,  is there any special time for Sannyasa, and are there\ndifferent kinds of it?\n", "Swamiji: There is no special time prescribed for a life of Sannyasa. The\nShruti  says: \" [x_sharat_chakravarty_06.jpg] \u2014 Directly the spirit of\nrenunciation comes, you should take to Sannyasa.\" The Yogav\u00e2sishtha also\nsays:\n", "[x_sharat_chakravarty_07.jpg]\n", "\u2014 \"Owing to life itself being frail and uncertain, one should be devoted to\nreligion even in one's youth. For who knows when one's body may fall off?\"\n", "The Sh\u00e2stras are found to speak of four kinds of Sannyasa: (1) Vidvat, (2)\nVividish\u00e2, (3) Markata, (4) \u00c2tura. The awakening of real renunciation all at\nonce and the consequent giving up of the world through Sannyasa is something\nthat  never  happens  unless there are strong Samsk\u00e2ras or tendencies,\ndeveloped from previous birth. And this is called the Vidvat Sannyasa.\nVividisha Sannyasa is the case of one who, out of a strong yearning for the\nknowledge of the Self through the pursuit of scriptural study and practice,\ngoes  to the man of realisation and from him embraces Sannyasa to give\nhimself up to those pursuits. Markata Sannyasa is the case of a man who is\ndriven out of the world by some of its chastisements such as the death of a\nrelative or the like and then takes to Sannyasa, though in such a case the\nrenouncing spirit does not endure long. Shri Ramakrishna used to say of it,\n\"With this kind of renunciation one hastens away to the up-country and then\nhappens to get hold of a nice job; and then eventually perhaps arranges to\nget his wife brought over to him or perhaps takes to a new one!\" And last,\nthere is another kind of Sannyasa which the Shastras prescribe for a man who\nis lying on his death-bed, the hope of whose life has been given up. For\nthen, if he dies, he dies with the holiest of vows upon him, and in his next\nbirth the merit of it will accrue to him. And in case he recovers, he shall\nnot go back to his old life again but live the rest of his days in the noble\nendeavour after Brahmajnana. Swami Shivananda gave this kind of Sannyasa to\nyour uncle. The poor man died; but through that initiation he will come to a\nnew birth of higher excellence. After all there is no other way to the\nknowledge of the Self but through Sannyasa.\n", "Disciple: What then, sir, will be the fate of the householders?\n", "Swamiji:  Why,  through  the merit of good Karma, they shall have this\nrenunciation in some future birth of theirs. And directly this renunciation\ncomes, there is an end of all troubles \u2014 with no further delay he gets\nacross  this  mystery of life and death. But then all rules have their\nexceptions. A few men, one or two, may be seen to attain the highest freedom\nby the true fulfilment of the householder's Dharma, as we have amongst us\nN\u00e2g Mah\u00e2shaya, for instance.\n", "Disciple:  Sir,  even  the  Upanishads etc. do not clearly teach about\nrenunciation and Sannyasa.\n", "Swamiji: You are talking like a madman! Renunciation is the very soul of the\nUpanishads. Illumination born of discriminative reflection is the ultimate\naim of Upanishadic knowledge. My belief, however, is that it was since the\ntime of Buddha that the monastic vow was preached more thoroughly all over\nIndia, and renunciation, the giving up of sense-enjoyment, was recognised as\nthe highest aim of religious life. And Hinduism has absorbed into itself\nthis  Buddhistic spirit of renunciation. Never was a great man of such\nrenunciation born in this world as Buddha.\n", "Disciple: Do you then mean, sir, that before Buddha's advent there was very\nlittle of the spirit of renunciation in the country, and there were hardly\nany Sannyasins at all?\n", "Swamiji:  Who  says  that? The monastic institution was there, but the\ngenerality of people did not recognise it as the goal of life; there was no\nsuch  staunch  spirit  for it, there was no such firmness in spiritual\ndiscrimination. So even when Buddha betook himself to so many Yogis and\nS\u00e2dhus, nowhere did he acquire the peace he wanted. And then to realise the\nHighest he fell back on his own exertions, and seated on a spot with the\nfamous words, \" [x_sharat_chakravarty_08.jpg] \u2014 Let my body wither away on\nthis seat\" etc., rose from it only after becoming the Buddha, the Illumined\nOne. The many monasteries that you now see in India occupied by monks were\nonce in the possession of Buddhism. The Hindus have only made them their own\nnow by modifying them in their own fashion. Really speaking, the institution\nof Sannyasa originated with Buddha; it was he who breathed life into the\ndead bones of this institution.\n", "Swami Ramakrishnananda, a brother-disciple of Swamiji, interposed, \"But the\nancient law-books and Puranas are good authority that all the four Ashramas\nhad existed in India before Buddha was born.\" Swamiji replied, \"Most of the\nPuranas, the codes of Manu and others, as well as much of the Mah\u00e2bh\u00e2rata\nform but recent literature. Bhagav\u00e2n Buddha was much earlier than all that.\"\n\"On that supposition,\" rejoined Swami Ramakrishnananda, \"discussions about\nBuddhism would be found in the Vedas, Upanishads, the law-books, Puranas,\nand the like. But since such discussions are not found in these ancient\nbooks, how can you say that Buddha antedated them all? In a few old Puranas,\nof course, accounts of the Buddhistic doctrine are partially given; but from\nthese, it can't be concluded that the scriptures of the Hindus such as the\nlaw-books and Puranas are of recent date.\"\n", "Swamiji: Please read history, (Evidently, during the argumentation, Swamiji\nwas  taking his stand on the conclusions of modern historical studies,\nthereby  giving  his encouragement and support to such new efforts and\nmethods. But we know from one of his letters to Swami Swarupananda (C.W.\nmodern scholars and worked out the pre-Buddhiscic origin of much of modern\nHinduism.) and you will find that Hinduism has become so great only by\nabsorbing all the ideas of Buddha.\n", "Swami Ramakrishnananda: It seems to me that Buddha has only left revivified\nthe great Hindu ideas, by thoroughly practicing in his life such principles\nas renunciation, non-attachment, and so on.\n", "Swamiji: But this position can't be proved. For we don't get any history\nbefore Buddha was born. If we accept history only as authority, we have to\nadmit that in the midst of the profound darkness of the ancient times,\nBuddha only shines forth as a figure radiant with the light of knowledge.\n", "Now the topic of Sannyasa was resumed and Swamiji said: Wheresoever might\nlie the origin of Sannyasa, the goal of human life is to become a knower of\nBrahman by embracing this vow of renunciation. The supreme end is to enter\nthe life of Sannyasa. They alone are blessed indeed who have broken off from\nworldly life through a spirit of renunciation.\n", "Disciple:  But many people are of opinion nowadays, sir, that with the\nincrease of wandering monks in the country, much harm has been done to its\nmaterial progress. They assert it on the ground that these monks idly roam\nabout depending on householders for their living, that these are of no help\nto the cause of social and national advancement.\n", "Swamiji: But will you explain to me first what is meant by the term material\nor secular advancement?\n", "Disciple: Yes, it is to do as people in the West are doing by securing the\nnecessaries of life through education, and promoting through science such\nobjects in life as commerce, industry, communications, and so on.\n", "Swamiji: But can all these be ever brought about, if real Rajas is not\nawakened  in man? Wandering all over India, nowhere I found this Rajas\nmanifesting itself. It is all Tamas and Tamas! The masses lie engulfed in\nTamas, and only among the monks could I find this Rajas and Sattva. These\npeople are like the backbone of the country. The real Sannyasin is a teacher\nof householders. It is with the light and teaching obtained from them that\nhouseholders  of old triumphed many a time in the battles of life. The\nhouseholders give food and clothing to the Sadhus, only in return for their\ninvaluable teachings. Had there been no such mutual exchange in India, her\npeople would have become extinct like the American Indians by this time. It\nis because the householders still give a few morsels of food to the Sadhus\nthat they are yet able to keep their foothold on the path of progress. The\nSannyasins are not idle. They are really the fountain-head of all activity.\nThe householders see lofty ideals carried into practice in the lives of the\nSadhus and accept from them such noble ideas; and this it is that has up\ntill now enabled them to fight their battle of life from the sphere of\nKarma. The example of holy Sadhus makes them work out holy ideas in life and\nimbibe real energy for work. The Sannyasins inspire the householders in all\nnoble causes by embodying in their lives the highest principle of giving up\neverything for the sake of God and the good of the world, and as a return\nthe householders give them a few doles of food. And the very disposition and\ncapacity to grow that food develops in the people because of the blessings\nand good wishes of the all-renouncing monks. It is because of their failure\nto understand the deeper issues that people blame the monastic institution.\nWhatever  may be the case in other countries, in this land the bark of\nhouseholders' life does not sink only because the Sannyasins are at its\nhelm.\n", "Disciple: But, sir, how many monks are to be found who are truly devoted to\nthe good of men?\n", "Swamiji: Ah, quite enough if one great Sannyasin like Shri Ramakrishna comes\nin a thousand years! For a thousand years after his advent, people may well\nguide themselves by those ideas and ideals he leaves behind. It is only\nbecause this monastic institution exists in the country that men of his\ngreatness  are  born here. There are defects, more or less, in all the\ninstitutions of life. But what is the reason that in spite of its faults,\nthis noble institution stands yet supreme over all the other institutions of\nlife? It is because the true Sannyasins forgo even their own liberation and\nlive simply for doing good to the world. If you don't feel grateful to such\na noble institution, lie on you again and again!\n", "While speaking these words, Swamiji's countenance became aglow. And before\nthe eyes of the disciple he shone as the very embodiment of Sannyasa.\n", "Then,  as  if  realising  deep  within  his soul the greatness of this\ninstitution, self-absorbed, he broke forth in sweetest symphony:\n", "[x_sharat_chakravarty_09.jpg]\n", "\u2014 \"Brooding blissful in mind over the texts of the Vedanta, quite contented\nwith food obtained as alms and wandering forth with a heart untouched by any\nfeeling  of  grief, thrice blessed are the Sannyasins, with only their\nloin-cloth for dress.\"\n", "Resuming the talk, he went on: For the good of the many, for the happiness\nof  the many is the Sannyasin born. His life is all vain, indeed, who,\nembracing Sannyasa, forgets this ideal. The Sannyasin, verily, is born into\nthis world to lay down his life for others, to stop the bitter cries of men,\nto wipe the tears of the widow, to bring peace to the soul of the bereaved\nmother, to equip the ignorant masses for the struggle for existence, to\naccomplish the secular and spiritual well-being of all through the diffusion\nof spiritual teachings and to arouse the sleeping lion of Brahman in all by\nthrowing in the light of knowledge. Addressing then his brothers of the\nOrder, he said: Our life is \" [x_sharat_chakravarty_03.jpg] \u2014 for the sake\nof our self-liberation as well as for the good of the world\". So what are\nyou sitting idle for? Arise, awake; wake up yourselves, and awaken others.\nAchieve the consummation of human life before you pass off \u2014 \"Arise, awake,\nand stop not till the goal is reached.\"\n"]
