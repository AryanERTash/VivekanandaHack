["\n", "THE CHIEF SYMBOLS\n", "\nThere are two Sanskrit words, Pratika and Pratim\u00e2. Pratika means coming\ntowards, nearing. In all countries you find various grades of worship. In\nthis country, for instance, there are people who worship images of saints,\nthere are people who worship certain forms and symbols. Then there are\npeople who worship different beings who are higher than men, and their\nnumber is increasing very rapidly \u2014 worshippers of departed spirits. I read\nthat there are something like eight millions of them here. Then there are\nother people who worship certain beings of higher grade \u2014 the angels, the\ngods, and so forth. Bhakti-Yoga does not condemn any one of these various\ngrades, but they are all classed under one name, Pratika. These people are\nnot worshipping God, but Pratika, something which is near, a step towards\nGod. This Pratika worship cannot lead us to salvation and freedom; it can\nonly  give us certain particular things for which we worship them. For\ninstance, if a man worships his departed ancestors or departed friends, he\nmay get certain powers or certain information from them. Any particular gift\nthat  is got from these objects of worship is called Vidy\u00e2, particular\nknowledge;  but freedom, the highest aim, comes only by worship of God\nHimself. Some Orientalists think, in expounding the Vedas, that even the\nPersonal God Himself is a Pratika. The Personal God may be a Pratika, but\nthe Pratikas are neither the Personal nor Impersonal God. They cannot be\nworshipped as God. So it would be a great mistake if people thought that by\nworshipping these different Pratikas, either as angels, or ancestors, or\nMah\u00e2tm\u00e2s (holy men, saints), etc., or departed spirits, they could ever\nreach to freedom. At best they can only reach to certain powers, but God\nalone can make us free. But because of that they are not to be condemned,\ntheir worship produces some result. The man who does not understand anything\nhigher may get some power, some enjoyment, by the worship of these Pratikas;\nand after a long course of experience, when he will be ready to come to\nfreedom, he will of his own accord give up the Pratikas.\n", "\nOf these various Pratikas the most prevalent form is the worship of departed\nfriends. Human nature \u2014 personal love, love for our friends \u2014 is so strong\nin us that when they die, we wish to see them once more \u2014 clinging on to\ntheir  forms.  We forget that these forms while living were constantly\nchanging, and when they die, we think they become constant, and that we\nshall see them so. Not only so, but if I have a friend or a son who has been\na scoundrel, as soon as he dies, I begin to think he is the saintliest\nperson in existence; he becomes a god. There are people in India who, if a\nbaby dies, do not burn it, but bury it and build a temple over it; and that\nlittle baby becomes the god of that temple. This is a very prevalent form of\nreligion in many countries, and there are not wanting philosophers who think\nthis has been the origin of all religions. Of course they cannot prove it.\nWe must remember, however, that this worship of Pratikas can never bring us\nto salvation or to freedom.\n", "\nSecondly,  it  is  very  dangerous. The danger is that these Pratikas,\n\"nearing-stages\", so far as they lead us on to a further stage, are all\nright; but the chances are ninety-nine to one that we shall stick to the\nPratikas all our lives. It is very good to be born in a church, but it is\nvery bad to die there. To make it clearer, it is very good to be born in a\ncertain sect and have its training \u2014 it brings out our higher qualities; but\nin the vast majority of cases we die in that little sect, we never come out\nor grow. That is the great danger of all these worships of Pratikas. One\nsays that these are all stages which one has to pass, but one never gets out\nof them; and when one becomes old, one still sticks to them. If a young man\ndoes not go to church, he ought to be condemned. But if an old man goes to\nchurch, he also ought to be condemned; he has no business with this child's\nplay  any  more;  the church should have been merely a preparation for\nsomething higher. What business has he any more with forms and Pratikas and\nall these preliminaries?\n", "\nBook worship is another strong form of this Pratika, the strongest form. You\nfind in every country that the book becomes the God. There are sects in my\ncountry  who believe that God incarnates and becomes man, but even God\nincarnate as man must conform to the Vedas, and if His teachings do not so\nconform, they will not take Him. Buddha is worshipped by the Hindus, but if\nyou say to them, \"If you worship Buddha, why don't you take His teachings?\"\nthey will say, because they, the Buddhists, deny the Vedas. Such is the\nmeaning of book worship. Any number of lies in the name of a religious book\nare all right. In India if I want to teach anything new, and simply state it\non my own authority, as what I think, nobody will come to listen to me; but\nif I take some passage from the Vedas, and juggle with it, and give it the\nmost impossible meaning, murder everything that is reasonable in it, and\nbring out my own ideas as the ideas that were meant by the Vedas, all the\nfools will follow me in a crowd. Then there are men preaching a sort of\nChristianity that would frighten the ordinary Christian out of his wits; but\nthey say, \"This is what Jesus Christ meant\", and many come round them.\nPeople do not want anything new, if it is not in the Vedas or the Bible It\nis  a  case of nerves: when you hear a new and striking thing, you are\nstartled;  or  when  you  see  a  new  thing,  you are startled; it is\nconstitutional. It is much more so with thoughts. The mind has been running\nin ruts, and to take up a new idea is too much of a strain; so the idea has\nto be put near the ruts, and then we slowly take it. It is a good policy,\nbut bad morality. Think of the mass of incongruities that reformers, and\nwhat you call the liberal preachers, pour into society today. According to\nChristian  Scientists,  Jesus  was  a  great  healer; according to the\nSpiritualists, He was a great psychic; according to the Theosophists, He was\na Mah\u00e2tm\u00e2. All these have to be deduced from the same text. There is a text\nin the Vedas which says, \"Existence (Sat) alone existed, O beloved, nothing\nelse existed in the beginning\". Many different meanings are given to the\nword Sat in this text. The Atomists say the word meant \"atoms\", and out of\nthese  atoms the world has been produced. The Naturalists say it meant\n\"nature\",  and  out  of  nature  everything has come. The Shunyav\u00e2dins\n(maintainers of the Void) say it meant \"nothing\", \"zero\", and out of nothing\neverything  has been produced. The Theists say it meant \"God\", and the\nAdvaitists say it was \"Absolute Existence\", and all refer to the same text\nas their authority.\n", "\nThese are the defects of book worship. But there is, on the other hand, a\ngreat  advantage  in  it:  it gives strength. All religious sects have\ndisappeared excepting those that have a book. Nothing seems to kill them.\nSome of you have heard of the Parsees. They were the ancient Persians, and\nat one time there were about a hundred millions of them. The majority of\nthem were conquered by the Arabs, and converted to Mohammedanism. A handful\nfled from their persecutors with their book, which is still preserving them.\nA book is the most tangible form of God. Think of the Jews; if they had not\nhad a book, they would have simply melted into the world. But that keeps\nthem up; the Talmud keeps them together, in spite of the most horrible\npersecution. One of the great advantages of a book is that it crystallises\neverything in tangible and convenient form, and is the handiest of all\nidols.  Just  put a book on an altar and everyone sees it; a good book\neveryone reads. I am afraid I may be considered partial. But, in my opinion\nbooks have produced more evil than good. They are accountable for many\nmischievous doctrines. Creeds all come from books, and books are alone\nresponsible for the persecution and fanaticism in the world. Books in modern\ntimes are making liars everywhere. I am astonished at the number of liars\nabroad in every country.\n", "\nThe next thing to be considered is the Pratima, or image, the use of images.\nAll over the world you will find images in some form or other. With some, it\nis in the form of a man, which is the best form. If I wanted to worship an\nimage I would rather have it in the form of a man than of an animal, or\nbuilding, or any other form. One sect thinks a certain form is the right\nsort of image, and another thinks it is bad. The Christian thinks that when\nGod came in the form of a dove it was all right, but if He comes in the form\nof a fish, as the Hindus say, it is very wrong and superstitious. The Jews\nthink if an idol be made in the form of a chest with two angels sitting on\nit, and a book on it, it is all right, but if it is in the form of a man or\na woman, it is awful. The Mohammedans think that when they pray, if they try\nto form a mental image of the temple with the Caaba, the black stone in it,\nand turn towards the west, it is all right, but if you form the image in the\nshape of a church it is idolatry. This is the defect of image-worship. Yet\nall these seem to be necessary stages.\n", "\nIn  this matter it is of supreme importance to think what we ourselves\nbelieve. What we have realised, is the question. What Jesus, or Buddha, or\nMoses did is nothing to us, unless we too do it for ourselves. It would not\nsatisfy our hunger to shut ourselves up in a room and think of what Moses\nate, nor would what Moses thought save us. My ideas are very radical on\nthese points. Sometimes I think that I am right when I agree with all the\nancient teachers, at other times I think they are right when they agree with\nme. I believe in thinking independently. I believe in becoming entirely free\nfrom the holy teachers; pay all reverence to them, but look at religion as\nan independent research. I have to find my light, just as they found theirs.\nTheir finding the light will not satisfy us at all. You have to become the\nBible, and not to follow it, excepting as paying reverence to it as a light\non the way, as a guide-post, a mark: that is all the value it has. But these\nimages and other things are quite necessary. You may try to concentrate your\nmind, or even to project any thought. You will find that you naturally form\nimages in your mind. You cannot help it. Two sorts of persons never require\nany image \u2014 the human animal who never thinks of any religion, and the\nperfected being who has passed through these stages. Between these two\npoints all of us require some sort of ideal, outside and inside. It may be\nin the form of a departed human being, or of a living man or woman. This is\nclinging to personality and bodies, and is quite natural. We are prone to\nconcretise. How could we be here if we did not concretise? We are concreted\nspirits, and so we find ourselves here on this earth. Concretisation has\nbrought us here, and it will take us out. Going after things of the senses\nhas made us human beings, and we are bound to worship personal beings,\nwhatever  we may say to the contrary. It is very easy to say \"Don't be\npersonal\"; but the same man who says so is generally most personal. His\nattachment for particular men and women is very strong; it does not leave\nhim when they die, he wants to follow them beyond death. That is idolatry;\nit is the seed, the very cause of idolatry; and the cause being there it\nwill come out in some form. Is it not better to have a personal attachment\nto an image of Christ or Buddha than to an ordinary man or woman? In the\nWest, people say that it is bad to kneel before images, but they can kneel\nbefore a woman and say, \"You are my life, the light of my eyes, my soul.\"\nThat is worse idolatry. What ifs this talk about my soul my life? It will\nsoon go away. It is only sense-attachment. It is selfish love covered by a\nmass of flowers. Poets give it a good name and throw lavender-water and all\nsorts of attractive things over it. Is it not better to kneel before a\nstatue of Buddha or the Jina conqueror and say, \"Thou art my life\"? I would\nrather do that.\n", "\nThere  is  another  sort of Pratika which is not recognised in Western\ncountries, bout is taught in our books. This teaches the worship of mind as\nGod. Anything that is worshipped as God is a stage, a nearing, as it were.\nAn  example  of  this  is the method of showing the fine star known as\nArundhati, near the group Pleiades. One is shown a big star near to it, and\nwhen he has fixed his attention on this and has come to know it, he is shown\na finer and still nearer star; and when he has fixed his attention on that,\nhe is led up to Arundhati. So all these various Pratikas and Pratimas lead\nto God. The worship of Buddha and of Christ constitute a Pratika. A drawing\nnear to the worship of God. But this worship of Buddha and of Christ will\nnot save a man, he must go beyond them to Him who manifested Himself as\nJesus  Christ,  for God alone can give us freedom. There are even some\nphilosophers who say these should he regarded as God; they are not Pratikas,\nbut God Himself. However, we can take all these different Pratikas, these\ndifferent stages of approach, and not be hurt by them: but if we think while\nwe are worshipping them that we are worshipping God, we are mistaken. If a\nman  worships Jesus Christ, and thinks he will be saved by that, he is\nmistaken entirely. If a man thinks that by worshipping an idol or the ghosts\nor spirits of the departed he will be saved, he is entirely mistaken. We may\nworship anything by seeing God in it, if we can forget the idol and see God\nthere. We must not project any image upon God. But we may fill any image\nwith that Life which is God. Only forget the image, and you are right enough\n\u2014 for \"Out of Him comes everything\". He is everything. We may worship a\npicture as God, but not God as the picture. God in the picture is right, but\nthe picture as God is wrong. God in the image is perfectly right. There is\nno danger there. This is the real worship of God. But the image-God is a\nmere Pratika.\n", "\nThe next great thing to consider in Bhakti is the \"word\", the N\u00e2mashakti,\nthe power of the name. The whole universe is composed of name and form.\nWhatever we see is either a compound of name and form, or simply name with\nform which is a mental image. So, after all, there is nothing that is not\nname and form. We all believe God to be without form or shape, but as soon\nas we begin to think of Him, He acquires both name and form The Chitta is\nlike the calm lake, thoughts being like waves upon this Chitta \u2014 and name\nand form are the normal ways in which these waves arise; no wave can rise\nwithout  name and form. The uniform cannot be thought of; it is beyond\nthought; as soon as it becomes thought and matter, it must have name and\nform. We cannot separate these. It is said in many books that God created\nthe universe out of the Word. Shabdabrahman, in Sanskrit, is the Christian\ntheory of the Word. An old Indian theory, it was taken to Alexandria by\nIndian preachers and was planted there. Thus the idea of the Word and the\nIncarnation became fixed there.\n", "\nThere is deep meaning in the thought that God created everything out of the\nWord. God Himself being formless, this is the best way to describe the\nprojection of forms, or the creation. The Sanskrit word for creation is\nSrishti, projection. What is meant by \"God created things out of nothing\"?\nThe universe is projected out of God. He becomes the universe, and it all\nreturns to Him, and again it proceeds forth, and again returns. Through all\neternity it will go on in that way. We have seen that the projection of\nanything in the mind cannot be without name and form. Suppose the mind to be\nperfectly calm, entirely without thought; nevertheless, as soon as thought\nbegins to rise it will immediately take name and form. Every thought has a\ncertain name and a certain form. In the same way the very fact of creation,\nthe very fact of projection is eternally connected with name and form. Thus\nwe find that every idea that man has, or can have, must be connected with a\ncertain name or word as its counterpart. This being so, it is quite natural\nto suppose that this universe is the outcome of mind, just as your body is\nthe  outcome  of  your idea \u2014 your idea, as it were, made concrete and\nexternalised. If it be true, moreover, that the whole universe is built on\nthe same plan, then, if you know the manner in which one atom is built, you\ncan understand how the whole universe is built. If it is true that in you,\nthe body forms the gross part outside and the mind forms the fine part\ninside, and both are eternally inseparable, then, when you cease to have the\nbody, you will cease to have the mind also. When a man's brain is disturbed,\nhis ideas also get disturbed, because they are but one, the finer and the\ngrosser parts. There are not two such things as matter and mind. As in a\nhigh column of air there are dense and rarefied strata of one and the same\nelement air, so it is with the body; it is one thing throughout, layer on\nlayer, from grosser to finer. Again, the body is like the finger nails. As\nthese continue growing even when they are cut, so from our subtle ideas\ngrows body after body. The finer a thing the more persistent it is; we find\nthat  always. The grosser it is the less persistent. Thus, form is the\ngrosser  and name the finer state of a single manifesting power called\nthought. But these three are one; it is the Unity and the Trinity, the three\ndegrees of existence of the same thing. Finer, more condensed, and most\ncondensed. Wherever the one is, the others are there also. Wherever name is,\nthere is form and thought.\n", "\nIt naturally follows that if the universe is built upon the same plan as the\nbody, the universe also must have the same divisions of form, name, and\nthought. The \"thought\" is the finest part of the universe, the real motive\npower. The thought behind our body is called soul, and the thought behind\nthe universe is called God. Then after that is the name, and last of all is\nthe form which we see and feel. For instance, you are a particular person, a\nlittle universe in this universe, a body with a particular form; then behind\nthat a name, John or Jane, and behind that again a thought; similarly there\nis this whole universe, and behind that is the name, what is called the\n\"Word\" in all religions, and behind that is God. The universal thought is\nMahat, as the S\u00e2nkhyas call it, universal consciousness. What is that name?\nThere must be some name. The world is homogeneous, and modern science shows\nbeyond doubt that each atom is composed of the same material as the whole\nuniverse. If you know one lump of clay you know the whole universe. Man is\nthe  most representative being in the universe, the microcosm, a small\nuniverse in himself. So in man we find there is the form, behind that the\nname, and behind that the thought, the thinking being. So this universe must\nbe on exactly the same plan. The question is: What is that name? According\nto the Hindus that word is Om. The old Egyptians also believed that. The\nKatha Upanishad says, \"That, seeking which a man practices Brahmacharya, I\nwill tell you in short what that is, that is Om. ... This is Brahman, the\nImmutable One, and is the highest; knowing this Immutable One, whatever one\ndesires one gets.\"\n", "\nThis Om stands for the name of the whole universe, or God. Standing midway\nbetween the external world and God, it represents both. But then we can take\nthe universe piecemeal, according to the different senses, as touch, as\ncolour, as taste, and in various other ways. In each case we can make of\nthis universe millions of universes from different standpoints, each of\nwhich will be a complete universe by itself, and each one will have a name,\nand a form, and a thought behind. These thoughts behind are Pratikas. Each\nof them has a name. These names of sacred symbols are used in Bhakti-Yoga.\nThey have almost infinite power. Simply by repetition of these words we can\nget  anything we desire, we can come to perfection. But two things are\nnecessary. \"The teacher must be wonderful, so also must be the taught\", says\nthe Katha Upanishad. Such a name must come from a person to whom it has\ndescended through right succession. From master to disciple, the spiritual\ncurrent has been coming; from ancient times, bearing its power. The person\nfrom whom such a word comes is called a Guru, and the person to whom it goes\nis called Shishya, the disciple. When the word has been received in the\nregular way, and when it has been repeated, much advance has been made in\nBhakti-Yoga.  Simply by the repetition of that word will come even the\nhighest state of Bhakti. \"Thou hast so many names. Thou understandest what\nis meant by them all these names are Thine, and in each is Thine infinite\npower; there is neither time nor place for repeating these names, for all\ntimes and places are holy. Thou art so easy, Thou art so merciful, how\nunfortunate am I, that I have no love for Thee!\"\n"]
