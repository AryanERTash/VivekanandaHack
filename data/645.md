["\n", "(RECORDED BY MISS S. E. WALDO, A DISCIPLE)\n", "TUESDAY, June 25, 1895.\n", "After every happiness comes misery; they may be far apart or near. The more\nadvanced the soul, the more quickly does one follow the other. What we want\nis neither happiness nor misery. Both make us forget our true nature; both\nare chains \u2014 one iron, one gold; behind both is the Atman, who knows neither\nhappiness nor misery. These are states and states must ever change; but the\nnature of the Soul is bliss, peace, unchanging. We have not to get it, we\nhave it; only wash away the dross and see it.\n", "Stand upon the Self, then only can we truly love the world. Take a very,\nvery high stand; knowing out universal nature, we must look with perfect\ncalmness upon all the panorama of the world. It is but baby's play, and we\nknow that, so cannot be disturbed by it. If the mind is pleased with praise,\nit will be displeased with blame. All pleasures of the senses or even of the\nmind are evanescent but within ourselves is the one true unrelated pleasure,\ndependent upon nothing. It is perfectly free, it is bliss. The more our\nbliss is within, the more spiritual we are. The pleasure of the Self is what\nthe world calls religion.\n", "The internal universe, the real, is infinitely greater than the external,\nwhich is only a shadowy projection of the true one. This world is neither\ntrue nor untrue, it is the shadow of truth. \"Imagination is the gilded\nshadow of truth\", says the poet.\n", "We enter into creation, and then for us it becomes living. Things are dead\nin themselves; only we give them life, and then, like fools, we turn around\nand are afraid of them, or enjoy them. But be not like certain fisher-women,\nwho, caught in a storm on their way home from market, took refuge in the\nhouse of a florist. They were lodged for the night in a room next to the\ngarden where the air was full of the fragrance of flowers. In vain did they\ntry to rest, until one of their number suggested that they wet their fishy\nbaskets and place them near their heads. Then they all fell into a sound\nsleep.\n", "The world is our fish basket, we must not depend upon it for enjoyment.\nThose who do are the T\u00e2masas or the bound. Then there are the R\u00e2jasas or the\negotistical, who talk always about \"I\", \"I\". They do good work sometimes and\nmay become spiritual. But the highest are the S\u00e2ttvikas, the introspective,\nthose who live only in the Self. These three qualities, Tamas, Rajas, and\nSattva  (idleness,  activity,  and illumination), are in everyone, and\ndifferent ones predominate at different times.\n", "Creation is not a \"making\" of something, it is the struggle to regain the\nequilibrium, as when atoms of cork are thrown to the bottom of a pail of\nwater and rush to rise to the top, singly or in clusters. Life is and must\nbe accompanied by evil. A little evil is the source of life; the little\nwickedness  that is in the world is very good; for when the balance is\nregained, the world will end, because sameness and destruction are one. When\nthis world goes, good and evil go with it; but when we can transcend this\nworld, we get rid of both good and evil and have bliss.\n", "There is no possibility of ever having pleasure without pain, good without\nevil;  for living itself is just the lost equilibrium. What we want is\nfreedom, not life, nor pleasure, nor good. Creation is infinite, without\nbeginning and without end \u2014 the ever-moving ripple in an infinite lake.\nThere are yet unreached depths and others where the equilibrium has been\nregained; but the ripple is always progressing, the struggle to regain the\nbalance is eternal. Life and death are only different names for the same\nfact, the two sides of the one coin. Both are Maya, the inexplicable state\nof striving at one time to live, and a moment later to die. Beyond this is\nthe true nature, the Atman. While we recognise a God, it is really only the\nSelf which we have separated ourselves from and worship as outside of us;\nbut it is our true Self all the time \u2014 the one and only God.\n", "To regain the balance we must counteract Tamas by Rajas; then conquer Rajas\nby Sattva, the calm beautiful state that will grow and grow until all else\nis gone. Give up bondage; become a son, be free, and then you can \"see the\nFather\", as did Jesus. Infinite strength is religion and God. Avoid weakness\nand slavery. You are only a soul, if you are free; there is immortality for\nyou, if you are free; there is God, if He is free. . . .\n", "The world for me, not I for the world. Good and evil are our slaves, not we\ntheirs.  It  is  the nature of the brute to remain where he is (not to\nprogress); it is the nature of man to seek good and avoid evil; it is the\nnature of God to seek neither, but just to be eternally blissful. Let us be\nGod! Make the heart like an ocean, go beyond all the trifles of the world,\nbe mad with joy even at evil; see the world as a picture and then enjoy its\nbeauty, knowing that nothing affects you. Children finding glass beads in a\nmud puddle, that is the good of the world. Look at it with calm complacency;\nsee good and evil as the same \u2014 both are merely \"God's play\"; enjoy all.\n", "*    *    *\n", "My Master used to say, \"All is God; but tiger-God is to be shunned. All\nwater is water; but we avoid dirty water for drinking.\"\n", "The whole sky is the censer of God, and sun and moon are the lamps. What\ntemple is needed? All eyes are Thine, yet Thou hast not an eye; all hands\nare Thine; yet Thou hast not a hand.\n", "Neither seek nor avoid, take what comes. It is liberty to be affected by\nnothing; do not merely endure, be unattached. Remember the story of the\nbull. A mosquito sat long on the horn of a certain bull. Then his conscience\ntroubled him, and he said, \"Mr. Bull, I have been sitting here a long time,\nperhaps I annoy you. I am sorry, I will go away.\" But the bull replied, \"Oh\nno, not at all! Bring your whole family and live on my horn; what can you do\nto me?\"\n"]
