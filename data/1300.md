["\n", "FOREWORD\n", "\n", "PERSONS: The Swami Vivekananda, Gurubhais, (Spiritual brethren; disciples of\none and the same master are so called.) and a party of European guests and\ndisciples, amongst whom were Dhira Mata, the \"Steady Mother\" [Mrs. Ole\nBull];  one  whose  name was Jaya [Miss Josephine MacLeod]; and Sister\nNivedita. (Dhira Mata and Jaya were Americans; Nivedita was British. \u2014\nPublisher.)\n", "PLACE: Different parts of India.\n", "TIME: The year 1898.\n", "\nBeautiful have been the days of this year. In them the Ideal has become the\nReal. First in our riverside cottage at Belur; then in the Himalayas, at\nNaini Tal and Almora; afterwards wandering here and there through Kashmir\n\u2014 everywhere have come hours never to be forgotten, words that will echo\nthrough our lives forever, and once, at least, a glimpse of the Beatific\nVision.\n", "It has been all play.\n", "We have seen a love that would be one with the humblest and most ignorant,\nseeing the world for the moment through his eyes, as if criticism were not;\nwe  have  laughed  over the colossal caprice of genius; we have warmed\nourselves at heroic fires; and we have been present, as it were, at the\nawakening of the Holy Child.\n", "But there has been nothing grim or serious about any of these things. Pain\nhas come close to all of us. Solemn anniversaries have been and gone. But\nsorrow was lifted into a golden light, where it was made radiant and did not\ndestroy.\n", "Fain, if I could, would I describe our journeys. Even as I write I see the\nirises  in  bloom  at Baramulla; the young rice beneath the poplars at\nIslamabad; starlight scenes in Himalayan forests; and the royal beauties of\nDelhi and the Taj. One longs to attempt some memorial of these. It would be\nworse than useless. Not, then, in words, but in the light of memory they are\nenshrined forever, together with the kindly and gentle folk who dwell among\nthem and whom we trust always to have left the gladder for our coming.\n", "We have learnt something of the mood in which new faiths are born and of the\npersons who inspire such faiths. For we have been with one who drew all men\nto him \u2014 listening to all, feeling with all and refusing none. We have known\na humility that wiped out all littleness, a renunciation that would die for\nscorn of oppression and pity of the oppressed, a love that would bless even\nthe oncoming feet of torture and of death. We have joined hands with that\nwoman who washed the feet of the Lord with her tears and wiped them with the\nhairs of her head. We have lacked not the occasion, but her passionate\nconsciousness of self.\n", "Seated under a tree in the garden of dead emperors there came to us a vision\nof all the rich and splendid things of Earth, offering themselves as a\nshrine for the great of soul. The storied windows of cathedrals and the\njewelled thrones of kings, the banners of great captains and the vestments\nof the priests, the pageants of cities and the retreats of the proud \u2014 all\ncame and all were rejected.\n", "In the garments of the beggar, despised by the alien, worshipped by the\npeople, we have seen him; and only the bread of toil, the shelter of cottage\nroofs, and the common road across the cornfields seem real enough for the\nbackground to this life.. . . Amongst his own the ignorant loved him as much\nas scholars and statesmen. The boatmen watched the river, in his absence,\nfor his return, and servants disputed with guests to do him service. And\nthrough it all the veil of playfulness was never dropped. \"They played with\nthe Lord\" and instinctively they knew it.\n", "To those who have known such hours, life is richer and sweeter, and in the\nlong  nights  even the wind in the palm trees seems to cry: \"Mahadeva!\nMahadeva! Mahadeva!\"\n"]
