["\n", "XVI\n", "To Mrs. G. W. Hale\n", "[C/O DR. EGBERT GUERNSEY\n", "528 FIFTH AVENUE]\n", "NEW YORK\n", "10 April 1894\n", "DEAR MOTHER,\n", "I  just  now  received your letter. I have the greatest regard for the\nSalvationists; in fact, they and the Oxford Mission gentlemen are the only\nChristian missionaries for whom I have any regard at all. They live with the\npeople, as the people, and for the people of India. Lord bless them. But I\nwould be very, very sorry of any trick being played by them. I never have\nheard of any Lord in India, much less in Ceylon. (Now Sri Lanka.) The people\nof Ceylon and northern India differ more than Americans and Hindus. Nor is\nthere any connection between the Buddhist priest and the Hindu. Our dress,\nmanners, religion, food, language differ entirely from southern India, much\nless to speak of Ceylon. You know already that I could not speak a word of\nNarasimha's language!! Although that was only Madras. Well, you have Hindu\nprincesses; why not a Lord, which is not a higher title.\n", "There was a certain Mrs. Smith in Chicago.[6]* I met her at Mrs. Stockham's.\nShe has introduced me to the Guernseys. Dr. Guernsey is one of the chief\nphysicians of this city and is a very good old gentleman. They are very fond\nof me and are very nice people. Next Friday I am going to Boston. I have not\nbeen lecturing in New York at all. I will come back and do some lecturing\nhere.\n", "For the last few days I was the guest of Miss Helen Gould \u2014 daughter of the\nrich Gould[7]* \u2014 at her palatial country residence, an hour's ride from the\ncity. She has one of the most beautiful and large green-houses in the world,\nfull of all sorts of curious plants and flowers. They are Presbyterians, and\nshe is a very religious lady. I had a very nice time there.\n", "I met my friend Mr. Flagg (William Joseph Flagg.) several times. He is\nflying merrily. There is another Mrs. Smith here who is very rich and pious.\nShe has invited me to dine today.\n", "As for lecturing, I have given up raising money. I cannot degenerate myself\nany more. When a certain purpose was in view, I could work; with that gone I\ncannot earn for myself. I have sufficient for going back. I have not tried\nto earn a penny here, and have refused some presents which friends here\nwanted to make to me. Especially Flagg \u2014 I have refused his money. I had in\nDetroit tried to refund the money back to the donors, and told them that,\nthere being almost no chance of my succeeding in my enterprise, I had no\nright to keep their money; but they refused and told me to throw that into\nthe waters if I liked. But I cannot take any more conscientiously. I am very\nwell off, Mother. Everywhere the Lord sends me kind persons and homes; so\nthere is no use of my going into beastly worldliness at all.\n", "The New York people, though not so intellectual as the Bostonians, are, I\nthink, more sincere. The Bostonians know well how to take advantage of\neverybody. And I am afraid even water cannot slip through their closed\nfingers!!! Lord bless them!!! I have promised to go and I must go; but,\nLord, make me live with the sincere, ignorant and the poor, and not cross\nthe shadow of the hypocrites and tall talkers who, as my Master used to say,\nare like vultures who soar high and high in their talks, but the heart is\nreally on a piece of carrion on the ground.\n", "I would be the guest of Mrs. Breed for a few days and, after seeing a little\nof Boston, I would come back to New York.\n", "Hope the sisters are all right and enjoying their concerts immensely. There\nis  not much of music in this city. That is a blessing (?) Went to see\nBarnum's circus the other day. It is no doubt a grand thing. I have not been\nas yet downtown. This street is very nice and quiet.\n", "I heard a beautiful piece of music the other day at Barnum's \u2014 they call it\na Spanish Serenada. Whatever it be, I liked it so much. Unfortunately, Miss\nGuernsey is not given to much thumping, although she has a good assortment\nof all the noisy stuffs in the world \u2014 and so she could not play it, which I\nregret ever so much.\n", "Yours obediently,\n", "VIVEKANANDA.\n", "PS \u2014 Most probably I will go to Annisquam as Mrs. Bagley's guest. She has\ngot a nice house there this summer. Before that, I will go back to Chicago\nonce more if I can.\n", "V.\n"]
