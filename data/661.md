["\n", "(RECORDED BY MISS S. E. WALDO, A DISCIPLE)\n", "FRIDAY, July 12, 1895. (Shankara's Commentary.)\n", "Fourth Vyasa Sutra. \"\u00c2tman (is) the aim of all.\"\n", "Ishvara is to be known from the Vedanta; all Vedas point to Him (Who is the\nCause; the Creator, Preserver and Destroyer). Ishvara is the unification of\nthe Trinity, known as Brahm\u00e2, Vishnu, and Shiva, which stand at the head of\nthe Hindu Pantheon. \"Thou art our Father who takest us to the other shore of\nthe dark ocean\" (Disciple's words to the Master).\n", "The Vedas cannot show you Brahman, you are That already; they can only help\nto take away the veil that hides the truth from our eyes. The first veil to\nvanish is ignorance; and when that is gone, sin goes; next desire ceases,\nselfishness ends, and all misery disappears. This cessation of ignorance can\nonly come when I know that God and I are one; in other words, identify\nyourself with Atman, not with human limitations. Dis-identify yourself with\nthe  body, and all pain will cease. This is the secret of healing. The\nuniverse is a case of hypnotisation; de-hypnotise yourself and cease to\nsuffer.\n", "In order to be free we have to pass through vice to virtue, and then get rid\nof both. Tamas is to be conquered by Rajas, both are to be submerged in\nSattva; then go beyond the three qualities. Reach a state where your very\nbreathing is a prayer.\n", "Whenever you learn (gain anything) from another man's words, know that you\nhad the experience in a previous existence, because experience is the only\nteacher.\n", "With all powers comes further misery, so kill desire. Getting any desire is\nlike putting a stick into a nest of hornets. Vair\u00e2gya is finding, out that\ndesires are but gilded balls of poison.\n", "\"Mind is not God\" (Shankara). \"Tat tvam asi\" \"Aham Brahm\u00e2smi\" (\"That thou\nart\", \"I am Brahman\"). When a man realises this, all the knots of his heart\nare cut asunder, all his doubts vanish\". Fearlessness is not possible as\nlong as we have even God over us; we must be God. What is disjoined will be\nfor ever disjoined; if you are separate from God, then you can never be one\nwith Him, and vice versa. If by virtue you are joined to God, when that\nceases, disjunction will come. The junction is eternal, and virtue only\nhelps to remove the veil. We are \u00e2z\u00e2d (free), we must realise it. \"Whom the\nSelf chooses\" means we are the Self and choose ourselves.\n", "Does seeing depend upon our own efforts or does it depend upon something\noutside? It depends upon ourselves; our efforts take off the dust, the\nmirror does not change. There is neither knower, knowing, nor known. \"He who\nknows that he does not know, knows It.\" He who has a theory knows nothing.\n", "The idea that we are bound is only an illusion.\n", "Religion is not of this world; it is \"heart-cleansing\", and its effect on\nthis world is secondary. Freedom is inseparable from the nature of the\nAtman. This is ever pure, ever perfect, ever unchangeable. This Atman you\ncan never know. We can say nothing about the Atman but \"not this, not this\".\n", "\"Brahman  is that which we can never drive out by any power of mind or\nimagination.\" (Shankara).\n", "*    *    *\n", "The universe is thought, and the Vedas are the words of this thought. We can\ncreate and uncreate this whole universe. Repeating the words, the unseen\nthought is aroused, and as a result a seen effect is produced. This is the\nclaim of a certain sect of Karmis. They think that each one of us is a\ncreator. Pronounce the words, the thought which corresponds will arise, and\nthe result will become visible. \"Thought is the power of the word, the word\nis the expression of the thought,\" say Mim\u00e2msakas, a Hindu philosophical\nsect.\n"]
