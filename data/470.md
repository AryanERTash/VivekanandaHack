["IX[1]\n", "(Translated from Bengali)\n", "17th Aug., 1889.\n", "DEAR SIR,\n", "You have expressed embarrassment in your last favour for being addressed\nreverentially. But the blame attaches not to me but to your own excellent\nqualities. I wrote in one letter before that from the way I feel attracted\nby your lofty virtues, it seems we had some affinity from previous births. I\nmake no distinction as to householder or Sannyasin in this, that for all\ntime my head shall bend low in reverence wherever I see greatness, broadness\nof heart, and holiness\u2014Sh\u00e2ntih! Sh\u00e2ntih! Sh\u00e2ntih! My prayer is that among\nthe  many people embracing Sanny\u00e2sa nowadays, greedy of honour, posing\nrenunciation for the sake of a living, and fallen off from the ideal on both\nsides, may one in a lakh at least become high-souled like you! To you my\nBrahmin fellow-disciples who have heard of your noble virtues tender their\nbest prostrations.\n", "About one amongst my several questions to which you sent your replies, my\nwrong idea is corrected. For this I shall remain indebted to you for ever.\nAnother  of  these  questions  was: Whether Acharya Shankara gives any\nconclusion regarding caste based on Gunas as mentioned in Puran\u00e2s like the\nMahabharata. If he does, where is it to be found? I have no doubt that\naccording to the ancient view in this country, caste was hereditary, and it\ncannot also be doubted that sometimes the Shudras used to be oppressed more\nthan the helots among the Spartans and the negroes among the Americans! As\nfor myself, I have no partiality for any party in this caste question,\nbecause I know it is a social law and is based on diversity of Guna and\nKarma. It also means grave harm if one bent on going beyond Guna and Karma\ncherishes in mind any caste distinctions. In these matters, I have got some\nsettled ideas through the grace of my Guru but, if I come to know of your\nviews, I may just confirm some points or rectify others in them. One doesn't\nhave honey dripping unless one pokes at the hive\u2014so I shall put you some\nmore questions; and looking upon me as ignorant and as a boy, please give\nproper replies without taking any offence.\n", "1. Is the Mukti, which the Vedanta-Sutras speaks of, one and the same with\nthe Nirvana of the Avadhuta-Git\u00e2 and other texts?\n", "2. What is really meant by Nirvana if, according to the aphorism, \"Without\nthe function of creating etc.\"[2] (ibid., IV. iv. 7), none can attain to\nthe fullest Godhead?\n", "3. Chaitanya-deva is said to have told S\u00e2rvabhauma at Puri, \"I understand\nthe Sutras (aphorisms) of Vyasa, they are dualistic; but the commentator\nmakes them, monistic, which I don't understand.\" Is this true? Tradition\nsays, Chaitanya-deva had a dispute with Prakashananda Sarasvati on the\npoint, and Chaitanya-deva won. One commentary by Chaitanya-deva was rumoured\nto have been existing in Prakashananda's Math.\n", "4. In the Tantra, Acharya Shankara has been called a crypto-Buddhist; views\nexpressed in Prajn\u00e2paramit\u00e2, the Buddhist M\u00e2h\u00e2yana book, perfectly tally\nwith the Vedantic views propounded by the Acharya. The author of Panchadashi\nalso says, \"What we call Brahman is the same truth as the Shunya of the\nBuddhist.\" What does all this mean?\n", "5. Why has no foundation for the authority of the Vedas been adduced in the\nVedanta-Satras? First, it has been said that the Vedas are the authority for\nthe existence of God, and then it has been argued that the authority for the\nVedas is the text: \"It is the breath of God.\" Now, is this statement not\nvitiated by what in Western logic is called an argument in a circle?\n", "6. The Vedanta requires of us faith, for conclusiveness cannot be reached by\nmere  argumentation. Then why, has the slightest flaw, detected in the\nposition  of the schools of S\u00e2nkhya and Ny\u00e2ya, been overwhelmed with a\nfusillade  of  dialectics? In whom, moreover, are we to put our faith?\nEverybody seems to be mad over establishing his own view; if, according to\nVyasa, even the great Muni Kapila, \"the greatest among perfected souls\",[3]\nis himself deeply involved in error, then who would say that Vyasa may not\nbe so involved in a greater measure? Did Kapila fail to understand the\nVedas?\n", "7. According to the Nyaya, \"Shabda or Veda (the criterion of truth), is the\nword of those who have realised the highest\"; so the Rishis as such are\nomniscient. Then how are they proved, according to the Surya-siddh\u00e2nta, to\nbe ignorant of such simple astronomical truths? How can we accept their\nintelligence as the refuge to ferry us across the ocean of transmigratory\nexistence, seeing that they speak of the earth as triangular, of the serpent\nV\u00e2suki as the support of the earth and so on?\n", "8. If in His acts of creation God is dependent on good and evil Karmas, then\nwhat does it avail us to worship Him? There is a fine song of Nareshchandra,\nwhere occurs the following: \"If what lies in one's destiny is to happen\nanyhow, O Mother, then what good all this invoking by the holy name of\nDurg\u00e2?\"\n", "9.  True,  it is improper to hold many texts on the same subject to be\ncontradicted by one or two. But why then are the long-continued customs of\nMadhuparka[4] and the like repealed by one or two such texts as, \"The horse\nsacrifice, the cow sacrifice, Sannyasa, meat-offerings in Shr\u00e2ddha\", etc.?\nIf the Vedas are eternal, then what are the meaning and justification of\nsuch specifications as \"this rule of Dharma is for the age of Dv\u00e2para,\"\n\"this for the age of Kali\", and so forth?\n", "10. The same God who gives out the Vedas becomes Buddha again to annul them;\nwhich  of  these dispensations is to be obeyed? Which of these remains\nauthoritative, the earlier or the later one?\n", "11. The Tantra says, in the Kali-Yuga the Veda-Mantras are futile. So which\nbehest of God, the Shiva, is to be followed?\n", "12. Vyasa makes out in the Vedanta-Sutras that it is wrong to worship the\ntetrad of divine manifestation, V\u00e2sudeva, Sankarshana, etc., and again that\nvery Vyasa expatiates on the great merits of that worship in the Bh\u00e2gavata!\nIs this Vyasa a madman?\n", "I have many doubts besides these, and, hoping to have them dispelled from my\nmind through your kindness, I shall lay them before you in future. Such\nquestions cannot be all set forth except in a personal interview; neither\ncan as much satisfaction be obtained as one expects to. So I have a mind to\nlay before you all these facts when presenting myself to you, which I expect\nwill be very soon, by the grace of the Guru.\n", "I  have  heard  it said that without inner progress in the practice of\nreligion, no true conclusion can be reached concerning these matters, simply\nby means of reasoning; but satisfaction, at least to some extent, seems to\nbe necessary at the outset.\n", "Yours etc., \nVivekananda.\n"]
