["\n", "CXXII\n", "To Swami Ramakrishnananda\n", "MATH, BELUR,\n", "HOWRAH P.O.,\n", "25th February, 1898.\n", "MY DEAR SHASHI,\n", "Our congratulations for the successful carrying out of the Mahotsava (Big\ncelebration of Shri Ramakrishna's birthday.)  in Madras. Hope you had a good\ngathering and plenty of spiritual food. We are all so glad that you have\ngirded yourself to teach more of spirituality to the Madras people than\nthose finger twistings and kling phat (Cryptic Mantras or sound formulae.)\nyou are so fond of. Really your lecture on Shriji (Shri Ramakrishna.) was\nsplendid. I could only catch a report in the Madras Mail in Khandwa, and the\nMath people have not had any. Why don't you send us over a copy?\n", "I learn that you complain about my silence, is it? I have written you more\nletters, however, than you ever wrote me, from Europe and America even. You\nought to give me all the news you can from Madras every week. Simplest way\nis to put down a few lines and a few items of news every day on a sheet.\n", "My health has not been all right of late; at present it is much better.\nCalcutta is unusually cool just now, and the American friends who are here\nare enjoying it ever so much. Today we take possession of the land we have\nbought, and though it is not practicable to have the Mahotsava on it just\nnow, I must have something on it on Sunday. Anyhow, Shriji's relics must be\ntaken to our place for the day and worshipped. Gangadhar is here and asks me\nto write to you that though he has succeeded in getting some subscriptions\nfor the Brahmavadin, the delivery being very irregular, he is afraid of\nlosing them also soon. I received your letter of recommendation for the\nyoung man with the old story of \"having nothing to eat, Your Honour\"; only\nadded in the Madras edition: \"got a number of children too\", for generating\nwhom no recommendation was needed! I would be very glad to help him, but the\nfact is, I have no money; every cent I had I have made over to Raja, (Rakhal\nor Swami Brahmananda.) as they all say I am a spendthrift and are afraid of\nkeeping money with me. I have, however, sent the letter to Rakhal if he can\nfind  the  way to help your friend, the young man, in having some more\nchildren. He writes that the Christians will help him out if he becomes a\nconvert, but he won't. Perhaps he is afraid that his conversion will make\nHindu India lose one of her brightest jewels and Hindu society the benefit\nof his propagating power to eternal misery!\n", "The boys here are rather seedy owing to the unusual amount of pure and cool\nair they are made to breathe in and live on the bank of the Ganga in the new\nMath. Sarada has his malaria brought over from Dinajpur. I made him eat a\ndose of opium the other day without much benefit to him except his brain\nwhich progressed for some hours towards its natural direction, namely,\nidiocy. Hari also has a touch; I hope it will take off a good bit of their\navoirdupois. By the by, we have once more started the dancing business here,\nand it would make your heart glad to see Hari and Sarada and my own good\nself in a waltz. How we keep balance at all is a wonder to me.\n", "Sharat  has  come  and is hard at work as usual. We have got some good\nfurniture now, and a big jump from the old Ch\u00e2t\u00e2i (mat) in the old Math to\nnice tables and chairs and three Kh\u00e2ts (cots), mind you. We have curtailed\nthe Puj\u00e2 (worship) work a good deal, and the amount of pruning your klings\nand phats and sv\u00e2h\u00e2s have undergone would make you faint. The puja occupied\nonly the day, and they slept soundly all night. How are Tulsi and Khoka? Are\nthey more tractable with you than under Rakhal? You may run in to Calcutta\nfor a few days giving charge to Tulsi, but it is so expensive, and then you\nmust  go back, as Madras has to be thoroughly worked up. I am going to\nAmerica again with Mrs. Bull in a few months.\n", "Give my love to Goodwin and tell him that we are going to see him at any\nrate on our way to Japan. Shivananda is here, and I have toned down a bit\nhis great desire to go to the Himalayas for food! Is Tulsi contemplating the\nsame? The bandicoot-hole will be a sufficient cave for him, I suppose.\n", "So the Math here is a fait accompli, and I am going over to get more help. .\n. . Work on with energy. India is a rotten corpse inside and outside. We\nshall revive it by the blessings of Shri Maharaj. With all love,\n", "Ever yours in the Lord,\n", "\nVIVEKANANDA.\n"]
