["\n", "CXXXVI\n", "To Miss Mary Hale\n", "MATH, BELUR,\n", "HOWRAH DISTRICT,\n", "16th March, 1899.\n", "MY DEAR MARY,\n", "Thanks to Mrs. Adams; she roused you naughty girls to a letter at last. \"Out\nof sight out of mind\" \u2014 as true in India as in America. And the other young\nlady,  who  just left her love as she flitted by, deserves a ducking I\nsuppose.\n", "Well, I have been in a sort of merry-go-round with my body which has been\ntrying to convince me for months that it too much exists.\n", "However, no fear, with four mental-healing sisters as I have, no sinking\njust now. Give me a strong pull and a long pull, will you, all together, and\nthen I am up!\n", "Why do you talk so much about me in your one-letter-a-year and so little\nabout the four witches mumbling Mantras over the boiling pot in a corner of\nChicago?\n", "Did  you  come across Max M\u00fcller's new book, Ramakrishna: His Life and\nSayings?\n", "If you have not, do, and let Mother see it. How is Mother? Growing grey? And\nFather Pope? Who have been our last visitors from America do you suppose?\n\"Brother, love is a drawing card\" and \"Misses Meel\"; they have been doing\nsplendid in Australia and elsewhere; the same old \"fellies\", little changed\nif any. I wish you could come to visit India \u2014 that will be some day in the\nfuture.  By  the by, Mary, I heard a few months ago, when I was rather\nworrying over your long silence, that you were just hooking a \"Willy\", and\nso  busy  with  your dances and parties; that explained of course your\ninability to write. But \"Willy\" or no \"Willy\", I must have my money, don't\nforget. Harriet is discreetly silent since she got her boy; but where is my\nmoney, please? Remind her and her husband of it. If she is Woolley, I am\ngreasy Bengali, as the English call us here \u2014 Lord, where is my money?\n", "I have got a monastery on the Ganga now, after all, thanks to American and\nEnglish friends. Tell Mother to look sharp. I am going to deluge your Yankee\nland with idolatrous missionaries.\n", "Tell  Mr.  Woolley he got the sister but has not paid the brother yet.\nMoreover, it was the fat black queerly dressed apparition smoking in the\nparlour that frightened many a temptation away, and that was one of the\ncauses which secured Harriet to Mr. Woolley; therefore, I want to be paid\nfor my great share in the work etc., etc. Plead strong, will you?\n", "I do so wish I could come over to America with Joe for this summer; but man\nproposes and who disposes? Not God surely always. Well, let things slide as\nthey will. Here is Abhayananda, Marie Louse you know, and she has been very\nwell received in Bombay and Madras. She will be in Calcutta tomorrow, and we\nare going to give her a good reception too.\n", "My love to Miss Howe, Mrs. Adams, to Mother Church, and Father Pope and all\nthe rest of my friends across the seven oceans. We believe in seven oceans\n\u2014 one of milk, one of honey, one of curd, one wine, one sugar-cane juice,\none salt, one I forget what. To you four sisters I waft my love across the\nocean of honey. . . .\n", "Ever sincerely, your brother,\n", "VIVEKANANDA.\n", "PS. Write when you find time between dances.\n", "V.\n"]
