["\n", "VEDIC TEACHING IN THEORY AND PRACTICE\n", "\nWhen the Swami's visit was drawing to a close, his friends in Almora invited\nhim to give a lecture in Hindi. He consented to make the attempt for the\nfirst time. He began slowly, and soon warmed to his theme, and found himself\nbuilding his phrases and almost his words as he went along. Those best\nacquainted with the difficulties and limitations of the Hindi language,\nstill undeveloped as a medium for oratory, expressed their opinion that a\npersonal triumph had been achieved by Swamiji and that he had proved by his\nmasterly use of Hindi that the language had in it undreamt-of possibilities\nof development in the direction of oratory.\n", "\nAnother lecture was delivered at the English Club in English, of which a\nbrief summary follows.\n", "\nThe subject was \"Vedic Reaching in Theory and Practice\". A short historical\nsketch of the rise of the worship of the tribal God and its spread through\nconquest of other tribes was followed by an account of the Vedas. Their\nnature, character, and teaching were briefly touched upon. Then the Swami\nspoke about the soul, comparing the Western method which seeks for the\nsolution of vital and religious mysteries in the outside world, with the\nEastern method which finding no answer in nature outside turns its inquiry\nwithin. He justly claimed for his nation the glory of being the discoverers\nof the introspective method peculiar to themselves, and of having given to\nhumanity the priceless treasures of spirituality which are the result of\nthat method alone. Passing from this theme, naturally so dear to the heart\nof a Hindu, the Swami reached the climax of his power as a spiritual teacher\nwhen he described the relation of the soul to God, its aspiration after and\nreal unity with God. For some time it seemed as though the teacher, his\nwords, his audience, and the spirit pervading them all were one. No longer\nwas there any consciousness of \"I\" and \"Thou\", of \"This\" or \"That\". The\ndifferent units collected there were for the time being lost and merged in\nthe spiritual radiance which emanated so powerfully from the great teacher\nand held them all more than spellbound.\n", "\nThose that have frequently heard him will recall similar experiences when he\nceased to be Swami Vivekananda lecturing to critical and attentive hearers,\nwhen all details and personalities were lost, names and forms disappeared,\nonly the Spirit remaining, uniting the speaker, hearer, and the spoken word.\n"]
