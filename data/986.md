This time not busy, not hustling about but quietly\nsettled down in a corner \u2014 waiting to start for the U.S. America on the\nfirst opportunity. My friends are nearly all out of London in the country\nand elsewhere, and my health not sufficiently strong.\n", "So you are happy in the midst of your lakes and gardens and seclusion in\nCanada. I am glad, so glad to know that you are up again on top of the tide.\nMay you remain there for ever!\n", "You could not finish the Raja-Yoga translation yet \u2014 all right, there is no\nhurry.  Time  and  opportunity must come if it is to be done you know,\notherwise we vainly strive.\n", "Canada must be beautiful now, with its short but vigorous summer, and very\nhealthy.\n", "I expect to be in New York in a few weeks, and don't know what next. I hope\nto come back to England next spring.\n", "I fervently wish no misery ever came near anyone; yet it is that alone that\ngives us an insight into the depths of our lives, does it not?\n", "In our moments of anguish, gates barred for ever seem to open and let in\nmany a flood of light.\n", "We learn as we grow. Alas! we cannot use our knowledge here. The moment we\nseem to learn, we are hurried off the stage. And this is M\u00e2y\u00e2!\n", "This toy world would not be here, this play could not go on, if we were\nknowing players. We must play blindfolded. Some of us have taken the part of\nthe rogue of the play, some heroic \u2014 never mind, it is all play. This is the\nonly consolation. There are demons and lions and tigers and what not on the\nstage, but they are all muzzled. They snap but cannot bite. The world cannot\ntouch our souls. If you want, even if the body be torn and bleeding, you may\nenjoy the greatest peace in your mind.\n", "And the way to that is to attain hopelessness. Do you know that? Not the\nimbecile attitude of despair, but the contempt of the conqueror for things\nhe  has attained, for things he struggled for and then throws aside as\nbeneath his worth.\n", "This hopelessness, desirelessness, aimlessness, is just the harmony with\nnature. In nature there is no harmony, no reason, no sequence; it was chaos\nbefore, it is so still.\n", "The lowest man is in consonance with nature in his earthy-headness; the\nhighest the same in the fullness of knowledge. All three aimless, drifting,\nhopeless \u2014 all three happy.\n", "You want a chatty letter, don't you? I have not much to chat about. Mr.\nSturdy came last two days. He goes home in Wales tomorrow.\n", "I have to book my passage for N.Y. in a day or two.\n", "None of my old friends have I seen yet except Miss Souter and Max Gysic, who\nare in London. They have been very kind, as they always were.\n", "I have no news to give you, as I know nothing of London yet. I don't know\nwhere Gertrude Orchard is, else would have written to her. Miss Kate Steel\nis also away. She is coming on Thursday or Saturday.\n", "I had an invitation to stay in Paris with a friend, a very well-educated\nFrenchman, but I could not go this time. I hope another time to live with\nhim some days.\n", "I expect to see some of our old friends and say good day to them.\n", "I hope to see you in America sure. Either I may unexpectedly turn up in\nOttawa in my peregrinations or you come to N.Y.\n", "Good-bye, all luck be yours.\n", "Ever yours in the Lord,\n", "\nVIVEKANANDA.\n"]
