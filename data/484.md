What you have written about Tibet is very\npromising, and I shall try to go there once. In Sanskrit Tibet is called the\nUttarakuruvarsha,  and  is not a land of Mlechchhas. It is my belief that the Tantras, in\nvogue amongst us, were the creation of the Buddhists themselves. Those\nTantrika rites are even more dreadful than our doctrine of V\u00e2m\u00e2ch\u00e2ra; for in\nthem adultery got a free rein, and it was only when the Buddhists became\ndemoralised  through immorality that they were driven away by Kum\u00e2rila\nBhatta.  As  some  Sannyasins  speak of Shankara, or the B\u00e2uls of Shri\nChaitanya, that he was in secret an epicure, a drunkard, and one addicted to\nall sorts of abominable practices\u2014so the modern Tantrika Buddhists speak\nof the Lord Buddha as a dire Vam\u00e2ch\u00e2ri and give an obscene interpretation to\nthe many beautiful precepts of the Prajn\u00e2p\u00e2ramit\u00e2, such as the Tattvag\u00e2th\u00e2\nand the like. The result of all this has been that the Buddhists are divided\ninto two sects nowadays; the Burmese and the Sinhalese have generally set\nthe Tantras at naught, have likewise banished the Hindu gods and goddesses,\nand at the same time have thrown overboard the Amit\u00e2bha Buddha held in\nregard among the Northern School of Buddhists. The long and the short of it\nis that the Amitabha Buddha and the other gods whom the Northern School\nworship are not mentioned in books like the Prajnaparamita, but a lot of\ngods and goddesses are recommended for worship. And the Southern people have\nwilfully transgressed the Sh\u00e2stras and eschewed the gods and goddesses. The\nphase of Buddhism which declares \"Everything for others\", and which you find\nspread throughout Tibet, has greatly struck modern Europe. Concerning that\nphase, however, I have a good deal to say\u2014which it is impossible to do in\nthis letter. What Buddha did was to break wide open the gates of that very\nreligion which was confined in the Upanishads to a particular caste. What\nspecial greatness does his theory of Nirvana confer on him? His greatness\nlies in his unrivalled sympathy. The high orders of Samadhi etc., that lend\ngravity to his religion are, almost all there in the Vedas; what are absent\nthere are his intellect and heart, which have never since been paralleled\nthroughout the history of the world.\n", "The  Vedic  doctrine  of Karma is the same as in Judaism and all other\nreligions, that is to say, the purification of the mind through sacrifices\nand such other external means\u2014and Buddha was the first man who stood\nagainst it. But the inner essence of the ideas remained as of old\u2014look at\nthat doctrine of mental exercises which he preached, and that mandate of his\nto believe in the Suttas instead of the Vedas. Caste also remained as of old\n(caste  was not wholly obsolete at the time of Buddha), but it was now\ndetermined by personal qualifications; and those that were not believers in\nhis religion were declared as heretics, all in the old style. \"Heretic\" was\na very ancient word with the Buddhists, but then they never had recourse to\nthe sword (good souls!) and had great toleration. Argument blew up the\nVedas. But what is the proof of your religion? Well, put faith in it!\u2014the\nsame procedure as in all religions. It was however an imperative necessity\nof the times; and that was the reason of his having incarnated himself. His\ndoctrine is like that of Kapila. But that of Shankara, how far more grand\nand rational! Buddha and Kapila are always saying the world is full of grief\nand nothing but that\u2014flee from it\u2014ay, for your life, do! Is happiness\naltogether absent here? It is a statement of the nature of what the Brahmos\nsay\u2014the world is full of happiness! There is grief, forsooth, but what can\nbe  done? Perchance some will suggest that grief itself will appear as\nhappiness when you become used to it by constant suffering. Shankara does\nnot take this line of argument. He says: This world is and is not\u2014manifold yet one; I shall unravel its mystery\u2014I shall know whether grief be there,\nor anything else; I do not flee from it as from a bugbear. I will know all\nabout  it  as to the infinite pain that attends its search, well, I am\nembracing it in its fullest measure. Am I a beast that you frighten me with\nhappiness and misery, decay and death, which are but the outcome of the\nsenses? I will know about it\u2014will give up my life for it. There is nothing\nto know about in this world\u2014therefore, if there be anything beyond this\nrelative existence\u2014what the Lord Buddha has designated as Prajn\u00e2p\u00e2ra\u2014the\ntranscendental\u2014if such there be, I want that alone. Whether happiness\nattends  it or grief, I do not care. What a lofty idea! How grand! The\nreligion of Buddha has reared itself on the Upanishads, and upon that also\nthe philosophy of Shankara. Only, Shankara had not the slightest bit of\nBuddha's wonderful heart, dry intellect merely!
