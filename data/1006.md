["\n", "CLIX\n", "To Mrs. Ole Bull\n", "1502 JONES STREET,\n", "SAN FRANCISCO,\n", "7th March, 1900.\n", "DEAR DHIRA MATA,\n", "Your letter, enclosing one from Saradananda only and the accounts, came. I\nam very much reassured by all the news I since received from India. As for\nthe accounts and the disposal of the Rs. 30,000, do just what you please. I\nhave given over the management to you, the Master will show you what is best\nto do. The money is Rs. 35,000; the Rs. 5,000, for building the cottage on\nthe Ganga, I wrote to Saradananda not to use just now. I have already taken\nRs. 5,000 of that money. I am not going to take more. I had paid back Rs.\n2,000 or more of that Rs. 5,000 in India. But it seems, Brahmananda, wanting\nto show as much of the Rs. 35,000 intact as he could, drew upon my Rs.\n2,000; so I owe them Rs. 5,000 still on that score.\n", "Anyway, I thought I could make money here in California and pay them up\nquietly. Now I have entirely failed in California financially. It is worse\nhere than in Los Angeles. They come in crowds when there is a free lecture\nand very few when there is something to pay.\n", "I have some hopes yet in England. It is necessary for me to reach England in\nMay. There is not the least use in breaking my health in San Francisco for\nnothing. Moreover, with all Joe's enthusiasm, I have not yet found any real\nbenefit from the magnetic healer, except a few red patches on my chest from\nscratching!  Platform work is nigh gone for me, and forcing it is only\nhastening the end. I leave here very soon, as soon as I can make money for a\npassage. I have 300 dollars in hand, made in Los Angeles. I will lecture\nhere next week and then I stop. As for the Math and the money, the sooner I\nam released of that burden the better.\n", "I am ready to do whatever you advise me to do. You have been a real mother\nto me. You have taken up one of my great burdens on yourself \u2014 I mean my\npoor cousin. I feel quite satisfied. As for my mother, I am going back to\nher \u2014 for my last days and hers. The thousand dollars I have in New York\nwill bring Rs. 9 a month; then I bought for her a bit of land which will\nbring about Rs. 6; and her old house \u2014 that will bring, say, Rs. 6. I leave\nthe house under litigation out of consideration, as I have not got it.\nMyself, my mother, my grandmother, and my brother will live on Rs. 20 a\nmonth easy. I would start just now, if I could make money for a passage to\nIndia, without touching the 1,000 dollars in New York.\n", "Anyhow I will scrape three or four hundred dollars \u2014 400 dollars will be\nenough for a second class passage and for a few weeks' stay in London. I do\nnot ask you to do anything more for me; I do not want it. What you have done\nis more, ever so much more than I deserve. I have given my place solemnly to\nyou in Shri Ramakrishna's work. I am out of it. All my life I have been a\ntorture to my poor mother. Her whole life has been one of continuous misery.\nIf it be possible, my last attempt should be to make her a little happy. I\nhave planned it all out. I have served the Mother all my life. It is done; I\nrefuse now to grind Her axe. Let Her find other workers \u2014 I strike.\n", "You have been one friend with whom Shri Ramakrishna has become the goal of\nlife \u2014 that is the secret of my trust in you. Others love me personally. But\nthey little dream that what they love me for is Ramakrishna; leaving Him, I\nam only a mass of foolish selfish emotions. Anyway this stress is terrible,\nthinking  of what may come next, wishing what ought to come next. I am\nunequal to the responsibility; I am found wanting. I must give up this work.\nIf the work has not life in it, let it die; if it has, it need not wait for\npoor workers like myself.\n", "Now the money, Rs. 30,000, is in my name, in Government Securities. If they\nare sold now, we shall lose fearfully, on account of the war; then, how can\nthey be sent over here without being sold there? To sell them there I must\nsign them. I do not know how all this is going to be straightened out. Do\nwhat  you  think best about it all. In the meanwhile, it is absolutely\nnecessary that I execute a will in your favour for everything, in case I\nsuddenly die. Send me a draft will as soon as possible and I shall register\nit in San Francisco or Chicago; then my conscience will be safe. I don't\nknow any lawyer here, else I would have got it drawn up; neither have I the\nmoney. The will must be done immediately; the trust and things have time\nenough for them.\n", "Ever your son,\n", "\nVIVEKANANDA.\n"]
