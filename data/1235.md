["\n", "CXCI\n", "To Mrs. Ole Bull (in London)\n", "DACCA,\n", "20 March 1901.\n", "MY DEAR MOTHER,\n", "At last I am in Eastern Bengal. This is the first time I am here, and never\nbefore knew Bengal was so beautiful. You ought to have seen the rivers here\n\u2014 regular rolling oceans of fresh water, and everything so green \u2014 continual\nproduction. The villages are the cleanest and prettiest in all India.\n", "Joe [Miss Josephine MacLeod] is perhaps by this time in Japan. I received a\nlong and beautiful letter from Margot. Tell Margot that there has been of\nlate a regular fall of fortune on the Kashmir Raja; things are all changing\nto his benefit. Mr. Mookherjey is now Governor of Kashmir. Saradananda had a\nbad fever. He is well now, but weak. He possibly goes to Darjeeling for a\nchange. Mrs. [M. N.] Banerjey, who is at Calcutta, is very anxious to take\nhim to the hills. Mohin [Mahendranath Datta], my brother, is in India, in\nKarachi near Bombay, and he corresponds with Saradananda. He writes to say\nhe is going to Burma, China, etc. The traders who lure him have shops in all\nthose places. I am not at all anxious about him. He is a very selfish man.\n", "I have no news from Detroit. I received one letter from Christina nine\nmonths ago, but I did not reply. Perhaps that may have vexed her.\n", "I  am peaceful and calm \u2014 and am finding every day the old begging and\ntrudging life is the best for me after all.\n", "Mrs. Sevier I left at Belur. She is the guest of Mrs. Banerjey, who has\nrented Nilambar Mookherjey's house on the river (the old Math). She goes\nvery soon to Europe.\n", "Things  are  going  on,  as is in the nature of things. To me has come\nresignation.\n", "With all love,\n", "Ever your Son,\n", "VIVEKANANDA.\n", "PS \u2014 All blessings on Margot's work. Mother is leading, I am sure.\n", "V.\n"]
