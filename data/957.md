["\n", "CX\n", "To Sister Nivedita\n", "SRINAGAR, KASHMIR,\n", "1st October, 1897.\n", "DEAR MARGO,\n", "Some people do the best work when led. Not every one is born to lead. The\nbest leader, however, is one who \"leads like the baby\". The baby, though\napparently depending on everyone, is the king of the household. At least, to\nmy  thinking,  that is the secret. . . . Many feel, but only a few can\nexpress. It is the power of expressing one's love and appreciation and\nsympathy for others, that enables one person to succeed better in spreading\nthe idea than others. . . .\n", "I shall not try to describe Kashmir to you. Suffice it to say, I never felt\nsorry to leave any country except this Paradise on earth; and I am trying my\nbest, if I can, to influence the Raja in starting a centre. So much to do\nhere, and the material so hopeful! . . .\n", "The great difficulty is this: I see persons giving me almost the whole of\ntheir love. But I must not give anyone the whole of mine in return, for that\nday the work would be ruined. Yet there are some who will look for such a\nreturn, not having the breadth of the impersonal view. It is absolutely\nnecessary to the work that I should have the enthusiastic love of as many as\npossible, while I myself remain entirely impersonal. Otherwise jealousy and\nquarrels would break up everything. A leader must be impersonal. I am sure\nyou understand this. I do not mean that one should be a brute, making use of\nthe  devotion  of  others for his own ends, and laughing in his sleeve\nmeanwhile. What I mean is what I am, intensely personal in my love, but\nhaving the power to pluck out my own heart with my own hand, if it becomes\nnecessary, \"for the good of many, for the welfare of many\", as Buddha said.\nMadness of love, and yet in it no bondage. Matter changed into spirit by the\nforce of love. Nay, that is the gist of our Vedanta. There is but One, seen\nby  the  ignorant  as  matter,  by the wise as God. And the history of\ncivilisation is the progressive reading of spirit into matter. The ignorant\nsee  the person in the non-person. The sage sees the non-person in the\nperson. Through pain and pleasure, joy and sorrow, this is the one lesson we\nare learning. . . .\n", "Yours ever with love and truth,\n", "\nVIVEKANANDA.\n"]
