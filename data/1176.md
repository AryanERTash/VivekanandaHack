["\n", "CXXXII\n", "To Sister Christine\n", "THE MATH, BELUR,\n", "DIST. HOWRAH, BENGAL, INDIA,\n", "26th January 1899.\n", "MY DEAR CHRISTINA,\n", "Excuse this long delay in replying to your very beautiful note. The fact is,\nI was once more in the vale of death. The old diabetes has now disappeared.\nIn its place has come what some doctors call asthma, others dyspepsia, owing\nto nervous prostration. However, it is a most worrying disease, giving one\nthe  sensation  of suffocation \u2014 sometimes for days. I am best only in\nCalcutta; so I am here for rest and quiet and low diet. If I get well by\nMarch, I am going to start for Europe. Mrs. Bull and others are gone; sorry\nI could not accompany them owing to this disease.\n", "I have carefully weighed your plans for coming over. I will be ever so glad\nto see you, you know it well; but, my dear, the Indian summer will not suit\nyou, and if you start now it will be midsummer when you reach India. Then,\nyou must not hope of making any living here. It is impossible for me to make\na living most times in my own country. Then all the surroundings are so, so\nwretched and different from what you see around you, e.g. you will find me\ngoing  about in loin-cloth \u2014 will that shock you? Three-fourths of the\npopulation only wearing a strip of white cloth about their loins \u2014 can you\nbear that?\n", "I must stop here; I am so weak. If I do not get well by March, I will write\nyou to come, for I wish it ever so much to see you once before I pass away.\n", "Do not be the least anxious, dear. Things must be as \"Mother\" wishes. Ours\nis only to obey and work.\n", "Ever yours in the Lord,\n", "VIVEKANANDA.\n", "PS. Mrs. Bull will reach Cambridge, Mass., soon. You may write to her there\non the particulars.\n", "Yours,\n", "V.\n", "PPS. I have again lost your address. Please give the correct one in your\nnext.\n", "V.\n"]
