["\n", "(RECORDED BY MISS S. E. WALDO, A DISCIPLE)\n", "TUESDAY, July 30, 1895.\n", "Christs and Buddhas are simply occasions upon which to objectify our own\ninner powers. We really answer our own prayers.\n", "It is blasphemy to think that if Jesus had never been born, humanity would\nnot have been saved. It is horrible to forget thus the divinity in human\nnature, a divinity that must come out. Never forget the glory of human\nnature. We are the greatest God that ever was or ever will be. Christs and\nBuddhas are but waves on the boundless ocean which I am. Bow down to nothing\nbut your own higher Self. Until you know that you are that very God of gods,\nthere will never be any freedom for you.\n", "All  our past actions are really good, because they lead us to what we\nultimately become. Of whom to beg? I am the real existence, and all else is\na dream save as it is I. I am the whole ocean; do not call the little wave\nyou have made \"I\"; know it for nothing but a wave. Satyak\u00e2ma (lover of\ntruth)  heard  the inner voice telling him, \"You are the infinite, the\nuniversal is in you. Control yourself and listen to the voice of your true\nSelf.\"\n", "The great prophets who do the fighting have to be less perfect than those\nwho live silent lives of holiness, thinking great thoughts and so helping\nthe  world. These men, passing out one after another, produce as final\noutcome the man of power who preaches.\n", "*            *            *\n", "Knowledge exists, man only discovers it. The Vedas are the eternal knowledge\nthrough which God created the world. They talk high philosophy \u2014 the highest\n\u2014 and make this tremendous claim. . . .\n", "Tell the truth boldly, whether it hurts or not. Never pander to weakness. If\ntruth is too much for intelligent people and sweeps them away, let them go;\nthe sooner the better. Childish ideas are for babies and savages; and these\nare not all in the nursery and the forests, some of them have fallen into\nthe pulpits.\n", "It is bad to stay in the church after you are grown up spiritually. Come out\nand die in the open air of freedom.\n", "All progression is in the relative world. The human form is the highest and\nman the greatest being, because here and now we can get rid of the relative\nworld entirely, can actually attain freedom, and this is the goal. Not only\nwe can, but some have reached perfection; so no matter what finer bodies\ncome, they could only be on the relative plane and could do no more than we,\nfor to attain freedom is all that can be done.\n", "The angels never do wicked deeds, so they never get punished and never get\nsaved. Blows are what awaken us and help to break the dream. They show us\nthe insufficiency of this world and make us long to escape, to have freedom.\n. . .\n", "A thing dimly perceived we call by one name; the same thing when fully\nperceived we call by another. The higher the moral nature, the higher the\nperception and the stronger the will.\n", "TUESDAY AFTERNOON.\n", "The reason of the harmony between thought and matter is that they are two\nsides of one thing, call it \"x\", which divides itself into the internal and\nthe external.\n", "The English word \"paradise\" comes from the Sanskrit para-desa, which was\ntaken over into the Persian language and means literally \"the land beyond\",\nor the other world. The old Aryans always believed in a soul, never that man\nwas the body. Their heavens and hells were all temporary, because no effect\ncan outlast its cause and no cause is eternal; therefore all effects must\ncome to an end.\n", "The whole of the Vedanta Philosophy is in this story: Two birds of golden\nplumage sat on the same tree. The one above, serene, majestic, immersed in\nhis own glory; the one below restless and eating the fruits of the tree, now\nsweet, now bitter. Once he ate an exceptionally bitter fruit, then he paused\nand looked up at the majestic bird above; but he soon forgot about the other\nbird and went on eating the fruits of the tree as before. Again he ate a\nbitter fruit, and this time he hopped up a few boughs nearer to the bird at\nthe top. This happened many times until at last the lower bird came to the\nplace of the upper bird and lost himself. He found all at once that there\nhad never been two birds, but that he was all the time that upper bird,\nserene, majestic, and immersed in his own glory.\n"]
