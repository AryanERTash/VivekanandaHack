["\n", "CVI\n", "To Mrs. Ole Bull\n", "ALAMBAZAR MATH\n", "CALCUTTA\n", "[DARJEELING]\n", "26th March 1897\n", "DEAR MRS. BULL \u2014\n", "The demonstrations and national jubilations over me are over \u2014 at least I\nhad to cut them short, as my health broke completely down. The result of\nthis steady work in the West and the tremendous work of a month in India\nupon the Bengalee constitution is \"diabetes\". It is a hereditary foe and is\ndestined to carry me off, at best, in a few years' time. Eating only meat\nand drinking no water seems to be the only way to prolong life \u2014 and, above\nall, perfect rest for the brain. I am giving my brain the needed rest in\nDarjeeling, from where I am writing you now.\n", "I am so glad to hear about Saradananda's success. Give him my best love and\ndo not allow him [to] do too much work. The Bengalee body is not the same as\nthe American.\n", "Mr. Chatterjy (Mohini) came to see me in Calcutta, and he was very friendly.\nI gave him your message. He is quite willing to work with me. Nothing more\nto write, only I am bent upon seeing my monastery started; and as soon as\nthat is done, I come to America once more.\n", "By the by, I will send to you a young lady from England \u2014 one Gertrude\nOrchard. She has been a governess, but she has talent in art etc., and I\nwished her to try her chance in America. I will give her a letter to you and\nMrs. [Florence] Adams.\n", "With my love to Mrs. Adams, Miss Thursby, Miss Farmer (the noble sister) and\nall the rest of our friends.\n", "With eternal love and gratitude,\n", "Yours affectionately,\n", "VIVEKANANDA\n"]
