["\n", "XLVII\n", "To Miss Mary Hale\n", "\n", "C/O MISS DUTCHER,\n", "THOUSAND ISLAND PARK, N.Y.\n", "26th June, 1895.\n", "DEAR SISTER,\n", "Many thanks for the Indian mail. It brought a good deal of good news. You\nare enjoying by this time, I hope, the articles by Prof. Max M\u00fcller on the\n\"Immortality of the Soul\" which I sent to Mother Church. The old man has\ntaken in Vedanta, bones and all, and has boldly come out. I am so glad to\nknow the arrival of the rugs. Was there any duty to pay? If so I will pay\nthat, I insist on it. There will come another big packet from the Raja of\nKhetri containing some shawls and brocades and nick-nacks. I want to present\nthem to different friends. But they are not going to arrive before some\nmonths, I am sure.\n", "I am asked again and again, as you will find in the letters from India, to\ngo over. They are getting desperate. Now if I go to Europe, I will go as the\nguest  of Mr. Francis Leggett of N.Y. He will travel all over Germany,\nEngland, France, and Switzerland for six weeks. From there I shall go to\nIndia, or I may return to America. I have a seed planted here and wish it to\ngrow. This winter's work in N.Y. was splendid, and it may die if I suddenly\ngo over to India, so I am not sure about going to India soon.\n", "Nothing noticeable has happened during this visit to the Thousand Islands.\nThe scenery is very beautiful and I have some of my friends here with me to\ntalk about God and soul ad libitum. I am eating fruits and drinking milk and\nso forth, and studying huge Sanskrit books on Vedanta which they have kindly\nsent me from India.\n", "If I come to Chicago I cannot come at least within six weeks or more. Baby\nneedn't alter any of her plans for me. I will see you all somehow or other\nbefore I go.\n", "You fussed so much over my reply to Madras, but it has produced a tremendous\neffect  there.  A late speech by the President of the Madras Christian\nCollege, Mr. Miller, embodies a large amount of my ideas and declares that\nthe West is in need of Hindu ideas of God and man and calls upon the young\nmen to go and preach to the West. This has created quite a furore of course\namongst the Missions. What you allude to as being published in the Arena I\ndid not see a bit of it. The women did not make any fuss over me at all in\nNew York. Your friend must have drawn on his imagination. They were not of\nthe \"bossing\" type at all. I hope Father Pope will go to Europe and Mother\nChurch too. Travelling is the best thing in life. I am afraid I shall die if\nmade to stick to one place for a long time. Nothing like a nomadic life!\n", "The more the shades around deepen, the more the ends approach and the more\none understands the true meaning of life, that it is a dream; and we begin\nto understand the failure of everyone to grasp it, for they only attempted\nto get meaning out of the meaningless. To get reality out of a dream is\nboyish enthusiasm. \"Everything is evanescent, everything is changeful\" \u2014\nknowing this, the sage gives up both pleasure and pain and becomes a witness\nof this panorama (the universe) without attaching himself to anything.\n", "\"They indeed have conquered Heaven even in this life whose mind has become\nfixed in sameness. God is pure and same to all, therefore they are said to\nbe in God\" (Gita, V.19). Desire, ignorance, and inequality \u2014 this is the\ntrinity of bondage.\n", "Denial of the will to live, knowledge, and same-sightedness is the trinity\nof liberation.\n", "Freedom is the goal of the universe.\n", "\"Nor love nor hate nor pleasure nor pain nor death nor life nor religion nor\nirreligion: not this, not this, not this.\"\n", "Yours ever,\n", "\nVIVEKANANDA.\n"]
