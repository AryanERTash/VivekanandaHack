["\n", "CXXVII\n", "To Miss Josephine MacLeod\n", "DARJEELING,\n", "29th April, 1898.\n", "MY DEAR JOE JOE,\n", "I have had several attacks of fever, the last being influenza.\n", "It has left me now, only I am very weak yet. As soon as I gather strength\nenough to undertake the journey, I come down to Calcutta.\n", "On  Sunday  I  leave Darjeeling, probably stopping for a day or two at\nKurseong, then direct to Calcutta. Calcutta must be very hot just now. Never\nmind, it is all the better for influenza. In case the plague breaks out in\nCalcutta, I must not go anywhere; and you start for Kashmir with Sadananda.\nHow did you like the old gentleman, Devendra Nath Tagore? Not as stylish as\n\"Hans  Baba\" with Moon God and Sun God of course. What enlightens your\ninsides on a dark night when the Fire God, Sun God, Moon God, and Star\nGoddesses have gone to sleep? It is hunger that keeps my consciousness up, I\nhave discovered. Oh, the great doctrine of correspondence of light! Think\nhow  dark  the  world has been all these ages without it! And all this\nknowledge and love and work and all the Buddhas and Krishnas and Christs \u2014\nvain, vain have been their lives and work, for they did not discover that\n\"which keeps the inner light when the Sun and Moon were gone to the limbo\"\nfor the night! Delicious, isn't it?\n", "If the plague comes to my native city, I am determined to make myself a\nsacrifice; and that I am sure is a \"Darn sight, better way to Nirv\u00e2na\" than\npouring oblations to all that ever twinkled.\n", "I have had a good deal of correspondence with Madras with the result that I\nneed not send them any help just now. On the other hand I am going to start\na paper in Calcutta. I will be ever so much obliged if you help me starting\nthat. As always with undying love,\n", "Ever yours in the Lord,\n", "\nVIVEKANANDA.\n"]
