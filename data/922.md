["\n", "LXXV\n", "To the Hale sisters\n", "HIGH VIEW, READING,\n", "20th April, 1896.\n", "DEAR SISTERS,\n", "Greetings to you from the other shore. The voyage has been pleasant and no\nsickness this time. I gave myself treatment to avoid it. I made quite a\nlittle run through Ireland and some of the Old English towns and now am once\nmore in Reading amidst Brahma and Maya and Jiva, the individual and the\nuniversal soul, etc. The other monk is here; he is one of the nicest of men\nI see, and is quite a learned monk too. We are busy editing books now.\nNothing of importance happened on the way. It was dull, monotonous, and\nprosaic as my life. I love America more when I am out of it. And, after all,\nthose years there have been some of the best I have yet seen.\n", "Are you trying to get some subscribers for the Brahmavadin? Give my best\nlove and kindest remembrance to Mrs. Adams and Mrs. Conger. Write me as soon\nas is convenient all about yourselves, and what you are doing, what breaks\nthe monotony of eating, drinking, and cycling. I am in a hurry just now,\nshall write a bigger letter later; so good-bye and may you be always happy.\n", "Your ever affectionate brother,\n", "VIVEKANANDA.\n", "PS. I will write to Mother Church as soon as I get time. Give my love to Sam\nand sister Locke.\n", "V.\n"]
