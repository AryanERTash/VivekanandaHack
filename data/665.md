["\n", "(RECORDED BY MISS S. E. WALDO, A DISCIPLE)\n", "TUESDAY, July 16, 1895. (Shankara)\n", "The \"unseen cause\" (Or mass of subtle impressions.) leads us to sacrifice\nand worship, which in turn produce seen results; but to attain liberation we\nmust first hear, then think or reason, and then meditate upon Brahman.\n", "The result of works and the result of knowledge are two different things.\n\"Do\" and \"Do not do\" are the background of all morality, but they really\nbelong  only  to  the  body and the mind. All happiness and misery are\ninextricably connected with the senses, and body is necessary to experience\nthem. The higher the body, the higher the standard of virtue, even up to\nBrahma; but all have bodies. As long as there is a body, there must be\npleasure and pain; only when one has got rid of the body can one escape\nthem. The Atman is bodiless, says Shankara.\n", "No law can make you free, you are free. Nothing can give you freedom, if you\nhave it not already. The Atman is self-illumined. Cause and effect do not\nreach there, and this disembodiedness is freedom. Beyond what was, or is, or\nis to be, is Brahman. As an effect, freedom would have no value; it would be\na compound, and as such would contain the seeds of bondage. It is the one\nreal factor. Not to be attained, hut the real nature of the soul.\n", "Work and worship, however, are necessary to take away the veil, to lift oh\nthe bondage and illusion. They do not give us freedom; but all the same,\nwithout effort on our own part we do not open our eyes and see what we are.\nShankara says further that Advaita-Vedanta is the crowning glory of the\nVedas; hut the lower Vedas are also necessary, because they teach work and\nworship, and through these many come to the Lord. Others may come without\nany help but Advaita. Work and worship lead to the same result as Advaita.\n", "Books cannot teach God, but they can destroy ignorance; their action is\nnegative. To hold to the books and at the same time open the way to freedom\nis  Shankara's  great  achievement.  But  after  all,  it is a kind of\nhair-splitting. Give man first the concrete, then raise him to the highest\nby slow degrees. This is the effort of the various religions and explains\ntheir existence and why each is suited to some stage of development. The\nvery books are a part of the ignorance they help to dispel. Their duty is to\ndrive out the ignorance that has come upon knowledge. \"Truth shall drive out\nuntruth.\" You are free and cannot he made so. So long as you have a creed,\nyou have no God. \"He who knows he knows, knows nothing.\" Who can know the\nKnower? There are two eternal facts in existence, God and the universe, the\nformer unchangeable, the latter changeable. The world exists eternally.\nWhere your mind cannot grasp the amount of change, you call it eternally. .\n. . You see the stone or the bas-relief on it, but not both at once; yet\nboth are one.\n", "*    *    *\n", "Can you make yourself at rest even for a second? All Yogis say you can. . .\n.\n", "The greatest sin is to think yourself weak. No one is greater: realise you\nare Brahman. Nothing has power except what you give it. We are beyond the\nsun, the stars, the universe. Teach the Godhood of man. Deny evil, create\nnone. Stand up and say, I am the master, the master of all. We forge the\nchain, and we alone can break it.\n", "No action can give you freedom; only knowledge can make you free, Knowledge\nis irresistible; the mind cannot take it or reject it. When it comes the\nmind has to accept it; so it is not a work of the mind; only, its expression\ncomes in the mind.\n", "Work or worship is to bring you back to your own nature. It is an entire\nillusion that the Self is the body; so even while living here in the body,\nwe can be free. The body has nothing in common with the Self. Illusion is\ntaking the real for the unreal \u2014 not \"nothing at all\".\n"]
