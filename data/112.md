["\n", "CHAPTER VIII\n", "WORSHIP OF SUBSTITUTES AND IMAGES\n", "\nThe next points to be considered are the worship of Pratikas or of things\nmore  or  less satisfactory as substitutes for God, and the worship of\nPratim\u00e2s or images. What is the worship of God through a Pratika?\n", "\u2014 Joining the mind with devotion to that which is not Brahman, taking it to\nbe Brahman\" \u2014 says Bhagav\u00e2n R\u00e2m\u00e2nuja. \"Worship the mind as Brahman this is\ninternal; and the \u00c2k\u00e2sha as Brahman, this is with regard to the Devas\", says\nShankara. The mind is an internal Pratika, the Akasha is an external one,\nand  both  have  to be worshipped as substitutes of God. He continues,\n\"Similarly \u2014 'the Sun is Brahman, this is the command', 'He who worships\nName as Brahman' \u2014 in all such passages the doubt arises as to the worship\nof  Pratikas.\" The word Pratika means going towards; and worshipping a\nPratika is worshipping something as a substitute which is, in some one or\nmore respects, like Brahman more and more, but is not Brahman. Along with\nthe Pratikas mentioned in the Shrutis there are various others to be found\nin the Pur\u00e2nas and the Tantras. In this kind of Pratika-worship may be\nincluded all the various forms of Pitri-worship and Deva-worship.\n", "\nNow worshipping Ishvara and Him alone is Bhakti; the worship of anything\nelse \u2014 Deva, or Pitri, or any other being \u2014 cannot be Bhakti. The various\nkinds of worship of the various Devas are all to be included in ritualistic\nKarma, which gives to the worshipper only a particular result in the form of\nsome celestial enjoyment, but can neither give rise to Bhakti nor lead to\nMukti. One thing, therefore, has to be carefully borne in mind. If, as it\nmay happen in some cases, the highly philosophic ideal, the supreme Brahman,\nis dragged down by Pratika-worship to the level of the Pratika, and the\nPratika itself is taken to be the Atman of the worshipper or his Antary\u00e2min\n(Inner Ruler), the worshipper gets entirely misled, as no Pratika can really\nbe the Atman of the worshipper.\n", "\nBut where Brahman Himself is the object of worship, and the Pratika stands\nonly as a substitute or a suggestion thereof, that is to say, where, through\nthe Pratika the omnipresent Brahman is worshipped \u2014 the Pratika itself being\nidealised  into  the cause of all, Brahman \u2014 the worship is positively\nbeneficial; nay, it is absolutely necessary for all mankind until they have\nall got beyond the primary or preparatory state of the mind in regard to\nworship. When, therefore, any gods or other beings are worshipped in and for\nthemselves,  such  worship is only a ritualistic Karma; and as a Vidy\u00e2\n(science) it gives us only the fruit belonging to that particular Vidya; but\nwhen  the  Devas  or  any  other beings are looked upon as Brahman and\nworshipped,  the  result obtained is the same as by the worshipping of\nIshvara. This explains how, in many cases, both in the Shrutis and the\nSmritis, a god, or a sage, or some other extraordinary being is taken up and\nlifted, as it were, out of his own nature and idealised into Brahman, and is\nthen worshipped. Says the Advaitin, \"Is not everything Brahman when the name\nand the form have been removed from it?\" \"Is not He, the Lord, the innermost\nSelf of every one?\" says the Vishisht\u00e2dvaitin.\n", "\u2014 \"The fruition of even the worship of Adityas etc. Brahman Himself bestows,\nbecause He is the Ruler of all.\" Says Shankara in his Brahma-Sutra-Bh\u00e2sya\u2014\n", "\"Here in this way does Brahman become the object of worship, because He, as\nBrahman,  is  superimposed  on  the  Pratikas, just as Vishnu etc. are\nsuperimposed upon images etc.\"\n", "\nThe  same ideas apply to the worship of the Pratimas as to that of the\nPratikas; that is to say, if the image stands for a god or a saint, the\nworship is not the result of Bhakti, and does not lead lo liberation; but if\nit stands for the one God, the worship thereof will bring both Bhakti and\nMukti. Of the principal religions of the world we see Vedantism, Buddhism,\nand certain forms of Christianity freely using images; only two religions,\nMohammedanism and Protestantism, refuse such help. Yet the Mohammedans use\nthe grave of their saints and martyrs almost in the place of images; and the\nProtestants, in rejecting all concrete helps to religion, are drifting away\nevery year farther and farther from spirituality till at present there is\nscarcely any difference between the advanced Protestants and the followers\nof  August  Comte,  or  agnostics  who  preach ethics alone. Again, in\nChristianity and Mohammedanism whatever exists of image worship is made to\nfall under that category in which the Pratika or the Pratima is worshipped\nin itself, but not as a \"help to the vision\" (Drishtisaukaryam) of God;\ntherefore it is at best only of the nature of ritualistic Karmas and cannot\nproduce  either  Bhakti  or  Mukti. In this form of image-worship, the\nallegiance  of  the  soul  is given to other things than Ishvara, and,\ntherefore, such use of images, or graves, or temples, or tombs, is real\nidolatry; it is in itself neither sinful nor wicked \u2014 it is a rite \u2014 a\nKarma, and worshippers must and will get the fruit thereof.\n"]
