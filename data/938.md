["\n", "XCI\n", "To Sister Nivedita\n", "ALAMBAZAR MATH,\n", "CALCUTTA,\n", "5th May, 1897.\n", "MY DEAR MISS NOBLE,\n", "Your very very kind, loving, and encouraging letter gave me more strength\nthan you think of.\n", "There are moments when one feels entirely despondent, no doubt \u2014 especially\nwhen one has worked towards an ideal during a whole life's time and just\nwhen there is a bit of hope of seeing it partially accomplished, there comes\na  tremendous  thwarting blow. I do not care for the disease, but what\ndepresses me is that my ideals have not had yet the least opportunity of\nbeing worked out. And you know, the difficulty is money.\n", "The Hindus are making processions and all that, but they cannot give money.\nThe only help I got in the world was in England, from Miss M\u00fcller, and Mr.\nSevier. I thought there that a thousand pounds was sufficient to start at\nleast the principal centre in Calcutta, but my calculation was from the\nexperience of Calcutta ten or twelve years ago. Since then the prices have\ngone up three or four times.\n", "The work has been started anyhow. A rickety old little house has been rented\nfor six or seven shillings, where about twenty-four young men are being\ntrained. I had to go to Darjeeling for a month to recover my health, and I\nam glad to tell you I am very much better, and would you believe it, without\ntaking any medicine, only by the exercise of mental healing! I am going\nagain to another hill station tomorrow, as it is very hot in the plains.\nYour society is still living, I am sure. I will send you a report, as least\nevery month, of the work done here. The London work is not doing well at\nall, I hear, and that was the main reason why I would not come to England\njust now \u2014 although some of our Rajas going for the Jubilee tried their best\nto get me with them \u2014 as I would have to work hard again to revive the\ninterest  in  Vedanta.  And  that  would mean a good deal more trouble\nphysically.\n", "I may come over for a month or so very soon however. Only if I could see my\nwork started here, how gladly and freely would I travel about!\n", "So  far  about work. Now about you personally. Such love and faith and\ndevotion and appreciation like yours, dear Miss Noble, repays a hundred\ntimes  over  any  amount of labour one undergoes in this life. May all\nblessings be yours. My whole life is at your service, as we may say in our\nmother tongue.\n", "It never was and never will be anything but very very welcome, any letters\nfrom you and other friends in England. Mr. and Mrs. Hammond wrote two very\nkind and nice letters and Mr. Hammond a beautiful poem in The Brahmavadin,\nalthough I did not deserve it a bit. I will write to you again from the\nHimalayas, where thought will be clear in sight of the snows and the nerves\nmore settled than in this burning plains. Miss M\u00fcller is already in Almora.\nMr. and Mrs. Sevier go to Simla. They have been in Darjeeling so long. So\nthings come and go, dear friend. Only the Lord is unchangeable and He is\nLove. May He make our heart His eternal habitation is the constant prayer\nof,\n", "VIVEKANANDA.\n"]
