["\n", "THE MESSAGE OF DIVINE WISDOM\n", "\n[The following three chapters were discovered among Swami Vivekananda's papers. He evidently intended to write a book and jotted down some points for the work.]\n", "\n", "\n", "1.  Desire is infinite, its fulfilment limited. Desire is unlimited in\neveryone; the power of fulfilment varies. Thus some are more successful than\nothers in life.\n", "2. This limitation is the bondage we are struggling against all our lives.\n", "3. We desire only the pleasurable, not the painful.\n", "4. The objects of desire are all complex\u2014pleasure-giving and pain-bringing\nmixed up.\n", "5. We do not or cannot see the painful parts in objects, we are charmed with\nonly  the  pleasurable portion; and, thus grasping the pleasurable, we\nunwittingly draw in the painful.\n", "6. At times we vainly hope that in our case only the pleasurable will come,\nleaving the painful aside, which never happens.\n", "7. Our desires also are constantly changing\u2014what we would prize today we\nwould reject tomorrow. The pleasure of the present will be the pain of the\nfuture, the loved hated, and so on.\n", "8. We vainly hope that in the future life we shall be able to gather in only\nthe pleasurable, to the exclusion of the painful.\n", "9. The future is only the extension of the present. Such a thing cannot be!\n", "10. Whosoever seeks pleasure in objects will get it, but he must take the\npain with it.\n", "11. All objective pleasure in the long run must bring pain, because of the\nfact of change or death.\n", "12. Death is the goal of all objects, change is the nature of all objective\nthings.\n", "13. As desire increases, so increases the power of pleasure, so the power of\npain.\n", "14. The finer the organism, the higher the culture\u2014the greater is the\npower to enjoy pleasure and the sharper are the pangs of pain.\n", "15. Mental pleasures are greatly superior to physical joys. Mental pains are\nmore poignant than physical tortures.\n", "16. The power of thought, of looking far away into the future, and the power\nof memory, of recalling the past to the present, make us live in heaven;\nthey make us live in hell also.\n", "17. The man who can collect the largest amount of pleasurable objects around\nhim  is  as  a  rule too unimaginative to enjoy them. The man of great\nimagination is thwarted by the intensity of his feeling of loss, or fear of\nloss, or perception of defects.\n", "18. We are struggling hard to conquer pain, succeeding in the attempt, and\nyet creating new pains at the same time.\n", "19. We achieve success, and we are overthrown by failure; we pursue pleasure\nand we are pursued by pain.\n", "20. We say we do, we are made to do. We say we work, we are made to labour.\nWe say we live, we are made to die every moment. We are in the crowd, we\ncannot stop, must go on\u2014it deserves no cheering. Had it not been so, no\namount of cheering would make us undertake all this pain and misery for a\ngrain of pleasure\u2014which, alas, in most cases is only a hope!\n", "21. Our pessimism is a dread reality, our optimism is a faint cheering,\nmaking the best of a bad job.\n", "1. The law is never separate from the phenomena, the principle from the\nperson.\n", "2. The law is the method of action or poise of every single phenomenon\nwithin its scope.\n", "3. We get our knowledge of law from the massing and welding of changes that\noccur. We never see law beyond these changes. The idea of law as something\nseparate from phenomena is a mental abstraction, a convenient use of words\nand nothing more. Law is a part of every change within its range, a manner\nwhich resides in the things governed by the law. The power resides in the\nthings, is a part of our idea of that thing\u2014its action upon something else\nis in a certain manner\u2014this is our law.\n", "4. Law is in the actual state of things\u2014it is in how they act towards each\nother, and not in how they should. It might have been better if fire did not\nburn or water wet; but that they do\u2014this is the law; and if it is a true\nlaw, a fire that does not burn or water that does not wet is neither fire\nnor water.\n", "5. Spiritual laws, ethical laws, social laws, national laws\u2014are laws if\nthey are parts of existing spiritual and human units and the unfailing\nexperience of the action of every unit said to be bound by such laws.\n", "6. We, by turn, are made by law and make it. A generalization of what man\ndoes invariably in certain circumstances is a law with regard to man in that\nparticular aspect. It is the invariable, universal human action that is law\nfor man\u2014and which no individual can escape\u2014and yet the summation of the\naction  of each individual is the universal Law. The sum total, or the\nuniversal,  or  the  infinite  is fashioning the individual, while the\nindividual is keeping by its action the Law alive. Law in this sense is\nanother  name  for  the universal. The universal is dependent upon the\nindividual, the individual dependent upon the universal. It is an infinite\nmade  up  of finite parts, an infinite of number, though involving the\ndifficulty  of assuming an infinity summed up of finites\u2014yet for all\npractical purposes, it is a fact before us. And as the law, or whole, or the\ninfinite cannot be destroyed\u2014and the destruction of a part of an infinite\nis  an  impossibility, as we cannot either add anything to or subtract\nanything from the infinite\u2014each part persists for ever.\n", "7. Laws regarding the materials of which the body of man is composed have\nbeen found out, and also the persistence of these materials through time has\nbeen shown. The elements which composed the body of a man a hundred thousand\nyears ago have been proved to be still existing in some place or other. The\nthoughts which have been projected also are living in other minds.\n", "8. But the difficulty is to find a law about the man beyond the body.\n", "9. The spiritual and ethical laws are not the method of action of every\nhuman being. The systems of ethics of morality, even of national laws, are\nhonoured more in the breach than in the observance. If they were laws how\ncould they be broken?\n", "10. No man is able to go against the laws of nature. How is it that we\nalways complain of his breaking the moral laws, national laws?\n", "11. The national laws at best are the embodied will of a majority of the\nnation\u2014always a state of things wished for, not actually existing.\n", "12. The ideal law may be that no man should covet the belongings of others,\nbut the actual law is that a very large number do.\n", "13. The\ngreat  and  good  of  the  world  are  those who have the latter power\npredominating. It comprehends\nall\u2014is the essence of all.\nIt is Sat-Chit-\u00c2nanda.\n", "(d) It has no parts, no attributes, neither pleasure nor pain, nor is it\nmatter nor mind. It is the Supreme, Infinite, Impersonal Self in everything,\nthe Infinite Ego of the Universe.\n", "(e) It is the Reality in me, in thee, and in everything\u2014therefore,\n\"That thou art\"\u2014Tattvamasi.\n", "2. The same Impersonal is conceived by the mind as the Creator, the Ruler,\nand the Dissolver of this universe, its material as well as its efficient\ncause, the Supreme Ruler\u2014the Living, the Loving, the Beautiful, in the\nhighest sense.\n", "(a)  The Absolute Being is manifested in Its highest in Isvara, or the\nSupreme Ruler, as the highest and omnipotent Life or Energy.\n", "(b) The Absolute Knowledge is manifesting Itself in Its highest as Infinite\nLove, in the Supreme Lord.\n", "(c) The Absolute Bliss is manifested as the Infinite Beautiful, in the\nSupreme Lord.
