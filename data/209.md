["\n", "TO MY BRAVE BOYS[1]\n", "\nPush on with the organization. Nothing else is necessary but these \u2014 love,\nsincerity, and patience. What is life but growth, i.e. expansion, i.e. love?\nTherefore all love is life, it is the only law of life; all selfishness is\ndeath, and this is true here or hereafter. It is life to do good, it is\ndeath not to do good to others. Ninety per cent of human brutes you see are\ndead, are ghosts \u2014 for none lives, my boys, but he who loves. Feel, my\nchildren, feel; feel for the poor, the ignorant, the downtrodden; feel till\nthe heart stops and the brain reels and you think you will go mad \u2014 then\npour the soul out at the feet of the Lord, and then will come power, help,\nand indomitable energy. Struggle, struggle, was my motto for the last ten\nyears. Struggle, still say I. When it was all dark, I used to say, struggle;\nwhen  light  is  breaking in, I still say, struggle. Be not afraid, my\nchildren. Look not up in that attitude of fear towards that infinite starry\nvault as if it would crush you. Wait! In a few hours more, the whole of it\nwill be under your feet. Wait, money does not pay, nor name; fame does not\npay, nor learning. It is love that pays; it is character that cleaves its\nway through adamantine walls of difficulties.\n", "\nNow the question before us is this. There cannot be any growth without\nliberty. Our ancestors freed religious thought, and we have a wonderful\nreligion. But they put a heavy chain on the feet of society, and our society\nis, in a word, horrid, diabolical. In the West, society always had freedom,\nand look at them. On the other hand, look at their religion.\n", "\nLiberty is the first condition of growth. Just as man must have liberty to\nthink and speak, so he must have liberty in food, dress, and marriage, and\nin every other thing, so long as he does not injure others.\n", "\nWe talk foolishly against material civilisation. The grapes are sour. Even\ntaking all that foolishness for granted, in all India there are, say, a\nhundred  thousand  really  spiritual  men  and  women.  Now,  for  the\nspiritualisation of these, must three hundred millions be sunk in savagery\nand starvation? Why should any starve? How was it possible for the Hindus to\nhave been conquered by the Mohammedans? It was due to the Hindus' ignorance\nof  material  civilization.  Even  the Mohammedans taught them to wear\ntailor-made clothes. Would the Hindus had learnt from the Mohammedans how to\neat in a cleanly way without mixing their food with the dust of the streets!\nMaterial civilization, nay, even luxury, is necessary to create work for the\npoor. Bread! Bread! I do not believe in a God, who cannot give me bread\nhere, giving me eternal bliss in heaven! Pooh! India is to be raised, the\npoor are to be fed, education is to be spread, and the evil of priestcraft\nis  to be removed. No priestcraft, no social tyranny! More bread, more\nopportunity for everybody! Our young fools organise meetings to get more\npower from the English. They only laugh. None deserves liberty who is not\nready to give liberty. Suppose the English give over to you all the power.\nWhy, the powers that be then, will hold the people down, and let them not\nhave it. Slaves want power to make slaves.\n", "\nNow,  this is to be brought about slowly, and by only insisting on our\nreligion and giving liberty to society. Root up priestcraft from the old\nreligion, and you get the best religion in the world. Do you understand me?\nCan you make a European society with India's religion? I believe it is\npossible, and must be.\n", "\nThe grand plan is to start a colony in Central India, where you can follow\nyour own ideas independently, and then a little leaven will leaven all. In\nthe meanwhile form a Central Association and go on branching off all over\nIndia. Start only on religious grounds now, and do not preach any violent\nsocial reform at present; only do not countenance foolish superstitions. Try\nto revive society on the old grounds of universal salvation and equality as\nlaid  down  by  the old Masters, such as Shankar\u00e2ch\u00e2rya, R\u00e2m\u00e2nuja, and\nChaitanya.\n", "\nHave fire and spread all over. Work, work. Be the servant while leading. Be\nunselfish, and never listen to one friend in private accusing another.\nHave infinite patience, and success is yours.\n", "\nNow take care of this: Do not try to \"boss\" others, as the Yankees say.\nBecause I always direct my letters to you, you need not try to show your\nconsequence over my other friends. I know you never can be such a fool, but\nstill I think it my duty to warn you. This is what kills all organizations.\nWork, work, for, to work only for the good of others is life.\n", "\nI want that there should be no hypocrisy, no Jesuitism, no roguery. I have\ndepended always on the Lord, always on Truth broad as the light of day. Let\nme not die with stains on my conscience for having played Jesuitism to get\nup  name  or fame, or even to do good. There should not be a breath of\nimmorality, nor a stain of policy which is bad.\n", "\nNo shilly-shally, no esoteric blackguardism, no secret humbug, nothing\nshould be done in a corner. No special favouritism of the Master, no Master\nat that, even. Onward, my brave boys \u2014 money or no money \u2014 men or no men!\nHave you love? Have you God? Onward and forward to the breach, you are\nirresistible.\n", "\nHow absurd! The Theosophical magazines saying that they, the Theosophists,\nprepared the way to my success! Indeed! Pure nonsense! Theosophists prepared\nthe way!\n", "\nTake care! Beware of everything that is untrue; stick to truth and we shall\nsucceed, maybe slowly, but surely. Work on as if I never existed. Work as if\non each of you depended the whole work. Fifty centuries are looking on you,\nthe future of India depends on you. Work on. I do not know when I shall be\nable to come. This is a great field for work. They can at best praise in\nIndia, but they will not give a cent for anything; and where shall they get\nit, beggars themselves? Then, they have lost the faculty of doing public\ngood for the last two thousand years or more.
