["\n", "XXVI\n", "U. S. A.,\n1894.\n", "Dear Dharmapala,\n", "\nI have forgotten your address in Calcutta; so I direct this to the Math. I\nheard about your speeches in Calcutta and how wonderful was the effect\nproduced  by them. A certain retired missionary here wrote me a letter\naddressing me as brother and then hastily went to publish my short answer\nand make a show. But you know what people here think of such gentlemen.\nMoreover, the same missionary went privately to some of my friends to ask\nthem not to befriend me. Of course he met with universal contempt. I am\nquite astonished at this man's behaviour \u2014 a preacher of religion to take to\nsuch underhand dealings! Unfortunately too much of that in every country and\nin every religion. Last winter I travelled a good deal in this country\nalthough the weather was very severe. I thought it would be dreadful, but I\ndid not find it so after all. You remember Col. Neggenson, President of the\nFree Religious Society. He makes very kind inquiries about you. I met Dr.\nCarpenter of Oxford (England) the other day. He delivered an address on the\nethics of Buddhism at Plymouth. It was very sympathetic and scholarly. He\nmade inquiries about you and your paper. Hope, your noble work will succeed.\nYou are a worthy servant of Him who came Bahujana Hit\u00e2ya Bahujana Sukh\u00e2ya\n(for the good of the many, for the happiness of the many).\n", "\n. . . The Christianity that is preached in India is quite different from\nwhat one sees here; you will be astonished to hear, Dharmapala, that I have\nfriends  in  this country amongst the clergy of the Episcopal and even\nPresbyterian churches, who are as broad, as liberal, and as sincere as you\nare in your own religion. The real spiritual man is broad everywhere. His\nlove forces him to be so. Those to whom religion is a trade are forced to\nbecome narrow and mischievous by their introduction into religion of the\ncompetitive, fighting, and selfish methods of the world.\n", "\n", "Yours ever in brotherly love,\nVivekananda.\n", "\n"]
