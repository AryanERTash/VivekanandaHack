["\n", "THE MISSION OF THE VEDANTA\n", "\nOn the occasion of his visit to Kumbakonam, the Swamiji was presented with\nthe following address by the local Hindu community:\n", "\n Revered Swamin ,\n", "\nOn behalf of the Hindu inhabitants of this ancient and religiously important\ntown of Kumbakonam, we request permission to offer you a most hearty welcome\non your return from the Western World to our own holy land of great temples\nand  famous  saints  and  sages. We are highly thankful to God for the\nremarkable success of your religious mission in America and in Europe, and\nfor His having enabled you to impress upon the choicest representatives of\nthe  world's  great religions assembled at Chicago that both the Hindu\nphilosophy and religion are so broad and so rationally catholic as to have\nin them the power to exalt and to harmonise all ideas of God and of human\nspirituality.\n", "\nThe conviction that the cause of Truth is always safe in the hands of Him\nwho is the life and soul of the universe has been for thousands of years\npart of our living faith; and if today we rejoice at the results of your\nholy work in Christian lands, it is because the eyes of men in and outside\nof India are thereby being opened to the inestimable value of the spiritual\nheritage of the preeminently religious Hindu nation. The success of your\nwork has naturally added great lustre to the already renowned name of your\ngreat Guru; it has also raised us in the estimation of the civilised world;\nmore than all, it has made us feel that we too, as a people, have reason to\nbe proud of the achievements of our past, and that the absence of telling\naggressiveness in our civilisation is in no way a sign of its exhausted or\ndecaying condition. With clear-sighted, devoted, and altogether unselfish\nworkers like you in our midst, the future of the Hindu nation cannot but be\nbright and hopeful. May the God of the universe who is also the great God of\nall nations bestow on you health and long life, and make you increasingly\nstrong and wise in the discharge of your high and noble function as a worthy\nteacher of Hindu religion and philosophy.\n", "\n", "\nA second address was also presented by the Hindu students of the town.\n", "\nThe  Swami  then delivered the following address on the Mission of the\nVedanta:\n", "\nA very small amount of religious work performed brings a large amount of\nresult. If this statement of the Gita wanted an illustration, I am finding\nevery day the truth of that great saying in my humble life. My work has been\nvery insignificant indeed, but the kindness and the cordiality of welcome\nthat have met me at every step of my journey from Colombo to this city are\nsimply beyond all expectation. Yet, at the same time, it is worthy of our\ntraditions as Hindus, it is worthy of our race; for here we are, the Hindu\nrace, whose vitality, whose life-principle, whose very soul, as it were, is\nin religion. I have seen a little of the world, travelling among the races\nof the East and the West; and everywhere I find among nations one great\nideal which forms the backbone, so to speak, of that race. With some it is\npolitics,  with  others  it  is  social culture; others again may have\nintellectual culture and so on for their national background. But this, our\nmotherland, has religion and religion alone for its basis, for its backbone,\nfor the bed-rock upon which the whole building of its life has been based.\nSome of you may remember that in my reply to the kind address which the\npeople of Madras sent over to me in America, I pointed out the fact that a\npeasant in India has, in many respects, a better religious education than\nmany a gentleman in the West, and today, beyond all doubt, I myself am\nverifying my own words. There was a time when I did feel rather discontented\nat the want of information among the masses of India and the lack of thirst\namong them for information, but now I understand it. Where their interest\nlies, there they are more eager for information than the masses of any other\nrace that I have seen or have travelled among. Ask our peasants about the\nmomentous political changes in Europe, the upheavals that are going on in\nEuropean society \u2014 they do not know anything of them, nor do they care to\nknow; but the peasants, even in Ceylon, detached from India in many ways,\ncut off from a living interest in India \u2014 I found the very peasants working\nin the fields there were already acquainted with the fact that there had\nbeen a Parliament of Religions in America, that an Indian Sannyasin had gone\nover there, and that he had had some success.\n", "\nWhere, therefore, their interest is, there they are as eager for information\nas any other race; and religion is the one and sole interest of the people\nof  India. I am not just now discussing whether it is good to have the\nvitality of the race in religious ideals or in political ideals, but so far\nit is clear to us that, for good or for evil, our vitality is concentrated\nin our religion. You cannot change it. You cannot destroy it and put in its\nplace another. You cannot transplant a large growing tree from one soil to\nanother and make it immediately take root there. For good or for evil, the\nreligious ideal has been flowing into India for thousands of years; for good\nor for evil, the Indian atmosphere has been filled with ideals of religion\nfor shining scores of centuries; for good or for evil, we have been born and\nbrought up in the very midst of these ideas of religion, till it has entered\ninto our very blood and tingled with every drop in our veins, and has become\none with our constitution, become the very vitality of our lives. Can you\ngive such religion up without the rousing of the same energy in reaction,\nwithout filling the channel which that mighty river has cut out for itself\nin the course of thousands of years? Do you want that the Gang\u00e2 should go\nback to its icy bed and begin a new course? Even if that were possible, it\nwould be impossible for this country to give up her characteristic course of\nreligious life and take up for herself a new career of politics or something\nelse.  You  can  work only under the law of least resistance, and this\nreligious line is the line of least resistance in India. This is the line of\nlife, this is the line of growth, and this is the line of well-being in\nIndia \u2014 to follow the track of religion.\n", "\nAy, in other countries religion is only one of the many necessities in life.\nTo use a common illustration which I am in the habit of using, my lady has\nmany  things  in her parlour, and it is the fashion nowadays to have a\nJapanese vase, and she must procure it; it does not look well to be without\nit. So my lady, or my gentleman, has many other occupations in life, and\nalso a little bit of religion must come in to complete it. Consequently he\nor she has a little religion. Politics, social improvement, in one word,\nthis world, is the goal of mankind in the West, and God and religion come in\nquietly as helpers to attain that goal. Their God is, so to speak, the Being\nwho helps to cleanse and to furnish this world for them; that is apparently\nall the value of God for them. Do you not know how for the last hundred or\ntwo hundred years you have been hearing again and again out of the lips of\nmen who ought to have known better, from the mouths of those who pretend at\nleast to know better, that all the arguments they produce against the Indian\nreligion is this \u2014 that our religion does not conduce to well-being in this\nworld, that it does not bring gold to us, that it does not make us robbers\nof nations, that it does not make the strong stand upon the bodies of the\nweak and feed themselves with the life-blood of the weak. Certainly our\nreligion does not do that. It cannot send cohorts, under whose feet the\nearth trembles, for the purpose of destruction and pillage and the ruination\nof races. Therefore they say \u2014 what is there in this religion? It does not\nbring any grist to the grinding mill, any strength to the muscles; what is\nthere in such a religion?\n", "\nThey little dream that that is the very argument with which we prove out\nreligion, because it does not make for this world. Ours is the only true\nreligion because, according to it, this little sense-world of three days'\nduration is not to be made the end and aim of all, is not to be our great\ngoal. This little earthly horizon of a few feet is not that which bounds the\nview of our religion. Ours is away beyond, and still beyond; beyond the\nsenses, beyond space, and beyond time, away, away beyond, till nothing of\nthis  world is left and the universe itself becomes like a drop in the\ntranscendent ocean of the glory of the soul. Ours is the true religion\nbecause it teaches that God alone is true, that this world is false and\nfleeting, that all your gold is but as dust, that all your power is finite,\nand that life itself is oftentimes an evil; therefore it is, that ours is\nthe true religion. Ours is the true religion because, above all, it teaches\nrenunciation and stands up with the wisdom of ages to tell and to declare to\nthe nations who are mere children of yesterday in comparison with us Hindus\n\u2014 who own the hoary antiquity of the wisdom, discovered by our ancestors\nhere in India \u2014 to tell them in plain words: \"Children, you are slaves of\nthe senses; there is only finiteness in the senses, there is only ruination\nin the senses; the three short days of luxury here bring only ruin at last.\nGive it all up, renounce the love of the senses and of the world; that is\nthe way of religion.\" Through renunciation is the way to the goal and not\nthrough enjoyment. Therefore ours is the only true religion.\n", "\nAy, it is a curious fact that while nations after nations have come upon the\nstage of the world, played their parts vigorously for a few moments, and\ndied almost without leaving a mark or a ripple on the ocean of time, here we\nare living, as it were, an eternal life. They talk a great deal of the new\ntheories about the survival of the fittest, and they think that it is the\nstrength of the muscles which is the fittest to survive. If that were true,\nany one of the aggressively known old world nations would have lived in\nglory today, and we, the weak Hindus, who never conquered even one other\nrace or nation, ought to have died out; yet we live here three hundred\nmillion strong! (A young English lady once told me: What have the Hindus\ndone? They never even conquered a single race!) And it is not at all true\nthat all its energies are spent, that atrophy has overtaken its body: that\nis not true. There is vitality enough, and it comes out in torrents and\ndeluges the world when the time is ripe and requires it.\n", "\nWe have, as it were, thrown a challenge to the whole world from the most\nancient times. In the West, they are trying to solve the problem how much a\nman can possess, and we are trying here to solve the problem on how little a\nman can live. This struggle and this difference will still go on for some\ncenturies. But if history has any truth in it and if prognostications ever\nprove true, it must be that those who train themselves to live on the least\nand control themselves well will in the end gain the battle, and that those\nwho run after enjoyment and luxury, however vigorous they may seem for the\nmoment, will have to die and become annihilated. There are times in the\nhistory of a man's life, nay, in the history of the lives of nations, when a\nsort of world-weariness becomes painfully predominant. It seems that such a\ntide of world-weariness has come upon the Western world. There, too, they\nhave their thinkers, great men; and they are already finding out that this\nrace after gold and power is all vanity of vanities; many, nay, most of the\ncultured men and women there, are already weary of this competition, this\nstruggle, this brutality of their commercial civilisation, and they are\nlooking forward towards something better. There is a class which still\nclings on to political and social changes as the only panacea for the evils\nin Europe, but among the great thinkers there, other ideals are growing.\nThey have found out that no amount of political or social manipulation of\nhuman conditions can cure the evils of life. It is a change of the soul\nitself for the better that alone will cure the evils of life. No amount of\nforce, or government, or legislative cruelty will change the conditions of a\nrace, but it is spiritual culture and ethical culture alone that can change\nwrong racial tendencies for the better. Thus these races of the West are\neager for some new thought, for some new philosophy; the religion they have\nhad, Christianity, although good and glorious in many respects, has been\nimperfectly  understood,  and  is, as understood hitherto, found to be\ninsufficient. The thoughtful men of the West find in our ancient philosophy,\nespecially in the Vedanta, the new impulse of thought they are seeking, the\nvery spiritual food and drink for which they are hungering and thirsting.\nAnd it is no wonder that this is so.\n", "\nI have become used to hear all sorts of wonderful claims put forward in\nfavour of every religion under the sun. You have also heard, quite within\nrecent times, the claims put forward by Dr. Barrows, a great friend of mine,\nthat Christianity is the only universal religion. Let me consider this\nquestion  awhile  and lay before you my reasons why I think that it is\nVedanta, and Vedanta alone that can become the universal religion of man,\nand that no other is fitted for the role. Excepting our own almost all the\nother great religions in the world are inevitably connected with the life or\nlives of one or more of their founders. All their theories, their teachings,\ntheir doctrines, and their ethics are built round the life of a personal\nfounder, from whom they get their sanction, their authority, and their\npower; and strangely enough, upon the historicity of the founder's life is\nbuilt, as it were, all the fabric of such religions. If there is one blow\ndealt to the historicity of that life, as has been the case in modern times\nwith the lives of almost all the so-called founders of religion \u2014 we know\nthat half of the details of such lives is not now seriously believed in, and\nthat the other half is seriously doubted \u2014 if this becomes the case, if that\nrock of historicity, as they pretend to call it, is shaken and shattered,\nthe whole building tumbles down, broken absolutely, never to regain its lost\nstatus.\n", "\nEvery one of the great religions in the world excepting our own, is built\nupon such historical characters; but ours rests upon principles. There is no\nman  or  woman  who  can claim to have created the Vedas. They are the\nembodiment of eternal principles; sages discovered them; and now and then\nthe names of these sages are mentioned \u2014 just their names; we do not even\nknow who or what they were. In many cases we do not know who their fathers\nwere, and almost in every case we do not know when and where they were born.\nBut what cared they, these sages, for their names? They were the preachers\nof principles, and they themselves, so far as they went, tried to become\nillustrations of the principles they preached. At the same time, just as our\nGod is an Impersonal and yet a Personal God, so is our religion a most\nintensely impersonal one \u2014 a religion based upon principles \u2014 and yet with\nan infinite scope for the play of persons; for what religion gives you more\nIncarnations, more prophets and seers, and still waits for infinitely more?\nThe Bh\u00e2gavata says that Incarnations are infinite, leaving ample scope for\nas many as you like to come. Therefore if any one or more of these persons\nin India's religious history, any one or more of these Incarnations, and any\none or more of our prophets proved not to have been historical, it does not\ninjure our religion at all; even then it remains firm as ever, because it is\nbased upon principles, and not upon persons. It is in vain we try to gather\nall the peoples of the world around a single personality. It is difficult to\nmake them gather together even round eternal and universal principles. If it\never becomes possible to bring the largest portion of humanity to one way of\nthinking  in  regard  to religion, mark you, it must be always through\nprinciples and not through persons. Yet as I have said, our religion has\nample scope for the authority and influence of persons. There is that most\nwonderful theory of Ishta which gives you the fullest and the freest choice\npossible among these great religious personalities. You may take up any one\nof the prophets or teachers as your guide and the object of your special\nadoration; you are even allowed to think that he whom you have chosen is the\ngreatest of the prophets, greatest of all the Avat\u00e2ras; there is no harm in\nthat, but you must keep to a firm background of eternally true principles.\nThe strange fact here is that the power of our Incarnations has been holding\ngood with us only so far as they are illustrations of the principles in the\nVedas. The glory of Shri Krishna is that he has been the best preacher of\nour eternal religion of principles and the best commentator on the Vedanta\nthat ever lived in India.\n", "\nThe second claim of the Vedanta upon the attention of the world is that, of\nall the scriptures in the world, it is the one scripture the teaching of\nwhich is in entire harmony with the results that have been attained by the\nmodern scientific investigations of external nature. Two minds in the dim\npast of history, cognate to each other in form and kinship and sympathy,\nstarted, being placed in different routes. The one was the ancient Hindu\nmind, and the other the ancient Greek mind. The former started by analysing\nthe internal world. The latter started in search of that goal beyond by\nanalysing the external world. And even through the various vicissitudes of\ntheir history, it is easy to make out these two vibrations of thought as\ntending to produce similar echoes of the goal beyond. It seems clear that\nthe  conclusions  of  modern  materialistic science can be acceptable,\nharmoniously with their religion, only to the Vedantins or Hindus as they\nare called. It seems clear that modern materialism can hold its own and at\nthe same time approach spirituality by taking up the conclusions of the\nVedanta. It seems to us, and to all who care to know, that the conclusions\nof modern science are the very conclusions the Vedanta reached ages ago;\nonly, in modern science they are written in the language of matter. This\nthen  is  another  claim of the Vedanta upon modern Western minds, its\nrationality, the wonderful rationalism of the Vedanta. I have myself been\ntold  by  some  of  the  best Western scientific minds of the day, how\nwonderfully rational the conclusions of the Vedanta are. I know one of them\npersonally  who  scarcely  has  time  to eat his meal or go out of his\nlaboratory, but who yet would stand by the hour to attend my lectures on the\nVedanta; for, as he expresses it, they are so scientific, they so exactly\nharmonise with the aspirations of the age and with the conclusions to which\nmodern science is coming at the present time.\n", "\nTwo such scientific conclusions drawn from comparative religion, I would\nspecially like to draw your attention to: the one bears upon the idea of the\nuniversality of religions, and the other on the idea of the oneness of\nthings.  We  observe in the histories of Babylon and among the Jews an\ninteresting religious phenomenon happening. We find that each of these\nBabylonian and Jewish peoples was divided into so many tribes, each tribe\nhaving a god of its own, and that these little tribal gods had often a\ngeneric name. The gods among the Babylonians were all called Baals, and\namong them Baal Merodach was the chief. In course of time one of these many\ntribes would conquer and assimilate the other racially allied tribes, and\nthe natural result would be that the god of the conquering tribe would be\nplaced at the head of all the gods of the other tribes. Thus the so-called\nboasted monotheism of the Semites was created. Among the Jews the gods went\nby the name of Molochs. Of these there was one Moloch who belonged to the\ntribe called Israel, and he was called the Moloch-Yahveh or Moloch-Yava. In\ntime, this tribe of Israel slowly conquered some of the other tribes of the\nsame race, destroyed their Molochs, and declared its own Moloch to be the\nSupreme Moloch of all the Molochs. And I am sure, most of you know the\namount of bloodshed, of tyranny, and of brutal savagery that this religious\nconquest entailed. Later on, the Babylonians tried to destroy this supremacy\nof Moloch-Yahveh, but could not succeed in doing so.\n", "\nIt seems to me, that such an attempt at tribal self-assertion in religious\nmatters might have taken place on the frontiers and India also. Here, too,\nall the various tribes of the Aryans might have come into conflict with one\nanother  for declaring the supremacy of their several tribal gods; but\nIndia's history was to be otherwise, was to be different from that of the\nJews. India alone was to be, of all lands, the land of toleration and of\nspirituality; and therefore the fight between tribes and their gods did not\nlong take place here. For one of the greatest sages that was ever born found\nout here in India even at that distant time, which history cannot reach, and\ninto whose gloom even tradition itself dares not peep \u2014 in that distant time\nthe sage arose and declared, \u090f\u0915\u0902 \u0938\u0926\u094d \u0935\u093f\u092a\u094d\u0930\u093e \u092c\u0939\u0941\u0927\u093e \u0935\u0926\u0928\u094d\u0924\u093f  \u2014 \"He who\nexists  is one; the sages call Him variously.\" This is one of the most\nmemorable sentences that was ever uttered, one of the grandest truths that\nwas ever discovered. And for us Hindus this truth has been the very backbone\nof our national existence. For throughout the vistas of the centuries of our\nnational life, this one idea \u2014 \u090f\u0915\u0902 \u0938\u0926\u094d \u0935\u093f\u092a\u094d\u0930\u093e \u092c\u0939\u0941\u0927\u093e \u0935\u0926\u0928\u094d\u0924\u093f  \u2014 comes\ndown, gaining in volume and in fullness till it has permeated the whole of\nour national existence, till it has mingled in our blood, and has become one\nwith us. We live that grand truth in every vein, and our country has become\nthe glorious land of religious toleration. It is here and here alone that\nthey build temples and churches for the religions which have come with the\nobject of condemning our own religion. This is one very great principle that\nthe world is waiting to learn from us. Ay, you little know how much of\nintolerance is yet abroad. It struck me more than once that I should have to\nleave  my bones on foreign shores owing to the prevalence of religious\nintolerance. Killing a man is nothing for religion's sake; tomorrow they may\ndo it in the very heart of the boasted civilisation of the West, if today\nthey are not really doing so. Outcasting in its most horrible forms would\noften come down upon the head of a man in the West if he dared to say a word\nagainst his country's accepted religion. They talk glibly and smoothly here\nin criticism of our caste laws. If you go, to the West and live there as I\nhave done, you will know that even some of the biggest professors you hear\nof  are arrant cowards and dare not say, for fear of public opinion, a\nhundredth part of what they hold to be really true in religious matter.\n", "\nTherefore the world is waiting for this grand idea of universal toleration.\nIt will be a great acquisition to civilisation. Nay, no civilisation can\nlong exist unless this idea enters into it. No civilisation can grow unless\nfanatics, bloodshed, and brutality stop. No civilisation can begin to lift\nup its head until we look charitably upon one another; and the first step\ntowards that much-needed charity is to look charitably and kindly upon the\nreligious convictions of others. Nay more, to understand that not only\nshould we be charitable, but positively helpful to each other, however\ndifferent our religious ideas and convictions may be. And that is exactly\nwhat we do in India as I have just related to you. It is here in India that\nHindus have built and are still building churches for Christians and mosques\nfor Mohammedans. That is the thing to do. In spite of their hatred, in spite\nof their brutality, in spite of their cruelly, in spite of their tyranny,\nand in spite of the vile language they are given to uttering, we will and\nmust  go  on  building churches for the Christians and mosques for the\nMohammedans until we conquer through love, until we have demonstrated to the\nworld that love alone is the fittest thing to survive and not hatred, that\nit is gentleness that has the strength to live on and to fructify, and not\nmere brutality and physical force.\n", "\nThe other great idea that the world wants from us today, the thinking part\nof Europe, nay, the whole world \u2014 more, perhaps, the lower classes than the\nhigher,  more the masses than the cultured, more the ignorant than the\neducated, more the weak than the strong \u2014 is that eternal grand idea of the\nspiritual oneness of the whole universe. I need not tell you today, men from\nMadras University, how the modern researches of the West have demonstrated\nthrough physical means the oneness and the solidarity of the whole universe;\nhow, physically speaking, you and I, the sun, moon, and stars are but little\nwaves or waveless in the midst of an infinite ocean of matter; how Indian\npsychology demonstrated ages ago that, similarly, both body and mind are but\nmere names or little waveless in the ocean of matter, the Samashti; and how,\ngoing one step further, it is also shown in the Vedanta that behind that\nidea of the unity of the whole show, the real Soul is one. There is but one\nSoul throughout the universe, all is but One Existence This great idea of\nthe real and basic solidarity of the whole universe has frightened many,\neven  in this country. It even now finds sometimes more opponents than\nadherents. I tell you, nevertheless, that it is the one great life-giving\nidea which the world wants from us today, and which the mute masses of India\nwant for their uplifting, for none can regenerate this land of ours without\nthe practical application and effective operation of this ideal of the\noneness of things.\n", "\nThe rational West is earnestly bent upon seeking out the rationality, the\nraison d' \u00eatre of all its philosophy and its ethics; and you all know well\nthat ethics cannot be derived from the mere sanction of any personage,\nhowever  great and divine he may have been. Such an explanation of the\nauthority of ethics appeals no more to the highest of the world's thinkers;\nthey want something more than human sanction for ethical and moral codes to\nbe binding, they want some eternal principle of truth as the sanction of\nethics. And where is that eternal sanction to be found except in the only\nInfinite Reality that exists in you and in me and in all, in the Self, in\nthe Soul? The infinite oneness of the Soul is the eternal sanction of all\nmorality, that you and I are not only brothers \u2014 every literature voicing\nman's struggle towards freedom has preached that for you \u2014 but that you and\nI are really one. This is the dictate of Indian philosophy. This oneness is\nthe rationale of all ethics and all spirituality. Europe wants it today just\nas much as our downtrodden masses do, and this great principle is even now\nunconsciously forming the basis of all the latest political and social\naspirations that are coming up in England, in Germany, in France, and in\nAmerica. And mark it, my friends, that in and through all the literature\nvoicing man's struggle towards freedom, towards universal freedom, again and\nagain you find the Indian Vedantic ideals coming out prominently. In some\ncases the writers do not know the source of their inspiration, in some cases\nthey try to appear very original, and a few there are, bold and grateful\nenough to mention the source and acknowledge their indebtedness to it.\n", "\nWhen I was in America, I heard once the complaint made that I was preaching\ntoo much of Advaita, and too little of dualism. Ay, I know what grandeur,\nwhat oceans of love, what infinite, ecstatic blessings and joy there are in\nthe dualistic love-theories of worship and religion. I know it all. But this\nis not the time with us to weep even in joy; we have had weeping enough; no\nmore is this the time for us to become soft. This softness has been with us\ntill we have become like masses of cotton and are dead. What our country now\nwants are muscles of iron and nerves of steel, gigantic wills which nothing\ncan resist, which can penetrate into the mysteries and the secrets of the\nuniverse, and will accomplish their purpose in any fashion even if it meant\ngoing down to the bottom of the ocean and meeting death face to face. That\nis what we want, and that can only be created, established, and strengthened\nby understanding and realising the ideal of the Advaita, that ideal of the\noneness of all. Faith, faith, faith in ourselves, faith, faith in God \u2014 this\nis the secret of greatness. If you have faith in all the three hundred and\nthirty  millions  of your mythological gods, and in all the gods which\nforeigners have now and again introduced into your midst, and still have no\nfaith  in  yourselves,  there  is  no salvation for you. Have faith in\nyourselves, and stand up on that faith and be strong; that is what we need.\nWhy is it that we three hundred and thirty millions of people have been\nruled for the last one thousand years by any and every handful of foreigners\nwho chose to walk over our prostrate bodies? Because they had faith in\nthemselves and we had not. What did I learn in the West, and what did I see\nbehind those frothy sayings of the Christian sects repeating that man was a\nfallen and hopelessly fallen sinner? There I saw that inside the national\nhearts of both Europe and America reside the tremendous power of the men's\nfaith in themselves. An English boy will tell you, \"I am an Englishman, and\nI can do anything.\" The American boy will tell you the same thing, and so\nwill any European boy. Can our boys say the same thing here?
