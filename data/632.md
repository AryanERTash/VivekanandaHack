["\n", "III\n", "( Translated from Bengali )\n", "( From the Diary of a Disciple )\n", "(The disciple is Sharatchandra Chakravarty, who published his records in a\nBengali book, Swami-Shishya-Samv\u00e2da, in two parts. The present series of\n\"Conversations and Dialogues\" is a revised translation from this book. Five\ndialogues of this series have already appeared in the Complete Works,Volume 5)\n", "[Place: Cossipore, at the garden of the late Gopal Lal Seal. Year: 1897.]\n", "After his first return from the West, Swamiji resided for a few days at the\ngarden of the late Gopal Lal Seal at Cossipore. Some well-known Pundits\nliving at Barabazar, Calcutta, came to the garden one day with a view to\nholding  a disputation with him. The disciple was present there on the\noccasion.\n", "All the Pundits who came there could speak in Sanskrit fluently. They came\nand greeting Swamiji, who sat surrounded by a circle of visitors, began\ntheir conversation in Sanskrit. Swamiji also responded to them in melodious\nSanskrit. The disciple cannot remember now the subject on which the Pundits\nargued with him that day. But this much he remembers that the Pundits,\nalmost all in one strident voice, were rapping out to Swamiji in Sanskrit\nsubtle questions of philosophy, and he, in a dignified serious mood, was\ngiving  out to them calmly his own well argued conclusions about those\nquestions.\n", "In the discussion with the Pundits Swamiji represented the side of the\nSiddh\u00e2nta or conclusions to be established, while the Pundits represented\nthat of the Purvapaksha or objections to be raised. The disciple remembers\nthat, while arguing, Swamiji wrongly used in one place the word Asti instead\nof  Svasti, which made the Pundits laugh out. At this, Swamiji at once\nsubmitted: \"\u092a\u0923\u094d\u0921\u093f\u0924\u093e\u0928\u093e\u0902 \u0926\u093e\u0938\u094b\u093d\u0939\u0902 \u0915\u094d\u0937\u0928\u094d\u0924\u0935\u094d\u092f\u092e\u0947\u0924\u0924\u094d \u0938\u094d\u0916\u0932\u0928\u092e\u094d\u2014I am but a servant of the\nPundits, please excuse this mistake.\" The Pundits also were charmed at this\nhumility of Swamiji. After a long dispute, the Pundits at last admitted that\nthe  conclusions of the Siddhanta side were adequate, and preparing to\ndepart, they made their greetings to Swamiji.\n", "After the Pundits had left, the disciple learnt from Swamiji that these\nPundits  who  took the side of the Purvapaksha were well versed in the\nPurva-Mim\u00e2ms\u00e2  Sh\u00e2stras,  Swamiji  advocated  the  philosophy  of  the\nUttara-Mim\u00e2ms\u00e2 or Vedanta and proved to them the superiority of the path of\nknowledge, and they were obliged to accept his conclusions.\n", "About the way the Pundits laughed at Swamiji, picking up one grammatical\nmistake, he said that this error of his was due to the fact of his not\nhaving spoken in Sanskrit for many years together. He did not blame the\nPundits a bit for all that. But he pointed out in this connection that in\nthe West it would imply a great incivility on the part of an opponent to\npoint  out any such slip in language, deviating from the real issue of\ndispute. A civilised society in such cases would accept the idea, taking no\nnotice of the language. \"But in your country, all the fighting is going on\nover the husk, nobody searches for the kernel within.\" So saying, Swamiji\nbegan to talk with the disciple in Sanskrit. The disciple also gave answers\nin broken Sanskrit. Yet Swamiji praised him for the sake of encouragement.\nFrom that day, at the request of Swamiji, the disciple used to speak with\nhim in Sanskrit off and on.\n", "In reply to the question, what is civilisation, Swamiji said that day: \"The\nmore advanced a society or nation is in spirituality, the more is that\nsociety or nation civilised. No nation can be said to have become civilised\nonly because it has succeeded in increasing the comforts of material life by\nbringing into use lots of machinery and things of that sort. The present-day\ncivilization  of the West is multiplying day by day only the wants and\ndistresses of men. On the other hand, the ancient Indian civilisation by\nshowing people the way to spiritual advancement, doubtless succeeded, if not\nin removing once for all, at least in lessening, in a great measure, the\nmaterial needs of men. In the present age, it is to bring into coalition\nboth these civilisations that Bhagavan Shri Ramakrishna was born. In this\nage, as on the one hand people have to be intensely practical, so on the\nother hand they have to acquire deep spiritual knowledge.\" Swamiji made us\nclearly  understand  that day that from such interaction of the Indian\ncivilization with that of the West would dawn on the world a new era. In the\ncourse of dilating upon this, he happened to remark in one place, \"Well,\nanother  thing.  People there in the West think that the more a man is\nreligious, the more demure he must be in his outward bearing\u2014no word about\nanything else from his lips! As the priests in the West would on the one\nhand be struck with wonder at my liberal religious discourses, they would be\nas much puzzled on the other hand when they found me, after such discourses,\ntalking frivolities with my friends. Sometimes they would speak out to my\nface: 'Swami, you are a priest, you should not be joking and laughing in\nthis way like ordinary men. Such levity does not look well in you.' To which\nI would reply, 'We are children of bliss, why should we look morose and\nsombre?' But I doubt if they could rightly catch the drift of my words.\"\n", "That  day Swamiji spoke many things about Bh\u00e2va Sam\u00e2dhi and Nirvikalpa\nSamadhi as well. These are produced below as far as possible:\n", "Suppose a man is cultivating that type of devotion to God which Hanum\u00e2n\nrepresents. The more intense the attitude becomes, the more will the pose\nand demeanour of that aspirant, nay even his physical configuration, be cast\nin that would. It is in this way that transmutation of species takes place.\nTaking up any such emotional attitude, the worshipper becomes gradually\nshaped into the very form of his ideal. The ultimate stage of any such\nsentiment is called Bhava Samadhi. While the aspirant in the path of Jnana,\npursuing the process of  Neti, Neti, \"not this, not this\", such as \"I am not\nthe  body, nor the mind, nor the intellect\", and so on, attains to the\nNirvikalpa Samadhi when he is established in absolute consciousness. It\nrequires striving through many births to reach perfection or the ultimate\nstage with regard to a single one of these devotional attitudes. But Shri\nRamakrishna, the king of the realm of spiritual sentiment, perfected himself\nin no less than eighteen different forms of devotion! He also used to say\nthat his body would not have endured, had he not held himself on to this\nplay of spiritual sentiment.\n", "The disciple asked that day, \"Sir, what sort of food did you use to take in\nthe West?\"\n", "Swamiji: The same as they take there. We are Sannyasins and nothing can take\naway our caste!\n", "On the subject of how he would work in future in this country, Swamiji said\nthat day that starting two centres, one in Madras and another in Calcutta,\nhe would rear up a new type of Sannyasins for the good of all men in all its\nphases. He further said that by a destructive method no progress either for\nthe society or for the country could be achieved. In all ages and times\nprogress has been effected by the constructive process, that is, by giving a\nnew mould to old methods and customs. Every religious preacher in India,\nduring the past ages, worked in that line. Only the religion of Bhagavan\nBuddha was destructive. Hence that religion has been extirpated from India.\n", "The disciple remembers that while thus speaking on, he remarked, \"If the\nBrahman is manifested in one man, thousands of men advance, finding their\nway  out  in that light. Only the knowers of Brahman are the spiritual\nteachers of mankind. This is corroborated by all scriptures and by reason\ntoo. It is only the selfish Brahmins who have introduced into this country\nthe system of hereditary Gurus, which is against the Vedas and against the\nShastras. Hence it is that even through their spiritual practice men do not\nnow succeed in perfecting themselves or in realising Brahman. To remove all\nthis corruption in religion, the Lord has incarnated Himself on earth in the\npresent age in the person of Shri Ramakrishna. The universal teachings that\nhe offered, if spread all over the world, will do good to humanity and the\nworld.  Not  for  many  a century past has India produced so great, so\nwonderful, a teacher of religious synthesis.\"\n", "A brother-disciple of Swamiji at that time asked him, \"Why did you not\npublicly preach Shri Ramakrishna as an Avat\u00e2ra in the West?\"\n", "Swamiji: They make much flourish and fuss over their science and philosophy.\nHence, unless you first knock to pieces their intellectual conceit through\nreasoning, scientific argument, and philosophy, you cannot build anything\nthere. Those who finding themselves off their moorings through their utmost\nintellectual reasoning would approach me in a real spirit of truth-seeking,\nto them alone, I would speak of Shri Ramakrishna. If, otherwise, I had\nforthwith spoken of the doctrine of incarnation, they might have said, \"Oh,\nyou do not say anything new\u2014why, we have our Lord Jesus for all that!\"\n", "After thus spending some three or four delightful hours, the disciple came\nback to Calcutta that day along with the other visitors.\n"]
