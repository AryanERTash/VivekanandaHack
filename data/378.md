["\n", "ON LANGUAGE\n", "\nSimplicity is the secret. My ideal of language is my Master's language, most\ncolloquial and yet most expressive. It must express the thought which is\nintended to be conveyed.\n", "\nThe attempt to make the Bengali language perfect in so short a time will\nmake  it  cut  and  dried. Properly speaking, it has no verbs. Michael\nMadhusudan Dutt attempted to remedy this in poetry. The greatest poet in\nBengal  was  Kavikankana.  The  best  prose in Sanskrit is Patanjali's\nMah\u00e2bh\u00e2shya. There the language is vigorous. The language of Hitopadesha is\nnot bad, but the language of K\u00e2dambari is an example of degradation.\n", "\nThe Bengali language must be modelled not after the Sanskrit, but rather\nafter  the  P\u00e2li,  which has a strong resemblance to it. In coining or\ntranslating technical terms in Bengali, one must, however, use all Sanskrit\nwords for them, and an attempt should be made to coin new words. For this\npurpose, if a collection is made from a Sanskrit dictionary of all those\ntechnical terms, then it ill help greatly the constitution of the Bengali\nlanguage.\n"]
