["\n", "LXXXI\n", "To Mr. J. J. Goodwin\n", "SWITZERLAND,\n", "8th August, 1896.\n", "DEAR GOODWIN,\n", "I am now taking rest. I read from different letters a lot about Kripananda.\nI am sorry for him. There must be something wrong in his head. Let him\nalone. None of you need bother about him.\n", "As for hurting me, that is not in the power of gods or devils. So be at\nrest.  It  is  unswerving  love and perfect unselfishness that conquer\neverything. We Vedantists in every difficulty ought to ask the subjective\nquestion, \"Why do I see that?\" \"Why can I not conquer this with love?\"\n", "I am very glad at the reception the Swami has met with, also at the good\nwork he is doing. Great work requires great and persistent effort for a long\ntime. Neither need we trouble ourselves if a few fail. It is in the nature\nof things that many should fall, that troubles should come, that tremendous\ndifficulties should arise, that selfishness and all the other devils in the\nhuman heart should struggle hard when they are about to be driven out by the\nfire of spirituality. The road to the Good is the roughest and steepest in\nthe universe. It is a wonder that so many succeed, no wonder that so many\nfall. Character has to be established through a thousand stumbles.\n", "I am much refreshed now. I look out of the window and see the huge glaciers\njust before me and feel that I am in the Himalayas. I am quite calm. My\nnerves have regained their accustomed strength; and little vexations, like\nthose you write of, do not touch me at all. How shall I be disturbed by this\nchild's play? The whole world is a mere child's play \u2014 preaching, teaching,\nand  all included. \"Know him to be the Sannyasin who neither hates not\ndesires\"  (Gita,  V.3). And what is there to be desired in this little\nmud-puddle of a world, with its ever-recurring misery, disease, and death?\n\"He who has given up all desires, he alone is happy.\"\n", "This rest, eternal, peaceful rest, I am catching a glimpse of now in this\nbeautiful spot. \"Having once known that the Atman alone, and nothing else,\nexists, desiring what, or for whose desire, shall you suffer misery about\nthe body?\" (Brihad\u00e2ranyaka, IV. iv. 12.)\n", "I feel as if I had my share of experience in what they call \"work\". I am\nfinished, I am longing now to get out. \"Out of thousands, but one strives to\nattain the Goal. And even of those who struggle hard, but few attain\" (Gita,\nVII. 3); for the senses are powerful, they drag men down.\n", "\"A  good world\", \"a happy world\", and \"social progress\", are all terms\nequally intelligible with \"hot ice\" or \"dark light\". If it were good, it\nwould  not  be the world. The soul foolishly thinks of manifesting the\nInfinite in finite matter, Intelligence through gross particles; but at last\nit finds out its error and tries to escape. This going-back is the beginning\nof religion, and its method, destruction of self, that is, love. Not love\nfor wife or child or anybody else, but love for everything else except this\nlittle self. Never be deluded by the tall talk, of which you will hear so\nmuch in America, about \"human progress\" and such stuff. There is no progress\nwithout corresponding digression. In one society there is one set of evils;\nin another, another. So with periods of history. In the Middle Ages, there\nwere more robbers, now more cheats. At one period there is less idea of\nmarried life; at another, more prostitution. In one, more physical agony; in\nanother, a thousandfold more mental. So with knowledge. Did not gravitation\nalready  exist  in  nature before it was observed and named? Then what\ndifference does it make to know that it exists? Are you happier than the Red\nIndians?\n", "The only knowledge that is of any value is to know that all this is humbug.\nBut few, very few, will ever know this. \"Know the Atman alone, and give up\nall other vain words.\" This is the only knowledge we gain from all this\nknocking about the universe. This is the only work, to call upon mankind to\n\"Awake, arise, and stop not till the goal is reached\". It is renunciation,\nTy\u00e2ga, that is meant by religion, and nothing else.\n", "Ishwara  is  the  sum  total of individuals; yet He Himself also is an\nindividual in the same way as the human body is a unit, of which each cell\nis  an  individual.  Samashti or the Collective is God. Vyashti or the\ncomponent is the soul of Jiva. The existence of Ishwara, therefore, depends\non that of Jiva, as the body on the cell, and vice versa. Jiva, and Ishwara\nare co-existent beings. As long as the one exists, the other also must.\nAgain, since in all the higher spheres, except on our earth, the amount of\ngood is vastly in excess of the amount of bad, the sum total or Ishwara may\nbe  said  to  be All-good, Almighty, and Omniscient. These are obvious\nqualities, and need no argument to prove, from the very fact of totality.\n", "Brahman is beyond both of these, and is not a state. It is the only unit not\ncomposed of many units. It is the principle which runs through all, from a\ncell to God, and without which nothing can exist. Whatever is real is that\nprinciple or Brahman. When I think \"I am Brahman\", then I alone exist. It is\nso  also  when  you so think, and so on. Each one is the whole of that\nprinciple. . . .\n", "A few days ago, I felt a sudden irresistible desire to write to Kripananda.\nPerhaps he was unhappy and thinking of me. So I wrote him a warm letter.\nToday  from the American news, I see why it was so. I sent him flowers\ngathered near the glaciers. Ask Miss Waldo to send him some money and plenty\nof love. Love never dies. The love of the father never dies, whatever the\nchildren may do or be. He is my child. He has the same or more share in my\nlove and help, now that he is in misery.\n", "Yours with blessings,\n", "\nVIVEKANANDA.\n"]
