["(Translated from Bengali)\n", "30th Dec., 1889.\n", "DEAR SIR,\n", "I wrote in a letter to you that I was to go to Varanasi in a day or two, but\nwho  can  nullify  the  decree  of  Providence? News reached me that a\nbrother-disciple, Yogen by name, had been attacked with smallpox after\narriving here from a pilgrimage to Chitrakuta, Omkarnath, etc., and so I\ncame to this place to nurse him. He has now completely recovered. Some\nBengali gentlemen here are of a greatly pious and loving disposition. They\nare very lovingly taking care of me, and their importunate desire is that I\nshould stay here during the month of M\u00e2gha (Jan.-Feb.) keeping the Kalpa\nvow.[2] But my mind is very keenly\nharping on the name of Varanasi and is quite agog to see you. Yes, I am\ngoing to try my best to slip away and avoid their importunities in a day or\ntwo and betake myself to the holy realm of the Lord of Varanasi. If one of\nmy monastic brother-disciples, Achyutananda Sarasvati by name, calls on you\nto enquire of me, please tell him I am soon coming to Varanasi. He is indeed\na very good man and learned. I was obliged to leave him behind at Bankipore.\nAre Rakhal and Subodh still there in Varanasi? Please inquire and inform me\nwhether the Kumbha fair this year is going to be held at Hardwar or not.\n", "Many a man of wisdom, of piety, many a S\u00e2dhu (holy man) and Pundit have I\nmet in so many places, and I have been very much favoured by them, but \"\u092d\u093f\u0928\u094d\u0928\u0930\u0941\u091a\u093f\u0930\u094d\u0939\u093f \u0932\u094b\u0915\u093e\u0903\u2014 Men are of varying tastes\" \u2014 Raghuvamsham.\nI know not what sort of soul-affinity there is between us, for nowhere else does it\nseem so pleasing and agreeable as with you.
