## Original Dataset Text
["\n", "CHAPTER II\n", "AT NAINI TAL AND ALMORA\n", "PERSONS: The Swami Vivekananda, Gurubh\u00e2is, and a party of Europeans and\ndisciples, amongst whom were Dhira Mata, the \"Steady Mother\"; one whose name\nwas Jaya; and Nivedita.\n", "PLACE: The Himalayas.\n", "TIME: May 11 to May 25, 1898.\n", "We were a large party, or, indeed, two parties, that left Howrah station on\nWednesday evening and on Friday morning came in sight of the Himalayas. . .\n.\n", "Naini Tal was made beautiful by three things \u2014 the Master's pleasure in\nintroducing to us his disciple the Raja of Khetri; the dancing girls who met\nus and asked us where to find him, and were received by him in spite of the\nremonstrances of others; and by the Mohammedan gentleman who said, \"Swamiji,\nif in after-times any claim you as an Avat\u00e2ra, an especial incarnation of\nthe Deity \u2014 remember that I, a Mohammedan, am the first!\"\n", "It was here too that we heard a long talk on Ram Mohan Roy in which he\npointed out three things as the dominant notes of this teacher's message \u2014\nhis acceptance of the Vedanta, his preaching of patriotism, and the love\nthat embraced the Mussulman equally with the Hindu. In all these things he\nclaimed himself to have taken up the task that the breadth and foresight of\nRam Mohan Roy had mapped out.\n", "The incident of the dancing girls occurred in consequence of our visit to\nthe two temples at the head of the tarn. . . . Here, offering worship, we\nfound two nautch-women. When they had finished, they came up to us, and we,\nin broken language, entered into conversation with them. We took them for\nrespectable ladies of the town and were much astonished later at the storm\nwhich had evidently passed over the Swami's audience at his refusal to have\nthem turned away. Am I mistaken in thinking that it was in connection with\nthese dancing-women of Naini Tal that he first told us the story, many times\nrepeated, of the nautch-girl of Khetri? He had been angry at the invitation\nto see her, but being prevailed upon to come, she sang:\n", "O Lord, look not upon my evil qualities!\nThy name, O Lord, is Same-Sightedness.\nMake us both the same Brahman!\nOne piece of iron is the knife in the hand of the butcher,\nAnd another piece of iron is the image in the temple.\nBut when they touch the philosopher's stone,\nBoth alike turn to gold!\nOne drop of water is in the sacred Jamuna,\nAnd one is foul in a ditch by the roadside.\nBut when they fall into the Ganges,\nBoth alike become holy!\nSo, Lord, look not upon my evil qualities!\nThy name, O Lord, is Same-Sightedness.\nMake us both the same Brahman!\n", "And then, said the Master of himself, the scales fell from his eyes, and\nseeing that all are indeed one, he condemned no more. . . .\n", "It was late in the afternoon when we left Naini Tal for Almora, and night\novertook us while still travelling through the forest. . . . till we reached\na quaintly placed Dak bungalow, on the mountain side in the midst of trees.\nThere after some time Swamiji arrived with his party, full of fun and keen\nin his appreciation of everything that concerned the comfort of his guests.\n. . .\n", "From the day that we arrived at Almora the Swami renewed his habit of coming\nover to us at our early breakfast and spending some hours in talk. Then and\nalways he was an exceedingly light sleeper, and I imagine that his visit to\nus, early as the hour might be, was often paid during the course of his\nreturn with his monks from a still earlier walk. Sometimes, but rarely, we\nsaw him again in the evening, either meeting him when out for a walk or\ngoing ourselves to Captain Sevier's, where he and his party were staying,\nand seeing him there. And once he came at that time to call on us.\n", "Into  these morning talks at Almora a strange new element, painful but\nsalutary to remember, had crept. There appeared to be on the one side a\ncurious bitterness and distrust, and on the other, irritation and defiance.\nThe youngest of the Swami's disciples at this time, it must be remembered,\nwas an English woman, and of how much this fact meant intellectually \u2014 what\na strong bias it implied, and always does imply, in the reading of India,\nwhat an idealism of the English race and all their deeds and history \u2014 the\nSwami himself had had no conception till the day after her initiation at the\nmonastery. Then he had asked her some exultant question, as to which nation\nshe now belonged, and had been startled to find with what a passion of\nloyalty and worship she regarded the English flag, giving to it much of the\nfeeling that an Indian woman would give to her Thakur. His surprise and\ndisappointment at the moment were scarcely perceptible. A startled look, no\nmore. Nor did his discovery of the superficial way in which this disciple\nhad joined herself with his people in any degree affect his confidence and\ncourtesy during the remaining weeks spent in the plains.\n", "But with Almora it seemed as if a going-to-school had commenced. . . . It\nwas never more than this; never the dictating of opinion or creed; never\nmore than emancipation from partiality. Even at the end of the terrible\nexperience when this method, as regarded race and country, was renounced,\nnever to be taken up systematically again, the Swami did not call for any\nconfession of faith, any declaration of new opinion. He dropped the whole\nquestion. His listener went free. But he had revealed a different standpoint\nin  thought  and  feeling, so completely and so strongly as to make it\nimpossible for her to rest, until later, by her own labours, she had arrived\nat a view in which both these partial presentments stood rationalized and\naccounted for.\n", "\"Really, patriotism like yours is sin!\" he exclaimed once, many weeks later,\nwhen the process of obtaining an uncoloured judgement on some incident had\nbeen more than commonly exasperating. \"All that I want you to see is that\nmost  people's  actions  are  the expression of self-interest, and you\nconstantly oppose to this the idea that a certain race are all angels.\nIgnorance so determined is wickedness!\" . . .\n", "These  morning  talks  at Almora, then, took the form of assaults upon\ndeep-rooted preconceptions \u2014 social, literary and artistic \u2014 or of long\ncomparisons of Indian and European history and sentiments, often containing\nextended observations of very great value. One characteristic of the Swami\nwas the habit of attacking the abuses of a country or society openly and\nvigorously when he was in its midst, whereas after he had left it, it would\noften seem as if nothing but its virtues were remembered by him. He was\nalways testing his disciples, and the manner of these particular discourses\nwas probably adopted in order to put to the proof the courage and sincerity\nof one who was both woman and European.\n"]
