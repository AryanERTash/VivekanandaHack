["\n", "VII\n", "To Mrs. G. W. Hale\n", "MINNEAPOLIS\n", "21 November 1893\n", "DEAR MOTHER,\n", "I reached Madison safely, went to a hotel, and sent a message to Mr. Updike.\nHe came to see me. He is a Congregational and so, of course, was not very\nfriendly at first; but in the course of an hour or so became very kind to\nme,  and took me over the whole place and the University. I had a fine\naudience and $100. Immediately after the lecture I took the night train to\nMinneapolis.\n", "I tried to get the clergymen's ticket, but they could not give me any, not\nbeing the headquarters. The thing to be done is to get a permit from every\nhead office of every line in Chicago. Perhaps it is possible for Mr. Hale to\nget the permits for me. If it is so, I hope he will take the trouble to send\nthem over to me to Minneapolis if they can reach me by the 25th, or to Des\nMoines if by the 29th. Else I would do it the next time in Chicago. I have\ntaken the money in a draft on the bank, which cost me 40\u00a2.\n", "May you be blessed for ever, my kind friend; you and your whole family have\nmade such a heavenly impression on me as I would carry all my life.\n", "Yours sincerely,\n", "VIVEKANANDA\n"]
