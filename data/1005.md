["\n", "CLVIII\n", "To Mrs. Ole Bull\n", "1502 JONES STREET,\n", "SAN FRANCISCO,\n", "4th March, 1900.\n", "DEAR DHIRA MATA,\n", "I have not had a word from you for a month. I am in Frisco. The people here\nhave been prepared by my writing beforehand, and they come in big crowds.\nBut it remains to be seen how much of that enthusiasm endures when it comes\nto paying at the door. Rev. Benjamin Fay Mills invited me to Oakland and\ngave me big crowds to preach to. He and his wife have been reading my works\nand  keeping  track of my movements all the time. I sent the letter of\nintroduction from Miss Thursby to Mrs. Hearst. She has invited me to one of\nher musicals Sunday next.\n", "My health is about the same; don't find much difference; it is improving,\nperhaps, but very imperceptibly. I can use my voice, however, to make 3,000\npeople hear me, as I did twice in Oakland, and get good sleep too after two\nhours of speaking.\n", "I learn Margot is with you. When are you sailing for France? I will leave\nhere in April and go to the East. I am very desirous of getting to England\nin May if I can. Must not go home before trying England once more.\n", "I have nice letters from Brahmananda and Saradananda; they are all doing\nwell. They are trying to bring the municipality to its senses; I am glad. In\nthis  world of Maya one need not injure, but \"spread the hood, without\nstriking\". That is enough.\n", "Things must get round; if they don't, it is all right. I have a very nice\nletter from Mrs. Sevier too. They are doing fine in the mountains. How is\nMrs. Vaughan? When is your conference to close? How is Turiyananda?\n", "With everlasting love and gratitude.\n", "Your son,\n", "\nVIVEKANANDA.\n"]
