["\n", "HINDOO PHILOSOPHY\n", "(Detroit Free Press, February 16, 1894)\n", "\nThe second lecture of the Hindoo monk, Swami Vive Kananda, was given last\nevening at the Unitarian church to a large and very appreciative audience.\nThe  expectation of the audience that the speaker would enlighten them\nregarding \"Hindoo Philosophy,\" as the lecture was entitled, was gratified to\nonly a limited extent. Allusions were made to the philosophy of Buddha, and\nthe  speaker  was  applauded  when he said that Buddhism was the first\nmissionary religion of the world, and that it had secured the largest number\nof converts without the shedding of a drop of blood; but he did not tell his\naudience anything about the religion or philosophy of Buddha. He made a\nnumber of cute little jabs at the Christian religion, and alluded to the\ntrouble and misery that had been caused by its introduction into heathen\ncountries,  but he skilfully avoided any comparison between the social\ncondition of the people in his own land and that of the people to whom he\nwas speaking. In a general way he said the Hindoo philosophers taught from a\nlower truth to a higher; whereas, a person accepting a newer Christian\ndoctrine is asked and expected to throw his former belief all away and\naccept the newer in its entirety. \"It is an idle dream when all of us will\nhave the same religious views,\" said he. \"No emotion can be produced except\nby clashing elements acting upon the mind. It is the revulsion of change,\nthe  new  light,  the presentation of the new to the old, that elicits\nsensation.\"\n", "\n[As the first lecture had antagonised some people, the Free Press reporter\nwas very cautious. Fortunately, however, the Detroit Tribune consistently\nupheld Swamiji, and thus in its report of February 16 we get some idea of\nhis lecture on \"Hindu Philosophy,\" although the Tribune reporter seems to\nhave taken somewhat sketchy notes:]\n", "\n", "(Detroit Tribune, February 16, 1894)\n", "\nThe Brahman monk, Swami Vive Kananda, again lectured last evening at the\nUnitarian church, his topic being \"Hindu Philosophy.\" The speaker dealt for\na time with general philosophy and metaphysics, but said that he would\ndevote the lecture to that part pertaining to religion. There is a sect that\nbelieves in a soul, but are agnostic in relation to God. Buddahism [sic] was\na great moral religion, but they could not live long without believing in a\ngod. Another sect known as the giants [Jains] believe in the soul, but not\nin the moral government of the country. There were several millions of this\nsect in India. Their priests and monks tie a handkerchief over their faces\nbelieving if their hot breath comes in contact with man or beast death will\nensue.\n", "\nAmong the orthodox, all believe in the revelation. Some think every cord in\nthe Bible comes directly from God. The stretching of the meaning of a word\nwould perhaps do in most religions, but in that of the Hindus they have the\nSanscrit, which always retains the full meaning and reasons of the world.\n", "\nThe distinguished Oriental thought there was a sixth sense far greater than\nany of the five we know we possess. It was the truth of revelation. A man\nmay read all the books on religion in the world and yet be the greatest\nblackguard in the country. Revelation means later reports of spiritual\ndiscoveries.\n", "\nThe  second position some take is a creation without beginning or end.\nSuppose there was a time when the world did not exist; what was God doing\nthen? To the Hindus the creation was only one of forms. One man is born with\na healthy body, is of good family and grows up a godly man. Another is born\nwith a maimed and crooked body and develops into a wicked man and pays the\npenalty. Why must a just and holy god create one with so many advantages and\nthe other with disadvantages? The person has no choice. The evildoer has a\nconsciousness of his guilt. The difference between virtue and vice was\nexpounded. If God willed all things there would be an end to all science.\nHow far can man go down? Is it possible for man to go back to brute again?\n", "\nKananda was glad he was a Hindu. When Jerusalem was destroyed by the Romans\nseveral thousand [Jews] settled in India. When the Persians were driven from\ntheir country by the Arabs several thousand found refuge in the same country\nand none were molested. The Hindu, believe all religions are true, but\ntheirs antedates all others. Missionaries are never molested by the Hindus.\nThe first English missionaries mere prevented from landing in that country\nby English and it was a Hindu that interceded for them and gave them the\nfirst hand. Religion is that which believes in all. Religion was compared to\nthe blind men and the elephant. Each man felt of a special part and from it\ndrew his conclusions of what an elephant was. Each was right in his way and\nyet all were needed to form a whole. Hindu philosophers say \"truth to truth,\nlower truth to higher.\" It is an idle dream of those who think that all will\nat some time think alike, for that would be the death of religion. Every\nreligion breaks up into little sects, each claiming to he the true one and\nall the others wrong. Persecution is unknown in Buddahism. They sent out the\nfirst missionaries and are the only ones who can say they have converted\nmillions without the shedding of a single drop of blood. Hindus, with all\ntheir faults and superstitions, never persecute. The speaker wanted to know\nhow it was the Christians allowed such iniquities as are everywhere present\nin Christian countries.\n"]
