As it went, he chanted one song after\nanother of R\u00e2mpras\u00e2d, and now and again he would translate a verse:\n", "I call upon thee, Mother.\nFor though his mother strikes him,\nThe child cries, \"Mother! Mother!\"\n", "And then with the haughty dignity of an offended child, some-thing that\nended, \"I am not the son to call any other woman 'Mother'!\"\n", "JULY 17.\n", "It must have been next day that he came into Dhira Mata's Dunga and talked\nof Bhakti. First it was that curious Hindu thought of Shiva and Um\u00e2 in one.\nIt is easy to give the words, but without the voice how comparatively dead\nthey seem! Externally it was a tapering pyramid \u2014 with its top\ntruncated,  to  give foot-hold to a bush \u2014 supported on a four-pierced\npedestal. In its architecture, trefoil and triangular arches were combined\nin an unusual fashion with each other and with the straight-lined lintel. It\nwas built with marvellous solidity, and the necessary lines were somewhat\nobscured by heavy ornament. . . .\n", "For  all  but  the  Swami  himself,  this was our first peep at Indian\narchaeology. So when he had been through it, he taught us how to observe the\ninterior.\n", "In the centre of the ceiling was a large sun-medallion, set in a square\nwhose points were the points of the compass. This left four equal triangles\nat the corners of the ceiling, which were filled with sculpture in low\nrelief, male and female figures intertwined with serpents, beautifully done.\nOn the wall were empty spaces, where seemed to have been a band of topes.\n", "Outside, carvings were similarly distributed.
