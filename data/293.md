["\n", "LVIII\n", "\n", "\nDear Alasinga,\n\n", "\n. . . I have used some very harsh words in my letters, which you ought to\nexcuse, as you know, I get nervous at times. The work is terribly hard; and\nthe more it is growing, the harder it is becoming. I need a long rest very\nbadly. Yet a great work is before me in England.\n", "\nHave patience, my son \u2014 it will grow beyond all your expectations. . . .\nEvery  work  has  got  to pass through hundreds of difficulties before\nsucceeding. Those that persevere will see the light, sooner or later.\n", "\nI have succeeded now in rousing the very heart of the American civilisation,\nNew York, but it has been a terrific struggle. . . . I have spent nearly,\nall I had on this New York work and in England. Now things are in such a\nshape that they will go on. Just as I am writing to you, every one of my\nbones is paining after last afternoon's long Sunday public lecture. Then you\nsee, to put the Hindu ideas into English and then make out of dry philosophy\nand intricate mythology and queer startling psychology, a religion which\nshall be easy, simple, popular, and at the same time meet the requirements\nof  the  highest  minds \u2014 is a task only those can understand who have\nattempted it. The dry, abstract Advaita must become living \u2014 poetic \u2014 in\neveryday life; out of hopelessly intricate mythology must come concrete\nmoral forms; and out of bewildering Yogi-ism must come the most scientific\nand practical psychology \u2014 and all this must be put in a form so that a\nchild may grasp it. That is my life's work. The Lord only knows how far I\nshall succeed. \"To work we have the right, not to the fruits thereof.\" It is\nhard work, my boy, hard work! To keep one's self steady in the midst of this\nwhirl of K\u00e2ma-K\u00e2nchana (lust and gold) and hold on to one's own ideals,\nuntil disciples are moulded to conceive of the ideas of realisation and\nperfect renunciation, is indeed difficult work, my boy. Thank God, already\nthere is great success. I cannot blame the missionaries and others for not\nunderstanding me \u2014 they hardly ever saw a man who did not care in the least\nabout women and money. At first they could not believe it to be possible;\nhow could they? You must not think that the Western nations have the same\nideas of chastity and purity as the Indians. Their equivalents are virtue\nand courage. . . . People are now flocking to me. Hundreds have now become\nconvinced that there are men who can really control their bodily desires;\nand reverence and respect for these principles are growing. All things come\nto him who waits. May you be blessed for ever and ever!\n", "\n", "Yours with love,\nVivekananda.\n", "\n"]
