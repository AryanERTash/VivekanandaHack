["\n", "THE HINDOO MONK\n", "(Appeal-Avalanche, January 16, 1894)\n", "\nSwami Vive Kananda, the Hindoo monk, who is to lecture at the Auditorium\n[Memphis] tonight, is one of the most eloquent men who has ever appeared on\nthe religious or lecture platform in this country. His matchless oratory,\ndeep penetration into things occult, his cleverness in debate, and great\nearnestness captured the closest attention of the world's thinking men at\nthe World's Fair Parliament of Religion, and the admiration of thousands of\npeople who have since heard him during his lecture tour through many of the\nstates of the Union.\n", "\nIn conversation he is a most pleasant gentleman; his choice of words are the\ngems of the English language, and his general bearing ranks him with the\nmost cultured people of Western etiquette and custom. As a companion he is a\nmost charming man, and as a conversationalist he is, perhaps, not surpassed\nin the drawing-rooms of any city in the Western World. He speaks English not\nonly distinctly, but fluently, and his ideas, as new as sparkling, drop from\nhis tongue in a perfectly bewildering overflow of ornamental language.\n", "\nSwami Vive Kananda, by his inherited religion or early teachings, grew up a\nBrahmin, but becoming converted to the Hindoo religion he sacrificed his\nrank and became a Hindoo priest, or as known in the country of oriental\nideality, a sanyasin. He had always been a close student of the wonderful\nand mysterious works of nature as drawn from God's high conception, and with\nyears spent as both a student and teacher in the higher colleges of that\neastern country, he acquired a knowledge that has given him a worldwide\nreputation as one of the most thoughtful scholars of the age.\n", "\nHis  wonderful  first  address  before the members of the World's Fair\nParliament stamped him at once as a leader in that great body of religious\nthinkers. During the session he was frequently heard in defence of his\nreligion, and some of the most beautiful and philosophical gems that grace\nthe English language rolled from his lips there in picturing the higher\nduties that man owed to man and to his Creator. He is an artist in thought,\nan idealist in belief and a dramatist on the platform.\n", "\nSince his arrival in Memphis he has been guest of Mr. Hu L. Brinkley, where\nhe has received calls day and evening from many in Memphis who desired to\npay their respects to him. He is also an informal guest at the Tennessee\nClub and was a guest at the reception given by Mrs. S. R. Shepherd, Saturday\nevening. Col. R. B. Snowden gave a dinner at his home at Annesdale in honor\nof the distinguished visitor on Sunday, where he met Assistant Bishop Thomas\nF. Gailor, Rev. Dr. George Patterson and a number of other clergymen.\n", "\nYesterday afternoon he lectured before a large and fashionable audience\ncomposed of the members of the Nineteenth Century Club in the rooms of the\nclub in the Randolph Building. Tonight he will be heard at the Auditorium on\n\"Hindooism\".\n"]
