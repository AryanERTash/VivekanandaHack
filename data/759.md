["\n", "XIV\n", "DETROIT,\n", "17th March, 1894.\n", "DEAR SISTER (Miss Harriet McKindley of Chicago.),\n", "\nGot your package yesterday. Sorry that you send those stockings \u2014 I could\nhave got some myself here. Glad that it shows your love. After all, the\nsatchel has become more than a thoroughly stuffed sausage. I do not know how\nto carry it along.\n", "I have returned today to Mrs. Bagley's as she was sorry that I would remain\nso long with Mr. Palmer. Of course in Palmer's house there was real \"good\ntime\". He is a real jovial heartwhole fellow, and likes \"good time\" a little\ntoo much and his \"hot Scotch\". But he is right along innocent and childlike\nin his simplicity.\n", "He  was  very  sorry that I came away, but I could not help. Here is a\nbeautiful  young girl. I saw her twice, I do not remember her name. So\nbrainy, so beautiful, so spiritual, so unworldly! Lord bless her! She came\nthis morning with Mrs. M'cDuvel and talked so beautifully and deep and\nspiritually \u2014 that I was quite astounded. She knows everything about the\nYogis and is herself much advanced in practice!!\n", "\"Thy ways are beyond searching out.\" Lord bless her \u2014 so innocent, holy, and\npure! This is the grandest recompense in my terribly toilsome, miserable\nlife \u2014 the finding of holy happy faces like you from time to time. The great\nBuddhist prayer is, \"I bow down to all holy men on earth\". I feel the real\nmeaning of this prayer whenever I see a face upon which the finger of the\nLord has written in unmistakable letters \"mine\". May you all be happy,\nblessed, good and pure as you are for ever and ever. May your feet never\ntouch the mud and dirt of this terrible world. May you live and pass away\nlike flowers as you are born \u2014 is the constant prayer of your brother.\n", "VIVEKANANDA.\n"]
