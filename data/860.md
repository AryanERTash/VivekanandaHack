["\n", "XIII\n", "To Shri Haridas Viharidas Desai\n", "KHETRI\n", "May, 1893.\n", "DEAR DIWANJI SAHEB,\n", "Surely my letter had not reached you before you wrote to me. The perusal of\nyour letter gave me both pleasure and pain simultaneously: pleasure, to see\nthat I have the good fortune to be loved by a man of your heart, power, and\nposition;  and  pain,  to  see  that my motive has been misinterpreted\nthroughout. Believe me, that I love you and respect you like a father and\nthat my gratitude towards you and your family is surely unbounded. The fact\nis this. You may remember that I had from before a desire to go to Chicago.\nWhen at Madras, the people there, of their own accord, in conjunction with\nH.H. of Mysore and Ramnad made every arrangement to send me up. And you may\nalso remember that between H.H. of Khetri and myself there are the closest\nties of love. Well, I, as a matter of course, wrote to him that I was going\nto America. Now the Raja of Khetri thought in his love that I was bound to\nsee him once before I departed, especially as the Lord has given him an heir\nto the throne and great rejoicings were going on here; and to make sure of\nmy coming he sent his Private Secretary all the way to Madras to fetch me,\nand of course I was bound to come. In the meanwhile I telegraphed to your\nbrother at Nadiad to know whether you were there, and, unfortunately, the\nanswer I could not get; therefore, the Secretary who, poor fellow, had\nsuffered terribly for his master in going to and from Madras and with his\neye wholly on the fact that his master would be unhappy if we could not\nreach Khetri within the Jalsa (festival), bought tickets at once for Jaipur.\nOn our way we met Mr. Ratilal who informed me that my wire was received and\nduly answered and that Mr. Viharidas was expecting me. Now it is for you to\njudge, whose duty it has been so long to deal even justice. What would or\ncould I do in this connection? If I would have got down, I could not have\nreached in time for the Khetri rejoicings; on the other hand, my motives\nmight be misinterpreted. But I know you and your brother's love for me, and\nI knew also that I would have to go back to Bombay in a few days on my way\nto Chicago. I thought that the best solution was to postpone my visit till\nmy  return.  As for my feeling affronted at not being attended by your\nbrothers, it is a new discovery of yours which I never even dreamt of; or,\nGod knows, perhaps, you have become a thought-reader. Jokes apart, my dear\nDiwanji Saheb, I am the same frolicsome, mischievous but, I assure you,\ninnocent boy you found me at Junagad, and my love for your noble self is the\nsame or increased a hundredfold, because I have had a mental comparison\nbetween yourself and the Diwans of nearly all the states in Dakshin, and the\nLord be my witness how my tongue was fluent in your praise (although I know\nthat my powers are quite inadequate to estimate your noble qualities) in\nevery Southern court. If this be not a sufficient explanation, I implore you\nto pardon me as a father pardons a son, and let me not be haunted with the\nimpression that I was ever ungrateful to one who was so good to me.\n", "Yours,\n", "VIVEKANANDA.\n", "PS. I depend on you to remove any misconception in the mind of your brother\nabout my not getting down and that, even had I been the very devil, I could\nnot forget their kindness and good offices for me.\n", "As to the other two Swamis, they were my Gurubhais, who went to you last at\nJunagad; of them one is our leader. I met them after three years, and we\ncame together as far as Abu and then I left them. If you wish, I can take\nthem back to Nadiad on my way to Bombay. May the Lord shower His blessings\non you and yours.\n", "Yours,\n", "\nV.\n"]
