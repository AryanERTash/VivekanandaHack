["\n", "CXI\n", "(Translated from Bengali)\n", "To Swami Brahmananda\n", "MURREE,\n", "11th October, 1897.\n", "MY DEAR RAKHAL,\n", "I feel I have been working as if under an irresistible impulse for the last\nten days, beginning from Kashmir. It may be either a physical or a mental\ndisease. Now I have come to the conclusion that I am unfit for further work.\n. . . I now understand that I have been very harsh to all of you. But I\nknew, however, that you would bear with all my shortcomings; in the Math\nthere is no one else who will do so. I have been increasingly harsh to you.\nWhatever has happened is now past \u2014 it is all the result of past Karma. What\nis the good of my repentance? I do not believe in it. It is all Karma.\nWhatever of Mother's work was to be accomplished through me, She made me do,\nand has now flung me aside breaking down my body and mind. Her will be done!\n", "Now I retire from all this work. In a day or two I shall give up everything\nand wander out alone; I shall spend the rest of my life quietly in some\nplace or other. Forgive me if you all will, or do what you like.\n", "Mrs.  Bull has given much of the money. She has implicit confidence in\nSharat. Do the work of the Math with Sharat's advice; or do as you will.\n", "But I have all along been like a hero \u2014 I want my work to be quick like\nlightning, and firm as adamant. Likewise shall I die also. Therefore kindly\ndo my work for me \u2014 no question of success or defeat enters here at all. I\nhave never retreated in a fight \u2014 shall I now . . .\u00a0? There is success and\nfailure in every work. But I am inclined to believe that one who is a coward\nwill be born after death as an insect or a worm, that there is no salvation\nfor a coward even after millions of years of penance. Well, shall I after\nall be born as a worm? . . . In my eyes this world is mere play \u2014 and it\nwill always remain as such. Should one spend six long months brooding over\nthe questions of honour and disgrace, gain and loss pertaining to this? . .\n. I am a man of action. Simply advice upon advice is being given \u2014 this one\nsays  this, that one says that; again that man threatens, and this one\nfrightens! This life is not, in my view, such a sweet thing that I would\nlong  to  live through so much care and caution and fear. Money, life,\nfriends, and relatives, and the love of men and myself \u2014 if one wants to\nenter into work fully assured beforehand of all these \u2014 if one has to be so\nmuch ridden with fear, then one will get just what Gurudeva used to say,\n\"The crow thinks itself very clever but . . .\" (The crow thinks itself very\nclever, but it cannot help eating filth.) \u2014 well, he will get that. After\nall, what is the purpose behind all these \u2014 money and wealth, Maths and\ninstitutions, preaching and lecturing? There is only one purpose in the\nwhole of life \u2014 education. Otherwise what is the use of men and women, land\nand wealth?\n", "So loss of money, or loss of anything else \u2014 I cannot bother about, and I\nwill  not. When I fight, I fight with girded loins \u2014 that much I fully\nunderstand; and I also understand that man, that hero, that god, who says,\n\"Don't care, be fearless. O brave one, here I am by your side!\" To such a\nman-god I offer a million salutations. Their presence purifies the world,\nthey are the saviours of the world. And the others who always wail, \"Oh,\ndon't  go forward, there is this danger, there is that danger\" \u2014 those\ndyspeptics \u2014 they always tremble with fear. But through the grace of the\nDivine Mother my mind is so strong that even the most terrible dyspepsia\nshall not make me a coward. To cowards what advice shall I offer? \u2014 nothing\nwhatsoever have I to say. But this I desire, that I should find shelter at\nthe feet of those brave souls who dared to do great deeds even though they\nfailed to succeed, of those heroes who never quailed nor shirked, of those\nfighters who never disobeyed orders through fear or pride. I am the child of\nthe Divine Mother, the source of all power and strength. To me, cringing,\nfawning, whining, degrading inertia and hell are one and the same thing. O\nMother of the Universe, O my Gurudeva, who would constantly say, \"This is a\nhero!\" \u2014 I pray that I may not have to die a coward. This is my prayer, O\nbrother. \" [111_rakhal_01.jpg] \u2014 certainly there is, or there will be born\none  equal  to  me\"; some one or other will certainly arise from these\nthousands of devotees of Shri Ramakrishna who will be like me, and who will\nbe able to understand me.\n", "O hero, awake, and dream no more. Death has caught you by the forelock . . .\nstill fear not. What I have never done \u2014 fleeing from the battle \u2014 well,\nwill that happen today? For fear of defeat shall I retreat from the fight?\nDefeat is the ornament the hero adorns himself with. What, to acknowledge\ndefeat without fighting! O Mother, Mother! . . . Not one capable of even\nplaying second fiddle and yet the mind filled with petty self-importance,\n\"We understand everything\". . . . Now I retire; . . . everything I leave in\nyour control. If Mother sends me men again in whose heart there is courage,\nin whose hands strength, in whose eyes there is fire, real children of the\nMother \u2014 if She gives me even one such, then I shall work again, then I\nshall return. Otherwise, I shall take it that, by Mother's will, this is the\nend. I am in a tremendous hurry, I want to work at hurricane speed, and I\nwant fearless hearts.\n", "I have rebuked poor Sarada severely. What to do? . . . I do scold; but I\nalso have much to complain. . . . Almost suffocated by short breathing,\nstanding and standing, I have written an article for him. . . . It is all\ngood, otherwise how will renunciation come? . . . Will Mother in the end\nkill me with attachment? I have offended all of you \u2014 do what you want.\n", "I bless you all with a full heart. May Mother enshrine Herself in your\nhearts as strength: [111_rakhal_02.jpg]  \u2014 the support that is fearlessness\n\u2014  may She make you all fearless. This I have seen in life \u2014 he who is\nover-cautious about himself falls into dangers at every step; he who is\nafraid of losing honour and respect, gets only disgrace; he who is always\nafraid of loss always loses. . . . May all good attend you all.\n", "Yours affectionately,\n", "\nVIVEKANANDA.\n"]
