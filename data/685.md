["\n", "(RECORDED BY MISS S. E. WALDO, A DISCIPLE)\n", "TUESDAY, August 6, 1895.\n", "Without the \"I\" there can be no \"you\" outside. From this some philosophers\ncame to the conclusion that the external world did not exist save in the\nsubject; that the \"you\" existed only in the \"I\". Others have argued that the\n\"I\" can only be known through the \"you\" and with equal logic. These two\nviews are partial truths, each wrong in part and each right in part. Thought\nis as much material and as much in nature as body is. Both matter and mind\nexist in a third, a unity which divides itself into the two. This unity is\nthe Atman, the real Self.\n", "There is being, \"x\", which is manifesting itself as both mind and matter.\nIts movements in the seen are along certain fixed lines called law. As a\nunity,  it  is free; as many, it is bound by law. Still, with all this\nbondage, an idea of freedom is ever present, and this is Nivritti, or the\n\"dragging from attachment\". The materialising forces which through desire\nlead us to take an active part in worldly affairs are called Pravritti.\n", "That action is moral which frees us from the bondage of matter and vice\nversa. This world appears infinite, because everything is in a circle; it\nreturns to whence it came. The circle meets, so there is no rest or peace\nhere in any place. We must get out. Mukti is the one end to be attained. . .\n.\n", "Evil changes in form but remains the same in quality. In ancient times force\nruled, today it is cunning. Misery in India is not so bad as in America,\nbecause  the  poor  man  here sees the greater contrast to his own bad\ncondition.\n", "Good and evil are inextricably combined, and one cannot be had without the\nother. The sum total of energy in this universe is like a lake, every wave\ninevitably leads to a corresponding depression. The sum total is absolutely\nthe same; so to make one man happy is to make another unhappy. External\nhappiness is material and the supply is fixed; so that not one grain can be\nhad  by  one person without taking from another. Only bliss beyond the\nmaterial world can be had without loss to any. Material happiness is but a\ntransformation of material sorrow.\n", "Those who are born in the wave and kept in it do not see the depression and\nwhat is there. Never think, you can make the world better and happier. The\nbullock in the oil-mill never reaches the wisp of hay tied in front of him,\nhe only grinds out the oil. So we chase the will-o'-the-wisp of happiness\nthat always eludes us, and we only grind nature's mill, then die, merely to\nbegin again. If we could get rid of evil, we should never catch a glimpse of\nanything higher; we would be satisfied and never struggle to get free. When\nman finds that all search for happiness in matter is nonsense, then religion\nbegins. All human knowledge is but a part of religion.\n", "In the human body the balance between good and evil is so even that there is\na chance for man to wish to free himself from both.\n", "The free never became bound; to ask how he did, is an illogical question.\nWhere no bondage is, there is no cause and effect. \"I became a fox in a\ndream and a dog chased me.\" Now how can I ask why the dog chased me? The fox\nwas a part of the dream, and the dog followed as a matter of course; but\nboth belong to the dream and have no existence outside. Science and religion\nare both attempts to help us out of the bondage; only religion is the more\nancient, and we have the superstition that it is the more holy. In a way it\nis, because it makes morality a vital point, and science does not.\n", "\"Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God.\" This sentence alone\nwould save mankind if all books and prophets were lost. This purity of heart\nwill bring the vision of God. It is the theme of the whole music of this\nuniverse. In purity is no bondage. Remove the veils of ignorance by purity,\nthen we manifest ourselves as we really are and know that we were never in\nbondage. The seeing of many is the great sin of all the world. See all as\nSelf and love all; let all idea of separateness go. . . .\n", "The diabolical man is a part of my body as a wound or a burn is. We have to\nnurse it and get it better; so continually nurse and help the diabolical\nman, until he \"heals\" and is once happy and healthy.\n", "While we think on the relative plane, we have the right to believe that as\nbodies we can be hurt by relative things and equally that we can be helped\nby them. This idea of help, abstracted, is what we call God. The sum total\nof all ideas of help is God.\n", "God is the abstract compound of all that is merciful and good and helpful;\nthat should be the sole idea. As Atman, we have no body; so to say, \"I am\nGod, and poison does not hurt me\", is an absurdity. While there is a body\nand we see it, we have not realised God. Can the little whirlpool remain\nafter the river vanishes? Cry for help, and you will get it; and at last you\nwill find that the one crying for help has vanished, and so has the Helper,\nand the play is over; only the Self remains.\n", "This once done, come back and play as you will. This body can then do no\nevil,  because it is not until the evil forces are all burned out that\nliberation comes. All dross has been burned out and there remains \"flame\nwithout heat and without smoke\".\n", "The past momentum carries on the body, but it can only do good, because the\nbad was all gone before freedom came. The dying thief on the cross reaped\nthe effects of his past actions. He had been a Yogi and had slipped; then he\nhad to be born again; again he slipped and became a thief; but the past good\nhe had done bore fruit, and he met Jesus in the moment when liberation could\ncome, and one word made him free.\n", "Buddha set his greatest enemy free, because he, by hating him (Buddha) so\nmuch, kept constantly thinking of him; that thought purified his mind, and\nhe became ready for freedom. Therefore think of God all the time, and that\nwill purify you. . . .\n", "(Thus ended the beautiful lessons of our beloved Guru. The following Monday\nhe left Thousand Island Park and returned to New York.)\n"]
