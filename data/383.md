["\n", "ISHVARA AND BRAHMAN\n", "\nIn reply to a question as to the exact position of Ishvara in Vedantic\nPhilosophy, the Swami Vivekananda, while in Europe, gave the following\ndefinition:\n", "\"Ishvara is the sum total of individuals, yet He is an Individual, as the\nhuman body is a unit, of which each cell is an individual. Samashti or\ncollected equals God; Vyashti or analysed equals the Jiva. The existence of\nIshvara, therefore, depends on that of Jiva, as the body on the cell, and\nvice versa. Thus, Jiva and Ishvara are coexistent beings; when one exists,\nthe  other must. Also, because, except on our earth, in all the higher\nspheres, the amount of good being vastly in excess of the amount of evil,\nthe  sum  total  (Ishvara) may be said to be all-good. Omnipotence and\nomniscience are obvious qualities and need no argument to prove from the\nvery fact of totality. Brahman is beyond both these and is not a conditioned\nstate; it is the only Unit not composed of many units, the principle which\nruns through all from a cell to God, without which nothing can exist; and\nwhatever is real is that principle, or Brahman. When I think I am Brahman, I\nalone  exist; so with others. Therefore, each one is the whole of that\nprinciple.\"\n"]
