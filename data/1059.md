["\n", "XV\n", "To Mrs. G. W. Hale\n", "C/O DR. GUERNSEY\n", "528 FIFTH AVENUE\n", "NEW YORK\n", "2 April 1894\n", "DEAR MOTHER,\n", "I am in New York. The gentleman [Dr. Guernsey] whose guest I am is a very\nnice and learned and well-to-do man. He had an only son whom he lost last\nJuly. Has only a daughter now. The old couple have received a great shock,\nbut they are pure and God-loving people and bear it manfully. The lady of\nthe house is very, very kind and good. They are trying to help me as much as\nthey can and they will do a good deal, I have no doubt.\n", "Awaiting further developments. This Thursday [April 5] they will invite a\nnumber of the brainy people of the Union League Club and other places of\nwhich the Doctor is a member, and see what comes out of it. Parlour lectures\nare a great feature in this city, and more can be made by each such lecture\nthan even platform talks in other cities.\n", "It is a very clean city. None of that black smoke tarring everyone in five\nminutes; and the street in which the Doctor lives is a nice, quiet one.\n", "Hope the sisters are doing well and enjoying their music, both in the opera\nand the parlour. I am sure I would have appreciated the music at the opera\nabout which Miss Mary wrote to me. I am sure the opera musicians do not show\nthe interior anatomy of their throats and lungs.\n", "Kindly give brother Sam[6]* my deep love. I am sure he is bewaring of the\nvidders.[7]* Some of the Baby Bagleys are going to Chicago. They will go to\nsee you, and I am sure you would like them very much.\n", "Nothing more to write. With all respect, love and obedience,\n", "Your son,\n", "VIVEKANANDA.\n", "PS  \u2014 I have not to ask now for addresses. Mrs. Sherman (Mrs. Bagley\u2019s\nmarried daughter.) has given me a little book with A., B., C., etc., marks\nand has written under them all the addresses I need; and I hope to write all\nthe future addresses in the same manner. What an example of self-help I\nam!![8]*\n", "V.\n"]
