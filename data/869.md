["\n", "XXII\n", "To Mrs. George W. Hale\n", "C/O Dr. E. Guernsey,\n", "FISHKILL LANDING, N.Y.,\n", "July, 1894.\n", "DEAR MOTHER,\n", "I came yesterday to this place, and shall remain here a few days. I received\nin New York a letter from you but did not receive any Interior, for which I\nam glad, because I am not perfect yet, and knowing the \"unselfish love\" the\nPresbyterian priests, especially the Interior has for \"me\", I want to keep\naloof from rousing bad feelings towards these \"sweet Christian gentlemen\" in\nmy heart.\n", "Our religion teaches that anger is a great sin, even if it is \"righteous\".\nEach must follow his own religion. I could not for my soul distinguish ever\nthe  distinction  between  \"religious  anger\" and \"commonplace anger\",\n\"religious killing\" and \"commonplace killing\", \"religious slandering and\nirreligious\", and so forth. Nor may that \"fine\" ethical distinction ever\nenter into the ethics of our nation! Jesting apart, Mother Church, I do not\ncare the least for the gambols these men play, seeing as I do through and\nthrough the insincerity, the hypocrisy, and love of self and name that is\nthe only motive power in these men.\n", "As to the photographs, the first time the Babies got a few copies, and the\nsecond time you brought a few copies; you know they are to give 50 copies in\nall. Sister Isabelle knows better than I.\n", "With my sincerest love and respects for you and Father Pope.\n", "I remain,\n", "Yours,\n", "VIVEKANANDA.\n", "PS. How are you enjoying the heat? I am bearing the heat very well here. I\nhad an invitation to Swampscott on the sea from a very rich lady whose\nacquaintance I made last winter in New York, but I declined with thanks. I\nam very careful not to take the hospitality of anybody here, especially the\nrich. I had a few other invitations from some very rich people here. I\nrefused; I have by this time seen the whole business through. Lord bless you\nand yours, Mother Church, for your sincerity. Oh! it is so rare in this\nworld.\n", "Yours affectionately,\n", "\nV.\n"]
