You have\nyour minds pitched high and steady, it will do you no harm. May you have\nexceeding love for one another among yourselves, and it would be enough to\nhave an attitude of indifference towards public criticisms. Kalikrishna Babu\nhas deep love for the cause and is a great man. Please convey my special\nlove to him. Indifference, indifference, indifference! I have already\nwritten  to  Shashi  (Ramakrishnananda)  in detail. Please do not send\nnewspapers and tracts any more. \"Take the husking hammer to heaven, and\nthere it will do its husking\", as the Bengali saying goes. The same trudging\nabout here as it was in India, only with the carrying of others' loads\nadded! How can I procure customers for people's books in this land? I am\nonly one amongst the many here and nothing more. Whatever the papers and\nthings of that sort in this country write about me, I make an offering of to\nthe Fire-God. You also do the same. That is the proper course.\n", "A bit of public demonstration was necessary for Guru Maharaja's work. It is\ndone, and so far so good. Now you must on no account pay any heed to what\nthe rabble may be prattling about us. Whether I make my pile or do whatever\nelse I am reported to, shall the opinions of the riff-raff stand in the way\nof His work? My dear brother, you are yet a boy, while I am growing grey.\nWhat regard I have for the pronouncements and opinions of such people, you\nshould guess from this. So long as you gird up your loins and rally behind\nme, there is no fear even if the whole world combine against us. This much I\nunderstand that I shall have to take up a very lofty attitude, I should not,\nI think, write to anyone except to you. By the by, where is Gunanidhi? Let anyone write anything for or\nagainst me in the papers; you shouldn't take the slightest notice. And my\ndear brother, I beseech you repeatedly not to send me any more newspapers by\nthe basketful. How can you talk of rest now? We shall have rest awhile only\nwhen we give up this body.
