["HISTORICAL EVOLUTION OF INDIA\n", "OM TAT SAT\n", "Om Namo Bhagavate R\u00e2makrishn\u00e2ya\n", "\u0928\u093e\u0938\u0924\u0903 \u0938\u0924\u094d \u091c\u093e\u092f\u0924\u0947  \u2014 Existence cannot be produced by non-existence.\n", "Non-existence can never be the cause of what exists. Something cannot come\nout of nothing. That the law of causation is omnipotent and knows no time or\nplace when it did not exist is a doctrine as old as the Aryan race, sung by\nits  ancient  poet-seers, formulated by its philosophers, and made the\ncorner-stone upon which the Hindu man even of today builds his whole scheme\nof life.\n", "There was an inquisitiveness in the race to start with, which very soon\ndeveloped into bold analysis, and though, in the first attempt, the work\nturned  out  might be like the attempts with shaky hands of the future\nmaster-sculptor, it very soon gave way to strict science, bold attempts, and\nstartling results.\n", "Its boldness made these men search every brick of their sacrificial altars;\nscan,  cement,  and pulverise every word of their scriptures; arrange,\nre-arrange, doubt, deny, or explain the ceremonies. It turned their gods\ninside  out,  and assigned only a secondary place to their omnipotent,\nomniscient,  omnipresent  Creator  of  the  universe,  their ancestral\nFather-in-heaven; or threw Him altogether overboard as useless, and started\na world-religion without Him with even now the largest following of any\nreligion. It evolved the science of geometry from the arrangements of bricks\nto build various altars, and startled the world with astronomical knowledge\nthat arose from the attempts accurately to time their worship and oblations.\nIt made their contribution to the science of mathematics the largest of any\nrace, ancient or modern, and to their knowledge of chemistry, of metallic\ncompounds in medicine, their scale of musical notes, their invention of the\nbow-instruments \u2014 (all) of great service in the building of modern European\ncivilisation.  It  led  them  to invent the science of building up the\nchild-mind through shining fables, of which every child in every civilised\ncountry learns in a nursery or a school and carries an impress through life.\n", "Behind and before this analytical keenness, covering it as in a velvet\nsheath, was the other great mental peculiarity of the race \u2014 poetic insight.\nIts religion, its philosophy, its history, its ethics, its politics were all\ninlaid in a flower-bed of poetic imagery \u2014 the miracle of language which was\ncalled  Sanskrit  or  \"perfected\",  lending  itself  to expressing and\nmanipulating them better than any other tongue. The aid of melodious numbers\nwas invoked even to express the hard facts of mathematics.\n", "This analytical power and the boldness of poetical visions which urged it\nonward are the two great internal causes in the make-up of the Hindu race.\nThey together formed, as it were, the keynote to the national character.\nThis combination is what is always making the race press onwards beyond the\nsenses \u2014 the secret of those speculations which are like the steel blades\nthe artisans used to manufacture \u2014 cutting through bars of iron, yet pliable\nenough to be easily bent into a circle.\n", "They wrought poetry in silver and gold; the symphony of jewels, the maze of\nmarble wonders, the music of colours, the fine fabrics which belong more to\nthe fairyland of dreams than to the real \u2014 have back of them thousands of\nyears of working of this national trait.\n", "Arts and sciences, even the realities of domestic life, are covered with a\nmass of poetical conceptions, which are pressed forward till the sensuous\ntouches the supersensuous and the real gets the rose-hue of the unreal.\n", "The earliest glimpses we have of this race show it already in the possession\nof this characteristic, as an instrument of some use in its hands. Many\nforms of religion and society must have been left behind in the onward\nmarch, before we find the race as depicted in the scriptures, the Vedas.\n", "An organised pantheon, elaborate ceremonials, divisions of society into\nhereditary classes necessitated by a variety of occupations, a great many\nnecessaries and a good many luxuries of life are already there.\n", "Most  modern  scholars  are agreed that surroundings as to climate and\nconditions, purely Indian, were not yet working on the race.\n", "Onwards through several centuries, we come to a multitude surrounded by the\nsnows of Himalayas on the north and the heat of the south \u2014 vast plains,\ninterminable forests, through which mighty rivers roll their tides. We catch\na glimpse of different races \u2014 Dravidians, Tartars, and Aboriginals pouring\nin their quota of blood, of speech, of manners and religions. And at last a\ngreat nation emerges to our view \u2014 still keeping the type of the Aryan \u2014\nstronger, broader, and more organised by the assimilation. We find the\ncentral assimilative core giving its type and character to the whole mass,\nclinging on with great pride to its name of \"Aryan\", and, though willing to\ngive  other races the benefits of its civilisation, it was by no means\nwilling to admit them within the \"Aryan\" pale.\n", "The Indian climate again gave a higher direction to the genius of the race.\nIn  a land where nature was propitious and yielded easy victories, the\nnational mind started to grapple with and conquer the higher problems of\nlife in the field of thought. Naturally the thinker, the priest, became the\nhighest class in the Indian society, and not the man of the sword. The\npriests again, even at that dawn of history, put most of their energy in\nelaborating  rituals;  and  when  the nation began to find the load of\nceremonies and lifeless rituals too heavy \u2014 came the first philosophical\nspeculations, and the royal race was the first to break through the maze of\nkilling rituals.\n", "On  the  one  hand, the majority of the priests impelled by economical\nconsiderations were bound to defend that form of religion which made their\nexistence a necessity of society and assigned them the highest place in the\nscale of caste; on the other hand, the king-caste, whose strong right hand\nguarded and guided the nation and who now found itself as leading in the\nhigher thoughts also, were loath to give up the first place to men who only\nknew how to conduct a ceremonial. There were then others, recruited from\nboth the priests and king-castes, who ridiculed equally the ritualists and\nphilosophers, declared spiritualism as fraud and priestcraft, and upheld the\nattainment of material comforts as the highest goal of life. The people,\ntired of ceremonials and wondering at the philosophers, joined in masses the\nmaterialists.  This  was the beginning of that caste question and that\ntriangular fight in India between ceremonials, philosophy, and materialism\nwhich has come down unsolved to our own days.\n", "The  first  solution  of  the difficulty attempted was by applying the\neclecticism which from the earliest days had taught the people to see in\ndifferences  the same truth in various garbs. The great leader of this\nschool, Krishna \u2014 himself of royal race \u2014 and his sermon, the Git\u00e2, have\nafter various vicissitudes, brought about by the upheavals of the Jains, the\nBuddhists, and other sects, fairly established themselves as the \"Prophet\"\nof India and the truest philosophy of life. Though the tension was toned\ndown for the time, it did not satisfy the social wants which were among the\ncauses \u2014 the claim of the king-race to stand first in the scale of caste and\nthe popular intolerance of priestly privilege. Krishna had opened the gates\nof spiritual knowledge and attainment to all irrespective of sex or caste,\nbut he left undisturbed the same problem on the social side. This again has\ncome  down  to  our own days, in spite of the gigantic struggle of the\nBuddhists, Vaishnavas, etc. to attain social equality for all.\n", "Modern India admits spiritual equality of all souls \u2014 but strictly keeps the\nsocial difference.\n", "Thus we find the struggle renewed all along the line in the seventh century\nbefore the Christian era and finally in the sixth, overwhelming the ancient\norder of things under Sh\u00e2kya Muni, the Buddha. In their reaction against the\nprivileged priesthood, Buddhists swept off almost every bit of the old\nritual of the Vedas, subordinated the gods of the Vedas to the position of\nservants to their own human saints, and declared the \"Creator and Supreme\nRuler\" as an invention of priestcraft and superstition.\n", "But the aim of Buddhism was reform of the Vedic religion by standing against\nceremonials requiring offerings of animals, against hereditary caste and\nexclusive  priesthood, and against belief in permanent souls. It never\nattempted  to  destroy that religion, or overturn the social order. It\nintroduced a vigorous method by organising a class of Sanny\u00e2sins into a\nstrong monastic brotherhood, and the Brahmav\u00e2dinis into a body of nuns \u2014 by\nintroducing images of saints in the place of altar-fires.\n", "It is probable that the reformers had for centuries the majority of the\nIndian people with them. The older forces were never entirely pacified, but\nthey  underwent  a  good  deal of modification during the centuries of\nBuddhistic supremacy.\n", "In ancient India the centres of national life were always the intellectual\nand spiritual and not political. Of old, as now, political and social power\nhas been always subordinated to spiritual and intellectual. The outburst of\nnational life was round colleges of sages and spiritual teachers. We thus\nfind  the  Samitis of the Panch\u00e2las, of the K\u00e2shyas (of Varanasi), the\nMaithilas standing out as great centres of spiritual culture and philosophy,\neven in tile Upanishads. Again these centres in turn became the focus of\npolitical ambition of the various divisions of the Aryans.\n", "The great epic Mah\u00e2bh\u00e2rata tells us of the war of the Kurus and Panchalas\nfor supremacy over the nation, in which they destroyed each other. The\nspiritual supremacy veered round and centred in the East among the Magadhas\nand Maithilas, and after the Kuru-Panchala war a sort of supremacy was\nobtained by the kings of Magadha.\n", "The Buddhist reformation and its chief field of activity were also in the\nsame eastern region; and when the Maurya kings, forced possibly by the bar\nsinister on their escutcheon, patronised and led the new movement, the new\npriest  power  joined  hands with the political power of the empire of\nPataliputra. The popularity of Buddhism and its fresh vigour made the Maurya\nkings the greatest emperors that India ever had. The power of the Maurya\nsovereigns made Buddhism that world-wide religion that we see even today.\n", "The exclusiveness of the old form of Vedic religions debarred it from taking\nready help from outside. At the same time it kept it pure and free from many\ndebasing elements which Buddhism in its propagandist zeal was forced to\nassimilate.\n", "This extreme adaptability in the long run made Indian Buddhism lose almost\nall its individuality, and extreme desire to be of the people made it unfit\nto  cope  with the intellectual forces of the mother religion in a few\ncenturies. The Vedic party in the meanwhile got rid of a good deal of its\nmost objectionable features, as animal sacrifice, and took lessons from the\nrival daughter in the judicious use of images, temple processions, and other\nimpressive performances, and stood ready to take within her fold the whole\nempire of Indian Buddhism, already tottering to its fall.\n", "And the crash came with the Scythian invasions and the total destruction of\nthe empire of Pataliputra.\n", "The invaders, already incensed at the invasion of their central Asiatic home\nby the preachers of Buddhism, found in the sun-worship of the Brahmins a\ngreat sympathy with their own solar religion \u2014 and when the Brahminist party\nwere ready to adapt and spiritualise many of the customs of the new-comers,\nthe invaders threw themselves heart and soul into the Brahminic cause.\n", "Then there is a veil of darkness and shifting shadows; there are tumults of\nwar, rumours of massacres; and the next scene rises upon a new phase of\nthings.\n", "The empire of Magadha was gone. Most of northern India was under the rule of\npetty chiefs always at war with one another. Buddhism was almost extinct\nexcept in some eastern and Himalayan provinces and in the extreme south and\nthe nation after centuries of struggle against the power of a hereditary\npriesthood awoke to find itself in the clutches of a double priesthood of\nhereditary Brahmins and exclusive monks of the new regime, with all the\npowers of the Buddhistic organisation and without their sympathy for the\npeople.\n", "A renascent India, bought by the velour and blood of the heroic Rajputs,\ndefined by the merciless intellect of a Brahmin from the same historical\nthought-centre of Mithila, led by a new philosophical impulse organised by\nShankara  and  his bands of Sannyasins, and beautified by the arts and\nliterature of the courts of M\u00e2lav\u00e2 \u2014 arose on the ruins of the old.\n", "The task before it was profound, problems vaster than any their ancestors\nhad ever faced. A comparatively small and compact race of the same blood and\nspeech and the same social and religious aspiration, trying to save its\nunity by unscalable walls around itself, grew huge by multiplication and\naddition during the Buddhistic supremacy; and (it) was divided by race,\ncolour, speech, spiritual instinct, and social ambitions into hopelessly\njarring factions. And this had to be unified and welded into one gigantic\nnation. This task Buddhism had also come to solve, and had taken it up when\nthe proportions were not so vast.\n", "So long it was a question of Aryanising the other types that were pressing\nfor admission and thus, out of different elements, making a huge Aryan body.\nIn spite of concessions and compromises, Buddhism was eminently successful\nand remained the national religion of India. But the time came when the\nallurements of sensual forms of worship, indiscriminately taken in along\nwith various low races, were too dangerous for the central Aryan core, and a\nlonger  contact would certainly have destroyed the civilisation of the\nAryans. Then came a natural reaction for self-preservation, and Buddhism and\nseparate sect ceased to live in most parts of its land of birth.\n", "The reaction-movement, led in close succession by Kum\u00e2rila in the north, and\nShankara and R\u00e2m\u00e2nuja in the south, has become the last embodiment of that\nvast accumulation of sects and doctrines and rituals called Hinduism. For\nthe last thousand years or more, its great task has been assimilation, with\nnow and then an outburst of reformation. This reaction first wanted to\nrevive the rituals of the Vedas \u2014 failing which, it made the Upanishads or\nthe philosophic portions of the Vedas its basis. It brought Vyasa's system\nof Mim\u00e2ms\u00e2 philosophy and Krishna's sermon, the Gita, to the forefront; and\nall succeeding movements have followed the same. The movement of Shankara\nforced its way through its high intellectuality; but it could be of little\nservice to the masses, because of its adherence to strict caste-laws, very\nsmall scope for ordinary emotion, and making Sanskrit the only vehicle of\ncommunication. Ramanuja on the other hand, with a most practical philosophy,\na great appeal to the emotions, an entire denial of birthrights before\nspiritual attainments, and appeals through the popular tongue completely\nsucceeded in bringing the masses back to the Vedic religion.\n", "The northern reaction of ritualism was followed by the fitful glory of the\nMalava empire. With the destruction of that in a short time, northern India\nwent to sleep as it were, for a long period, to be rudely awakened by the\nthundering onrush of Mohammedan cavalry across the passes of Afghanistan. In\nthe south, however, the spiritual upheaval of Shankara and Ramanuja was\nfollowed by the usual Indian sequence of united races and powerful empires.\nIt was the home of refuge of Indian religion and civilisation, when northern\nIndia from sea to sea lay bound at the feet of Central Asiatic conquerors.\nThe Mohammedan tried for centuries to subjugate the south, but can scarcely\nbe said to have got even a strong foothold; and when the strong and united\nempire of the Moguls was very near completing its conquest, the hills and\nplateaus of the south poured in their bands of fighting peasant horsemen,\ndetermined to die for the religion which R\u00e2md\u00e2s preached and Tuk\u00e2 sang; and\nin a short time the gigantic empire of the Moguls was only a name.\n", "The  movements  in  northern  India  during  the Mohammedan period are\ncharacterised by their uniform attempt to hold the masses back from joining\nthe religion of the conquerors \u2014 which brought in its train social and\nspiritual equality for all.\n", "The friars of the orders founded by R\u00e2m\u00e2nanda, Kabir, D\u00e2du, Chaitanya, or\nN\u00e2nak were all agreed in preaching the equality of man, however differing\nfrom each other in philosophy. Their energy was for the most part spent in\nchecking the rapid conquest of Islam among the masses, and they had very\nlittle left to give birth to new thoughts and aspirations. Though evidently\nsuccessful in their purpose of keeping the masses within the fold of the old\nreligion, and tempering the fanaticism of the Mohammedans, they were mere\napologists, struggling to obtain permission to live.\n", "One great prophet, however, arose in the north, Govind Singh, the last Guru\nof the Sikhs, with creative genius; and the result of his spiritual work was\nfollowed by the well-known political organisation of the Sikhs. We have seen\nthroughout the history of India, a spirtitual upheaval is almost always\nsucceeded by a political unity extending over more or less area of the\ncontinent, which in its turn helps to strengthen the spiritual aspiration\nthat brings it to being. But the spiritual aspiration that preceded the rise\nof the Mahratta or the Sikh empire was entirely reactionary. We seek in vain\nto find in the court of Poona or Lahore even a ray of reflection of that\nintellectual glory which surrounded the courts of the Muguls, much less the\nbrilliance of Malava or Vidy\u00e2nagara. It was intellectually the darkest\nperiod of Indian history; and both these meteoric empires, representing the\nupheaval of mass-fanaticism and hating culture with all their hearts, lost\nall their motive power as soon as they had succeeded in destroying the rule\nof the hated Mohammedans.\n", "Then there came again a period of confusion. Friends and foes, the Mogul\nempire and its destroyers, and the till then peaceful foreign traders,\nFrench and English, all joined in a m\u00eal\u00e9e of fight. For more than half a\ncentury there was nothing but war and pillage and destruction. And when the\nsmoke and dust cleared, England was stalking victorious over the rest. There\nhas  been  half a century of peace and law and order under the sway of\nBritain. Time alone will prove if it is the order of progress or not.\n", "There have been a few religious movements amongst the Indian people during\nthe British rule, following the same line that was taken up by northern\nIndian sects during the sway of the empire of Delhi. They are the voices of\nthe dead or the dying \u2014 the feeble tones of a terrorised people, pleading\nfor permission to live. They are ever eager to adjust their spiritual or\nsocial surroundings according to the tastes of the conquerors \u2014 if they are\nonly  left  the  right to live, especially the sects under the English\ndomination, in which social differences with the conquering race are more\nglaring than the spiritual. The Hindu sects of the century seem to have set\none ideal of truth before them \u2014 the approval of their English masters. No\nwonder that these sects have mushroom lives to live. The vast body of the\nIndian  people  religiously hold aloof from them, and the only popular\nrecognition they get is the jubilation of the people when they die.\n", "But possibly, for some time yet, it cannot be otherwise.\n"]
