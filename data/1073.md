["\n", "XXIX\n", "To Mrs. G. W. Hale\n", "ANNISQUAM\n", "23 August 1894\n", "DEAR MOTHER\n", "The photographs reached safely yesterday. I cannot tell exactly whether\nHarrison ought to give me more or not. They had sent only two to me at\nFishkill[6]* \u2014 not the pose I ordered, though.\n", "Narasimha has perhaps got his passage by this time. He will get it soon,\nwhether his family gives him the money or not. I have written to my friends\nin Madras to look to it, and they write me they will.\n", "I would be very glad if he becomes a Christian or Mohammedan or any religion\nthat suits him; but I am afraid for some time to come none will suit our\nfriend. Only if he becomes a Christian he will have a chance to marry again,\neven in India \u2014 the Christians there permitting it. I am so sorry to learn\nthat it is the \"bondage of heathen India\" that, after all, was the cause of\nall this mischief. We learn as we live. So we were all this time ignorantly\nand  blindly  blaming  our  much suffering, persecuted, saintly friend\nNarasimha, while all the fault was really owing to the \"bondage of heathen\nIndia\"!!!!\n", "But to give the devil his due, this heathen India has been supplying him\nwith money to go on a spree again and again. And this time too \"heathen\nIndia\" will [take] or already has taken our \"enlightened\" and persecuted\nfriend from out of his present scrape, and not \"Christian America\"!! Mrs.\nSmith's plan is not bad after all \u2014 to turn Narasimha into a missionary of\nChrist. But unfortunately for the world, many and many a time the flag of\nChrist has been entrusted to such hands. But I would beg to add that he will\nthen be only a missionary of Smithian American Christianity, not Christ's.\nArrant humbug! That thing to preach Lord Jesus!!! Is He in want of men to\nuphold  His banner? Pooh! the very idea is revolting. Do good to India\nindeed! Thank your charity and call back your dog \u2014 as the tramp said. Keep\nsuch good workers for America. The Hindus will have a quarantine against all\nsuch [outcasting] to protect their society. I heartily advise Narasimha to\nbecome a Christian \u2014 I beg your pardon, a convert to Americanism \u2014 because I\nam sure such a jewel is unsaleable in poor India. He is welcome to anything\nthat will fetch a price. I know the gentleman whom you name perfectly well,\nand you may give him any information about me you like. I do not care for\nsending scraps[7]* and getting a boom for me. And these friends from India\nbother me enough for newspaper nonsense. They are very devoted, faithful and\nholy friends. I have not much of these scraps now. After a long search I\nfound a bit in a Boston Transcript. I send it over to you.[8]* This public\nlife is such a botheration. I am nearly daft.\n", "Where to fly? In India I have become horribly public \u2014 crowds will follow me\nand take my life out. I got an Indian letter from Landsberg. Every ounce of\nfame can only be bought at the cost of a pound of peace and holiness. I\nnever thought of that before. I have become entirely disgusted with this\nblazoning. I am disgusted with myself. Lord will show me the way to peace\nand purity. Why, Mother, I confess to you: no man can live in an atmosphere\nof public life, even in religion, without the devil of competition now and\nthen  thrusting his head into the serenity of his heart. Those who are\ntrained to preach a doctrine never feel it, for they never knew religion.\nBut those that are after God, and not after the world, feel at once that\nevery bit of name and fame is at the cost of their purity. It is so much\ngone from that ideal of perfect unselfishness, perfect disregard of gain or\nname or fame. Lord help me. Pray for me, Mother. I am very much disgusted\nwith myself. Oh, why the world be so that one cannot do anything without\nputting himself to the front; why cannot one act hidden and unseen and\nunnoticed? The world has not gone one step beyond idolatry yet. They cannot\nact from ideas, they cannot be led by ideas. But they want the person, the\nman. And any man that wants to do something must pay the penalty \u2014 no hope.\nThis nonsense of the world. Shiva, Shiva, Shiva.\n", "By the by, I have got such a beautiful edition of Thomas \u00e0 Kempis. How I\nlove that old monk. He caught a wonderful glimpse of the \"behind the veil\"\n\u2014  few ever got such. My, that is religion. No humbug of the world. No\nshilly-shallying, tall talk, conjecture \u2014 I presume, I believe, I think. How\nI  would  like to go out of this piece of painted humbug they call the\nbeautiful world with Thomas \u00e0 Kempis \u2014 beyond, beyond, which can only be\nfelt, never expressed.\n", "That is religion. Mother, there is God. There all the saints, prophets and\nincarnations meet. Beyond the Babel of Bibles and Vedas, creeds and crafts,\ndupes and doctrines \u2014 where is all light, all love, where the miasma of this\nearth can never reach. Ah! who will take me thither? Do you sympathize with\nme, Mother? My soul is groaning now under the hundred sorts of bondage I am\nplacing on it. Whose India? Who cares? Everything is His. What are we? Is He\ndead? Is He sleeping? He, without whose command a leaf does not fall, a\nheart does not beat, who is nearer to me than my own self. It is bosh and\nnonsense \u2014 to do good or do bad or do fuzz. We do nothing. We are not. The\nworld is not. He is, He is. Only He is. None else is. He is.\n", "Om, the one without a second. He in me, I in Him. I am like a bit of glass\nin an ocean of light. I am not, I am not. He is, He is, He is.\n", "Om, the one without a second.\n", "Yours ever affectionately,\n", "VIVEKANANDA.\n"]
