["\n", "THE CAUSE OF ILLUSION\n", "\nThe question \u2014 what is the cause of M\u00e2y\u00e2 (illusion)? \u2014 has been asked for\nthe last three thousand years; and the only answer is: when the world is\nable to formulate a logical question, we shall answer it. The question is\ncontradictory. Our position is that the Absolute has become this relative\nonly apparently, that the Unconditioned has become the conditioned only in\nMaya. By the very admission of the Unconditioned, we admit that the Absolute\ncannot be acted upon by anything else. It is uncaused, which means that\nnothing  outside  Itself  can  act  upon  It.  First  of all, if It is\nunconditioned, It cannot have been acted upon by anything else. In the\nUnconditioned there cannot be time, space, or causation. That granted your\nquestion will be: \"What caused that which cannot be caused by anything to be\nchanged into this?\" Your question is only possible in the conditioned. But\nyou take it out of the conditioned, and want to ask it in the Unconditioned.\nOnly  when the Unconditioned becomes conditioned, and space, time, and\ncausation come in, can the question be asked. We can only say ignorance\nmakes the illusion. The question is impossible. Nothing can have worked on\nthe Absolute. There was no cause. Not that we do not know, or that we are\nignorant; but It is above knowledge, and cannot be brought down to the plane\nof knowledge. We can use the words, \"I do not know\" in two senses. In one\nway, they mean that we are lower than knowledge, and in the other way, that\nthe thing is above knowledge. The X-rays have become known now. The very\ncauses of these are disputed, but we are sure that we shall know them. Here\nwe can say we do not know about the X-rays. But about the Absolute we cannot\nknow. In the case of the X-rays we do not know, although they are within the\nrange of knowledge; only we do not know them yet. But, in the other case, It\nis so much beyond knowledge that It ceases to be a matter of knowing. \"By\nwhat means can the Knower be known?\" You are always yourself and cannot\nobjectify yourself. This was one of the arguments used by our philosophers\nto prove immortality. If I try to think I am lying dead, what have I to\nimagine? That I am standing and looking down at myself, at some dead body.\nSo that I cannot objectify myself.\n"]
