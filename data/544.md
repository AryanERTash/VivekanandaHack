I shall stay here for about a month and\ngo back to America. Next summer I shall again come to England. At present\nthere is not much prospect in England, but the Lord is omnipotent. Let us\nwait and see. . . .\n", "It is impossible for \u2014 to come now. The thing is, the money belongs to Mr.\nSturdy, and we must have the kind of man he likes. Mr. Sturdy has taken\ninitiation from me, and is a very enterprising and good man.\n", "In the first place we want a man who has a thorough mastery of English and\nSanskrit. It is true that will be able to pick up English soon should he\ncome here but I am as yet unable to bring men here to learn. We want them,\nfirst, who will be able to teach. In the second place, I trust those that\nwill  not  desert me in prosperity and adversity alike. . . . The most\ntrustworthy men are needed. Then, after the foundation is laid, let him who\nwill, come and make a noise, there is no fear. \u2014 gave no proof of wisdom in\nbeing carried away by a hubbub and joining the party of those charlatans.\nSir, granted that Ramakrishna Paramahamsa was a sham, granted that it has\nbeen a very serious mistake, indeed, to take refuge in him, but what is the\nway out now? What if one life is spent in vain, but shall a man eat his own\nwords? Can there be such a thing as having a dozen husbands? Any of you may\njoin any party you like, I have no objection, no, not in the least, but\ntravelling this world over I find that save and except his circle alone,\neverywhere else thought and act are at variance. For those that belong to\nhim, I have the utmost love, the utmost confidence. I have no alternative in\nthe matter. Call me one-sided if you will, but there you have my bona fide\navowal. If but a thorn pricks the foot of one who has surrendered himself to\nShri Ramakrishna, it makes my bones ache. All others I love; you will find\nvery few men so unsectarian as I am; but you must excuse me, I have that bit\nof bigotry. If I do not appeal to his name, whose else shall I? It will be\ntime enough to seek for a big Guru in our next birth; but in this, it is\nthat unlearned Brahmin who has bought this body of mine for ever.\n", "I give you a bit of my mind; don't be angry, pray. I am your slave so long\nas you are his \u2014 step a hair's breadth outside that, and you and I are on a\npar. All the sects and societies that you see, the whole host of them,\ninside the country or out, he has already swallowed them all, my brother.\n", "\" [083_rakhal_01.jpg]\n", "\u2014 These have verily been killed by Myself long ago, be only the instrument,\nO Arjuna.\" Today or tomorrow they will be merged in your own body. O man of\nlittle faith! Through his grace, \" [083_rakhal_02.jpg] \u2014 The whole universe\nbecomes  a  hoof-mark of the cow.\" Be not traitors, that is a sin past\natonement.  Name,  fame,  good deeds, \" [083_rakhal_03.jpg] \u2014 Whatever\nsacrifices you perform, whatever penances you undergo, whatever you eat\" \u2014\nsurrender everything to his feet. What on earth do we want? He has given us\nrefuge, what more do we want? Bhakti is verily its own reward \u2014 what else is\nneeded?  My brother, he who made men of us by feeding and clothing and\nimparting wisdom and knowledge, who opened the eyes of our self, whom day\nand night we found the living God \u2014 must we be traitors to him!!! And you\nforget the mercy of such a Lord! The lives of Buddha and Krishna and Jesus\nare matters of ancient history, and doubts are entertained about their\nhistoricity, and you in spite of seeing the greatness of Shri Ramakrishna's\nlife in flesh and blood sometimes lose your head! Fie upon you! I have\nnothing to say. His likeness is being worshipped in and out of your country,\nby godless and heartless men, and you are stranded at times on disbelief\u00a0!!\nIn a breath he will create for himself hundreds of thousands of such as you\nare. Blessed is your birth, blessed your lineage, and blessed your country\nthat you were allowed to take the dust of his feet. Well I can't help. He is\nprotecting us, forsooth \u2014 I see it before my eyes. Haramohan has written about his troubled circumstances, and says he\nwill be dislodged from his home soon. I could send him at once, but I\nsuspect that the money I last sent was miscarried, therefore I postpone\nsending it. Secondly, I know, besides, of no address to send it to. I see\nthe  Madras people have failed to start the paper. Practical wisdom is\naltogether wanting in the Hindu race, I see. If Master\nMahashaya  be willing, tell him to be my Calcutta agent, for I have an\nimplicit faith in him, and he understands a good deal of these things; it is\nnot for a childish and noisy rabble to do it.
