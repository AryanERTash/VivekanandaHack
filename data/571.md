["\n", "CX\n", "\n", "AIRLIE LODGE, RIDGEWAY GARDENS,\n", "WIMBLEDON,\n", "8th October, 1896.\n", "DEAR (MISS S. E. WALDO),\n", ". . . I had a fine rest in Switzerland and made a great friend of Prof. Paul\nDeussen. My European work in fact is becoming more satisfactory to me than\nany other work, and it tells immensely on India. The London classes were\nresumed, and today is the opening lecture. I now have a hall to myself\nholding two hundred or more. ...\n", "You know of course the steadiness of the English; they are the least jealous\nof each other of all nations, and that is why they dominate the world. They\nhave solved the secret of obedience without slavish cringing \u2014 great freedom\nwith great law-abidingness.\n", "I know very little of the young man R\u2014. He is a Bengali and can teach a\nlittle Sanskrit. You know my settled doctrine. I do not trust any one who\nhas not conquered \"lust and gold\". You may try him in theoretical subjects,\nbut keep him off from teaching Raja-Yoga \u2014 that is a dangerous game except\nfor the regularly trained to play at. Of Saradananda, the blessing of the\ngreatest Yogi of modern India is on him \u2014 and there is no danger. Why do you\nnot begin to teach? . . . You have a thousand times more philosophy than\nthis boy R\u2014. Send notices to the class and hold regular talks and lectures.\n", "I will be thousand times more pleased to see one of you start than any\nnumber of Hindus securing success in America \u2014 even one of my brethren. \"Man\nwants Victory from everywhere, but defeat from his own children\". . . . Make\na blaze! Make a blaze!\n", "With all love and blessings,\n", "\nVIVEKANANDA.\n"]
