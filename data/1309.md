["\n", "CHAPTER IX\n", "WALKS AND TALKS BESIDE THE JHELUM\n", "PERSONS: The Swami Vivekananda and a party of Europeans and disciples,\namongst whom were Dhira Mata, the \"Steady Mother\"; one whose name was Jaya;\nand Nivedita.\n", "PLACE: Kashmir.\n", "TIME: July 20 to July 29, 1898.\n", "JULY 20.\n", ". . . . . .\n", "That morning the river was broad and shallow and clear, and two of us walked\nwith the Swami across the fields and along the banks about three miles. He\nbegan by talking of the sense of sin, how it was Egyptian, Semitic and\nAryan.  It  appears in the Vedas, but quickly passes out. The devil is\nrecognized there as the Lord of Anger. Then, with the Buddhists he became\nMara, the Lord of Lust, and one of the most loved of the Lord Buddha's\ntitles was \"Conqueror of Mara\". (Vide the Sanskrit lexicon Amarkosha that\nSwami learnt to patter as a child of four!) But while Satan is the Hamlet of\nthe Bible, in the Hindu scriptures the Lord of Anger never divides creation.\nHe always represents defilement, never duality.\n", "Zoroaster was a reformer of some old religion. Even Ormuzd and Ahriman with\nhim were not supreme; they were only manifestations of the Supreme. That\nolder religion must have been Vedantic. So the Egyptians and Semites cling\nto the theory of sin while the Aryans, as Indians and Greeks, quickly lose\nit. In India righteousness and sin become Vidy\u00e2 and Avidy\u00e2 \u2014 both to be\ntranscended. Amongst the Aryans, Persians and Europeans become Semitized by\nreligious ideas; hence the sense of sin.[6]*\n", "And then the talk drifted, as it was always so apt to do, to questions of\nthe country and the future. What idea must be urged on a people to give them\nstrength? The line of their own development runs in one way, A. Must the new\naccession  of  force  be  a  compensating one, B? This would produce a\ndevelopment midway between the two, C \u2014 a geometrical alteration merely. But\nit was not so.\n[niveditachap_ix.jpg]\n", "National  life was a question of organic forces. We must reinforce the\ncurrent of that life itself, and leave it to do the rest. Buddha preached\nrenunciation, and India heard. Yet within a thousand years she had reached\nher highest point of national prosperity. The national life in India has\nrenunciation as its source. Its highest ideals are service and Mukti. The\nHindu mother eats last. Marriage is not for individual happiness, but for\nthe welfare of the nation and the caste. Certain individuals of the modern\nreform, having embarked on an experiment which could not solve the problem,\n\"are the sacrifices over which the race has to walk\".\n", "And then the trend of conversation changed again and became all fun and\nmerriment, jokes and stories. And as we laughed and listened, the boats came\nup and talk was over for the day.\n", "The whole of that afternoon and night the Swami lay in his boat, ill. But\nnext day, when we landed at the temple of Bijbehara \u2014 already thronged with\nAmarnath pilgrims \u2014 he was able to join us for a little while. \"Quickly up\nand quickly down\", as he said of himself, was always his characteristic.\nAfter that he was with us most of the day, and in the afternoon we reached\nIslamabad. . . .\n", "In the dusk that evening one came into the little group amongst the apple\ntrees and found the Master engaged in the rarest of rare happenings, a\npersonal talk with Dhira Mata and her whose name was Jaya. He had taken two\npebbles into his hand and was saying how, when he was well, his mind might\ndirect itself to this and that, or his will might seem less firm; but let\nthe least touch of pain or illness come, let him look death in the face for\na while, and \"I am as hard as that (knocking the stones together), for I\nhave touched the feet of God\".\n", "And one remembered, apropos of this coolness, the story of a walk across the\nfields in England, where he and an Englishman and woman had been pursued by\nan angry bull. The Englishman frankly ran and reached the other side of the\nhill in safety. The woman ran as far as she could and then sank to the\nground, incapable of further effort. Seeing this, and unable to aid her, the\nSwami \u2014 thinking \"So this is the end, after all\" \u2014 took up his stand in\nfront of her, with folded arms. He told afterwards how his mind was occupied\nwith a mathematical calculation as to how far the bull would be able to\nthrow. But the animal suddenly stopped a few paces off and then, raising his\nhead, retreated sullenly.\n", "A like courage \u2014 though he himself was far from thinking of these incidents\n\u2014  had shown itself in his early youth when he quietly stepped up to a\nrunaway horse and caught it in the streets of Calcutta, thus saving the life\nof the woman who occupied the carriage behind.\n", "The talk drifted on, as we sat on the grass beneath the trees, and became,\nfor an hour or two, half grave, half gay. We heard much of the tricks the\nmonkeys could play in Vrindaban. And we elicited stories of two separate\noccasions in his wandering life when he had had clear previsions of help\nwhich had been fulfilled. One of these I remember. It may possibly have\noccurred at the time when he was under the vow to ask for nothing, and he\nhad been several days (perhaps five) without food. Suddenly, as he lay\nalmost dying of exhaustion in a railway-station, it flashed into his mind\nthat he must rise up and go out along a certain road and that there he would\nmeet a man bringing him help. He obeyed and met one carrying a tray of food.\n\"Are you he to whom I was sent?\" said this man, coming up to him and looking\nat him closely.\n", "Then a child was brought to us, with its hand badly cut, and the Swami\napplied an old wives' cure. He bathed the wound with water and then laid on\nit, to stop the bleeding, the ashes of a piece of calico. The villagers were\nsoothed and consoled, and our gossip was over for the evening.\n", "JULY 23.\n", "The  next  morning a motley gathering of coolies assembled beneath the\napple-trees and waited some hours to take us to the ruins of Marttanda. It\nhad been a wonderful old building \u2014 evidently more abbey than temple \u2014 in a\nwonderful position; and its great interest lay in the obvious agglomeration\nof styles and periods in which it had grown up. . . . Its presence is a\nperpetual reminder that the East was the original home of monasticism. The\nSwami was hard at work in an instant on observations and theories, pointing\nout the cornice that ran along the nave from the entrance to the sanctuary,\nto the west, surmounted by the high trefoils of the two arches and also by a\nfrieze; or showing us the panels containing cherubs; and before we had done,\nhad picked up a couple of coins. The ride back through the sunset light was\ncharming. From all these hours, the day before and the day after, fragments\nof talk come back to me.\n", "\"No nation, not Greek or another, has ever carried patriotism so far as the\nJapanese. They don't talk, they act \u2014 give up all for country. There are\nnoblemen now living in Japan as peasants, having given up their princedoms\nwithout a word to create the unity of the empire.[7]* And not one traitor\ncould be found in the Japanese war. Think of that!\"\n", "Again,  talking  of the inability of some to express feeling, \"Shy and\nreserved people, I have noticed, are always the most brutal when roused\".\n", "Again,  evidently  talking of the ascetic life and giving the rules of\nBrahmacharya \u2014 \"The Sanny\u00e2sin who thinks of gold, to desire it, commits\nsuicide\", and so on.\n", "JULY 24.\n", "The darkness of night and the forest, a great pine-fire under the trees, two\nor three tents standing out white in the blackness, the forms and voices of\nmany servants at their fires in the distance, and the Master with three\ndisciples, such is the next picture. . . . Suddenly the Master turned to one\nmember of the party and said, \"You never mention your school now. Do you\nsometimes forget it? You see\", he went on, \"I have much to think of. One day\nI turn to Madras and think of the work there. Another day I give all my\nattention to America or England or Ceylon or Calcutta. Now I am thinking\nabout yours\".\n", "At that moment the Master was called away to dine, and not till he came back\ncould the confidence he had invited be given.\n", "He  listened  to it all, the deliberate wish for a tentative plan, for\nsmallness of beginnings, and the final inclination to turn away from the\nidea of inclusiveness and breadth and to base the whole of an educational\neffort on the religious life and on the worship of Shri Ramakrishna.\n", "\"Because you must be sectarian to get that enthusiasm, must you not?\" he\nsaid.  \"You  will  make a sect in order to rise above all sects. Yes I\nunderstand\".\n", "There would be obvious difficulties. The thing sounded on this scale almost\nimpossible for many reasons. But for the moment the only care need be to\nwill rightly; and if the plan was sound, ways and means would be found to\nhand, that was sure.\n", "He waited a little when he had heard it all, and then he said, \"You ask me\nto criticize, but that I cannot do. For I regard you as inspired, quite as\nmuch  inspired  as  I am. You know that's the difference between other\nreligions and us. Other people believe their founder was inspired, and so do\nwe. But so am I also, just as much so as he, and you as I; and after you,\nyour girls and their disciples will be. So I shall help you to do what you\nthink best\".\n", "Then he turned to Dhira Mata and to Jaya and spoke of the greatness of the\ntrust that he would leave in the hands of that disciple who should represent\nthe interests of women when he should go West, of how it would exceed the\nresponsibility of work for men. And he added, turning to the worker of the\nparty, \"Yes, you have faith, but you have not that burning enthusiasm that\nyou need. You want to be consumed [with] energy. Shiva! Shiva!\" And so,\ninvoking the blessing of Mah\u00e2deva, he said goodnight and left us, and we\npresently went to bed.\n", "JULY 25.\n", "The next morning we breakfasted early in one of the tents and went on to\nAchhabal. One of us had had a dream of old jewels lost and restored, all\nbright and new. But the Swami, smiling, stopped the tale, saying, \"Never\ntalk of a dream as good as that!\"\n", "At Achhabal we found more gardens of Jehangir. Was it here or at Verinag\nthat had been his favourite resting-place?\n", "We roamed about the gardens and bathed in a still pool opposite the Pathan\nKhan's Zenana, and then we lunched in the first garden and rode down in the\nafternoon to Islamabad.\n", "As we sat at lunch, the Swami invited his daughter to go to the cave of\nAmarnath with him and be dedicated to Shiva. Dhira Mata smiled permission,\nand the next half-hour was given to pleasure and congratulations. It had\nalready been arranged that we were all to go to Pahalgam and wait there for\nthe  Swami's  return from the pilgrimage. So we reached the boats that\nevening, packed and wrote letters, and next day in the afternoon started for\nBawan.\n"]
