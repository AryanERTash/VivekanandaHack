["\n", "XXXII\n", "(Translated from Bengali)\n", "\n", "HIGH VIEW, CAVERSHAM,\n", "READING,\n", "27th April, 1896.\n", "DEAR (Members of the Alambazar Math),\n", ". . . Let me write something for you all. It is not for gaining personal\nauthority that I do this, but for your good and for fulfilling the purpose\nfor which the Lord came. He gave me the charge of you all, and you shall\ncontribute to the great well-being of the world \u2014 though most of you are not\nyet aware of it \u2014 this is the special reason of my writing to you. It will\nbe a great pity if any feeling of jealousy or egotism gain ground amongst\nyou. Is it possible for those to establish cordial relations on earth who\ncannot cordially live with one another for any length of time? No doubt it\nis an evil to be bound by laws, but it is necessary at the immature stage to\nbe guided by rules; in other words, as the Master used to say that the\nsapling must be hedged round, and so on. Secondly, it is quite natural for\nidle minds to indulge in gossip, and faction-mongering, and so forth. Hence\nI jot down the following hints. If you follow them, you will undoubtedly\nprosper, but if you don't do so, then there is a danger of all our labours\ncoming to naught.\n", "First let me write about the management of the Math:\n", "1. For the purposes of the Math please hire a commodious house or garden,\nwhere everyone may have a small room to himself. There must be a spacious\nhall  where  the books may be kept, and a smaller room for meeting the\nvisitors. If possible, there should be another big hall in the house where\nstudy of the scriptures and religious discourses will be held every day for\nthe public.\n", "2. Anyone wishing to visit anybody in the Math should see him only and\ndepart, without troubling others.\n", "3. By turns someone should be present in the hall for a few hours every day\nfor the public, so that they may get satisfactory replies to what they come\nto ask.\n", "4. Everyone must keep to his room and except on special business must not go\nto others' rooms. Anyone who wishes may go to the Library and read, but it\nshould be strictly forbidden to smoke there or talk with others. The reading\nshould be silent.\n", "5. It shall be wholly forbidden to huddle together in a room and chat the\nwhole day away, with any number of outsiders coming and joining in the\nhubbub.\n", "6. Only those that are seekers after religion may come and peacefully wait\nin the Visitors' Hall and when they have seen the particular persons they\nwant, they should depart. Or, if they have any general question to ask, they\nshould refer to the person in charge of that function for the day and leave.\n", "7. Tale-bearing, caballing, or reporting scandals about others should be\naltogether eschewed.\n", "8. A small room should serve as the office. The Secretary should live in\nthat  room,  which  should contain paper, ink, and other materials for\nletter-writing. He should keep an account of the income and expenditure. All\ncorrespondence  should  come to him, and he should deliver all letters\nunopened to their addressees. Books and pamphlets should be sent to the\nLibrary.\n", "9. There will be a small room for smoking, which should not be indulged in\noutside this room.\n", "10. He who wants to indulge in invectives or show temper must do so outside\nthe boundaries of the Math. This should not be deviated from even by an\ninch.\n", "\n", "THE GOVERNING BODY\n", "1. Every year a President should be elected by a majority of votes. The next\nyear, another, and so on.\n", "2. For this year make Brahmananda the President and likewise make another\nthe Secretary, and elect a third man for superintending the worship etc., as\nwell as the arrangement of food.\n", "3. The Secretary shall have another function, viz to keep watch over the\ngeneral health. Regarding this I have three instructions to give:\n(i)     In every room for each man there shall be a Nair charpoy,\nmattress, etc. Everyone must keep his room clean.\n(ii)    All arrangements must be made to provide clear and pure\nwater for drinking and cooking purposes, for it is a deadly sin to cook\nsacramental food in impure or unclean water.\n(iii)   Give everyone two ochre cloaks of the type that you have\nmade for Saradananda, and see that clothing is kept clean.\n", "4. Anyone wishing to be a Sanny\u00e2sin should be admitted as a Brahmacharin\nfirst. He should live one year at the Math and one year outside, after which\nhe may be initiated into Sanny\u00e2sa.\n", "5. Make over charge of the worship to one of these Brahmacharins, and change\nthem now and then.\n", "\n", "DEPARTMENTS\n", "There shall be the following departments in the Math:\n", "I. Study. II. Propaganda. III. Religious Practice.\n", "I. Study  \u2014 The object of this department is to provide books and teachers\nfor those who want to study. Every morning and evening the teachers should\nbe ready for them.\n", "II. Propaganda \u2014 Within the Math, and abroad. The preachers in the Math\nshould teach the inquirers by reading out scriptures to them and by means of\nquestion-classes. The preachers abroad will preach from village to village\nand try to start Maths like the above in different places.\n", "III. Religious Practice \u2014 This department will try to provide those who want\nto practise with the requisites for this. But it should not be allowed that\nbecause one has taken to religious practice he will prevent others from\nstudy or preaching. Any one infringing this rule shall be immediately asked\nto clear out, and this is imperative.\n", "The preachers at home should give lessons on devotion, knowledge, Yoga, and\nwork by turns; for this, the days and hours should be fixed, and the routine\nhung  up at the door of the class-room. That is to say, a seeker after\ndevotion may not present himself on the day fixed for knowledge and feel\nwounded thereby; and so on.\n", "None of you are fit for the V\u00e2m\u00e2ch\u00e2ra form of practice. Therefore this\nshould on no account be practised at the Math. Anyone demurring to this must\nstep out of this Order. This form of practice must never even be mentioned\nin the Math. Ruin shall seize the wicked man, both here and hereafter, who\nwould introduce vile Vamachara into His fold!\n", "SOME GENERAL REMARKS\n", "1. If any woman comes to have a talk with a Sannyasin, she should do it in\nthe Visitors' Hall. No woman shall be allowed to enter any other room \u2014\nexcept the Worship-room.\n", "2. No Sannyasin shall be allowed to reside in the Women's Math. Anyone\nrefusing to obey this rule shall be expelled from the Math. \"Better an empty\nfold than a wicked herd.\"\n", "3. Men of evil character shall be rigorously kept out. On no pretence shall\ntheir shadow even cross the threshold of my room. If anyone amongst you\nbecome wicked, turn him out at once, whoever he be. We want no black sheep.\nThe Lord will bring lots of good people.\n", "4. Any woman can come to the class-room (or preaching hall) during class\ntime or preaching hour, but must leave the place directly when that period\nis over.\n", "5. Never show temper, or harbour jealousy, or backbite another in secret. It\nwould be the height of cruelty and hard-heartedness to take note of others'\nshortcoming instead of rectifying one's own.\n", "6. There should be fixed hours of meals. Everyone must have a seat and a low\ndining table. He will sit on the former and put his plate on the latter, as\nis the custom in Rajputana.\n", "\n", "THE OFFICE-BEARERS\n", "All the office-bearers you should elect by ballot, as was the mandate of\nLord Buddha. That is to say, one should propose that such and such should be\nthe President this year; and all should write on bits of paper 'yes' or 'no'\nand  put them in a pitcher. If the 'yes' have a majority, he should be\nelected President, and so on. Though you should elect office-bearers in this\nway,  yet  I  suggest  that this year Brahmananda should be President,\nNirmalananda,   Secretary  and  Treasurer,  Sadananda  Librarian,  and\nRamakrishnananda, Abhedananda, Turiyananda, and Trigunatitananda should take\ncharge of the teaching and preaching work by turns, and so on.\n", "It is no doubt a good idea that Trigunatita has of starting a magazine. But\nI shall consent to it if only you can work jointly.\n", "About doctrines and so forth I have to say only this, that if anyone accepts\nParamahamsa Deva as Avat\u00e2ra etc., it is all right; if he doesn't do so, it\nis  just  the  same. The truth about it is that in point of character,\nParamahamsa Deva beats all previous records; and as regards teaching, he was\nmore liberal, more original, and more progressive than all his predecessors.\nIn other words, the older Teachers were rather one-sided, while the teaching\nof this new Incarnation or Teacher is that the best point of Yoga, devotion,\nknowledge, and work must be combined now so as to form a new society. . . .\nThe older ones were no doubt good, but this is the new religion of this age\n\u2014 the synthesis of Yoga, knowledge, devotion, and work \u2014 the propagation of\nknowledge and devotion to all, down to the very lowest, without distinction\nof age or sex. The previous Incarnations were all right, but they have been\nsynthesised in the person of Ramakrishna. For the ordinary man and the\nbeginner, steady devotion (Nishth\u00e2) to an ideal is of paramount importance.\nThat is to say, teach them that all great Personalities should be duly\nhonoured, but homage should be paid now to Ramakrishna. There can be no\nvigour  without steady devotion. Without it one cannot preach with the\nintensity of a Mah\u00e2vira (Hanum\u00e2n). Besides, the previous ones have become\nrather old. Now we have a new India, with its new God, new religion, and new\nVedas. When, O Lord, shall our land be free from this eternal dwelling upon\nthe past? Well, a little bigotry also is a necessity. But we must harbour no\nantagonistic feelings towards others.\n", "If you consider it wise to be guided by my ideas and if you follow these\nrules, then I shall supply on all necessary funds. . . . Moreover, please\nshow  this  letter  to Gour-M\u00e2, Yogin-M\u00e2, and others, and through them\nestablish a Women's Math. Let Gour-Ma be the President there for one year,\nand so on. But none of you shall be allowed to visit the place. They will\nmanage their own affairs. They will not have to work at your dictation. I\nshall supply all necessary expenses for that work also.\n", "May the Lord guide you in the right direction! Two persons went to see the\nLord Jagannatha. One of them beheld the Deity \u2014 while the other saw some\ntrash that was haunting his mind! My friends, many have no doubt served the\nMaster,  but  whenever anyone would be disposed to consider himself an\nextraordinary personage, he should think that although he was associated\nwith Shri Ramakrishna, he has seen only the trash that was uppermost in his\nmind! Were it not so, he would manifest the results. The Master himself used\nto quote, \"They would sing and dance in the name of the Lord but come to\ngrief in the end.\" The root of that degeneration is egotism \u2014 to think that\none is just as great as any other, indeed! \"He used to love me too!\" \u2014 one\nwould plead. Alas, Nick Bottom, would you then be thus translated? Would\nsuch a man envy or quarrel with another and degrade himself? Bear in mind\nthat through His grace lots of men will be turned out with the nobility of\ngods \u2014 ay, wherever His mercy would drop! . . . Obedience is the first duty.\nWell, just do with alacrity what I ask you to. Let me see how you carry out\nthese few small things. Then gradually great things will come to pass.\n", "Yours,\n", "\nVIVEKANANDA.\n", "PS. Please read the contents of this letter to all, and let me know whether\nyou consider the suggestions worth carrying out. Please tell Brahmananda\nthat he who is the servant of all is their true master. He never becomes a\nleader in whose love there is a consideration of high or low. He whose love\nknows no end, and never stops to consider high or low, has the whole world\nlying at his feet.\n", "V.\n"]
