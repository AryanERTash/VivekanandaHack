["\n", "CLIV\n", "To Mrs. Ole Bull\n", "\n", "17th January, 1900.\n", "MY DEAR DHIRA MATA,\n", "I received yours with the enclosures for Saradananda; and there was some\ngood  news.  I hope to get some more news this week. You did not write\nanything about your plans. I had a letter from Miss Greenstidel expressing\nher deep gratitude for your kindness \u2014 and who does not? Turiyananda is\ngetting well by this time, I hope.\n", "I have been able to remit Rs. 2,000 to Saradananda, with the help of Miss\nMacLeod and Mrs. Leggett. Of course they contributed the best part. The rest\nwas got by lectures. I do not expect anything much here or anywhere by\nlecturing. I can scarcely make expenses. No, not even that; whenever it\ncomes to paying, the people are nowhere. The field of lecturing in this\ncountry has been overworked; the people have outgrown that.\n", "I am decidedly better in health. The healer thinks I am now at liberty to go\nanywhere I choose, the process will go on, and I shall completely recover in\na few months. She insists on this, that I am cured already; only nature will\nhave to work out the rest.\n", "Well, I came here principally for health. I have got it; in addition I got\nRs. 2,000, to defray the law expenses. Good.\n", "Now it occurs to me that my mission from the platform is finished, and I\nneed not break my health again by that sort of work.\n", "It is becoming clearer to me that I lay down all the concerns of the Math\nand for a time go back to my mother. She has suffered much through me. I\nmust try to smooth her last days. Do you know, this was just exactly what\nthe great Shankar\u00e2ch\u00e2rya himself had to do! He had to go back to his mother\nin the last few days of her life! I accept it, I am resigned. I am calmer\nthan ever. The only difficulty is the financial part. Well, the Indian\npeople owe something. I will try Madras and a few other friends in India.\nAnyhow, I must try, as I have forebodings that my mother has not very many\nyears to live. Then again, this is coming to me as the greatest of all\nsacrifices to make, the sacrifice of ambition, of leadership, of fame. I am\nresigned and must do the penance. The one thousand dollars with Mr. Leggett\nand if a little more is collected, will be enough to fall back upon in case\nof need. Will you send me back to India? I am ready any time. Don't go to\nFrance without seeing me. I have become practical at least compared to the\nvisionary dreams of Joe and Margot. Let them work their dreams out for me\n\u2014 they are not more than dreams. I want to make out a trust-deed of the Math\nin the names of Saradananda, Brahmananda, and yourself. I will do it as soon\nas I get the papers from Saradananda. Then I am quits. I want rest, a meal,\na few books, and I want to do some scholarly work. Mother shows this light\nvividly now. Of course you were the one to whom She showed it first. I would\nnot believe it then. But then, it is now shown that \u2014 leaving my mother was\na great renunciation in 1884 \u2014 it is a greater renunciation to go back to my\nmother now. Probably Mother wants me to undergo the same that She made the\ngreat \u00c2ch\u00e2rya undergo in old days. Is it? I am surer of your guidance than\nof my own. Joe and Margot are great souls, but to you Mother is now sending\nthe light for my guidance. Do you see light? What do you advise? At least do\nnot go out of this country without sending me home.\n", "I am but a child; what work have I to do? My powers I passed over to you. I\nsee it. I cannot any more tell from the platform. Don't tell it to anyone\n\u2014 not even to Joe. I am glad. I want rest; not that I am tired, but the next\nphase will be the miraculous touch and not the tongue \u2014 like Ramakrishna's.\nThe word has gone to you and the voice to Margo. No more it is in me. I am\nglad. I am resigned. Only get me out to India, won't you? Mother will make\nyou do it. I am sure.\n", "Ever your son,\n", "\nVIVEKANANDA.\n"]
