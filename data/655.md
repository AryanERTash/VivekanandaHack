["\n", "(RECORDED BY MISS S. E. WALDO, A DISCIPLE)\n", "SATURDAY, July 6, 1895. (Today we had Shankaracharya's commentary on Vy\u00e2sa's\nVed\u00e2nta Sutras.)\n", "Om tat sat! According to Shankara, there are two phases of the universe, one\nis I and the other thou; and they are as contrary as light and darkness, so\nit goes without saying that neither can be derived from the other. On the\nsubject, the object has been superimposed; the subject is the only reality,\nthe other a mere appearance. The opposite view is untenable. Matter and the\nexternal world are but the soul in a certain state; in reality there is only\none.\n", "All our world comes from truth and untruth coupled together. Sams\u00e2ra (life)\nis the result of the contradictory forces acting upon us, like the diagonal\nmotion of a ball in a parallelogram of forces. The world is God and is real,\nbut  that  is  not  the  world  we  see;  just as we see silver in the\nmother-of-pearl  where  it is not. This is what is known as Adhy\u00e2sa or\nsuperimposition, that is, a relative existence dependent upon a real one, as\nwhen we recall a scene we have seen; for the time it exists for us, but that\nexistence is not real. Or some say, it is as when we imagine heat in water,\nwhich does not belong to it; so really it is something which has been put\nwhere it does not belong, \"taking the thing for what it is not\". We see\nreality, but distorted by the medium through which we see it.\n", "You can never know yourself except as objectified. When we mistake one thing\nfor another, we always take the thing before us as the real, never the\nunseen; thus we mistake the object for the subject. The Atman never becomes\nthe  object.  Mind  is  the  internal  sense, the outer senses are its\ninstruments. In the subject is a trifle of the objectifying power that\nenables him to know \"I am\"; but the subject is the object of its own Self,\nnever of the mind or the senses. You can, however, superimpose one idea on\nanother idea, as when we say, \"The sky is blue\", the sky itself being only\nan idea. Science and nescience there are, but the Self is never affected by\nany nescience. Relative knowledge is good, because it leads to absolute\nknowledge; but neither the knowledge of the senses, nor of the mind, nor\neven of the Vedas is true, since they are all within the realm of relative\nknowledge. First get rid of the delusion, \"I am the body\", then only can we\nwant  real knowledge. Man's knowledge is only a higher degree of brute\nknowledge.\n", "*    *    *\n", "One part of the Vedas deals with Karma \u2014 form and ceremonies. The other part\ndeals with the knowledge of Brahman and discusses religion. The Vedas in\nthis  part  teach of the Self; and because they do, their knowledge is\napproaching real knowledge. Knowledge of the Absolute depends upon no book,\nnor upon anything; it is absolute in itself. No amount of study will give\nthis knowledge; is not theory, it is realization. Cleanse the dust from the\nmirror, purify your own mind, and in a flash you know that you are Brahman.\n", "God  exists, not birth nor death, not pain nor misery, nor murder, nor\nchange,  nor  good nor evil; all is Brahman. We take the \"rope for the\nserpent\", the error is ours. . . . We can only do good when we love God and\nHe reflects our love. The murderer is God, and the \"clothing of murderer\" is\nonly superimposed upon him. Take him by the hand and tell him the truth.\n", "Soul has no caste, and to think it has is a delusion; so are life and death,\nor any motion or quality. The Atman never changes, never goes nor comes. It\nis the eternal Witness of all Its own manifestations, but we take It for the\nmanifestation; an eternal illusion, without beginning or end, ever going on.\nThe Vedas, however, have to come down to our level, for if they told us the\nhighest truth in the highest way, we could not understand it.\n", "Heaven is a mere superstition arising from desire, and desire is ever a\nyoke, a degeneration. Never approach any thing except as God; for if we do,\nwe see evil, because we throw a veil of delusion over what we look at, and\nthen we see evil. Get free from these illusions; be blessed. Freedom is to\nlose all illusions.\n", "In one sense Brahman is known to every human being; he knows, \"I am\"; but\nman does not know himself as he is. We all know we are, but not how we are.\nAll lower explanations are partial truths; but the flower, the essence of\nthe Vedas, is that the Self in each of us is Brahman. Every phenomenon is\nincluded  in  birth,  growth,  and death \u2014 appearance, continuance and\ndisappearance. Our own realisation is beyond the Vedas, because even they\ndepend upon that. The highest Vedanta is the philosophy of the Beyond.\n", "To say that creation has any beginning is to lay the axe at the root of all\nphilosophy.\n", "Maya is the energy of the universe, potential and kinetic. Until Mother\nreleases us, we cannot get free.\n", "The universe is ours to enjoy. But want nothing. To want is weakness. Want\nmakes us beggars, and we are sons of the king, not beggars.\n"]
