["\n", "CXXIII\n", "To Miss Mary Hale\n", "MATH, BELUR,\n", "HOWRAH DISTRICT,\n", "BENGAL, INDIA,\n", "2nd March, 1898.\n", "MY DEAR MARY,\n", "You have news of me already, I hope, through the letter I wrote to Mother\nChurch. You are all so kind, the whole family, to me, I must have belonged\nto  you  in  the  past,  as  we Hindus say. My only regret is that the\nmillionaires do not materialise: and I want them so badly just now that I am\ngrowing decrepit and old and hot in the midst of building and organising.\nThough Harriet has got one of a million virtues, a few millions of cash\nvirtue would have made it more shining, I am sure; so you do not commit the\nsame mistake.\n", "A certain young couple had everything favourable to make them man and wife\nexcept that the bride's father was determined not to give his daughter to\nanyone who had not a million. The young people were in despair when a clever\nmatchmaker  came to the rescue. He asked the bridegroom whether he was\nwilling to part with his nose on payment of a million \u2014 which he refused.\nThe matchmaker then swore before the bride's father that the bridegroom had\nin store goods worth several millions, and the match was completed. Don't\nyou take like millions.\n", "Well, well, you could not get the millionaire, so I could not get the money;\nso I had to worry a good deal and work hard to no purpose; so I got the\ndisease. It requires brains like mine to find out the true cause \u2014 I am\ncharmed with myself!\n", "Well, it was in Southern India, when I came from London and when the people\nwere feting and feasting and pumping all the work out of me, that an old\nhereditary disease made its appearance. The tendency was always there, and\nexcess of mental work made it \"express\" itself. Total collapse and extreme\nprostration followed, and I had to leave Madras immediately for the cooler\nNorth; a day's delay meant waiting for a week in that awful heat for another\nsteamer. By the by, I learnt afterwards that Mr. Barrows arrived in Madras\nnext day and was very much chagrined at not finding me as he expected,\nthough  I  helped  getting  up an address for him and arranged for his\nreception. Poor man, he little knew I was at death's door then.\n", "I have been travelling in the Himalayas all through last summer; and a cold\nclimate, I found immediately, brought me round; but as soon as I come into\nthe heat of the plains I am down again. From today the heat in Calcutta is\nbecoming intense, and I will soon have to fly. This time to cool America as\nMrs. Bull and Miss MacLeod are here. I have bought a piece of land for the\ninstitution on the river Ganga near Calcutta, on which is a little house\nwhere they are living now; within a stone's throw is the house where the\nMath is situated at present in which we live.\n", "So I see them every day and they are enjoying it immensely \u00e0 L\u2019Inde. They\nintend making a trip to Kashmir in a month, and I am going with them as a\nguide and friend and philosopher perhaps, if they are willing. After that we\nall sail for the land of freedom and scandal.\n", "You need not be alarmed with me as the disease will take two or three years\nat worst to carry me off. At best it may remain a harmless companion. I am\ncontent. Only I am working hard to set things all right and always so that\nthe machine moves forward when I am off the stage. Death I have conquered\nlong ago when I gave up life. My only anxiety is the work, and even that to\nthe Lord I dedicate, and He knows best.\n", "Ever yours in the Lord,\n", "\nVIVEKANANDA.\n"]
