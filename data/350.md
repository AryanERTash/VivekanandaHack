A copy\nof a Madras journal that I received through the kindness of Joe delighted me\nexceedingly, as the reception Nivedit\u00e2 had in Madras was for the good of\nboth Nivedita and Madras. Her speech was indeed beautiful.\n", "\nI hope you are resting well after your long journey, and so is Nivedita. I\nwish it so much that you should go for a few hours to a few villages west of\nCalcutta to see the old Bengali structures made of wood, bamboo, cane, mica,\nand grass.\n", "\nThese  are  the bungalows, most artistic. Alas! the name is travestied\nnowadays by every pigsty appropriating the name.\n", "\nIn  old  days  a man who built a palace still built a bungalow for the\nreception of guests. The art is dying out. I wish I could build the whole of\nNivedita's School in that style. Yet it is good to see the few that yet\nremained, at least one.\n", "\nBrahmananda will arrange for it, and you have only to take a journey of a\nfew hours.\n", "\nMr.  Okakura  has started on his short tour. He intends to visit Agra,\nGwalior, Ajanta, Ellora, Chittore, Udaipur, Jaipur, and Delhi.\n", "\nA very well-educated rich young man of Varanasi, with whose father we had a\nlong-standing friendship, came back to this city yesterday. He is especially\ninterested in art, and spending purposely a lot of money in his attempts to\nrevive dying Indian arts. He came to see me only a few hours after Mr.\nOkakura left. He is just the man to show him artistic India (i.e. what\nlittle  is left), and I am sure he will be much benefited by Okakura's\nsuggestions. Okakura just found a common terracotta water-vessel here used\nby the servants. The shape and the embossed work on it simply charmed him,\nbut as it is common earthenware and would not bear the journey, he left a\nrequest with me to have it reproduced in brass. I was at my wit's end as to\nwhat to do. My young friend comes a few hours after, and not only undertakes\nto have it done but offers to show a few hundreds of embossed designs in\nterracotta infinitely superior to the one Okakura fancied.\n", "\nHe also offers to show us old paintings in that wonderful old style. Only\none family is left in Varanasi who can paint after the old style yet. One of\nthem has painted a whole hunting scene on a pea, perfect in detail and\naction!\n", "\nI hope Okakura will come to this city on his return and be this gentleman's\nguest and see a bit of what is left.\n", "\nNiranjan has gone with Mr. Okakura, and as he is a Japanese, they don't\nobject to his going into any temple. It seems that the Tibetans and the\nother Northern Buddhists have been coming here to worship Shiva all along.\n", "\nThey allowed him to touch the sign of Shiva and worship. Mrs. Annie Besant\ntried once, but, poor woman, although she bared her feet, put on a Sari, and\nhumiliated herself to the dust before the priests, she was not admitted even\ninto the compound of the temple. The Buddhists are not considered non-Hindus\nin any of our great temples. My plans are not settled; I may shift from this\nplace very soon.\n", "\nShivananda and the boys send you all their welcome, regards, and love.\n", "\nI am, as ever, your most affectionate son\n", "Vivekananda.\n", "\n"]
