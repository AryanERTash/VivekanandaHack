["\n", "LXXVI\n", "(Translated from Bengali)\n", "\n", "C/O E. T. STURDY, ESQ.,\n", "READING, CAVERSHAM, ENGLAND,\n", "1895.\n", "DEAR RAKHAL,\n", "Glad to receive your letters. There are two defects in the letters which you\nall write, specially in yours. The first is that very few of the important\npoints I ask are answered. Secondly, there is unusual delay in replying. . .\n. I have to work day and night, and am always whirling from place to place\nbesides....  These  are countries where the people are most luxurious,\nfashionable folk, and nobody would touch a man who has but a speck of dirt\non his body. ... I hoped that somebody would come while I was still here,\nbut as yet nothing has been settled I see. ... Business is business, that\nis, you must do everything promptly; delay and shuffling won't do. By the\nend of next week I shall go to America, so there is no chance of my meeting\nhim who is coming. . . . These are countries of gigantic scholars. Is it a\njoke to make disciples of such people? You are but children and talk like\nchildren. Only this much is needed that there should be someone to teach a\nlittle Sanskrit, or translate a bit in my absence, that's all. Why not let\nGirish Babu visit these lands? It is a good idea. It will cost him but 3000\nrupees to visit England and America, and go back. The more people come to\nthese countries, the better. But then it sets my nerves on edge to look at\nthose who don hats and pose as Sahibs!\n", "Black as chimney sweeps, and calling themselves Europeans! Why not wear\none's country-dress, as befits gentlemen? \u2014 Instead of that, to add to that\nfrightfulness of appearance! Good heavens! . . . Here, as in our country one\nhas to spend from one's own pocket to give lectures, but one can make good\nthe expenses if one lives long enough and makes a reputation. Another thing,\nmy  incessant lecturing tours are making my constitution very nervous,\ncausing insomnia and other troubles. Over and above that, I have to work\nsingle-handed. It is no use depending on my countrymen. No one (in Bengal)\nhas hitherto helped me with a penny, nor has a single soul stepped forward\nto my assistance. Everybody in this world seeks help, and the more you help\nhim, the more he wants. And if you can do no further, he will call you a\ncheat.... I love \u2014 and trust him.... He will be free from disease through\nthe Lord's grace. I take all his responsibility. . . .\n", "Yours affectionately,\n", "\nVIVEKANANDA.\n"]
