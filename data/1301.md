["\n", "CHAPTER I\n", "THE HOUSE ON THE GANGES\n", "PLACE: A cottage at Belur, beside the Ganges.\n", "TIME: March to May, 1898.\n", "Of the home by the Ganges the Master had said to one, \"You will find that\nlittle house of Dhira Mata like heaven, for it is all love, from beginning\nto end.\n", "It was so indeed. Within, an unbroken harmony, and without, everything alike\nbeautiful \u2014 the green stretch of grass, the tall cocoanut palms, the little\nbrown villages in the jungle, and the Nilkantha that built her nest in a\ntree \u2014 top beside us, on purpose to bring us the blessings of Shiva. In the\nmorning the shadows lay behind the house, but in the afternoons we could sit\nin front worshipping the Ganges herself \u2014 great leonine mother! \u2014 and in\nsight of Dakshineswar.\n", "There came one and another with traditions of the past, and we learnt of the\nMaster's  eight years' wanderings; of the name changed from village to\nvillage; of the Nirvikalpa Sam\u00e2dhi; and of that sacred sorrow, too deep for\nwords or for common sight, that one who loved had alone seen. And there too\ncame the Master himself, with his stories of Um\u00e2 and Shiva, of R\u00e2dh\u00e2 and\nKrishna, and his fragments of song and poetry.\n", "It seemed as if he knew that the first material of a new consciousness must\nbe a succession of vivid but isolated experiences, poured out without proper\nsequence  so as to provoke the mind of the learner to work for its own\nconception of order and relation. . . . For the most part it was the Indian\nreligions that he portrayed for us\u2014today dealing with one and tomorrow with\nanother \u2014 his choice guided, seemingly, by the whim of the moment. But it\nwas not religion only that he poured out upon us. Sometimes it would be\nhistory. Again, it would be folk-lore. On still another occasion it would be\nthe manifold anomalies and inconsistencies of race, caste and custom. In\nfact India herself became, as heard in him, as the last and noblest of the\nPur\u00e2nas, uttering itself through his lips.\n", "Another point in which he had caught a great psychological secret was that\nof  never  trying  to soften for us that which would at first sight be\ndifficult or repellent. In matters Indian he would rather put forward, in\nits extreme form at the beginning of our experience, all that might seem\nimpossible for European minds to enjoy. Thus he would quote, for instance,\nsome verses about Gauri and Shankar in a single form:\n", "On one side grows the hair in long black curls,\nAnd on the other, corded like rope.\nOn one side are seen the beautiful garlands,\nOn the other, bone earrings and snake-like coils.\nOne side is white with ashes, like the snow mountains,\nThe other, golden as the light of dawn.\nFor He, the Lord, took a form,\nAnd that was a divided form,\nHalf-woman and half-man\n", ". . . . . . .\n", "Whatever might be the subject of the conversation, it ended always on the\nnote  of  the infinite. . . . He might appear to take up any subject \u2014\nliterary, ethnological or scientific \u2014 but he always made us feel it as an\nillustration of the Ultimate Vision. There was for him nothing secular. He\nhad a loathing for bondage and a horror of those who \"cover chains with\nflowers\", but he never failed to make the true critic's distinction between\nthis and the highest forms of art.\n", "One day we were receiving European guests and he entered into a long talk\nabout Persian poetry. Then suddenly, finding himself quoting the poem that\nsays, \"For one mole on the face of my Beloved, I would give all the wealth\nof Samarkand!\" he turned and said energetically, \"I would not give a straw,\nyou know, for the man who was incapable of appreciating a love song!\" His\ntalk too teemed with epigrams.\n", "It was that same afternoon, in the course of a long political argument, that\nhe said, \"In order to become a nation, it appears that we need a common hate\nas well as a common love\".\n", "Several months later he remarked that before one who had a mission he never\ntalked of any of the gods save Uma and Shiva. For Shiva and the Mother made\nthe great workers. Yet I have sometimes wondered if he knew at this time how\nthe  end  of  every theme was Bhakti. Much as he dreaded the luxury of\nspiritual emotion for those who might be enervated by it, he could not help\ngiving glimpses of what it meant to be consumed with the intoxication of\nGod. And so he would chant for us such poems as:\n", "They have made Radha queen, in the beautiful\n", "groves of Vrindaban.\n", "At her gate stands Krishna, on guard.\nHis flute is singing all the time:\nRadha is about to distribute infinite wealth of love.\nThough I am guard, all the world may enter.\nCome all ye who thirst! Say only 'Glory unto Radha!'\nEnter the region of love!\n", "Or he would give us the great antiphonal Chorus of the Cowherds, written by\nhis friend: (The Bengali dramatist Girish Chandra Ghosh.)\n", "Men:                Thou art the Soul of souls,\n", "Thou yellow-garbed,\nWith thy blue eyes.\n", "Women:           Thou dark One! Thou\n", "Shepherd of Vrindaban!\nKneeling at the feet of the Shepherdesses.\n", "Men:                My soul sings the praise of the glory\n", "of the Lord,\n", "Who took the human form.\n", "Women:           Thy beauty for us, the Gopis.\nMen:                Thou Lord of Sacrifice.\n", "Saviour of the weak.\n", "Women:           Who lovest Radha and thy body floats on its\n", "own tears.\n", ". . . . . .\n", "MARCH 25.\n", ". . . At this time the Swami kept the custom of coming to the cottage early\nand  spending the morning hours there, and again returning in the late\nafternoon.  On the second morning of this visit, however \u2014 Friday, the\nChristian feast of the Annunciation \u2014 he took us all three back to the Math,\nand there in the worship-room was held a little service of initiation where\none was made a Brahmach\u00e2rini. That was the happiest of mornings.\n", "After the service we were taken upstairs. The Swami put on the ashes and\nbone-earrings and matted locks of a Shiva-Yogi and sang and played to us \u2014\nIndian music on Indian instruments \u2014 for an hour.\n", "And in the evening in our boat on the Ganges, he opened his heart to us and\ntold us much of his questions and anxieties regarding the trust that he held\nfrom his own Master.\n", "Another week and he was gone to Darjeeling; and till the day that the plague\ndeclaration brought him back, we saw him again no more.\n", "MAY 3.\n", "Then two of us met him in the house of our Holy Mother. The political sky\nwas black. It seemed as if a storm were about to burst. . . . Plague, panic\nand riot were doing their fell work. And the Master turned to the two and\nsaid, \"There are some who scoff at the existence of K\u00e2li. Yet today She is\nout there amongst the people. They are frantic with fear, and the soldiery\nhave been called to deal out death. Who can say that God does not manifest\nHimself as evil as well as good? But only the Hindu dares to worship Him in\nthe evil\".\n", "He had come back and the old life was resumed once more, as far as could be,\nseeing that an epidemic was in prospect and that measures were on hand to\ngive  the  people confidence. As long as this possibility darkened the\nhorizon, he would not leave Calcutta. But it passed away, and those happy\ndays with it, and the time came that we should go.\n"]
