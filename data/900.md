["\n", "LIII\n", "(Translated from Bengali)\n", "To Swami Abhedananda\n", "C/O E. T. STURDY, ESQ.,\n", "HIGH VIEW, CAVERSHAM,\n", "READING, ENGLAND,\n", "October, 1895.\n", "DEAR KALI,\n", "You may have got my earlier letter. At present send all letters to me at the\nabove address. Mr. Sturdy is known to T\u00e2rakd\u00e2. He has brought me to his\nplace, and we are both trying to create a stir in England. I shall this year\nleave again in November for America. So I require a man well-up in Sanskrit\nand English, particularly the latter language \u2014 either Shashi or you or\nS\u00e2rad\u00e2.  Now,  if  you have completely recovered, very well, you come;\notherwise send Sharat. The work is to teach the devotees I shall be leaving\nhere, to make them study the Vedanta, to do a little translation work into\nEnglish, and to deliver occasional lectures. \"Work is apt to cloud spiritual\nvision.\" X__ is very eager to come, but unless the foundation is strongly\nlaid, there is every likelihood of everything toppling down. I am sending\nyou a cheque along with this letter. Buy clothes and other necessary things\n\u2014 whoever comes. I am sending the cheque in the name of Master Mahashay\nMahendra Babu. Gang\u00e2dhar's Tibetan choga is in the Math; get the tailor to\nmake a similar choga of gerua colour. See that the collar is a little high,\nthat is, the throat and neck should be covered. . . . Above all, you must\nhave  a  woolen overcoat, for it is very cold. If you do not put on an\novercoat on the ship, you will suffer much. . . . I am sending a second\nclass ticket, as there is not much difference between a first class and a\nsecond class berth. . . . If it is decided to send Shashi then inform the\npurser of the ship beforehand to provide him with vegetarian diet.\n", "Go to Bombay and see Messrs. King, King & Co., Fort, Bombay, and tell them\nthat you are Mr. Sturdy's man. They will then give you a ticket to England.\nA letter is being sent from here to the Company with instructions. I am\nwriting to the Maharaja of Khetri to instruct his Bombay agent to look after\nthe booking of your passage. If this sum of Rs. 150/- is not sufficient for\nyour outfit, get the remainder from Rakhal. I shall send him the amount\nafterwards. Keep another Rs. 50/- for pocket expenses \u2014 take it from Rakhal;\nI shall pay back later. I have not up to now got any acknowledgement of the\namount I sent to Chuni Babu. Start as quickly as possible. Inform Mahendra\nBabu that he is my Calcutta agent. Tell him to send a letter to Mr. Sturdy\nby next mail informing him that he is ready to look after all business\ntransactions  in  Calcutta on your behalf. In effect, Mr. Sturdy is my\nsecretary in England, Mahendra Babu in Calcutta, and Alasinga in Madras.\nSend this information to Madras also. Can any work be done unless all of us\ngird up our loins? And be up and doing! \"Fortune favours the brave and\nenergetic.\" Don't look back \u2014 forward, infinite energy, infinite enthusiasm,\ninfinite daring, and infinite patience \u2014 then alone can great deeds be\naccomplished. We must set the whole world afire.\n", "Now on the day the steamer is due to start, write a letter to Mr. Sturdy\ninforming him by which steamer you are leaving for England. Otherwise there\nis some likelihood of your having difficulties when you reach London. Take\nthe ship that comes directly to London, for even if it takes a few days\nlonger on the voyage, the fares are less. At the moment our purse is lean.\nIn time we shall send preachers in large numbers to all the quarters of the\nglobe.\n", "Yours affectionately,\n", "VIVEKANANDA.\n", "PS. Write at once to the Maharaja of Khetri, that you are going to Bombay\nand  that you will be glad if his agent attends to the booking of your\npassage and sees you off the board.\n", "Keep my address with you written in a pocket-book, lest there should be\ndifficulties afterwards.\n"]
