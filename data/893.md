["\n", "XLVI\n", "To Miss Mary Hale\n", "54 W. 33RD STREET,\n", "NEW YORK,\n", "22nd June, 1895.\n", "DEAR SISTER,\n", "The letters from India and the parcel of books reached me safe. I am so\nhappy  to  know of Mr. Sam's arrival. I am sure he is \"bewaring of the\nvidders\" nicely. I met a friend of Mr. Sam's one day on the street. He is an\nEnglishman with a name ending in \"ni\". He was very nice. He said he was\nliving in the same house with Sam somewhere in Ohio.\n", "I am going on pretty nearly in the same old fashion. Talking when I can and\nsilent when forced to be. I do not know whether I will go to Greenacre this\nsummer. I saw Miss Farmer the other day. She was in a hurry to go away, so I\nhad but very little talk with her. She is a noble, noble lady.\n", "How are you going on with your Christian Science lessons? I hope you will go\nto  Greenacre. There you will find quite a number of them and also the\nSpiritualists, table turnings, palmists, astrologers, etc., etc. You will\nget all the \"cures\" and all the \"isms\" presided over by Miss Farmer.\n", "Landsberg has gone away to live in some other place, so I am left alone. I\nam living mostly on nuts and fruits and milk, and find it very nice and\nhealthy too. I hope to lose about 30 to 40 lbs. this summer. That will be\nall right for my size. I am afraid I have forgotten all about Mrs. Adam's\nlessons in walking. I will have to renew them when she comes again to N.Y.\nGandhi has gone to England en route to India from Boston, I suppose.\n", "I  would like to know about his \"chaperon\" Mrs. Howard and her present\nbereaved state. I am very glad to hear that the rugs did not go down to the\nbottom of the Atlantic and are at last coming.\n", "This year I could hardly keep my head up, and I did not go about lecturing.\nThe three great commentaries on the Vedanta philosophy belonging to the\nthree great sects of dualists, qualified dualists, and monists are being\nsent to me from India. Hope they will arrive safe. Then I will have an\nintellectual feast indeed. I intend to write a book this summer on the\nVedanta philosophy. This world will always be a mixture of good and evil, of\nhappiness and misery; this wheel will ever go up and come down; dissolution\nand resolution is the inevitable law. Blessed are those who struggle to go\nbeyond. Well, I am glad all the babies are doing well but sorry there was no\n\"catch\" even this winter, and every winter the chances are dwindling down.\nHere near my lodgings is the Waldorf-Hotel, the rendezvous of lots of titled\nbut penniless Europeans on show for \"Yankee\" heiresses to buy. You may have\nany selection here, the stock is so full and varied. There is the man who\ntalks no English; there are others who lisp a few words which no one can\nunderstand; and others are there who talk nice English, but their chance is\nnot so great as that of the dumb ones \u2014 the girls do not think them enough\nforeign who talk plain English fluently.\n", "I read somewhere in a funny book that an American vessel was being foundered\nin  the  sea;  the men were desperate and as a last solace wanted some\nreligious service being done. There was \"Uncle Josh\" on board who was an\nelder in the Presbyterian Church. They all began to entreat, \"Do something\nreligious, Uncle Josh! We are all going to die.\" Uncle Joseph took his hat\nin his hand and took up a collection on the spot!\n", "That is all of religion he knew. And that is more or less characteristic of\nthe majority of such people. Collections are about all the religion they\nknow or will ever know. Lord bless them. Good-bye for present. I am going to\neat something; I feel very hungry.\n", "Yours affectionately,\n", "\nVIVEKANANDA.\n"]
