["\n", "LXXVII\n", "(Translated from Bengali)\n", "\n", "C/O E. T. STURDY, ESQ.,\n", "HIGH VIEW, CAVERSHAM, ENGLAND,\n", "1895.\n", "DEAR SHASHI,\n", ". . . I am in receipt of Rakhal's letter today. I am sorry to hear that \u2014\nhas suffered from gravel. Most probably it was due to indigestion. Gopal's\ndebts  have  been cleared; now ask him to join the monastic order. The\nworldly-wise instinct is most difficult to root out. . . . Let him come and\nwork  in  the Math. One is apt to imbibe a lot of mischievous ideas by\nconcerning oneself too long in worldly affairs. If he refuses to take the\nmonastic vow, please tell him to clear out. I don't want amphibious type of\nmen who will be half monks and half householders. . . . Haramohan has coined\na Lord Ramakrishna Paramahamsa, I see. What does he mean? English Lord, or\nDuke? Tell Rakhal, let people say whatever they will \u2014 \"Men (who wrongly\ncriticise) are to be treated as worms!\" as Shri Ramakrishna used to say. Let\nthere be no disparity between what you profess and what you do, also eschew\nthe very name of Jesuitism. Was I ever an orthodox, Paur\u00e2nika Hindu, an\nadherent of social usages? I do not pose as one. You will not have to say\nthings that will be pleasant to any section of people. You must not so much\nas notice what the Bengalis say for or against us. . . . They could not do a\npenny-worth of service to him whose birth has sanctified their country where\nthe primary laws of health and sanitation are trampled, and yet they would\ntalk big! What matters is, my brother, what such men have got to say! . . .\nIt  is  for  you  to go on doing your own work. Why look up to men for\napprobation, look up to God! I hope Sharat will be able to teach them the\nGita and the Upanishads and their commentaries somehow, with the help of the\ndictionary. \u2014 Or, is it an empty Vair\u00e2gya that you have? The days of such\nVairagya are gone! It is not for everyone, my boy, to become Ramakrishna\nParamahamsa! I hope Sharat has started by this time. Please send a copy of\nthe Panchadashi, a copy of the Gita (with as many commentaries as possible),\na copy each of the N\u00e2rada and Sh\u00e2ndilya Sutras (published in Varanasi), a\ntranslation (good, not worthless) of the Panchadashi \u2014 if it is available\n\u2014 and the translation by K\u00e2livara Ved\u00e2ntav\u00e2gisha of Shankara's Commentary.\nAnd if there be any translation, Bengali or English (by Shrish Babu of\nAllahabad), of P\u00e2nini's Sutras, or the K\u00e2shik\u00e2-Vritti, or the Phani-Bh\u00e2shya,\nplease send a copy of each. ... Now, just tell your Bengalis to send me a\ncopy of the V\u00e2chaspatya Dictionary, and that will be a good test for those\ntall-talking  people.  In  England, religious movements make very slow\nprogress. These people here are either bigots or atheists. And the former\nagain have only a bit of formal religion. They say, \"Patriotism is our\nreligion.\" That is all.\n", "Send the books to America, c/o Miss Mary Phillips, 19 West 38th Street, New\nYork, U.S.A. That is my American address. By the end of November I shall go\nto America. So send my books etc., there. If Sharat has started immediately\non your receipt of my letter, then only I may meet him, otherwise not.\nBusiness  is business, no child's play. Mr. Sturdy will see to him and\naccommodate him. This time I have come to England just to probe a little.\nNext summer I shall try to make some stir. The winter after that, I shall go\nto India. . . . Correspond regularly with those who are interested in us, so\nas  to  keep up their interest. Try to open centres in places all over\nBengal.... This much for the present. In my next I shall give you more\ndetails. Mr. Sturdy is a very nice gentleman, a staunch Vedantist, and\nunderstands a smattering of Sanskrit. It is with a good deal of labour that\nyou can do a little bit of work in these countries; a sheer uphill task,\nwith  cold  and rain into the bargain. Moreover, here you must support\nyourself and do your labour of love. Englishmen won't spend a penny on\nlectures or things of that sort. If they do come to listen to you, well,\nthank your stars \u2014 as is the case in our country. Besides, the common people\nhere do not even know of me now. In addition to all this, they will give you\na wide berth if you preach God and such things to them. They think this must\nbe another clergyman! Well, you just patiently do one thing \u2014 set about\ncollecting everything that books, beginning with the Rig-Veda down to the\nmost insignificant of Puranas and Tantras, have got to say about creation\nand annihilation of the universe, about race, heaven, and hell, the soul,\nconsciousness, and intellect, etc., the sense-organs, Mukti, transmigration,\nand suchlike things. No child's play would do, I want real scholarly work.\nThe most important thing is to collect the materials. My love to you all.\n", "Yours affectionately,\n", "\nVIVEKANANDA.\n"]
