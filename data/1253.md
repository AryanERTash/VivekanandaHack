BELUR, HOWRAH,\n", "25th November 1901.\n", "DEAR CHRISTINE,\n", "It  seems  your  bottle  of nerve tonic did not do you much good, your\nassurances to the contrary. It must have been a curious error. I must have\nbeen down with fever or asthma or something else at that time. Still a\nthousand, thousand pardons. This was my first, and it will be my last,\noffence. Your letter that went to Miss [Josephine] MacLeod has not come back\nyet. Perhaps Miss MacLeod is bringing the letter with her, as she is coming\nover to India from Japan herself, accompanied by her Japanese converts\n(male, of course, as she is a lady missionary).\n", "Well, well, I so wish things would so arrange themselves that I could see\nyou once more. Mother knows. By the by, my right eye is failing me badly. I\nsee very little with that one. It will be hard for me for some time either\nto read or write; and as it is getting worse every day, my people are urging\nme to go to Calcutta and consult a doctor. I will go soon, as soon as I\nrecover from a bad cold I have on.\n", "I am so glad you were so taken by Abhedananda; only I thought one Hindu was\ngood for a lifetime.\n", "Poor Miss Joe [Miss Josephine MacLeod] \u2014 so she remains ignorant as to the\nreal cause of my not going over to Japan! You need not be the least anxious\n\u2014 there is no harm done; and if there were, Joe and especially Mrs. [Ole]\nBull make it their life's duty to befriend those I love.\n", "I will try your tonic when it arrives; and the gift, I pray, will even be\nfollowed  by  the  giver,  for  surely a [words excised] . . . is more\nstimulating and healing than dead drugs.\n", "With all love,\n", "VIVEKANANDA.\n"]
