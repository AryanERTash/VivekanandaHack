["\n", "I\n", "(Translated from Bengali)\n", "Glory to Ramakrishna!\n", "\n", "BAIDYANATH,\n", "25th December, 1889.\n", "DEAR SIR (Shri Balaram Bose),\n", "\nI have been staying for the last few days at Baidyanath in Purna Babu's\nLodge. It is not so cold, and my health too is indifferent. I am suffering\nfrom indigestion, probably due to excess of iron in the water. I have found\nnothing agreeable here \u2014 neither the place, nor the season, nor the company.\nI leave for Varanasi tomorrow. Achyutananda stopped at Govinda Chaudhury's\nplace at Deoghar, and the latter, as soon as he got news of us, earnestly\ninsisted on our becoming his guests. Finally, he met us once again and\nprevailed on us to accede to his request. The man is a great worker, but has\na  number  of women with him \u2014 old women most of them, of the ordinary\nVaishnava type. . . . His clerks too revere us much; some of them are very\nmuch ill-disposed towards him, and they spoke of his misdeeds. Incidentally,\nI raised the topic of __. You have many wrong ideas or doubts about her;\nhence I write all this after particular investigation. Even the aged clerks\nof this establishment highly respect and revere her. She came to stop with\n__ while she was a mere child, and ever lived as his wife. . . . Everyone\nadmits in one voice that her character is spotless. She was all along a\nperfectly chaste woman and never behaved with __ in any relation but that of\nwife to husband, and she was absolutely faithful. She came at too early an\nage to have incurred any moral taint. After she had separated from __, she\nwrote to him to say that she had never treated him as anything but her\nhusband, but that it was impossible for her to live with a man with a loose\ncharacter.  His  old  office-bearers  too believe him to be satanic in\ncharacter; but they consider __ a Devi (angel), and remark that it was\nfollowing her departure that __ lost all sense of shame.\n", "My object in writing all this is that formerly I was not a believer in the\ntale of the lady's early life. The idea that there might be such purity in\nthe midst of a relation which society does not recognise, I used to consider\nas romance. But after thorough investigation I have come to know that it is\nall right. She is very pure, pure from her infancy \u2014 I have not the least\ndoubt about it. For entertaining those doubts, you and I and everyone are\nguilty to her; I make repeated salutations to her, and ask her pardon for my\nguilt. She is not a liar.\n", "I take this opportunity to record that such courage is impossible in a lying\nand unchaste woman. I have also been told that she had a lifelong ardent\nfaith in religion also.\n", "Well, your disease is not yet improving! I don't think this is a place for\npatients unless one is ready to spend a good deal of money. Please think out\nsome judicious course. Here every article will have to be procured from\nelsewhere.\n", "Yours sincerely,\n", "VIVEKANANDA.\n"]
