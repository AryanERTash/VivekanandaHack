["\n", "REPLY TO THE ADDRESS OF WELCOME AT MADRAS\n", "\nWhen the Swami Vivekananda arrived at Madras an address of welcome was\npresented to him by the Madras Reception Committee. It read as follows:\n", "\n Revered Swamin, \n", "\nOn behalf of your Hindu co-religionists in Madras, we offer you a most\nhearty welcome on the occasion of your return from your Religious Mission in\nthe  West.  Our object in approaching you with this address is not the\nperformance of any merely formal or ceremonial function; we come to offer\nyou  the  love  of our hearts and to give expression to our feeling of\nthankfulness for the services which you, by the grace of God, have been able\nto render to the great cause of Truth by proclaiming India's lofty religious\nideals.\n", "\nWhen the Parliament of Religions was organised at Chicago, some of our\ncountrymen felt naturally anxious that our noble and ancient religion should\nbe worthily represented therein and properly expounded to the American\nnation, and through them to the Western world at large. It was then our\nprivilege to meet you and to realise once again, what has so often proved\ntrue in the history of nations, that with the hour rises the man who is to\nhelp forward the cause of Truth. When you undertook to represent Hinduism at\nthe Parliament of Religions, most of us felt, from what we had known of your\ngreat  gifts,  that  the cause of Hinduism would be ably upheld by its\nrepresentative in that memorable religious assembly. Your representation of\nthe doctrines of Hinduism at once clear, correct, and authoritative, not\nonly produced a remarkable impression at the Parliament of Religions itself,\nbut has also led a number of men and women even in foreign lands to realise\nthat out of the fountain of Indian spirituality refreshing draughts of\nimmortal life and love may be taken so as to bring about a larger, fuller,\nand holier evolution of humanity than has yet been witnessed on this globe\nof ours. We are particularly thankful to you for having called the attention\nof the representatives of the World's Great Religions to the characteristic\nHindu doctrine of the Harmony and Brotherhood of Religions. No longer is it\npossible for really enlightened and earnest men to insist that Truth and\nHoliness are the exclusive possessions of any particular locality or body of\nmen or system of doctrine and discipline, or to hold that any faith or\nphilosophy will survive to the exclusion and destruction of all others. In\nyour own happy language which brings out fully the sweet harmony in the\nheart  of  the  Bhagavad-Git\u00e2, \"The whole world of religions is only a\ntravelling,  a  coming  up  of different men and women through various\nconditions and circumstances to the same goal.\"\n", "\nHad you contented yourself with simply discharging this high and holy duty\nentrusted to your care, even then, your Hindu co-religionists would have\nbeen glad to recognise with joy and thankfulness the inestimable value of\nyour work. But in making your way into Western countries you have also been\nthe bearer of a message of light and peace to the whole of mankind, based on\nthe old teachings of India's \"Religion Eternal\". In thanking you for all\nthat you have done in the way of upholding the profound rationality of the\nreligion of the Vedanta, it gives us great pleasure to allude to the great\ntask you have in view, of establishing an active mission with permanent\ncentres for the propagation of our religion and philosophy. The undertaking\nto  which  you  propose to devote yours energies is worthy of the holy\ntraditions you represent and worthy, too, of the spirit of the great Guru\nwho has inspired your life and its aims. We hope and trust that it may be\ngiven to us also to associate ourselves with you in this noble work. We\nfervently pray to Him who is the all-knowing and all-merciful Lord of the\nUniverse to bestow on you long life and full strength and to bless your\nlabours with that crown of glory and success which ever deserves to shine on\nthe brow of immortal Truth.\n", "\nNext was read the following address from the Maharaja of Khetri:\n", "\n Your Holiness, \n", "\nI wish to take this early opportunity of your arrival and reception at\nMadras to express my feelings of joy and pleasure on your safe return to\nIndia and to offer my heartfelt congratulation on the great success which\nhas attended your unselfish efforts in Western lands, where it is the boast\nof the highest intellects that, \"Not an inch of ground once conquered by\nscience has ever been reconquered by religion\" \u2014 although indeed science has\nhardly ever claimed to oppose true religion. This holy land of \u00c2ry\u00e2varta has\nbeen  singularly  fortunate  in having been able to secure so worthy a\nrepresentative of her sages at the Parliament of Religions held at Chicago,\nand it is entirely due to your wisdom, enterprise, and enthusiasm that the\nWestern  world  has  come to understand what an inexhaustible store of\nspirituality India has even today. Your labours have now proved beyond the\npossibility of doubt that the contradictions of the world's numerous creeds\nare all reconciled in the universal light of the Vedanta, and that all the\npeoples of the world have need to understand and practically realise the\ngreat truth that \"Unity in variety\" is nature's plan in the evolution of the\nuniverse, and that only by harmony and brotherhood among religions and by\nmutual  toleration and help can the mission and destiny of humanity be\naccomplished. Under your high and holy auspices and the inspiring influence\nof your lofty teachings, we of the present generation have the privilege of\nwitnessing the inauguration of a new era in the world's history, in which\nbigotry, hatred, and conflict may, I hope, cease, and peace, sympathy, and\nlove reign among men. And I in common with my people pray that the blessings\nof God may rest on you and your labours.\n", "\nWhen the addresses had been read, the Swami left the hall and mounted to the\nbox seat of a carriage in waiting. Owing to the intense enthusiasm of the\nlarge crowd assembled to welcome him, the Swami was only able to make the\nfollowing short reply, postponing his reply proper to a future occasion:\n", "\nMan proposes and God disposes. It was proposed that the addresses and the\nreplies should be carried in the English fashion. But here God disposes \u2014 I\nam speaking to a scattered audience from a chariot in the Git\u00e2 fashion.\nThankful we are, therefore, that it should have happened so. It gives a zest\nto the speech, and strength to what I am going to tell you. I do not know\nwhether my voice will reach all of you, but I will try my best. I never\nbefore had an opportunity of addressing a large open-air meeting.\n", "\nThe wonderful kindness, the fervent and enthusiastic joy with which I have\nbeen received from Colombo to Madras, and seem likely to be received all\nover India, have passed even my most sanguine expectations; but that only\nmakes me glad, for it proves the assertion which I have made again and again\nin the past that as each nation has one ideal as its vitality, as each\nnation has one particular groove which is to become its own, so religion is\nthe peculiarity of the growth of the Indian mind. In other parts of the\nworld, religion is one of the many considerations, in fact it is a minor\noccupation. In England, for instance, religion is part of the national\npolicy. The English Church belongs to the ruling class, and as such, whether\nthey believe in it or not, they all support it, thinking that it is their\nChurch. Every gentleman and every lady is expected to belong to that Church.\nIt  is  a sign of gentility. So with other countries, there is a great\nnational power; either it is represented by politics or it is represented by\nsome intellectual pursuits; either it is represented by militarism or by\ncommercialism. There the heart of the nation beats, and religion is one of\nthe many secondary ornamental things which that nation possesses.\n", "\nHere in India, it is religion that forms the very core of the national\nheart. It is the backbone, the bed-rock, the foundation upon which the\nnational edifice has been built. Politics, power, and even intellect form a\nsecondary consideration here. Religion, therefore, is the one consideration\nin India. I have been told a hundred times of the want of information there\nis among the masses of the Indian people; and that is true. Landing in\nColombo I found not one of them had heard of the political upheavals going\non in Europe \u2014 the changes, the downfall of ministries, and so forth. Not\none of them had heard of what is meant by socialism, and anarchism, and of\nthis and that change in the political atmosphere of Europe. But that there\nwas a Sannyasin from India sent over to the Parliament of Religions, and\nthat he had achieved some sort of success had become known to every man,\nwoman, and child in Ceylon. It proves that there is no lack of information,\nnor lack of desire for information where it is of the character that suits\nthem, when it falls in line with the necessities of their life. Politics and\nall these things never formed a necessity of Indian life, but religion and\nspirituality have been the one condition upon which it lived and thrived and\nhas got to live in the future.\n", "\nTwo great problems are being decided by the nations of the world. India has\ntaken up one side, and the rest of the world has taken the other side. And\nthe problem is this: who is to survive? What makes one nation survive and\nthe others die? Should love survive or hatred, should enjoyment survive or\nrenunciation, should matter survive or the spirit, in the struggle of life?\nWe think as our ancestors did, away back in pre-historic ages. Where even\ntradition cannot pierce the gloom of that past, there our glorious ancestors\nhave taken up their side of the problem and have thrown the challenge to the\nworld. Our solution is renunciation, giving up, fearlessness, and love;\nthese  are the fittest to survive. Giving up the senses makes a nation\nsurvive. As a proof of this, here is history today telling us of mushroom\nnations  rising  and  falling  almost every century \u2014 starting up from\nnothingness, making vicious play for a few days, and then melting. This big,\ngigantic race which had to grapple with some of the greatest problems of\nmisfortunes, dangers, and vicissitudes such as never fell upon the head of\nany other nation of the world, survives because it has taken the side of\nrenunciation; for without renunciation how can there be religion? Europe is\ntrying to, solve the other side of the problem as to how much a man can\nhave, how much more power a man can possess by hook or by crook, by some\nmeans or other. Competition \u2014 cruel, cold, and heartless \u2014 is the law of\nEurope. Our law is caste \u2014 the breaking of competition, checking its forces,\nmitigating its cruelties, smoothing the passage of the human soul through\nthis mystery of life.\n", "\nAt this stage the crowd became so unmanageable that the Swami could not make\nhimself heard to advantage. He, therefore ended his address with these\nwords:\n", "\nFriends, I am very much pleased with your enthusiasm. It is marvellous. Do\nnot think that I am displeased with you at all; I am, on the other hand,\nintensely pleased at the show of enthusiasm. That is what is required \u2014\ntremendous enthusiasm. Only make it permanent; keep it up. Let not the fire\ndie out. We want to work out great things in India. For that I require your\nhelp; such enthusiasm is necessary. It is impossible to hold this meeting\nany  longer.  I thank you very much for your kindness and enthusiastic\nwelcome.  In  calm  moments we shall have better thoughts and ideas to\nexchange; now for the time, my friends, good-bye.\n", "\nIt is impossible to address you on all sides, therefore you must content\nyourselves this evening with merely seeing me. I will reserve my speech for\nsome other occasion. I thank you very much for your enthusiastic welcome.\n"]
