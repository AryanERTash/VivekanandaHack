["\n", "CLIX\n", "To Sister Christine\n", "921 WEST 21ST STREET,\n", "LOS ANGELES,\n", "9th December 1899.\n", "MY DEAR CHRISTINA,\n", "After all, it is good for me, and good for those I love, that I should come\nhere. Here at last in California! One of our poets says: \"Where is Benares,\nwhere is Kashmir, where Khorasan, where Gujarat! O Tulsi! thus, man's past\nKarma drags him on\". And I am here. After all it is best, isn't it? Are you\ngoing to Boston? I am afraid you are not. I have not unsettled any of your\nplans, have I? \u2014 unnecessary expenses? Well, if any, I will make it up. Only\nthe trouble is yours. I am ashamed of my eccentricities. Well, how are you?\nWhat are you doing? How are things going with you? Sleep if you can; it is\nbetter to sleep than get awakened. I pray that all good may come to thee \u2014\nall peace, all strength to do and suffer. I have a great deal of strength to\ndo, but very little to suffer.\n", "I am so selfish again, always thinking of my own sufferings and paying no\nheed to others. Pray for me; send strong thoughts that I may have strength\nto suffer. I know you will. Now, I mean to remain a few weeks in this city.\nAfter that, \"Mother\" knows. I am physically much better now than I have been\nfor months. The weakness of the heart is nearly gone. The dyspepsia is also\nmuch  better, and [there is] very little. I can walk miles now without\nfeeling it in the heart. If this continues, I expect to have a new lease on\nlife. I am so, so sorry of asking you to come to Boston and flying away. If\nyou are there, I hope you will enjoy the place and the meetings. If you have\ngiven it up \u2014 well, did you take leave and not go to Boston? My! what a\nbungle! Well, I ask a thousand pardons, if such is the case. Things must\nlook brighter anyway, sooner or later. What of these little, few days of\nlife!\n", "How is Mrs. Funke? Loads of love for her. How long a leave [do] you get at\nChristmas? When does it begin? If you feel inclined and willing, write me a\nlong note, will you? But don't tell my friends my whereabouts. I want to be\noff from the world for a time, if I can. Will you kindly send Mr. Freer's\naddress to Mrs. Bull? She needs it. I had a lecture here last night. The\nhall was not crowded, as there was very little ad[vertisement], but a fairly\ngood \u2014 sized audience though. I hope they were pleased. If I feel better, I\nam going to have classes in this city soon. I am on the business path this\ntime, you know. Want a few dollars quick, if I can.\n", "Ever yours in the Lord,\n", "VIVEKANANDA.\n"]
