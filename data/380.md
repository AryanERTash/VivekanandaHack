["\n", "THE SANNYASIN AND THE HOUSEHOLDER\n", "\nThe men of the world should have no voice in the affairs of the Sanny\u00e2sins.\nThe Sannyasin should have nothing to do with the rich, his duty is with the\npoor. He should treat the poor with loving care and serve them joyfully with\nall his might. To pay respects to the rich and hang on them for support has\nbeen  the bane of all the Sannyasin communities of our country. A true\nSannyasin should scrupulously avoid that. Such conduct becomes a public\nwoman rather than one who professes to have renounced the world. How should\na man immersed in K\u00e2ma-K\u00e2nchana (lust and greed) become a devotee of one\nwhose central ideal is the renunciation of Kama-Kanchana? Shri Ramakrishna\nwept and prayed to the Divine Mother to send him such a one to talk with as\nwould not have in him the slightest tinge of Kama-Kanchana; for he would\nsay, \"My lips burn when I talk with the worldly-minded.\" He also used to say\nthat he could not even bear the touch of the worldly-minded and the impure.\nThat King of Sannyasins (Shri Ramakrishna) can never be preached by men of\nthe world. The latter can never be perfectly sincere; for he cannot but have\nsome selfish motives to serve. If Bhagav\u00e2n (God) incarnates Himself as a\nhouseholder, I can never believe Him to be sincere. When a householder takes\nthe position of the leader of a religious sect, he begins to serve his own\ninterests in the name of principle, hiding the former in the garb of the\nlatter, and the result is the sect becomes rotten to the core. All religious\nmovements  headed  by  householders have shared the same fate. Without\nrenunciation religion can never stand.\n", "\nHere  Swamiji  was  asked  \u2014  What  are we Sannyasins to understand by\nrenunciation of Kanchana (wealth)? He answered as follows:\n", "With a view to certain ends we have to adopt certain means. These means vary\naccording to the conditions of time, place, individual, etc.; but the end\nalways remains unaltered. In the case of the Sannyasin, the end is the\nliberation   of   the   Self   and   doing   good   to  humanity  \u2014\n\"\u0906\u0924\u094d\u092e\u0928\u094b \u092e\u094b\u0915\u094d\u0937\u093e\u0930\u094d\u0925\u0902 \u091c\u0917\u0926\u094d\u0927\u093f\u0924\u093e\u092f \u091a\"; and of the ways to attain it,\nthe  renunciation  of  Kama-Kanchana  is the most important. Remember,\nrenunciation consists in the total absence of all selfish motives and not in\nmere abstinence from external contact, such as avoiding to touch one's money\nkept with another at the same time enjoying all its benefits. Would that be\nrenunciation? For accomplishing the two above-mentioned ends, the begging\nexcursion  would  be  a  great  help to a Sannyasin at a time when the\nhouseholders strictly obeyed the injunctions of Manu and other law-givers,\nby setting apart every day a portion of their meal for ascetic guests.\nNowadays things have changed considerably, especially, as in Bengal, where\nno M\u00e2dhukari[1] \nsystem prevails. Here it would be mere waste of energy to\ntry to live on Madhukari, and you would profit nothing by it. The injunction\nof Bhiksh\u00e2 (begging) is a means to serve the above two ends, which will not\nbe served by that way now. It does not, therefore, go against the principle\nof renunciation under such circumstances if a Sannyasin provides for mere\nnecessaries of life and devotes all his energy to the accomplishment of his\nends for which he took Sannyasa. Attaching too much importance ignorantly to\nthe means brings confusion. The end should never be lost sight of.\n"]
