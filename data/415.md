["\n", "THE NATURE OF THE SOUL AND ITS GOAL\n", "\nThe earliest idea is that a man, when he dies, is not annihilated. Something\nlives and goes on living even after the man is dead. Perhaps it would be\nbetter  to compare the three most ancient nations\u2014the Egyptians, the\nBabylonians, and the ancient Hindus\u2014and take this idea from all of them.\nWith the Egyptians and the Babylonians, we find a sort of soul idea\u2014that\nof a double. Inside this body, according to them, there is another body\nwhich is moving and working here; and when the outer body dies, the double\ngets out and lives on for a certain length of time; but the life of the\ndouble is limited by the preservation of the outer body. If the body which\nthe double has left is injured in any part, the double is sure to be injured\nin  that  part.  That  is why we find among the ancient Egyptians such\nsolicitude to preserve the dead body of a person by embalming, building\npyramids, etc. We find both with the Babylonians and the ancient Egyptians\nthat this double cannot live on through eternity; it can, at best, live on\nfor a certain time only, that is, just so long as the body it has left can\nbe preserved.\n", "\nThe next peculiarity is that there is an element of fear connected with this\ndouble. It is always unhappy and miserable; its state of existence is one of\nextreme pain. It is again and again coming back to those that are living,\nasking for food and drink and enjoyments that it can no more have. It is\nwanting to drink of the waters of the Nile, the fresh waters which it can no\nmore drink. It wants to get back those foods it used to enjoy while in this\nlife; and when it finds it cannot get them, the double becomes fierce,\nsometimes  threatening the living with death and disaster if it is not\nsupplied with such food.\n", "\nComing to Aryan thought, we at once find a very wide departure. There is\nstill the double idea there, but it has become a sort of spiritual body; and\none great difference is that the life of this spiritual body, the soul, or\nwhatever you may call it, is not limited by the body it has left. On the\ncontrary, it has obtained freedom from this body, and hence the peculiar\nAryan custom of burning the dead. They want to get rid of the body which the\nperson  has  left, while the Egyptian wants to preserve it by burying,\nembalming, and building pyramids. Apart from the most primitive system of\ndoing away with the dead, amongst nations advanced to a certain extent, the\nmethod of doing away with the bodies of the dead is a great indication of\ntheir idea of the soul. Wherever we find the idea of a departed soul closely\nconnected with the idea of the dead body, we always find the tendency to\npreserve the body, and we also find burying in some form or other. On the\nother hand, with those in whom the idea has developed that the soul is a\nseparate entity from the body and will not be hurt if the dead body is even\ndestroyed, burning is always the process resorted to. Thus we find among all\nancient Aryan races burning of the dead, although the Parsees changed it to\nexposing the body on a tower. But the very name of the tower (Dakhma) means\na burning-place, showing that in ancient times they also used to burn their\nbodies. The other peculiarity is that among the Aryans there was no element\nof fear with these doubles. They are not coming down to ask for food or\nhelp; and when denied that help, they do not become ferocious or try to\ndestroy those that are living. They rather are joyful, are glad at getting\nfree. The fire of the funeral pyre is the symbol of disintegration. The\nsymbol is asked to take the departed soul gently up and to carry it to the\nplace where the fathers live, where there is no sorrow, where there is joy\nfor ever, and so on.\n", "\nOf these two ideas we see at once that they are of a similar nature, the one\noptimistic, and the other pessimistic\u2014being the elementary. The one is the\nevolution of the other. It is quite possible that the Aryans themselves had,\nor  may  have  had, in very ancient times exactly the same idea as the\nEgyptians. In studying their most ancient records, we find the possibility\nof this very idea. But it is quite a bright thing, something bright. When a\nman dies, this soul goes to live with the fathers and lives there enjoying\ntheir happiness. These fathers receive it with great kindness; this is the\nmost ancient idea in India of a soul. Later on, this idea becomes higher and\nhigher. Then it was found out that what they called the soul before was not\nreally the soul. This bright body, fine body, however fine it might be, was\na body after all; and all bodies must be made up of materials, either gross\nor  fine. Whatever had form or shape must be limited, and could not be\neternal. Change is inherent in every form. How could that which is changeful\nbe eternal? So, behind this bright body, as it were, they found something\nwhich was the soul of man. It was called the \u00c2tman, the Self. This Self idea\nthen began. It had also to undergo various changes. By some it was thought\nthat this Self was eternal; that it was very minute, almost as minute as an\natom; that it lived in a certain part of the body, and when a man died, his\nSelf went away, taking along with it the bright body. There were other\npeople who denied the atomic nature of the soul on the same ground on which\nthey had denied that this bright body was the soul.\n", "\nOut of all these various opinions rose S\u00e2nkhya philosophy, where at once we\nfind immense differences. The idea there is that man has first this gross\nbody; behind the gross body is the fine body, which is the vehicle of the\nmind, as it were; and behind even that is the Self, the Perceiver, as the\nS\u00e2nkhyas call it, of the mind; and this is omnipresent. That is, your soul,\nmy soul, everyone's soul is everywhere at the same time. If it is formless,\nhow can it be said to occupy space? Everything that occupies space has form.\nThe formless can only be infinite. So each soul is everywhere. The second\ntheory put forward is still more startling. They all saw in ancient times\nthat human beings are progressive, at least many of them. They grew in\npurity and power and knowledge; and the question was asked: Whence was this\nknowledge, this purity, this strength which men manifested? Here is a baby\nwithout any knowledge. This baby grows and becomes a strong, powerful, and\nwise man. Whence did that baby get its wealth of knowledge and power? The\nanswer was that it was in the soul; the soul of the baby had this knowledge\nand power from the very beginning. This power, this purity, this strength\nwere in that soul, but they were unmanifested; they have become manifested.\nWhat is meant by this manifestation or unmanifestation? That each soul is\npure and perfect, omnipotent and omniscient, as they say in the Sankhya; but\nit can manifest itself externally only according to the mind it has got. The\nmind is, as it were, the reflecting mirror of the soul. My mind reflects to\na certain extent the powers of my soul; so your soul, and so everyone's.\nThat mirror which is clearer reflects the soul better. So the manifestation\nvaries according to the mind one possesses; but the souls in themselves are\npure and perfect.\n", "\nThere was another school who thought that this could not be. Though souls\nare pure and perfect by their nature, this purity and perfection become, as\nthey  say, contracted at times, and expanded at other times. There are\ncertain actions and certain thoughts which, as it were, contract the nature\nof the soul; and then also other thoughts and acts, which bring its nature\nout, manifest it. This again is explained. All thoughts and actions that\nmake the power and purity of the soul get contracted are evil actions, evil\nthoughts; and all those thoughts and actions which make the soul manifest\nitself\u2014make the powers come out, as it were\u2014are good and moral actions.\nThe difference between the two theories is very slight; it is more of less a\nplay on the words expansion and contraction. The one that holds that the\nvariation  only  depends  on  the  mind the soul has got is the better\nexplanation, no doubt, but the contracting and expanding theory wants to\ntake refuge behind the two words; and they should be asked what is meant by\ncontraction of soul, or expansion. Soul is a spirit. You can question what\nis meant by contraction or expansion with regard to material, whether gross\nwhich we call matter, or fine, the mind; but beyond that, if it is not\nmatter, that which is not bound by space or by time, how to explain the\nwords contraction and expansion with regard to that? So it seems that this\ntheory which holds that the soul is pure and perfect all the time, only its\nnature is more reflected in some minds than in others, is the better. As the\nmind changes, its character grows, as it were, more and more clear and gives\na better reflection of the soul. Thus it goes on, until the mind has become\nso purified that it reflects fully the quality of the soul; then the soul\nbecomes liberated.\n", "\nThis is the nature of the soul. What is the goal? The goal of the soul among\nall the different sects in India seems to be the same. There is one idea\nwith all, and that is liberation. Man is infinite; and this limitation in\nwhich he exists now is not his nature. But through these limitations he is\nstruggling upward and forward until he reaches the infinite, the unlimited,\nhis birthright, his nature. All these combinations and recombinations and\nmanifestations that we see round us are not the aim or the goal, but merely\nby the way and in passing. These combinations as earths and suns, and moons\nand stars, right and wrong, good and bad, our laughter and our tears, our\njoys and sorrows, are to enable us to gain experience through which the soul\nmanifests its perfect nature and throws off limitation. No more, then, is it\nbound by laws either of internal or external nature. It has gone beyond all\nlaw, beyond all limitation, beyond all nature. Nature has come under the\ncontrol of the soul, not the soul under the control of nature, as it thinks\nit is now. That is the one goal that the soul has; and all the succeeding\nsteps  through which it is manifesting, all the successive experiences\nthrough which it is passing in order to attain to that goal\u2014freedom\u2014are\nrepresented as its births. The soul is, as it were, taking up a lower body\nand trying to express itself through that. It finds that to be insufficient,\nthrows it aside, and a higher one is taken up. Through that it struggles to\nexpress itself. That also is found to be insufficient, is rejected, and a\nhigher one comes; so on and on until a body is found through which the soul\nmanifests its highest aspirations. Then the soul becomes free.\n", "\nNow the question is: If the soul is infinite and exists everywhere, as it\nmust  do, if it is a spirit, what is meant by its taking up bodies and\npassing through body after body? The idea is that the soul neither comes nor\ngoes, neither is born nor dies. How can the omnipresent be born? It is\nmeaningless nonsense to say that the soul lives in a body. How can the\nunlimited live in a limited space? But as a man having a book in his hands\nreads one page and turns it over, goes to the next page, reads that, turns\nit over, and so on, yet it is the book that is being turned over, the pages\nthat are revolving, and not he\u2014he is where he is always\u2014even so with\nregard to the soul. The whole of nature is that book which the soul is\nreading. Each life, as it were, is one page of that book; and that read, it\nis turned over, and so on and on, until the whole of the book is finished,\nand that soul becomes perfect, having got all the experiences of nature. Yet\nat the same time it never moved, nor came, nor went; it was only gathering\nexperiences. But it appears to us that we are moving. The earth is moving,\nyet we think that the sun is moving instead of the earth, which we know to\nbe a mistake, a delusion of the senses. So is also this, delusion that we\nare born and that we die, that we come or that we go. We neither come nor\ngo, nor have we been born. For where is the soul to go? There is no place\nfor it to go. Where is it not already?\n", "\nThus the theory comes of the evolution of nature and the manifestation of\nthe soul. The processes of evolution, higher and higher combinations, are\nnot in the soul; it is already what it is. They are in nature. But as nature\nis evolving forward into higher and higher combinations, more and more of\nthe majesty of the soul is manifesting itself. Suppose here is a screen, and\nbehind the screen is wonderful scenery. There is one small hole in the\nscreen through which we can catch only a little bit of that scenery behind.\nSuppose that hole becomes increased in size. As the hole increases in size,\nmore and more of the scenery behind comes within the range of vision; and\nwhen the whole screen has disappeared, there is nothing between the scenery\nand you; you see the whole of it. This screen is the mind of man. Behind it\nis the majesty, the purity, the infinite power of the soul, and as the mind\nbecomes clearer and clearer, purer and purer, more of the majesty of the\nsoul manifests itself. Not that the soul is changing, but the change is in\nthe screen. The soul is the unchangeable One, the immortal, the pure, the\never-blessed One.\n", "\nSo, at last, the theory comes to this. From the highest to the lowest and\nmost wicked man, in the greatest of; human beings and the lowest of crawling\nworms  under  our  feet,  is  the soul, pure and perfect, infinite and\never-blessed. In the worm that soul is manifesting only an infinitesimal\npart of its power and purity, and in the greatest man it is manifesting most\nof it. The difference consists in the degree of manifestation, but not in\nthe essence. Through all beings exists the same pure and perfect soul.\n", "\nThere are also the ideas of heavens and other places, but these are thought\nto be second-rate. The idea of heaven is thought to be a low idea. It arises\nfrom the desire for a place of enjoyment. We foolishly want to limit the\nwhole universe with our present experience. Children think that the whole\nuniverse is full of children. Madmen think the whole universe a lunatic\nasylum, and so on. So those to whom this world is but sense-enjoyment, whose\nwhole life is in eating and feasting, with very little difference between\nthem and brute beasts\u2014such are naturally found to conceive of places where\nthey will have more enjoyments, because this life is short. Their desire for\nenjoyment is infinite, so they are bound to think of places where they will\nhave unobstructed enjoyment of the senses; and we see, as we go on, that\nthose who want to go to such places will have to go; they will dream, and\nwhen this dream is over, they will be in another dream where there is plenty\nof sense-enjoyment; and when that dream breaks, they will have to think of\nsomething else. Thus they will be driving about from dream to dream.\n", "\nThen comes the last theory, one more idea about the soul. If the soul is\npure and perfect in its essence and nature, and if every soul is infinite\nand omnipresent, how is it that there can be many souls? There cannot be\nmany infinites. There cannot be two even, not to speak of many. If there\nwere two infinites, one would limit the other, and both become finite. The\ninfinite can only be one, and boldly the last conclusion is approached\u2014\nthat it is but one and not two.\n", "\nTwo birds are sitting on the same tree, one on the top, the other below,\nboth of most beautiful plumage. The one eats the fruits, while the other\nremains, calm and majestic, concentrated in its own glory. The lower bird is\neating fruits, good and evil, going after sense-enjoyments; and when it eats\noccasionally a bitter fruit, it gets higher and looks up and sees the other\nbird sitting there calm and majestic, neither caring for good fruit nor for\nbad, sufficient unto itself, seeking no enjoyment beyond itself. It itself\nis enjoyment; what to seek beyond itself? The lower bird looks at the upper\nbird and wants to get near. It goes a little higher; but its old impressions\nare  upon  it, and still it goes about eating the same fruit. Again an\nexceptionally bitter fruit comes; it gets a shock, looks up. There the same\ncalm and majestic one! It comes near but again is dragged down by past\nactions,  and  continues to eat the sweet and bitter fruits. Again the\nexceptionally bitter fruit comes, the bird looks up, gets nearer; and as it\nbegins to get nearer and nearer, the light from the plumage of the other\nbird is reflected upon it. Its own plumage is melting away, and when it has\ncome sufficiently near, the whole vision changes. The lower bird never\nexisted, it was always the upper bird, and what it took for the lower bird\nwas only a little bit of a reflection.\n", "\nSuch is the nature of the soul. This human soul goes after sense-enjoyments,\nvanities of the world; like animals it lives only in the senses, lives only\nin momentary titillations of the nerves. When there comes a blow, for a\nmoment the head reels, and everything begins to vanish, and it finds that\nthe world was not what it thought it to be, that life was not so smooth. It\nlooks upward and sees the infinite Lord a moment, catches a glimpse of the\nmajestic  One,  comes a little nearer, but is dragged away by its past\nactions. Another blow comes, and sends it back again. It catches another\nglimpse of the infinite Presence, comes nearer, and as it approaches nearer\nand nearer, it begins to find out that its individuality\u2014its low, vulgar,\nintensely selfish individuality\u2014is melting away; the desire to sacrifice\nthe whole world to make that little thing happy is melting away; and as it\ngets gradually nearer and nearer, nature begins to melt away. When it has\ncome sufficiently near, the whole vision changes, and it finds that it was\nthe other bird, that this infinity which it had viewed as from a distance\nwas its own Self, this wonderful glimpse that it had got of the glory and\nmajesty was its own Self, and it indeed was that reality. The soul then\nfinds  That  which is true in everything. That which is in every atom,\neverywhere present, the essence of all things, the God of this universe\u2014\nknow that thou art He, know that thou art free.\n", "\n"]
