["\n", "CHAPTER IV\n", "ON THE WAY TO KATHGODAM\n", "JUNE 11.\n", "On Saturday morning we left Almora. It took us two days and a half to reach\nKathgodam. . . .\n", "Somewhere en route near a curious old water-mill and deserted forge, the\nSwami told Dhira Mata of a legend that spoke of this hill-side as haunted by\na race of centaur-like phantoms, and of an experience known to him by which\none had first seen forms there and only afterwards heard the folk tale.\n", "The roses were gone by this time, but a flower was in bloom that crumbled at\na touch, and he pointed this out because of its wealth of associations in\nIndian poetry.\n", "JUNE 12.\n", "On Sunday afternoon we rested near the plains in what we took to be an\nout-of-the-way hotel above a lake and fall, and there he translated for us\nthe Rudra prayer:\n", "From the unreal lead us to the Real.\nFrom darkness lead us unto light.\nFrom death lead us to immortality.\nReach us through and through our self.\nAnd evermore protect us \u2014 O Thou Terrible! \u2014\nFrom ignorance, by Thy sweet, compassionate face.\n", "He hesitated a long time over the fourth line, thinking of rendering it,\n\"Embrace us in the heart of our heart\". But at last he put his perplexity to\nus, saying shyly, \"The real meaning is, Reach us through and through our\nself\". He had evidently feared that this sentence, with its extraordinary\nintensity, might not make good sense in English. . . . I have understood\nthat a more literal rendering would be, \"O Thou who art manifest only unto\nThyself, manifest Thyself also unto us!\" I now regard his translation as a\nrapid and direct transcript of the experience of Sam\u00e2dhi itself. It tears\nthe living heart out of the Sanskrit, as it were, and renders it again in an\nEnglish form.\n", "It was indeed an afternoon of translations, and he gave us fragments of the\ngreat benediction after mourning, which is one of the most beautiful of the\nHindu sacraments:\n", "The blissful winds are sweet to us.\nThe seas are showering bliss on us.\nMay the corn in our fields bring bliss to us.\nMay the plants and herbs bring bliss to us.\nMay the cattle give us bliss.\nO Father in Heaven, be Thou blissful unto us!\nThy very dust of the earth is full of bliss.\n", "And then, the voice dying down into meditation:\n", "It is all bliss \u2014 all bliss \u2014 all bliss.\n", "And again we had Surad\u00e2sa's song, which the Swami heard from the nautch-girl\nat Khetri:\n", "O Lord, look not upon my evil qualities!\nThy name, O Lord, is Same-Sightedness.\nMake of us both the same Brahman! . . .\n", "Was it that same day or some other that he told us of the old Sanny\u00e2sin in\nBenares who saw him annoyed by troops of monkeys and, afraid that he might\nturn and run, shouted, \"Always face the brute!\"?\n", "Those journeys were delightful. We were always sorry to reach a destination.\nAt this time it took us a whole afternoon to cross the Terai by rail \u2014 that\nstrip of malarial country on which, as he reminded us, Buddha had been\nborn.\n", "As we had come down the mountain roads, we had met parties of country-folk\nfleeing to the upper hills with their families and all their goods, to\nescape the fever which would be upon them with the rains. And now in the\ntrain there was the gradual change of vegetation to watch and the Master\u2019s\npleasure,  greater than that of any proprietor, in showing us the wild\npeacocks, or here and there an elephant or a train of camels. . . .\n"]
