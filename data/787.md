["\n", "XLII\n", "ALMORA,\n", "29th July, 1897.\n", "MY DEAR MISS NOBLE,\n", "A  letter  from Sturdy reached me yesterday, informing me that you are\ndetermined to come to India and see things with your own eyes. I replied to\nthat yesterday, but what I learnt from Miss Muller about your plans makes\nthis further note necessary, and it is better that it should be direct.\n", "Let me tell you frankly that I am now convinced that you have a great future\nin the work for India. What was wanted was not a man, but a woman \u2014 a real\nlioness \u2014 to work for the Indians, women specially.\n", "India  cannot yet produce great women, she must borrow them from other\nnations. Your education, sincerity, purity, immense love, determination, and\nabove all, the Celtic blood make you just the woman wanted.\n", "Yet the difficulties are many. You cannot form any idea of misery, the\nsuperstition, and the slavery that are here. You will be in the midst of a\nmass of half-naked men and women with quaint ideas of caste and isolation,\nshunning the white skin through fear or hatred and hated by them intensely.\nOn the other hand, you will be looked upon by the white as a crank, and\nevery one of your movements will be watched with suspicion.\n", "Then the climate is fearfully hot; our winter in most places being like your\nsummer, and in the south it is always blazing.\n", "Not one European comfort is to be had in places out of the cities. If in\nspite  of all this, you dare venture into the work, you are welcome, a\nhundred times welcome. As for me, I am nobody here as elsewhere, but what\nlittle influence I have shall be devoted to your service.\n", "You must think well before you plunge in; and after work, if you fail in\nthis or get disgusted, on my part I promise you, I will stand by you unto\ndeath whether you work for India or not, whether you give up Vedanta or\nremain in it. \"The tusks of the elephant come out, but never go back\"; so\nare the words of a man never retracted. I promise you that. Again, I must\ngive you a bit of warning. You must stand on your own feet and not be under\nthe wings of Miss Muller or anybody else. Miss Muller is a good lady in her\nown way, but unfortunately it got into her head, when she was a girl, that\nshe was a born leader and that no other qualifications were necessary to\nmove world but money! This idea is coming on the surface again and again in\nspite of herself, and you will find it impossible to pull on with her in a\nfew  days. She now intends to take a house in Calcutta for herself and\nyourself and other European or American friends who may come.\n", "It is very kind and good of her, but her Lady Abbess plan will never be\ncarried out for two reasons \u2014 her violent temper and overbearing conduct,\nand  her  awfully  vacillating mind. Friendship with many is best at a\ndistance, and everything goes well with the person who stands on his own\nfeet.\n", "Mrs. Sevier is a jewel of a lady \u2014so good, so kind! The Seviers are the only\nEnglish people who do not hate the natives, Sturdy not excepted. Mr. and\nMrs. Sevier are the only persons who did not come to patronise us, but they\nhave no fixed plans yet. When you come, you may get them to work with you,\nand that will be really helpful to them and to you. But after all it is\nabsolutely necessary to stand on one's own feet.\n", "I learn from America that two friends of mine, Mrs. Ole Bull of Boston and\nMiss MacLeod, are coming on a visit to India this autumn. Miss MacLeod you\nalready know in London, that Paris-dressed young American lady; Mrs. Ole\nBull is about fifty and has been a kind friend to me in America. I may\nsuggest  that  your joining the party may while away the tedium of the\njourney, as they also are coming by way of Europe.\n", "I am glad to receive a note at least from Sturdy after long. But it was so\nstiff and cold. It seems he is disappointed at the collapse of the London\nwork.\n", "With everlasting love,\n", "Yours ever in the Lord,\n", "\nVIVEKANANDA.\n"]
