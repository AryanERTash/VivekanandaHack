["\n", "(RECORDED BY MISS S. E. WALDO, A DISCIPLE)\n", "WEDNESDAY, June 26, 1895.\n", "Our  best work is done, our greatest influence is exerted, when we are\nwithout thought of self. All great geniuses know this. Let us open ourselves\nto the one Divine Actor, and let Him act, and do nothing ourselves. \"O\nArjuna! I have no duty in the whole world\", says Krishna. Be perfectly\nresigned, perfectly unconcerned; then alone can you do any true work. No\neyes can see the real forces, we can only see the results. Put out self,\nlose it, forget it; just let God work, it is His business. We have nothing\nto do but stand aside and let God work. The more we go away, the more God\ncomes in. Get rid of the little \"I\", and let only the great \"I\" live.\n", "We are what our thoughts have made us; so take care of what you think. Words\nare secondary. Thoughts live, they travel far. Each thought we think is\ntinged with our own character, so that for the pure and holy man, even his\njests or abuse will have the twist of his own love and purity and do good.\n", "Desire nothing; think of God and look for no return. It is the desireless\nwho bring results. The begging monks carry religion to every man's door; but\nthey  think  that  they  do nothing, they claim nothing, their work is\nunconsciously done. If they should eat of the tree of knowledge, they would\nbecome egoists, and all the good they do would fly away. As soon as we say\n\"I\", we are humbugged all the time; and we call it \"knowable\", but it is\nonly going round and round like a bullock tied to a tree. The Lord has\nhidden Himself best, and His work is best; so he who hides himself best,\naccomplishes most. Conquer yourself, and the whole universe is yours.\n", "In the state of Sattva we see the very nature of things, we go beyond the\nsenses and beyond reason. The adamantine wall that shuts us in is egoism; we\nrefer everything to ourselves, thinking. \"I do this, that, and the other.\"\nGet rid of this puny \"I\"; kill this diabolism in us; \"Not I, but Thou\" \u2014 say\nit, feel it, live it. Until we give up the world manufactured by the ego,\nnever can we enter the kingdom of heaven. None ever did, none ever will. To\ngive up the world is to forget the ego, to know it not at all \u2014 living in\nthe body, but not of it. This rascal ego must be obliterated. Bless men when\nthey revile you. Think how much good they are doing you; they can only hurt\nthemselves. Go where people hate you, let them thrash the ego out of you,\nand you will get nearer to the Lord. Like the mother-monkey, we hug our\n\"baby\", the world, as long as we can, but at last when we are driven to put\nit under our feet and step on it[6]* then we are ready to come to God.\nBlessed it is to be persecuted for the sake of righteousness. Blessed are we\nif we cannot read, we have less to take us away from God.\n", "Enjoyment is the million-headed serpent that we must tread under foot. We\nrenounce and go on, then find nothing and despair; but hold on, hold on. The\nworld is a demon. It is a kingdom of which the puny ego is king. Put it away\nand stand firm. Give up lust and gold and fame and hold fast to the Lord,\nand at last we shall reach a state of perfect indifference. The idea that\nthe  gratification  of  the  senses  constitutes  enjoyment  is purely\nmaterialistic. There is not one spark of real enjoyment there; all the joy\nthere is, is a mere reflection of the true bliss.\n", "Those who give themselves up to the Lord do more for the world than all the\nso-called workers. One man who has purified himself thoroughly accomplishes\nmore than a regiment of preachers. Out of purity and silence comes the word\nof power.\n", "\"Be like a lily \u2014 stay in one place and expand your petals; and the bees\nwill come of themselves.\" There was a great contrast between Keshab Chandra\nSen and Shri Ramakrishna. The second never recognised any sin or misery in\nthe world, no evil to fight against. The first was a great ethical reformer,\nleader,  and founder of the Brahmo-Samaj. After twelve years the quiet\nprophet of Dakshineswar had worked a revolution not only in India, but in\nthe world. The power is with the silent ones, who only live and love and\nthen withdraw their personality. They never say \"me\" and \"mine\"; they are\nonly blessed in being instruments. Such men are the makers of Christs and\nBuddhas, ever living fully identified with God, ideal existences, asking\nnothing, and not consciously doing anything. They are the real movers, the\nJivanmuktas, (Literally, free even while living.) absolutely selfless, the\nlittle personality entirely blown away, ambition non-existent. They are all\nprinciple, no personality.\n"]
