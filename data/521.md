["\n", "LX\n", "\n54 W. 33rd ST., NEW YORK,\n14th Feb., 1895.\n", "DEAR MRS. BULL,\n", "Accept my heartfelt gratitude for your motherly advice. I hope I will be\nable to carry out them in life.\n", "How can I express my gratitude to you for what you have already done for me\nand  my  work, and my eternal gratitude to you for your offering to do\nsomething more this year. But I sincerely believe that you ought to turn all\nyour help to Miss Farmer's Greenacre work this year. India can wait as she\nis waiting centuries and an immediate work at hand should always have the\npreference.\n", "Again, according to Manu, collecting funds even for a good work is not good\nfor a Sannyasin, and I have begun to feel that the old sages were right.\n\"Hope is the greatest misery, despair is the greatest happiness.\" It appears\nlike a hallucination. I am getting out of them. I was in these childish\nideas of doing this and doing that.\n", "\"Give up all desire and be at peace. Have neither friends nor foes, and live\nalone.  Thus  shall we travel having neither friends nor foes, neither\npleasure nor pain, neither desire nor jealousy, injuring no creatures, being\nthe  cause of injury to no creatures \u2014 from mountain to mountain, from\nvillage to village, preaching the name of the Lord.\"\n", "\"Seek no help from high or low, from above or below. Desire nothing \u2014 and\nlook upon this vanishing panorama as a witness and let it pass.\"\n", "Perhaps these mad desires were necessary to bring me over to this country.\nAnd I thank the Lord for the experience.\n", "I am very happy now. Between Mr. Landsberg and me, we cook some rice and\nlentils or barley and quietly eat it, and write something or read or receive\nvisits from poor people who want to learn something, and thus I feel I am\nmore a Sannyasin now than I ever was in America.\n", "\"In wealth is the fear of poverty, in knowledge the fear of ignorance, in\nbeauty the fear of age, in fame the fear of backbiters, in success the fear\nof jealousy, even in body is the fear of death. Everything in this earth is\nfraught  with  fear. He alone is fearless who has given up everything\"\n(Vair\u00e2gya-Shatakam, 31).\n", "I went to see Miss Corbin the other day, and Miss Farmer and Miss Thursby\nwere also there. We had a nice half-hour and she wants me to hold some\nclasses in her home from next Sunday.\n", "I am no more seeking for these things. If they come, the Lord be blessed, if\nnot, blessed more be He.\n", "Again accept my eternal gratitude.\n", "Your devoted son,\n", "\nVIVEKANANDA.\n"]
