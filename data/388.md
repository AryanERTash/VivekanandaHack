["\n", "ON THE VEDANTA PHILOSOPHY\n", "\nThe Vedantist says that a man is neither born nor dies nor goes to heaven,\nand that reincarnation is really a myth with regard to the soul. The example\nis given of a book being turned over. It is the book that evolves, not the\nman. Every soul is omnipresent, so where can it come or go? These births and\ndeaths are changes in nature which we are mistaking for changes in us.\n", "\nReincarnation is the evolution of nature and the manifestation of the God\nwithin.\n", "\nThe Vedanta says that each life is built upon the past, and that when we can\nlook back over the whole past we are free. The desire to be free will take\nthe form of a religious disposition from childhood. A few years will, as it\nwere,  make all truth clear to one. After leaving this life, and while\nwaiting for the next, a man is still in the phenomenal.\n", "\nWe would describe the soul in these words: This soul the sword cannot cut,\nnor  the  spear  pierce;  the  fire  cannot  burn  nor  water melt it;\nindestructible, omnipresent is this soul. Therefore weep not for it.\n", "\nIf it has been very bad, we believe that it will become good in the time to\ncome. The fundamental principle is that there is eternal freedom for every\none. Every one must come to it. We have to struggle, impelled by our desire\nto be free. Every other desire but that to be free is illusive. Every good\naction, the Vedantist says, is a manifestation of that freedom.\n", "\nI do not believe that there will come a time when all the evil in the world\nwill vanish. How could that be? This stream goes on. Masses of water go out\nat one end, but masses are coming in at the other end.\n", "\nThe Vedanta says that you are pure and perfect, and that there is a state\nbeyond good and evil, and that is your own nature. It is higher even than\ngood. Good is only a lesser differentiation than evil.\n", "\nWe have no theory of evil. We call it ignorance.\n", "\nSo far as it goes, all dealing with other people, all ethics, is in the\nphenomenal world. As a most complete statement of truth, we would not think\nof  applying such things as ignorance to God. Of Him we say that He is\nExistence, Knowledge, and Bliss Absolute. Every effort of thought and speech\nwill make the Absolute phenomenal and break Its character.\n", "\nThere is one thing to be remembered: that the assertion\u2014I am God\u2014cannot\nbe made with regard to the sense-world. If you say in the sense-world that\nyou are God, what is to prevent your doing wrong? So the affirmation of your\ndivinity  applies  only  to the noumenal. If I am God, I am beyond the\ntendencies of the senses and will not do evil. Morality of course is not the\ngoal of man, but the means through which this freedom is attained. The\nVedanta says that Yoga is one way that makes men realise this divinity. The\nVedanta says this is done by the realisation of the freedom within and that\neverything  will  give way to that. Morality and ethics will all range\nthemselves in their proper places.\n", "\nAll the criticism against the Advaita philosophy can be summed up in this,\nthat it does not conduce to sense-enjoyments; and we are glad to admit that.\n", "\nThe Vedanta system begins with tremendous pessimism, and ends with real\noptimism. We deny the sense-optimism but assert the real optimism of the\nSupersensuous.  That real happiness is not in the senses but above the\nsenses; and it is in every man. The sort of optimism which we see in the\nworld is what will lead to ruin through the senses.\n", "\nAbnegation has the greatest importance in our philosophy. Negation implies\naffirmation  of the Real Self. The Vedanta is pessimistic so far as it\nnegatives the world of the senses, but it is optimistic in its assertion of\nthe real world.\n", "\nThe Vedanta recognises the reasoning power of man a good deal, although it\nsays there is something higher than intellect; but the road lies through\nintellect.\n", "\nWe need reason to drive out all the old superstitions; and what remains is\nVedantism. There is a beautiful Sanskrit poem in which the sage says to\nhimself: \"Why weepest thou, my friend? There is no fear nor death for thee.\nWhy  weepest  thou? There is no misery for thee, for thou art like the\ninfinite blue sky, unchangeable in thy nature. Clouds of all colours come\nbefore it, play for a moment, and pass away; it is the same sky. Thou hast\nonly to drive away the clouds.\"\n", "\nWe have to open the gates and clear the way. The water will rush in and fill\nin by its own nature, because it is there already.\n", "\nMan is a good deal conscious, partly unconscious, and there is a possibility\nof getting beyond consciousness. It is only when we become men that we can\ngo beyond all reason. The words higher or lower can be used only in the\nphenomenal world. To say them of the noumenal world is simply contradictory,\nbecause there is no differentiation there. Man-manifestation is the highest\nin the phenomenal world. The Vedantist says he is higher than the Devas. The\ngods will all have to die and will become men again, and in the man-body\nalone they will become perfect.\n", "\nIt is true that we create a system, but we have to admit that it is not\nperfect, because the reality must be beyond all systems. We are ready to\ncompare it with other systems and are ready to show that this is the only\nrational system that can be; but it is not perfect, because reason is not\nperfect. It is, however, the only possible rational system that the human\nmind can conceive.\n", "\nIt is true to a certain extent that a system must disseminate itself to be\nstrong. No system has disseminated itself so much as the Vedanta. It is the\npersonal contact that teaches even now. A mass of reading does not make men;\nthose who were real men were made so by personal contact. It is true that\nthere are very few of these real men, but they will increase. Yet you cannot\nbelieve that there will come a day when we shall all be philosophers. We do\nnot believe that there will come a time when there will be all happiness and\nno unhappiness.\n", "\nNow and then we know a moment of supreme bliss, when we ask nothing, give\nnothing,  know nothing but bliss. Then it passes, and we again see the\npanorama of the universe moving before us; and we know that it is but a\nmosaic work set upon God, who is the background of all things.\n", "\nThe Vedanta teaches that Nirv\u00e2na can be attained here and now, that we do\nnot have to wait for death to reach it. Nirvana is the realisation of the\nSelf; and after having once known that, if only for an instant, never again\ncan one be deluded by the mirage of personality. Having eyes, we must see\nthe apparent, but all the time we know what it is; we have found out its\ntrue nature. It is the screen that hides the Self, which is unchanging. The\nscreen opens, and we find the Self behind it. All change is in the screen.\nIn the saint the screen is thin, and the reality can almost shine through.\nIn the sinner the screen is thick, and we are liable to lose sight of the\ntruth  that the Atman is there, as well as behind the saint's screen. When\nthe screen is wholly removed, we find it really never existed\u2014that we were\nthe Atman and nothing else, even the screen is forgotten.\n", "\nThe two phases of this distinction in life are\u2014first, that the man who\nknows the real Self, will not be affected by anything; secondly, that that\nman alone can do good to the world. That man alone will have seen the real\nmotive of doing good to others, because there is only one, it cannot be\ncalled egoistic, because that would be differentiation. It is the only\nselflessness. It is the perception of the universal, not of the individual.\nEvery case of love and sympathy is an assertion of this universal. \"Not I,\nbut thou.\" Help another because you are in him and he is in you, is the\nphilosophical way of putting it. The real Vedantist alone will give up his\nlife for a fellow-man without any compunction, because he knows he will not\ndie. As long as there is one insect left in the world, he is living; as long\nas one mouth eats, he eats. So he goes on doing good to others; and is never\nhindered by the modern ideas of caring for the body. When a man reaches this\npoint of abnegation, he goes beyond the moral struggle, beyond everything.\nHe sees in the most learned priest, in the cow, in the dog, in the most\nmiserable places, neither the learned man, nor the cow, nor the dog, nor the\nmiserable place, but the same divinity manifesting itself in them all. He\nalone is the happy man; and the man who has acquired that sameness has, even\nin this life, conquered all existence. God is pure; therefore such a man is\nsaid to be living in God. Jesus says, \"Before Abraham was, I am.\" That means\nthat Jesus and others like him are free spirits; and Jesus of Nazareth took\nhuman form, not by the compulsion of his past actions, but just to do good\nto mankind. It is not that when a man becomes free, he will stop and become\na dead lump; but he will be more active than any other being, because every\nother being acts only under compulsion, he alone through freedom.\n", "\nIf we are inseparable from God, have we no individuality? Oh, yes: that is\nGod. Our individuality is God. This is not the individuality you have now;\nyou are coming towards that. Individuality means what cannot be divided. How\ncan you call this individuality? One hour you are thinking one way, and the\nnext hour another way, and two hours after, another way. Individuality is\nthat which changes not\u2014is beyond all things, changeless. It would be\ntremendously dangerous for this state to remain in eternity, because then\nthe thief would always remain a thief and the blackguard a blackguard. If a\nbaby died, he would have to remain a baby. The real individuality is that\nwhich never changes and will never change; and that is the God within us.\n", "\nVedantism is an expansive ocean on the surface of which a man-of-war could\nbe near a catamaran. So in the Vedantic ocean a real Yogi can be by the side\nof an idolater or even an atheist. What is more, in the Vedantic ocean, the\nHindu, Mohammedan, Christian, and Parsee are all one, all children of the\nAlmighty God.\n", "\n"]
