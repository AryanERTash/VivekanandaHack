["\n", "SECTS AND DOCTRINES IN INDIA\n", "(Harvard Crimson, May 17, 1894)\n", "\nSwami Vivekananda, the Hindoo monk, gave an address last evening in Sever\nHall under the auspices of the Harvard Religious Union. The address was very\ninteresting, the clear and eloquent voice of the speaker, and his low,\nearnest delivery making his words singularly impressive.\n", "\nThere are various sects and doctrines in India, said Vivekananda, some of\nwhich accept the theory of a personal God, and others which believe that God\nand the universe are one; but whatever sect the Hindoo belongs to he does\nnot say that his is the only right belief, and that all others must be\nwrong. He believes that there are many ways of coming to God; that a man who\nis truly religious rises above the petty quarrels of sects or creed. In\nIndia if a man believes that he is a spirit, a soul, and not a body, then he\nis said to have religion and not till then.\n", "\nTo become a monk in India it is necessary to lose all thought of the body;\nto look upon other human beings as souls. So monks can never marry. Two vows\nare taken when a man becomes a monk, poverty and chastity. He is not allowed\nto receive or possess any money whatever. The first ceremony to be performed\non joining the order is to be burnt in effigy, which supposed to destroy\nonce for all the old body, name and caste. The man then receives a new name,\nand is allowed to go forth and preach or travel, but must take no money for\nwhat he does.\n"]
