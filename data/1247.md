HOWRAH, BENGAL,\n", "2nd September 1901.\n", "MY DEAR CHRISTINE,\n", "I have been looking at one of your old photos \u2014 the only one you sent four\nor five years ago; and then I remember how changed and reduced you looked\nlast summer; and it came to me that you must be awfully thin now, as it\nseems very hard for you to get rid of anxieties. This is simply foolish.\nThings will, of course, take their shape. We only make ourselves miserable\nby moping. It is very hard to manipulate one's philosophy to contribute to\none's daily need. So it is with you, as with me. But it is easiest to take\nthe  teacher's  chair  and read a lecture. And that has been my life's\nbusiness!! Indeed, that is the reason why there are more disciples up to the\nmark than teachers. The upshot of all this is that you must create a huge\nappetite,  then gorge, then sleep and grow fat, fat, fat. Plump is the\nEnglish word, is it not?\n", "As for me, I am very happy. Of course, Bengal brings the asthma now and\nthen, but it is getting tame, and the terrible things \u2014 Bright's disease and\ndiabetes \u2014 have disappeared altogether. Life in any dry climate will stop\nthe asthma completely, I am sure. I get reduced, of course, during a fit,\nbut then it takes me no time to lay on a few layers of fat. I have a lot of\ncows, goats, a few sheep, dogs, geese, ducks, one tame gazelle, and very\nsoon I am going to have some milk buffaloes. These are not your American\nbison, but huge things \u2014 hairless, half-aquatic in habits, and [that] give\nan enormous quantity of very rich milk.\n", "Within the last few months, I got two fits [of asthma] by going to two of\nthe dampest hill stations in Bengal \u2014 Shillong and Darjeeling. I am not\ngoing to try the Bengalee mountains any more.\n", "Mrs. Bull and Nivedita are in Norway. I don't know when they [will] come\nover to India. Miss MacLeod is in Japan. I have not heard from her [for] a\nlong while. They all are expected here in November, and will have a \"hot\ntime in this old town\"[6]* etc. I pray you can come, and the Mother will\nopen the door for it. I cannot but say my prayers mostly have been heard, up\nto date.\n", "Well now, Christina, send me one of your latest photos next mail, will you?\nI want to see how much of fat you have accumulated in one year.\n", "Anyhow,  I  will  have  to  go  to  America with Mrs. Bull, I am sure.\n[Excision][7]* By the by, excusez-moi,[8]*\nour Calcutta is never so hot as your Detroit or New York, with its added\nadvantage \u2014 we are not required by our society to wear many things. The old\nGreeks used to think that wearing too many clothes and [feeling] shame to\nshow any part of the body a peculiarity of barbarians! So the Hindus think,\ndown to the present day. We are the most scantily clothed people in the\nwhole world. Bless the Lord! How one would live otherwise in our climate!\n", "3rd September \u2014\n", "I left the letter unfinished last night. The foreign English mail starts day\nafter tomorrow. So begin again. The moon is not up yet, but there is a\nsunless glow upon the river. Our mighty Ganges (She is indeed mighty now,\nduring the rains) is splashing against the walls of the house. Numerous tiny\nboats are flitting up and down in the dark; they have come to fish for our\nshads, which come up the river this season.\n", "How I wish you were here to taste our shads \u2014 one of the most delicate fish\nin the world. It is raining outside \u2014 pouring. But the moment this downpour\nceases, I rain through every pore \u2014 it is so hot yet. My whole body is\ncovered by big patches of prickly heat. Thank goodness there are no ladies\nabout! If I had to cover myself in this state of things, I surely would go\ncrazy.\n", "I have also my theme, but I am not despondent. I am sure very soon to pan it\nout into a beautiful ecstasy [excision]. I am half crazy by nature; then my\novertaxed nerves make me outrageous now and then. As a result I don't find\nanybody who would patiently bear with me! I am trying my best to make myself\ngentle as a lamb. I hope I shall succeed in some birth. You are so gentle.\nSometimes I did frighten you very much, did I not, Christina? I wish I were\nas gentle as you are. Mother knows which is best.\n", "I  would  not take any supper tonight, as I ate rather heartily of the\naforesaid shad! Then I have to think, think, think on my theme; and some\nsubjects I think best in bed because the whole is made clear to me in dream.\nTherefore, I am going to bed, and gute Nacht, bon soir,[9]* etc., etc. No,\nno, it is now about 10 a.m. in Detroit. Therefore, a very happy day to you.\nMay all good realities reach you today while I am expecting dreams.\n", "Ever yours with love and blessings,\n", "VIVEKANANDA.\n"]
