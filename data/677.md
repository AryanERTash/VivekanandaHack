["\n", "(RECORDED BY MISS S. E. WALDO, A DISCIPLE)\n", "MONDAY, July 29, 1895.\n", "We sometimes indicate a thing by describing its surroundings. When we say\n\"Sachchidananda\" (Existence-Knowledge-Bliss), we are merely indicating the\nshores of an indescribable Beyond. Not even can we say \"is\" about it, for\nthat too is relative. Any imagination, any concept is in vain. Neti, neti\n(\"Not this, not this\") is all that can be said, for even to think is to\nlimit and so to lose.\n", "The senses cheat you day and night. Vedanta found that out ages ago; modern\nscience is just discovering the same fact. A picture has only length and\nbreadth, and the painter copies nature in her cheating by artificially\ngiving  the appearance of depth. No two people see the same world. The\nhighest  knowledge will show you that there is no motion, no change in\nanything; that the very idea of it is all Maya. Study nature as a whole,\nthat is, study motion. Mind and body are not our real self; both belong to\nnature, but eventually we can know the ding an sich.  Then mind and body\nbeing transcended, all that they conceive goes. When you cease utterly to\nknow and see the world, then you realise Atman. The superseding of relative\nknowledge is what we want. There is no infinite mind or infinite knowledge,\nbecause both mind and knowledge are limited. We are now seeing through a\nveil; then we reach the \"x\", which is the Reality of all our knowing.\n", "If we look at a picture through a pin-hole in a cardboard, we get an utterly\nmistaken notion; yet what we see is really the picture. As we enlarge the\nhole, we get a clearer and clearer idea. Out of the reality we manufacture\nthe different views in conformity with our mistaken perceptions of name and\nform. When we throw away the cardboard, we see the same picture, but we see\nit as it is. We put in all the attributes, all the errors; the picture\nitself is unaltered thereby. That is because Atman is the reality of all;\nall we see is Atman, but not as we see it, as name and form; they are all in\nour veil, in Maya.\n", "They are like spots in the object-glass of a telescope, yet it is the light\nof the sun that shows us the spots; we could not even see the illusion save\nfor the background of reality which is Brahman. Swami Vivekananda is just\nthe speck on the object-glass; I am Atman, real, unchangeable, and that\nreality alone enables me to see Swami Vivekananda. Atman is the essence of\nevery hallucination; but the sun is never identified with the spots on the\nglass, it only shows them to us. Our actions, as they are evil or good,\nincrease or decrease the \"spots\"; but they never affect the God within us.\nPerfectly cleanse the mind of spots and instantly we see, \"I and my father\nare one\".\n", "We first perceive, then reason later. We must have this perception as a\nfact, and it is called religion, realisation. No matter if one never heard\nof creed or prophet or book. Let him get this realisation, and he needs no\nmore. Cleanse the mind, this is all of religion; and until we ourselves\nclear off the spots, we cannot see the Reality as it is. The baby sees no\nsun; he has not yet the measure of it in himself. Get rid of the defects\nwithin yourself, and you will not be able to see any without. A baby sees\nrobbery done, and it means nothing to him. Once you find the hidden object\nin a puzzle picture, you see it ever more; so when once you are free and\nstainless, you see only freedom and purity in the world around. That moment\nall the knots of the heart are cut asunder, all crooked places are made\nstraight, and this world vanishes as a dream. And when we awake, we wonder\nhow we ever came to dream such trash!\n", "\"Getting whom, misery mountain high has no power to move the soul.\"\n", "With the axe of knowledge cut the wheels asunder, and the Atman stands free,\neven though the old momentum carries on the wheel of mind and body. The\nwheel can now only go straight, can only do good. If that body does anything\nbad, know that the man is not Jivanmukta; he lies if he makes that claim.\nBut  it  is only when the wheels have got a good straight motion (from\ncleansing the mind) that the axe can be applied. All purifying action deals\nconscious or unconscious blows on delusion. To call another a sinner is the\nworst thing you can do. Good action done ignorantly produces the same result\nand helps to break the bondage.\n", "To identify the sun with the spots on the object-glass is the fundamental\nerror. Know the sun, the \"I\", to be ever unaffected by anything, and devote\nyourself to cleansing the spots. Man is the greatest being that ever can be.\nThe highest worship there is, is to worship man as Krishna, Buddha, Christ.\nWhat you want, you create. Get rid of desire. . . .\n", "The angels and the departed are all here, seeing this world as heaven. The\nsame \"x\" is seen by all according to their mental attitude. The best vision\nto be had of the \"x\" is here on this earth. Never want to go to heaven, that\nis the worst delusion. Even here, too much wealth and grinding poverty are\nboth bondages and hold us back from religion. Three great gifts we have:\nfirst, a human body. (The human mind is the nearest reflection of God, we\nare \"His own image\".) Second, the desire to be free. Third, the help of a\nnoble soul, who has crossed the ocean of delusion, as a teacher. When you\nhave these three, bless the Lord; you are sure to be free.\n", "What you only grasp intellectually may be overthrown by a new argument; but\nwhat you realise is yours for ever. Talking, talking religion is but little\ngood. Put God behind everything \u2014 man, animal, food, work; make this a\nhabit.\n", "Ingersoll once said to me: \"I believe in making the most out of this world,\nin squeezing the orange dry, because this world is all we are sure of.\" I\nreplied: \"I know a better way to squeeze the orange of this world than you\ndo, and I get more out of it. I know I cannot die, so I am not in a hurry; I\nknow there is no fear, so I enjoy the squeezing. I have no duty, no bondage\nof wife and children and property; I can love all men and women. Everyone is\nGod to me. Think of the joy of loving man as God! Squeeze your orange this\nway and get ten thousandfold more out of it. Get every single drop.\"\n", "That which seems to be the will is the Atman behind, it is really free.\n", "MONDAY AFTERNOON.\n", "Jesus was imperfect because he did not live up fully to his own ideal, and\nabove all because he did not give woman a place equal to man. Women did\neverything for him, and yet he was so bound by the Jewish custom that not\none was made an apostle. Still he was the greatest character next to Buddha,\nwho in his turn was not fully perfect. Buddha, however, recognised woman's\nright to an equal place in religion, and his first and one of his greatest\ndisciples was his own wife, who became the head of the whole Buddhistic\nmovement among the women of India. But we ought not to criticise these great\nones, we should only look upon them as far above ourselves. Nonetheless we\nmust not pin our faith to any man, however great; we too must become Buddhas\nand Christs.\n", "No man should be judged by his defects. The great virtues a man has are his\nespecially, his errors are the common weaknesses of humanity and should\nnever be counted in estimating his character.\n", "*            *            *\n", "Vira, the Sanskrit word for \"heroic\", is the origin of our word \"virtue\",\nbecause in ancient times the best fighter was regarded as the most virtuous\nman.\n"]
